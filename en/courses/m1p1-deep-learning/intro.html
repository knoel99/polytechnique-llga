Introduction and overview

Status in the programRecommended — LLGA track M1 · S1 / Period 1 (fall semester, from 17/09/26 to 18/12/26)

This course builds, from perceptrons to transformers, the toolkit that today carries most of applied AI: deep networks, backpropagation, CNNs, RNNs, attention, generative models. The LLMs that give the LLGA program its name are transformers: without this module, neither their architecture, nor their training, nor their fine-tuning is understandable. The course offers as a guiding thread a "graph" reading of the architectures — a deep network is a computation graph, and attention will be reread as a GNN in Period 2.

What you will be able to do

  • Build a neural network from scratch: layers, activations, initialization, and understand what depth buys.
  • Unroll backpropagation by hand on a small network, and code it in PyTorch without copying a tutorial.
  • Diagnose a failing training run: gradient explosion/vanishing, learning rate, normalization, numerical stabilization.
  • Choose an architecture according to the data: CNNs for images, RNN/LSTM for sequences, transformers for everything else.
  • Explain the attention mechanism and the transformer architecture, up to the explicit link with GNNs.
  • Distinguish the generative families: autoencoders, VAEs, GANs — their objectives, their losses, their uses.
  • Adapt a pretrained model to a new task by transfer learning and fine-tuning, with the right layer freezing/tuning reflexes.
  • Launch a realistic training run: GPU, mixed precision, experiment tracking, reproducibility.

Where this course fits in the program

Recommended option of the LLGA track in Year 1 · Period 1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), right after the Machine Learning module, of which it is the natural continuation: the perceptron is reread there as a linear model, optimization as stochastic gradient descent. The official 2026-2027 sheet publishes no formal prerequisites ("not disclosed"); the positioning below describes the foundations actually assumed. The two refreshers — Refresher in Statistics and Refresher in Computer Science — are assumed acquired, notably NumPy matrix computation for the labs.

Downstream, this module is the mandatory gateway to the whole "large models" part of the program: Introduction to Text Mining and NLP and Large Language Models (M2) build directly on the "Attention and transformers" chapter; Multimodal Generative AI extends the generative models (VAE, GAN) and fine-tuning. The attention chapter is also reread as a GNN in the Graph Machine and Deep Learning for Generative AI module: a transformer processes a set of tokens as a complete graph, and this reading unifies the two sides of the program.

The "Training in practice" chapter finally prepares the large-scale projects of Period 2 and the internships: this is where research code becomes engineering code.

Course outline

  • Review: matrix differential calculus and numerical stability: gradients of matrix functions and conditioning — the background that avoids 80% of training bugs.
  • From the neuron to the deep network: perceptron, activations, initialization, expressivity of depths — and, in closing (section 1.4), the reinforcement learning paradigm required by the official syllabus.
  • Backpropagation: the chain rule on a computation graph, unrolled then automated.
  • Optimization in practice: SGD and variants (momentum, Adam), learning-rate scheduling, batch normalization.
  • Convolutional networks (CNN): convolution, pooling, classical architectures — learned vision.
  • Sequences: RNN, LSTM: processing the sequential before attention, and understanding their limits.
  • Attention and transformers: the mechanism underlying LLMs, also read as message passing on a graph.
  • Generative models: AE, VAE, GAN: representing and generating data, three strategies compared.
  • Transfer learning and fine-tuning: reusing a pretrained model — the most frequent professional gesture.
  • Research of the teaching team: the research of Michalis Vazirgiannis, Johannes Lutzeyer and Ye Zhu, from transformers to graphs.
  • Training in practice: GPU, mixed precision, experiment tracking — from research code to reproducible code.

How to study this module

  • Take the review chapter seriously: numerical stability and matrix computation are presented there because they condition backpropagation, the pivotal chapter of the course.
  • Unroll backpropagation by hand on a two-layer network before coding it: this is the exercise that separates those who understand from those who copy.
  • Run all the notebooks: the course alternates derivation and experiment, and each chapter has its PyTorch lab whose reflexes (shapes, dtypes, device) serve until M2.
  • After the attention chapter, redraw the transformer diagram from memory and identify where a "graph" would be: this reading will be asked of you again in the Graph ML module.
  • Actually fine-tune a small pretrained model on your own data: the transfer learning chapter is a gesture, not a reading.
  • If you have a gap in optimization or generalization, go back to the Machine Learning module: chapters 2 and 3 here are its direct continuation.
A word from the teaching team The LLGA program draws its identity from the crossing of LLMs and graphs: this module is its hub, since the transformer can already be read here as a neural network on a graph. Whether you aim for large-model engineering jobs or research, hands-on mastery of training — not only of architecture — is the common expectation.
