9. Research of the teaching team

This chapter connects the course to the current research of the three instructors, all three rooted in the previous chapters. The arXiv references are in the bibliography.

9.1. Michalis Vazirgiannis: from the "Graph of Words" to textual GNNs

Worked example — Graph of Words (Rousseau & Vazirgiannis, CIKM 2013) The bag-of-words representation throws away syntactic and order structure; the word graph restores it at almost no cost:
  • Construction: each distinct term of the document = one node; a sliding window of size \(w\) over the text creates an edge between two terms if they co-occur in the window (weighted by the co-occurrence frequency). The document becomes a small, sparse weighted graph.
  • From TF-IDF to TW-IDF: in a bag of words, the weight of a term is its frequency; in a word graph, it is its centrality — TW-IDF weights the term by its average weighted degree across documents — the group then used the k-core decomposition of the word graph for keyword extraction (Rousseau, Kiagias & Vazirgiannis, ECIR 2015). "Ad hoc" retrieval gains over TF-IDF: structurally important words emerge even when they are rare.
  • Positioning with respect to the course: this is the first building block of the program — the data itself becomes a graph. The NLP module reuses these representations; the Graph ML module generalizes them.
GNN lineage: texts, expressivity, generation
  • Inductive text classification via GNNs (Skianis, Rousseau & Vazirgiannis, ACL 2020): each document is its own word graph; a GNN applied to the document graph can then process words never seen at training time (inductivity) whereas TextGCN (Yao et al., AAAI 2019) built a single corpus graph.
  • DGoW-GNN (Abbahaddou, Lutzeyer & Vazirgiannis, NeurIPS 2023): discriminative word graphs — the edges are no longer fixed co-occurrences but are filtered/learned for the task, then a GNN propagates the representations. The loop is closed with chapter 2: the computation graph (backprop) and the data graph (message passing) answer each other.
  • PathNN (Michel, Nikolentzos & Vazirgiannis, ICML 2023): going beyond classical message passing by aggregating paths (shortest paths, simple paths) emanating from each node; two variants are strictly more expressive than the Weisfeiler–Lehman test. This is the language of the Graph ML module, expanded in Advanced GNN (M2).

9.2. Johannes Lutzeyer: where are the parameters? Expressivity and position

Sparsifying the update step (Lutzeyer, Wu & Vazirgiannis, 2021) An MPNN (message passing neural network) chains two phases: the aggregation of neighbor messages and an "update step" — precisely the linear transformation + non-linearity of chapter 1, applied node by node. The paper's question: what remains if one sparsifies this trainable step, initializing it directly sparse (ExpanderGNN, with a tunable sparsity rate) or even reducing it to a non-linearity alone (Activation-Only GNN)? Key result: performance comparable to dense models with far fewer parameters — a direct empirical insight into how expressivity is distributed between aggregation (structure, not parametrized) and the update (parametrized), in the spirit of ch. 1 (what, in a network, carries the expressivity?).
Positional encodings on graphs (SHAKE-GNN, Cui, Lutzeyer et al., 2025) Chapter 6 showed that a transformer needs a positional encoding because attention is permutation-invariant. On a graph, the problem is deeper: there is no canonical ordering of the nodes — the "position" of a node is a structural notion (Laplacian spectrum, distances, spanning forests). SHAKE-GNN proposes hierarchical encodings based on Kirchhoff forests, computed in a scalable way. The parallel to remember: RoPE (relative absolute position, suited to sequences) vs spectral/structural encodings (position defined by the geometry of the graph); both answer the same fundamental invariance (exercise 4). The Advanced GNN module (M2) systematizes this question.

9.3. Ye Zhu: steering diffusion models

From the loss to semantic control Chapter 7 left diffusion in sketch form (noising/denoising); the work of Ye Zhu shows how to steer it:
  • BoundaryDiffusion (Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2023): control the semantics of generation without retraining (learning-free), by warping the decision boundaries of the model's latent space in the guidance plane — an elegant point of contact with prop. 7.6: where the GAN sought an equilibrium, here already-learned latent regions are moved geometrically.
  • Discrete contrastive diffusion (ICLR 2023) and Diffusion in Diffusion (ICLR 2024): discrete cross-modal music-image diffusion and cyclic text-vision conditioning — the argument of the ch. 7 table ("multimodal") made concrete.
  • NoiseQuery (ICCV 2025): the initial noise \(z_T\) as an implicit control signal for goal-guided generation — it reverses the perspective: the "noise" becomes a structured input, which connects to the sensitivity to the initial noise studied for GANs/diffusion.
This work directly extends ch. 7 and is expanded in the Multimodal Generative AI module.
