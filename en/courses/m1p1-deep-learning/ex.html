Exercises

  1. Backprop on paper. Recover the formulas of example 2.2 (two layers, ReLU), then check them numerically by finite differences \(\frac{\mathcal{L}(\theta + \epsilon e_j) - \mathcal{L}(\theta - \epsilon e_j)}{2\epsilon}\) on a small network.
  2. Activation. Show that if \(\sigma\) is linear, an MLP of arbitrary depth represents exactly the affine maps. Deduce why linear cascades are useless.
  3. He initialization. Recover the factor 2 of He initialization: for \(h = \mathrm{ReLU}(a)\) with \(a\) symmetric zero-mean, compute \(\mathbb{E}[h^2]\) then the variance of the next layer.
  4. 1D attention by hand. For \(n = 3\) tokens, \(d_k = 2\), compute by hand \(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k}\), the row-wise softmax, and the result \(\mathrm{softmax}(\cdot)V\). Implement it, compare, and check that the rows of the attention matrix sum to 1.
  5. Permutation-invariance. Check experimentally that a transformer block without positional encoding is invariant to the permutation of tokens, and that adding a positional encoding breaks this invariance.
  6. Quadratic SGD. For \(A = \mathrm{diag}(4, 1)\), \(b = 0\), \(\theta_0 = (1, 1)\): write the sequence \((\theta_k)\) for \(\eta = 1/2\) then \(\eta = 1\), and verify theorem 3.1 (per-mode contraction, bound \(2/L\)).
  7. Adam bias correction. Prove proposition 3.5 for \(v_t\) (second moment), then compute the update \(\hat m_1/\sqrt{\hat v_1}\) at the first step and explain why the warmup protects.
  8. Convolution arithmetic. A \(32 \times 32\) input, three successive convs (5×5, stride 2, padding 2; 3×3, stride 1, padding 1; 3×3, stride 1, padding 1, dilation 2): intermediate output sizes and final receptive field (proposition 4.4).
  9. Gaussian KL. Derive the formula of proposition 7.4 in dimension 1, plot \(\mathrm{KL}(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) \| \mathcal{N}(0,1))\) as a function of \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\), and locate the minimum.
  10. Gaussian GAN. For \(p_{\mathrm{data}} = \mathcal{N}(0, 1)\) and \(p_G = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\), write the logit of \(D^\star\) (difference of the log-densities) and check that it is a trinomial in \(x\). Where does the equilibrium lie?
  11. LoRA. Count the trainable parameters of a GPT-like model with 12 blocks (\(d = 768\), attention + MLP) adapted by LoRA \(r = 8\) on the attention projections only, and compare with full fine-tuning.
  12. Project. In PyTorch: classify MNIST with (a) an MLP, (b) a small CNN, (c) a mini-transformer on patches (16 patches of 4×4). Plot the train/val curves, compare the parameter counts and accuracy, comment with the bias-variance decomposition, then apply the protocol of chapter 10 (overfitting a batch, LR range test).
Solutions and hints (even-numbered exercises)

Ex. 2. By induction: if \(h^{(\ell)} = \sigma(W^{(\ell)} h^{(\ell-1)} + b^{(\ell)})\) with \(\sigma(t) = \alpha t\), then \(h^{(\ell)} = \alpha W^{(\ell)} h^{(\ell-1)} + \alpha b^{(\ell)}\) remains affine in \(h^{(0)} = x\) — composing, \(f_\theta(x) = W x + c\) with \(W = \prod_\ell \alpha W^{(\ell)}\): a single matrix, all the deep layers collapse into one.  Ex. 4. The row-wise softmax makes each row a vector of positive weights of sum 1; the multiplication by \(V\) makes each output a weighted average of the values: this is a convex interpolation, exactly the message passing of chapter 6.  Ex. 6. Decoupled modes: \((\theta_k)_1 = (1 - 4\eta)^k\), \((\theta_k)_2 = (1-\eta)^k\). For \(\eta = 1/2\): factors \(-1\) (oscillation without decay: exactly \(\eta = 2/L\), limiting case) and \(1/2\). For \(\eta = 1\): factors \(-3\) (divergence: \(\eta > 2/L\)) and \(0\) (convergence in one step: \(\eta = 1/\lambda_2\)).  Ex. 8. Theorem 4.2: \(32 \to 16 \to 16 \to 14\) (the third one: \(\lfloor (16 + 2 - 2\cdot 2 - 1)/1 \rfloor + 1 = 14\)). Receptive field (proposition 4.4, with dilation: replace \((k-1)\) by \(d(k-1)\)): \(\mathrm{RF}_1 = 5\), \(\mathrm{RF}_2 = 5 + 2 \cdot 2 = 9\), \(\mathrm{RF}_3 = 9 + (2 \cdot 2)\cdot 2 = 17\) pixels, for a product of strides \(\prod s = 2\).  Ex. 10. Unique global minimum at \((\mu, \sigma) = (0, 1)\); the logit of \(D^\star\) is \(\frac{x^2}{2}\big(\frac{1}{\sigma^2} - 1\big) - \frac{x\mu}{\sigma^2} + \frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2} + \log\sigma\): a trinomial in \(x\), identically zero at the equilibrium \((\mu, \sigma) = (0, 1)\).

Hints (odd-numbered exercises)

Ex. 1. Take \(\epsilon \sim 10^{-6}\) in float64 for the check; the error of the central finite difference is \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)\).  Ex. 3. \(\mathbb{E}[h^2] = \mathbb{E}[a^2 \mathbf{1}_{a>0}] = \tfrac12\mathbb{E}[a^2]\) by symmetry of \(a\): the variance of the next layer is halved, hence \(\mathrm{Var}(W) = 2/n_{\mathrm{in}}\).  Ex. 5. Permute the rows of the input batch (\(x[\mathrm{perm}]\)) and compare the outputs up to a transformation: the property is exact (not an approximation), the softmax being unchanged by re-indexing.  Ex. 7. Re-derive the geometric sum with \(g_k^2\) constant; at step 1, \(\hat m_1 = g_1\) and \(\hat v_1 = g_1^2\) — the ratio is \(\mathrm{sign}(g_1)\), a step of size \(\eta\) whether \(g_1\) is tiny or huge: hence the caution of the warmup when \(g_1\) is atypical.  Ex. 9. The minimum of \(\tfrac12(\mu^2 + \sigma^2 - 1 - \log\sigma^2)\) zeroes \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\) (derivative with respect to \(\sigma^2\): \(\tfrac12(1 - 1/\sigma^2) = 0\)).  Ex. 11. Per block: 4 attention matrices \(d \times d\), i.e. \(4 \times 2 \times 8 \times 768 = 49{,}152\) LoRA parameters, versus \(4 \times 768^2 \approx 2.36\) M for full — and the MLP is not adapted; LoRA total ~0.6 M for ~85 M full.
