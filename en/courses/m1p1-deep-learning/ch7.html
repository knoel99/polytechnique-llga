7. Generative models: AE, VAE, GAN

7.1. Deterministic autoencoder and its pathology

An autoencoder compresses \(x\) into a code \(z = E_\phi(x)\) and reconstructs \(\hat x = D_\theta(z)\), trained on \(\|x - \hat x\|^2\). Useful for representation learning though it is, it is not a generative model: the latent space has no imposed structure — sampling \(z\) at random gives aberrant reconstructions. The VAE corrects precisely this.

7.2. VAE: the ELBO fully derived

Definition 7.1 — VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013) Diagonal Gaussian encoder \(q_\phi(z \mid x) = \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_\phi(x), \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2(x))\big)\), decoder \(p_\theta(x \mid z)\), prior \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\). One maximizes the ELBO (evidence lower bound): $$\mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi; x) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\big[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z \mid x)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0, I)\big).$$
Theorem 7.2 — Exact decomposition of the log-likelihood $$\log p_\theta(x) = \mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi; x) + \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\big\|\,p_\theta(z \mid x)\big) \;\ge\; \mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi; x).$$ Maximizing the ELBO = maximizing a lower bound of \(\log p_\theta(x)\) and bringing \(q_\phi(z|x)\) closer to the exact posterior \(p_\theta(z|x)\).
Start from the definition of the KL between \(q_\phi(\cdot \mid x)\) and the posterior \(p_\theta(\cdot \mid x)\), both over \(z\) (at fixed \(x\)), and expand with \(p_\theta(x, z) = p_\theta(x \mid z)\, p(z)\) (Bayes): $$\mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi \| p_\theta(\cdot|x)\big) = \mathbb{E}_q[\log q] - \mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x|z)] - \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(z)] + \log p_\theta(x).$$ Now, by definition of the KL against the prior, \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log q] - \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(z)] = \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi \| \mathcal{N}(0,I))\). Substituting: $$\mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi \| p_\theta(\cdot|x)\big) = \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi \| \mathcal{N}) - \mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x|z)] + \log p_\theta(x),$$ that is, rearranging: $$\log p_\theta(x) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x|z)] - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi \| \mathcal{N})}_{=\ \mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi;x)} + \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi \| p_\theta(\cdot|x)\big).$$ The KL is nonnegative (Jensen applied to \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log(q/p)] \ge -\log \mathbb{E}_q[p/q] = 0\)), giving the bound. Equality iff \(q_\phi(z|x) = p_\theta(z|x)\) exactly. ∎
Reading the proof One cannot optimize \(\log p_\theta(x) = \log \int p_\theta(x|z)\, p(z)\, dz\) directly: the integral is intractable. The ELBO is the only quantity that requires only (i) samples from \(q_\phi\) (Monte Carlo: a single one suffices in practice) and (ii) a closed-form KL. It is the same trade-off as in EM (Machine Learning module): the residual KL measures exactly what the bound loses.
Proposition 7.3 — Reparameterization: why \(z = \mu + \sigma \odot \varepsilon\) works If \(\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) and \(z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \varepsilon\), then \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x), \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2(x)))\) and \(\nabla_\phi \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[f(z)] = \mathbb{E}_\varepsilon\big[\nabla_\phi f(\mu_\phi + \sigma_\phi \odot \varepsilon)\big]\): the gradient gets through. Sampling \(z \sim q_\phi\) directly blocks the gradient (the sampling operation has no derivative with respect to its parameters).
The law of \(z\) is obtained by an affine change of variable (the Jacobian \(\partial z/\partial \varepsilon = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma)\) is constant): the density of \(z\) is that of a Gaussian with mean \(\mu\), covariance \(\mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2)\). For the gradient: the expectation under \(q_\phi\) rewrites as an expectation under the fixed law of \(\varepsilon\), \(\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[f(z)] = \mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon}[f(\mu_\phi + \sigma_\phi \odot \varepsilon)]\); the distribution is no longer parameterized by \(\phi\) — \(\phi\) now appears only in the integrand, which gradient descent knows how to differentiate (theorem 2.1). ∎
Proposition 7.4 — Closed-form KL between diagonal Gaussians For \(q = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2))\) and \(p = \mathcal{N}(0, I)\) in dimension \(k\): $$\mathrm{KL}(q \| p) = \frac12 \sum_{i=1}^{k} \big(\mu_i^2 + \sigma_i^2 - 1 - \log \sigma_i^2\big).$$
\(\mathrm{KL}(q\|p) = \mathbb{E}_q[\log q(z) - \log p(z)]\). Coordinate by coordinate (the densities factorize), with \(\varphi\) the standard Gaussian density: \(\log q(z_i) - \log \varphi(z_i) = -\tfrac12\log\sigma_i^2 - \frac{(z_i - \mu_i)^2}{2\sigma_i^2} + \frac{z_i^2}{2}\). Under \(q\), \(z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)\), so \(\mathbb{E}_q[(z_i - \mu_i)^2] = \sigma_i^2\) and \(\mathbb{E}_q[z_i^2] = \mu_i^2 + \sigma_i^2\). Taking the expectation: \(-\tfrac12\log\sigma_i^2 - \tfrac12 + \tfrac12(\mu_i^2 + \sigma_i^2)\), that is \(\tfrac12(\mu_i^2 + \sigma_i^2 - 1 - \log \sigma_i^2)\). Along the way one checks that each term is \(\ge 0\), zero iff \(\mu_i = 0, \sigma_i = 1\) (study of \(u - 1 - \log u \ge 0\)). ∎
The ELBO trade-off in practice First term: reconstruction. Second: a regularizer that keeps the codes close to the prior — too strong, everything blurs together (codes ignored, blurry sampling); too weak, the latent space develops "holes" (partial posterior collapse). The connection with variational inference and EM is taken up again in the Machine Learning module; the extensions (\(\beta\)-VAE, VQ-VAE) are in Multimodal Generative AI.

7.3. GAN: the minimax game and its equilibrium

Definition 7.5 — GAN (Goodfellow et al., 2014) Minimax game between a generator \(G\) (often \(G(z)\) with \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)\)) and a discriminator \(D : \mathcal{X} \to (0,1)\): $$\min_G \max_D \ V(G, D), \qquad V(G, D) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}\big[\log D(x)\big] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)}\big[\log(1 - D(G(z)))\big].$$
Theorem 7.6 — Optimal discriminator and global equilibrium 1) For fixed \(G\), the maximand reads \(V(G,D) = \int \big[p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\log D(x) + p_G(x) \log(1 - D(x))\big] dx\) (with \(p_G\) the law of \(G(z)\)), maximized pointwise at $$D^\star(x) = \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x) + p_G(x)}.$$ 2) The value of the game is then \(V(G, D^\star) = -\log 4 + 2\,\mathrm{JSD}(p_{\mathrm{data}} \| p_G)\), minimized over \(G\) exactly when \(p_G = p_{\mathrm{data}}\): the global equilibrium exists and coincides with the law of the data.
1) For each fixed \(x\), maximize \(a\log D + b\log(1-D)\) with \(a = p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\), \(b = p_G(x)\): the derivative \(a/D - b/(1-D) = 0\) gives \(D^\star = a/(a+b)\) (strict concavity in \(D \in (0,1)\)). 2) Rewrite \(\log D^\star = \log \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}}{p_{\mathrm{data}}+p_G} = \log\frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}}{m} - \log 2\) and \(\log(1 - D^\star) = \log \frac{p_G}{m} - \log 2\), where \(m = \frac12(p_{\mathrm{data}} + p_G)\). Then $$V(G, D^\star) = \int p_{\mathrm{data}} \log\frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}}{m} + \int p_G \log\frac{p_G}{m} - 2\log 2 = 2\,\mathrm{JSD}(p_{\mathrm{data}} \| p_G) - \log 4,$$ by definition of the Jensen–Shannon divergence (average of the KLs toward \(m\)). Since \(\mathrm{JSD} \ge 0\) with equality if and only if \(p_{\mathrm{data}} = p_G\) almost everywhere (the two laws share the same harmonic mean \(m\)), the minimum \(-\log 4\) is reached exactly at equilibrium. Gaussian case: if \(p_{\mathrm{data}} = \mathcal{N}(\mu_d, \sigma_d^2)\) and \(G(z) = \mu + \sigma z\), then \(p_G = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) and the equilibrium corresponds to the exact parameters \((\mu, \sigma) = (\mu_d, \sigma_d)\); the logit of \(D^\star\), \(\log p_{\mathrm{data}} - \log p_G\), is a degree-2 polynomial in \(x\) — a quadratic discriminator suffices. ∎
Why it is unstable nonetheless The theorem describes the equilibrium, not the dynamics: the two players descend their gradients simultaneously, and if \(D\) becomes too good too fast, the gradient of \(G\) vanishes (\(\log(1 - D)\) saturates). Emblematic pathology: mode collapse — \(G\) concentrates on the "easiest" modes of \(p_{\mathrm{data}}\) (a few very credible faces instead of all of them). Historical remedies: Wasserstein-GAN (transportation distance instead of JSD — cf. Optimal Transport module), gradient penalization, feature matching. In imaging, the family was superseded by diffusion (iterative denoising): Ye Zhu's models (chapter 9) are recent developments of it, and the Multimodal Generative AI module treats them in detail. LLMs, for their part, remain autoregressive: \(\mathbb{P}(x_t \mid x_{<t})\), cross-entropy loss over tokens (LLMs module, M2).
