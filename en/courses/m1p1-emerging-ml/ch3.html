3. Federated learning

Algorithm 3.1 — FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) Clients \(\{1..K\}\) with local data \(\mathcal{D}_k\) that is non-iid (each has its own data, never shared). At each round:
  1. the server broadcasts \(\theta_r\) to a subset of clients;
  2. each client \(k\) performs \(E\) epochs of local SGD: \(\theta_r^{(k)} \approx \arg\min F_k\);
  3. weighted aggregation: \(\theta_{r+1} = \sum_k \tfrac{n_k}{n}\, \theta_r^{(k)}\).
Theorem 3.2 — Client drift: proving it on a quadratic example Heterogeneity makes clients drift: local SGD solves their own problems before aggregation. Exact computation on \(F_k(\theta) = \tfrac{a_k}{2}(\theta - z_k)^2\) (local SGD = exact gradient, step size \(\eta\)):
One local step: \(\theta \leftarrow (1 - \eta a_k)\theta + \eta a_k z_k\), so after \(E\) steps starting from \(\theta_r\) (geometric sum): $$\theta_r^{(k)} = z_k + c_k\,(\theta_r - z_k), \qquad c_k = (1 - \eta a_k)^E.$$ The aggregation \(\theta_{r+1} = \frac1K\sum_k \theta_r^{(k)}\) is an affine iteration whose fixed point satisfies \(\theta_\infty = \frac1K\sum_k (1 - c_k)\, z_k \,\Big/\, \frac1K \sum_k (1 - c_k)\) — a weighted average by the effective curvatures \(1 - c_k\). Now the global optimum of \(F = \frac1K\sum F_k\) is \(\theta^\star = \sum a_k z_k / \sum a_k\). The two coincide only if the \(a_k\) are equal: FedAvg converges to a point that is NOT the global optimum as soon as the curvatures differ. Numbers. \(K=2\), \(a_1 = 1, z_1 = 0\), \(a_2 = 10, z_2 = 1\), \(\eta = 0.05\), \(E = 10\): \(c_1 = 0.95^{10} \approx 0.60\), \(c_2 = 0.5^{10} \approx 0.001\); \(\theta^\star = 10/11 \approx 0.909\), but \(\theta_\infty = \frac{1 \cdot 1}{0.40 + 1} \approx 0.713\): a structural bias of ~20% that does not vanish as the number of rounds grows. ∎
Theorem 3.3 — Convergence of FedAvg (convex setting, statement) Under (A1)-(A3) per client and bounded heterogeneity, the Theorem-0.1-style analysis applied to FedAvg (Li et al., 2020) yields, with \(E\) local steps per round of \(K\) clients: $$\mathbb{E}\big[F(\theta_R)\big] - F^\star \;\leq\; \mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{D^2}{\eta E R}\Big) + \mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\sigma^2}{\mu^2 K E R}\Big) + \mathcal{O}\big(\Gamma\big), \qquad \Gamma = F^\star - \sum_k \frac{n_k}{n} F_k^\star \ \text{(or equivalent)},$$ where the heterogeneity term \(\Gamma > 0\) measures the gap between the global optimum and the local optima: it is the analytic manifestation of Theorem 3.2. The first two terms decrease with the rounds (communication gain: \(E\) steps for 1 exchange); the third is a bias that only a correction (SCAFFOLD) or a penalty (FedProx) can mitigate.
Proposition 3.4 — FedProx: the role of the proximal term FedProx (Li et al., 2020): each client minimizes a regularized objective $$\theta^{(k)} \approx \arg\min_\theta\ F_k(\theta) + \frac{\mu}{2}\|\theta - \theta_r\|^2,$$ then aggregates. The proximal penalty (i) curbs the drift of Theorem 3.2 — the client stays in a neighborhood of \(\theta_r\) of radius \(\propto 1/\mu\); (ii) makes \(E\) arbitrary (tolerance to slow or partial clients — work with what has been received); (iii) can be read as a proximal step of the global problem: in the limit \(\mu \to \infty\), FedAvg with 1 step. Trade-off: too large a \(\mu\) crushes local work (back to communication-slow FedSGD), too small a \(\mu\) lets clients drift.
Algorithm 3.5 — SCAFFOLD: variance correction via control variates (Karimireddy et al., ICML 2020.) The server and the clients maintain control variates \(c\) and \(c_k\) (initially zero). Local step: $$\theta_{j+1}^{(k)} = \theta_j^{(k)} - \eta\big(g_k(\theta_j^{(k)}) - c_k + c\big),$$ update of the client control at the end of the round: $$c_k^{+} = c_k - c + \frac{\theta_r - \theta_E^{(k)}}{\eta E}, \qquad c^{+} = \frac1K \sum_k c_k^{+}.$$ Reading. The term \(-c_k + c\) replaces the local gradient with a "recentered" version: \(c_k - c\) estimates the mean difference between the local gradient and the global gradient — exactly the drift of Theorem 3.2. On the quadratic example above, the correction is exact: the drift disappears and the fixed point becomes \(\theta^\star\) again (exercise 4). Result: the number of rounds to reach \(\varepsilon\) goes from \(\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon^2)\) (FedAvg, with large \(E\)) to \(\mathcal{O}(1/\varepsilon)\) — the same motif as SVRG (Proposition 0.3): a control variate buys a factor of \(\varepsilon\).
Proposition 3.6 — Personalization: Ditto and local fine-tuning When clients want different models (hospitals with distinct practices), heterogeneity becomes a resource: learn a good global model that serves as an initialization for everyone, then personalize. Ditto (Li et al., 2021) solves, per client, $$\min_{\theta_k}\ F_k(\theta_k) + \frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta_k - \theta^\star_{\mathrm{glob}}\|^2$$ jointly with the global problem: \(\lambda\) arbitrates specialization/collaboration — and the solution improves fairness across clients (the worst client is not sacrificed for the benefit of the average client). Pragmatic version: local fine-tuning of the federated model (standard in production, e.g. keyboards); probabilistic version: mixtures of posteriors (see ch. 8, FedEM).
