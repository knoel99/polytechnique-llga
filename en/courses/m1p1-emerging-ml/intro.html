Today's models are no longer trained on a single machine, but on clusters, distributed data, and participants who do not all trust one another. This course covers the "systems" viewpoint of modern machine learning: distributed and federated learning, Byzantine robustness, differential privacy, scaling models and compute. These are precisely the constraints that govern LLM training in industry and in the lab: who sees the data, who does the computation, what happens when a participant is faulty or malicious.

What you will be able to do

  • Analyze distributed stochastic gradient descent: convergence speed, the effect of mini-batch size, step size, and number of workers.
  • Distinguish distributed learning architectures (centralized, local, federated) and their communication/computation trade-offs.
  • Explain the federated learning model (FedAvg) and its assumptions, in particular data heterogeneity across clients.
  • Identify Byzantine failures in a learning cluster and apply the robust aggregation rules that neutralize them.
  • Define differential privacy (ε-differential privacy), apply it to SGD, and interpret the utility/privacy trade-off.
  • Reason about collaboration challenges: per-client personalization, fairness across participants, incentives.
  • Estimate the cost of a training run (scaling models and compute) and read the scaling laws that structure the field.

Where this course fits in the program

Recommended option of the LLGA track in Year 1 · Period 1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS). The official sheet recommends MAP433 for students of the engineering cycle; in practice, this module assumes the optimization foundation of the Machine Learning module, whose opening chapter covers the analysis of SGD — extended here to the distributed case — and the theory of generalization. The two refreshers (Refresher in Statistics, Refresher in Computer Science) are its upstream prerequisites. The course unit is shared with the engineering cycle (X), the MJH master, exchange programs and the MScT IoT and TRAI tracks.

Downstream, this module opens three directions. Privacy and Uncertainty Quantification (M2) extends the differential privacy seen here toward private inference and uncertainty quantification; ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles (M2) picks up scaling and the systems viewpoint for industrializing LLMs; Reinforcement Learning and Autonomous Agents (Period 2) draws on the same questions of collaboration and multiple agents, up to multi-agent RL.

Course outline

  • Review: SGD and its analysis: convergence of stochastic gradient descent, taken from the Machine Learning module and extended to distributed systems.
  • Landscape of emerging topics: a map of the research fronts that structure large-scale ML today.
  • Distributed learning: parallelizing training, synchronizing workers, arbitrating between communication and computation.
  • Federated learning: learning on data that never leaves its owners — the FedAvg framework and its variants.
  • Byzantine robustness: detecting and tolerating faulty or malicious workers in aggregation.
  • Differential privacy: formalizing and guaranteeing the protection of individuals during training.
  • Collaboration, personalization, fairness: learning models together that remain useful to everyone — and fair.
  • Scaling and compute: what large models cost, and what the field's scaling laws say.
  • Research of the teaching team: the research of El Mahdi El Mhamdi (Byzantine robustness) and Aymeric Dieuleveut (stochastic SGD, federated learning), CMAP — the official 2026-2027 team being El Mhamdi & Mangold.

How to study this module

  • First secure your understanding of SGD analysis: the review chapter is short, but mastering it is a prerequisite for all the distributed and federated chapters.
  • For each aggregation scheme (mean, median, trimmed mean), ask yourself "what happens with a malicious worker?" — that is the course's guiding thread.
  • Simulate a small federated learning setup with clients holding heterogeneous data: the gap between homogeneous theory and heterogeneous practice can only be understood by running it.
  • Do the differential privacy exercises on paper: composing mechanisms, computing an ε budget, interpreting a guarantee.
  • Follow the news on scaling laws and compute in parallel with the scaling chapter: that chapter ages the fastest, and critical reading is the targeted skill.
  • If you need a refresher on the basics, go back to the Machine Learning module: optimization and concentration are covered there at the level expected here.
