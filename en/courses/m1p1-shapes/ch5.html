5. Smoothing: from uniform to cotangent Laplacian

Definition 5.1 — Combinatorial Laplacian and smoothing scheme On a closed mesh, the uniform (combinatorial) Laplacian of a vertex \(i\) with neighborhood \(\mathcal{N}(i)\) is \(\delta_i = \dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{N}(i)|}\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} p_j - p_i\), and Laplacian smoothing iterates $$p_i \leftarrow p_i + \lambda\,\delta_i, \qquad \lambda \in (0, 1].$$ This is a local projection method; "mean curvature flow" version: \(p_i \leftarrow p_i + \lambda\,H_i \mathbf{n}_i\) (chapter 7 for \(H_i\)). In matrix form: \(\mathbf{x} \leftarrow (I - \lambda L)\,\mathbf{x}\) with \(L = D^{-1}A - I\) (normalized) or \(L = D - A\) (non-normalized).
Theorem 5.2 — Uniform smoothing shrinks: 1D spectral proof On a ring of \(n\) vertices (cycle) with positions \(x_i\), the iteration \(x_i \leftarrow (1-\lambda)x_i + \lambda \bar{x}_{\mathcal{N}(i)}\) preserves the mean \(\bar{x}\) and makes every non-constant component decrease: \(x^{(k)} \to \bar{x}\mathbf{1}\) — the 1D mesh shrinks down to the barycenter. The decay rate of a mode of frequency \(j\) is \(\rho_j = 1 - \lambda\big(1 - \cos\tfrac{2\pi j}{n}\big) \in [0, 1)\).
Decompose \(x\) on the orthogonal basis of discrete Fourier modes \(u^{(j)}_i = e^{2i\pi ji/n}\). The average of neighbors multiplies \(u^{(j)}\) by \(\frac{1}{2}(e^{2i\pi j/n} + e^{-2i\pi j/n}) = \cos\frac{2\pi j}{n}\); hence the iteration multiplies mode \(j\) by \((1-\lambda) + \lambda\cos\frac{2\pi j}{n}\). For \(j = 0\) (constant): factor 1 — conservation of the mean. For \(j \neq 0\): \(\cos\frac{2\pi j}{n} < 1\) gives \(\rho_j < 1\) (and \(\geq -1\) for \(\lambda \leq 1\)): all non-constant modes decay geometrically, high frequencies fastest. Limit = constant component = barycenter. ∎
Definition 5.3 — Remedies: Taubin λ|μ, cotangents
  • Taubin λ|μ: alternate a smoothing step (\(\lambda \approx 0.5\)) and an "unsmoothing" step (\(\mu \approx -0.53\)); the frequencies in the pass band \((\rho_\mu, \rho_\lambda)\) are preserved — the mean volume is conserved instead of collapsing;
  • Cotangent (geometric) Laplacian: $$\delta_i = \frac{1}{2 A_i}\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \big(\cot \alpha_{ij} + \cot \beta_{ij}\big)\,(p_j - p_i),$$ where \(\alpha_{ij}, \beta_{ij}\) are the angles opposite the edge \((i,j)\) and \(A_i\) the mixed Voronoi area of the vertex — this is the operator converging to the Laplace–Beltrami operator (theory of hat function forms; cf. Geometric Deep Learning module, operators chapter);
  • Filtering order: smoothing in position regularizes geometry; smoothing the normals then re-projecting (or shrink-and-grow) separates denoising from deformation — the two families coexist in DCCs.
Example 5.4 — Cotangents on an isosceles right triangle Single triangle \(A = (0,0)\), \(B = (1,0)\), \(C = (0,1)\), vertex \(A\). Its two adjacent edges are \((A,B)\) and \((A,C)\): for edge \((A,B)\), the opposite angle is \(\angle ACB = 90°\), of zero cotangent; for edge \((A,C)\), the opposite \(\angle ABC = 90°\), zero cotangent as well. Edge \((B,C)\) is not adjacent to \(A\): the sum of cotangents around \(A\) is zero, \(\delta_A = 0\) — consistent, a mesh reduced to a single triangle is "all boundary". The real implementation test is the planar test: embed a planar mesh in its plane ⟹ all interior \(\delta_i\) are zero (discrete equivalent of \(\Delta f = 0\) for harmonic functions). The proof comes from the discrete Green formula: \(\sum_i A_i\, f_i\, \delta_i = -\sum_{(i,j)} w_{ij}(f_j - f_i)^2 \leq 0\), with equality if and only if \(f\) is constant on each component.
From smoothing to geometric deep learning The cotangent matrix is the central object of the M2 Geometric Deep Learning module: diffusion of descriptors (HKS), spectral convolutions (eigenfunction bases, chapter 8 below), DiffusionNet. The smoothing of this chapter is its "deterministic" version: same operator, without learned data — and already non-trivial: choice of area/mixed Voronoi, negative weights on obtuse meshes (to be truncated or regularized).
