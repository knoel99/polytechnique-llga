Introduction and overview

How do we numerically represent a 3D shape, sample it, reconstruct it from a point cloud, smooth it, simplify it, measure its curvature, its spectrum, its topology? This module lays down all the geometric and discrete foundations needed to manipulate surfaces on a machine. Within the LLGA master's program, it directly prepares the M2 geometric deep learning: one cannot learn on shapes without knowing what a mesh, a discrete Laplacian or the spectrum of a shape are.

What you will be able to do

  • Represent surfaces with meshes and manipulate the half-edge structure that encodes them.
  • Generate smooth 2D curves — interpolation, splines, Hermite, Bézier — and evaluate them with the de Casteljau algorithm.
  • Build Voronoi diagrams and Delaunay triangulations, and move from one to the other by duality.
  • Reconstruct a continuous surface from a point cloud, with sampling guarantees.
  • Smooth a mesh with the discrete Laplacian — from the uniform Laplacian to cotangent weights — while preserving details.
  • Simplify and remesh a surface without denaturing its geometry.
  • Compute discrete curvatures and verify the Gauss–Bonnet relation on real meshes.
  • Use the spectrum of a shape (eigenvalues of the Laplacian) as a signature to compare it with others.
  • Read the topological invariants of a shape: Euler characteristic, Betti numbers, persistence.

Official objectives and syllabus (2026-2027 catalog)

The official description announces the project: "this course will introduce the fundamental concepts of computer-based shape creation and analysis", starting with "generating and representing smooth curves in 2D using splines and Bézier curves", then the 3D representation techniques — with the emphasis on "triangular meshes and the associated methods" — and, in parallel, shape analysis methods up to "the definition and the computation of the similarity between shapes and shape correspondences". These materials follow exactly this arc, from 2D curves to non-rigid correspondences. Official prerequisites: not provided (not communicated); the assessment rests on the lab assignments turned in and the quizzes, with no written exam.

Where this course fits in the program

Module of period 1 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), offered as an option outside the recommended track: a geometric specialization choice particularly relevant if you are aiming for 3D data, image synthesis or learning on shapes.

  • Prerequisites — official: not communicated on the course description. Internally: the programming fluency of the Refresher in Computer Science and foundations in linear algebra; the refresher chapter covers convexity, least squares and eigenvalues, the three recurring tools of the course.
  • Downstream modules — the Laplacian and the spectrum of shapes studied here are at the heart of Analysis and Deep Learning on Geometric Data (M2), which generalizes neural networks to surfaces and graphs; the representation of meshes also serves Image Synthesis: Theory and Practice in P2 for surface rendering.

Course outline

  • Refresher: convexity, least squares, eigenvalues — the three algebraic pillars reactivated before tackling geometry.
  • Representing a shape: meshes and half-edges — first the smooth 2D curves (interpolation, splines, Bézier, de Casteljau), then the fundamental data structure of discrete surfaces.
  • Voronoi diagrams and duality — partitioning space by nearest neighbor, and what duality says about it.
  • Delaunay triangulations: the empty circle criterion — the canonical triangulation and its properties.
  • Sampling and reconstruction of surfaces — from point cloud to surface, with guarantees — not forgetting rigid registration (ICP), which aligns the acquisitions.
  • Smoothing: from uniform to cotangent Laplacian — the central operator of all shape analysis.
  • Simplification and remeshing — reducing the number of elements while preserving geometry, or on the contrary refining by surface subdivision.
  • Discrete curvature and Gauss–Bonnet — measuring curvature on a polyhedral object, and the theorem that ties it all together.
  • The spectrum of shapes — the eigenvalues of the Laplacian as a shape's fingerprint, up to non-rigid correspondences.
  • Combinatorial topology: Euler, Betti, persistence — first steps toward topological data analysis.
  • Research of the teaching team — the research work of Desbrun and Memari related to the course.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Work through the refresher chapter seriously, even if it seems easy: least squares and eigenvalues come back in half of the course.
  • Draw! Voronoi, Delaunay and duality are understood with pencil and paper on small 2D examples before being coded.
  • Implement the half-edge structure yourself once: you will durably understand what a mesh is.
  • For each operator (smoothing, curvature), test on a sphere then on a real shape: the cases where theory is exact serve as unit tests.
  • Connect every concept to the Laplacian: smoothing, spectrum, and later the spectral filters of GNNs are the same object seen from different angles.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next; they build the reflexes used in the graded assignments.
The LLGA positioning A mesh is a graph: the discrete Laplacian you manipulate here is the direct cousin of the graph Laplacian of the spectral filters of the Graph Machine and Deep Learning for Generative AI module. This module is thus the royal road toward Analysis and Deep Learning on Geometric Data (M2) and toward careers in 3D data — industry, imaging, synthesis — as well as toward research in digital geometry of the LIX team.
