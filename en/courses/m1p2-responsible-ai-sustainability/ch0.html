0. Refresher: optimization, duality, complexity

L3 reminders — the foundation (see Machine Learning module, ch. 0; Optimal Transport module, ch. 0; Optimization for AI module, ch. 0)
  • Linear program (LP): \(\min_x c^\top x\) subject to \(Ax \leq b\), \(x \geq 0\) — the feasible set is a polyhedron; the optimum (if it exists) is attained at a vertex (extreme point); solved in polynomial time (interior points, simplex in practice).
  • Duality: the dual of an LP is an LP; \(d^\star \leq p^\star\) always (weak duality), equality under conditions (Slater); the dual variables \(\lambda \geq 0\) are interpreted as marginal prices — how much an extra unit of each resource is worth. This "economic" reading will be our central tool: the price of carbon, the price of a MWh, the price of a GPU hour.
  • Integer variables (ILP): as soon as one variable is integral (choose / do not choose), the problem becomes NP-hard in general (see Refresher CS module, ch. 2); it is handled by continuous relaxation + branch and bound (ch. 4).
  • Partial derivatives, convexity: convexity distinguishes "easy" (a local minimum is global) from "hard" — see the Optimization for AI module, ch. 1.
Proposition 0.1 — The lemma that serves the whole module (proved) For an LP \(\min\{c^\top x : Ax = b,\ x \geq 0\}\) and any feasible dual solution \((\lambda, \mu \geq 0)\) (i.e. \(A^\top \lambda \leq c\) in the appropriate sense): \(b^\top \lambda \leq c^\top x\) for every feasible \(x\). If equality is attained, both solutions are optimal.
\(c^\top x \geq (A^\top\lambda)^\top x = \lambda^\top A x = \lambda^\top b\), using dual feasibility first, then \(Ax = b\). If \(c^\top x = b^\top\lambda\), then \(x\) attains a dual lower bound: \(x\) and \(\lambda\) are optimal (and the gap \(c^\top x - b^\top\lambda\), zero, decomposes as \(\mu^\top x\), whence the complementary slackness \(\mu_i x_i = 0\)). ∎
Position of the module — the "decision" point of view The other modules of the master's program optimize models (loss, generalization). Here we optimize decisions: when to launch which training run, on which data center, with what numerical precision, which performance/energy trade-off to accept. Three new ingredients: several incommensurable objectives (ch. 1), constrained physical resources (ch. 2), and the requirement to measure what we claim to optimize (ch. 6–7) — for a mis-counted kg CO2 optimizes a fable, not the climate.
