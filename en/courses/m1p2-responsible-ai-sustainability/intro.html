Introduction and overview

ModuleOptimization and Responsible AI for Sustainability
CodeCSC_52073_EP
Team and volumeInstructor: Sonia Vanier (École Polytechnique, LIX; ORAILIX team) · 36 h · 4.5 ECTS · optimizing under several objectives and resource constraints — from the Pareto front to carbon-aware scheduling, from mixed integer linear programming to honest measurement of emissions, with constant vigilance against greenwashing.
Status in the programP2 OptionOutside recommended track

This module confronts AI with a double constraint: several simultaneous objectives — performance, energy, cost, impact — and finite resources. From the Pareto front to carbon-aware scheduling, from linear programming to honest measurement of emissions, it treats optimization as a lever for sustainability, with constant vigilance against greenwashing. It brings to the master's program its responsible compass: models and decisions one can justify with figures in hand.

What you will be able to do

  • Formulate an AI problem as a multi-objective trade-off and explore the Pareto front.
  • Integrate resource constraints — compute, energy, budget — into the very formulation of a problem.
  • Make a model frugal through distillation and quantization.
  • Model sustainable decision support in linear and integer linear programming.
  • Quantify the emissions of an AI system with a defensible methodology.
  • Analyze real case studies: data centers, electric vehicles.
  • Identify rebound effects and greenwashing in a technical discourse, and dismantle it argument by argument.

Where this course fits in the program

An option of period 2 of M1, outside the recommended track. The optimization tools of the Optimization for AI module are directly useful here — convexity, duality and complexity are reactivated in chapter 0. Downstream, the module sheds light on Deep Learning (P2 variant), where compute budgets become a primary quantity, and on ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles (M2), which places frugality and green ops at the heart of ML system deployment.

Course outline

  • Refresher: optimization, duality, complexity — the optimization toolbox reloaded for use under constraints.
  • Multi-objective optimization and the Pareto front — thinking in trade-offs rather than a single score, and representing the arbitrages.
  • Optimizing under resource constraints — integrating energy, compute and cost into the problem, not as an afterthought.
  • Model frugality: distillation, quantization — reducing a model's footprint while preserving the essence of its performance.
  • Sustainable decision support: LP and ILP — linear and integer linear programming for concrete, auditable decisions.
  • Case studies: data centers, electric vehicles — real deployments where the trade-offs are genuinely quantified.
  • Limits, rebound effect and greenwashing — what optimization does not solve, and the discourses that must be dismantled.
  • Research of the teaching team — the research work of the ORAILIX team (LIX), read with the module's tools.
  • Exercises — formulations and trade-offs to defend with figures in hand.
  • References — the methodological sources and the case studies.

How to study this module

  • Return to chapter 0 whenever duality or complexity become fuzzy: the whole module uses them continuously.
  • For every emission or consumption figure, ask for the methodology: scope, units, source — this is the first weapon against greenwashing.
  • In the exercises, explicitly separate objectives and constraints before coding anything.
  • Connect frugality (distillation, quantization) to the other P2 courses: the same levers return under other names.
  • Play devil's advocate systematically: for every proposed solution, look for the rebound effect it triggers.
  • Study the cases (data centers, electric vehicles) like a professional: what data, what actors, what real trade-offs.
The module in the LLGA master's program The master's program trains for a powerful and measured AI: here, the M2 LLMs — whose training and inference are costly — meet the questions of budget and impact, while optimization methods serve as the common language. The module's skills are sought in the emerging careers of responsible AI and green ops, and sustainable operations research is an open field for research and industry alike.
