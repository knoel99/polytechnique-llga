Exercises

  1. Pareto front by hand. Objectives \(F_1(x)=(x-1)^2+1\), \(F_2(x)=(x+1)^2+1\), \(x \in [-3,3]\). (a) Plot the attainable set in the \((F_1,F_2)\) plane and the front. (b) Show that \(x=-2\) and \(x=2\) are dominated. (c) For \(w=(1,1)\), solve the scalarization and verify that it gives \(x=0\). (d) On the objectives \(F_1(x)=x\), \(F_2(x)=1-x^2\) of prop. 1.3, show that \(x=0{,}5\) is Pareto-optimal but is not the minimizer of any weighted scalarization \(w>0\). Solution: (c) \(F_w = 2x^2+4\) minimal at 0; (d) \(F_w\) is concave in \(x\), hence minimal at the endpoints, see prop. 1.3.
  2. Chebyshev. Still on \(F_1(x)=x\), \(F_2(x)=1-x^2\), \(x \in [0,1]\) (ideal point \(z^\star=(0,0)\)). (a) Verify that every point is Pareto-optimal. (b) Show that \(g_w(x) = \max\{w_1 F_1(x),\, w_2 F_2(x)\}\) attains its minimum at the equilibrium \(w_1 x = w_2(1-x^2)\) (one grows, the other decreases). (c) Deduce that every point of the front is produced by some \(w>0\) — a complete contrast with prop. 1.3 — and compute \(w_1/w_2\) to obtain \(x = 0{,}5\). Solution: (c) at equilibrium \(w_1/w_2 = (1-x^2)/x = 3/2\) for \(x=0{,}5\).
  3. Carbon scheduling. Take up example 2.3 with an additional constraint: deadline at +24 h. (a) Solve by hand with the exchange argument of theorem 2.2. (b) Write the dual of the "knapsack" \(\min \sum c_t x_t\), \(\sum x_t = T\), \(0 \leq x_t \leq 1\) and interpret the dual variable (price of an hour of delay). (c) What becomes the optimum if the job can be sequenced in pieces of one hour minimum? Hint: nothing changes — why?
  4. Quantization error propagation. A two-layer network, \(\mathrm{Lip}_1 = 3\), \(\mathrm{Lip}_2 = 2\), 8-bit quantizations of step \(\Delta_1 = \Delta_2 = 2^{-7}\) over a weight domain in \([-0{,}5, 0{,}5]\). (a) Verify the step with definition 3.2. (b) Apply proposition 3.2 and bound the output error. (c) Redo at 4 bits; from what depth \(L\) (identical layers) does the error exceed 0.1? Conclude on the necessity of QAT for int4 deep networks.
  5. Distillation lab. Teacher: a ~1M-parameter CNN on MNIST (98.5% expected); student: an MLP with ~20k. (a) Train the student alone (baseline ~97%). (b) Distill (\(\tau \in \{1,2,4\}\), \(\alpha \in \{0{,}3, 0{,}7\}\)). (c) Measure inference energy per batch and parameters; plot the (energy, accuracy) point of the two models on the Pareto plot of example 1.5 (ch. 1). Partial solution (PyTorch): 
    import torch, torch.nn as nn, torch.nn.functional as F
def distill_loss(zs, zt, y, tau=4.0, alpha=0.7):
    kd = F.kl_div(F.log_softmax(zs/tau, 1), F.softmax(zt/tau, 1),
                  reduction="batchmean") * tau*tau
    return alpha*kd + (1-alpha)*F.cross_entropy(zs, y)
# loop: zs = student(x); with torch.no_grad(): zt = teacher(x)
  6. Branch and bound by hand. Binary knapsack: capacity \(C = 10\), items \((v,w) \in \{(7,6), (5,5), (4,4), (2,1)\}\). (a) Solve the greedy relaxation at the root. (b) Branch on the most fractional variable, draw the complete tree, indicate for each node: bound (relaxation), pruned or explored, and why. (c) Compare with the PuLP solution (ch. 4); (d) how many nodes if one branches on the least fractional variable? Conclusion on branching heuristics.
  7. Framed measurement. With codecarbon (ch. 6): measure the energy of the same training (logistic regression on MNIST, 10 epochs) (a) on your machine, (b) with country="FR", then "DE", then "WW". (c) Produce the protocol sheet (ch. 6): scope, hardware, uncertainty. (d) Sensitivity: replace the hourly intensity with the annual average and quantify the gap. Deliverable: 10 lines, two figures, one range.
  8. Audit. Find a public "green AI" claim (corporate report, lab blog). Apply grid 7.1 to it: scope, intensity, reference, reproducibility, real benefit; write a one-page audit note, with at least one quantified test redone by yourself (e.g. recompute the emission via the ch. 6 protocol). Grading grid: technical accuracy, factuality (no unsourced claim), and nuance — distinguish what is false, imprecise, and merely incomplete.
