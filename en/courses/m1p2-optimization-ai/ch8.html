8. Constraints in machine learning

Real life: "error rate \(\leq 5\,\%\) and demographic disparity \(\leq \delta\) and latency \(\leq 100\) ms". The average loss alone cannot express requirements; constraints are needed — and hence the duality of ch. 0, applied.

Definition 8.1 — Learning under constraints $$\min_{\theta}\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \ell(h_\theta(x_i), y_i) \quad\text{subject to}\quad \frac{1}{n}\sum_i c_j(h_\theta(x_i), x_i) \leq 0, \quad j = 1, \dots, m.$$ Examples: demographic parity (gap of positive rates between groups), memory/latency budget at inference, false-negative rate in health. The empirical Lagrangian \(\mathcal{L}(\theta,\lambda)\) and the dual \(\max_{\lambda \geq 0} d(\lambda)\) are formed as in ch. 0.

Theorem 8.3 — Projected gradient descent (proved) For \(f\) convex \(L\)-smooth and \(\mathcal{C}\) closed convex, the iteration \(x_{k+1} = \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x_k - t\nabla f(x_k))\), \(t \leq 1/L\), preserves the rates of ch. 2 (\(O(1/k)\), linear under strong convexity).
Projection lemma. For every \(y \in \mathcal{C}\), \(\langle x - \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x),\, y - \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x)\rangle \leq 0\): otherwise the point \(\Pi_\mathcal{C}(x) + \varepsilon(y - \Pi_\mathcal{C}(x)) \in \mathcal{C}\) (by convexity) would be closer to \(x\) for small \(\varepsilon > 0\) — contradicting the definition of the projection. With \(p_k = x_k - t\nabla f(x_k)\) and \(x_{k+1} = \Pi_\mathcal{C}(p_k)\), the same computation as in theorem 2.2 applies, the extra cross term being precisely controlled by the lemma (taken at \(y = x^\star\)). ∎
Example 8.4 — SVM: the constraint that becomes a penalty, that becomes a dual Hard-margin SVM (see the Machine Learning module, ch. 3): \(\min \tfrac12\|w\|^2\) subject to \(y_i w^\top x_i \geq 1\). (i) The Wolfe dual gives the box problem \(\max_\alpha -\tfrac12\|X^\top(\alpha\odot y)\|^2 + \mathbf{1}^\top\alpha\) subject to \(\alpha \geq 0\), \(\alpha \perp y\) — a projection onto a simplex: projected descent of theorem 8.3. (ii) The regularized primal (hinge): \(\min \tfrac1n\sum \max(0, 1 - y_i w^\top x_i) + \lambda\|w\|^2\) is a special case of the \(f + g\) form of ch. 4 — this time with a non-smooth \(f\), solved by subgradient or by duality (SVRG shines there too). Three chapters of the module, a single problem.
Beyond the empirical optimum: "PAC" constrained learning Satisfying the constraints on average over the training set does not guarantee they will hold in test. This is the question addressed by L. Chamon (ch. 11): do generalization guarantees exist for learning under requirements? Positive answer (in the PAC framework, under conditions); and the empirical dual approaches the population dual well (empirical duality gap zero under good assumptions). Until ch. 11, remember: duality is not a computational artifact — it is what makes requirements learnable.
