This module builds the complete theory of the optimization algorithms that train AI — gradient descent, SGD, Nesterov acceleration, proximal methods, variance reduction, quasi-Newton — with the proofs of the convergence rates. The goal is not merely to know how to use these algorithms, but to understand why they converge, at what speed, and what none of them can do better. This theoretical foundation is reinvested throughout the master's program, whenever one trains, parallelizes or optimizes under constraints.

What you will be able to do

  • Prove the convergence of gradient descent and its acceleration under convexity and strong convexity assumptions.
  • Explain the role of conditioning and that of Nesterov acceleration.
  • Handle non-smooth objectives with proximal methods (ISTA, FISTA).
  • Analyze stochastic gradient descent and the effect of variance reduction (SAGA, SVRG).
  • Choose between first-order and quasi-Newton methods according to the problem and its dimension.
  • Formulate constraints in learning via duality, projections and penalties.
  • Optimize hyperparameters in a reasoned way rather than blindly.
  • Connect these theoretical results to the actual training of neural networks.

Official objectives and syllabus (2026-2027 catalog)

According to the APM_52067_EP — “Optimization for AI” sheet: the course introduces the fundamentals of optimization for machine learning and AI. Rather than a panorama of existing algorithms, it is organized around the pillar of modern ML/AI — stochastic gradient descent (SGD) — and studies its extensions: variance reduction and adaptive methods, with convergence guarantees in convex and non-convex settings. The official syllabus also covers minimax optimization (notably in connection with constraints and duality), zeroth-order methods (optimizing without gradients), and the challenges of online and distributed optimization, combining theoretical foundations with practical applications.

Where this course fits in the program

An option of period 2 of M1, outside the recommended track. Official prerequisites: not communicated (the “Prerequisites” tab of the SynapseS sheet is empty). The module assumes the foundation of the P1 Machine Learning course, in particular the convex analysis basics revisited in chapter 0. It directly feeds three neighboring courses: Deep Learning (P2 variant), whose analysis of parallel training builds on the rates established here; Emerging Subjects in ML & Collaborative Learning, where distributed SGD extends the stochastic chapter; and Optimization and Responsible AI for Sustainability, which reinvests optimization under resource constraints.

Course outline

  • Refresher: convexity and duality — the essentials of convex analysis reloaded for the rest of the module.
  • Optimization inside the AI loop — why modern learning is first an optimization problem, and what that imposes.
  • Gradient descent: convergence proved — the founding theorem, its assumptions and its rate, proved in detail.
  • Conditioning and Nesterov acceleration — what the geometry of the problem does to speed, and the acceleration that answers it.
  • Proximal methods: ISTA and FISTA — handling non-differentiabilities (regularizations, constraints) cleanly.
  • Stochastic gradient descent — the workhorse algorithm of machine learning, analyzed as such.
  • Variance reduction: SAGA and SVRG — keeping the best of both worlds: the cost of the stochastic, the accuracy of the deterministic.
  • Newton and quasi-Newton — exploiting curvature, and doing so at a controlled cost.
  • Constraints in machine learning — duality, projections and penalties to encode what the model must respect.
  • Hyperparameter optimization — optimizing the level above: methods, costs and pitfalls.
  • Optimizing the training of networks — the junction with practice: what theory really illuminates in heavy training runs.
  • Research of the teaching team — the instructors' papers, read with the module's tools.
  • Exercises — proofs and implementations for each family of algorithms.
  • References — the theoretical sources of each chapter.

How to study this module

  • Redo every proof on the board, without the notes: convergence rates are the heart of the exam as of the module.
  • Never separate an algorithm from its assumptions: convexity, L-Lipschitz continuity and strong convexity change everything.
  • Implement each method on simple problems where the exact solution is known, and check the observed rates against the proven rates.
  • Keep chapter 0 at hand: convexity and duality return at every turn.
  • Connect every result to the P1 machine learning module: regularization, SVM and regression become concrete examples.
  • Prepare the exercises in a small group, but write the proofs alone: it is the writing that reveals the gaps.
The module in the LLGA master's program Optimization is the common grammar of AI: it connects the M2 LLMs (whose training is a vast optimization task) to distributed systems and frugal AI. The theoretical perspective gained here — knowing what an algorithm can and cannot do — distinguishes the engineer who designs from the one who configures, and is a natural prerequisite toward careers in research and large-scale ML.
