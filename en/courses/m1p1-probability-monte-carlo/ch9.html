9. Langevin dynamics and generative models

Definition 9.1 — Overdamped Langevin diffusion Let \(U : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}\) (the potential) be \(\mathcal{C}^1\), with \(e^{-U} \in L^1\). The Langevin diffusion is the SDE $$dX_t = -\nabla U(X_t)\,dt + \sqrt{2}\,dW_t,$$ where \((W_t)\) is a \(d\)-dimensional Brownian motion. Its invariant distribution (if it exists: this is the case if \(U\) is confining, e.g. \(U(x) \geq a\|x\| - b\)) is $$\pi(x) = \frac{e^{-U(x)}}{\int e^{-U}\,d\lambda},$$ the Gibbs distribution associated with \(U\). In ML, one rather writes \(U = -\log \pi\): the drift \(-\nabla U = \nabla \log \pi\) is the score — a central quantity in generative models (see the Multimodal GenAI module, chapter 3).
Proposition 9.2 — \(\pi\) invariant for the Langevin diffusion (proved) For every test function \(\varphi \in \mathcal{C}^\infty_c\) and \(X \sim \pi\): \(\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{A}\varphi(X)] = 0\), where \(\mathcal{A}\varphi = -\nabla U \cdot \nabla \varphi + \Delta \varphi\) is the generator of the diffusion. This characterizes invariance (expectations are preserved along the trajectories).
With \(\pi = e^{-U}/Z\) and \(d\pi = Z^{-1}e^{-U(x)}dx\), integrations by parts (the boundary terms vanish, \(\varphi\) being compactly supported): $$\int (\Delta\varphi)\,e^{-U}\,dx = -\int \nabla\varphi \cdot \nabla(e^{-U})\,dx = \int \nabla\varphi \cdot \nabla U\; e^{-U}\,dx,$$ using \(\nabla(e^{-U}) = -(\nabla U)\,e^{-U}\). Substituting: $$Z\,\mathbb{E}_\pi[\mathcal{A}\varphi] = \int \big[-\nabla U\cdot\nabla\varphi + \Delta\varphi\big]\,e^{-U}dx = \int \big[-\nabla U\cdot\nabla\varphi + \nabla U \cdot \nabla\varphi\big]e^{-U}dx = 0.$$ ∎
Direct check in the Gaussian case \(U(x) = \|x\|^2/2\): the diffusion is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion \(dX_t = -X_t dt + \sqrt2\,dW_t\), and \(\mathcal{N}(0,I_d)\) is stationary (chap. 4, Example 4.5).
Definition 9.3 — ULA and MALA The Euler-Maruyama discretization with step \(\gamma > 0\) gives the ULA algorithm (unadjusted, without correction): $$X_{k+1} = X_k - \gamma\,\nabla U(X_k) + \sqrt{2\gamma}\,Z_{k+1}, \qquad Z_{k+1} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I_d).$$ ULA is a biased Markov chain: its limiting distribution \(\pi_\gamma\) differs from \(\pi\) (an \(\mathcal{O}(\gamma)\) gap). MALA adds a Metropolis-Hastings step (Def. 8.1) with the Gaussian proposal above, removing the bias exactly (at the price of rejections); the bias/cost trade-off is the subject of the work described in chapter 10.
Example 9.4 — The ULA bias computed exactly (Gaussian case) For \(\pi = \mathcal{N}(0,1)\) (\(U(x)=x^2/2\)): \(X_{k+1} = (1-\gamma)X_k + \sqrt{2\gamma}Z_{k+1}\) — an AR(1) (Example 4.5). Its stationary distribution is \(\mathcal{N}\big(0,\ \sigma_\gamma^2\big)\) with $$\sigma_\gamma^2 = \frac{2\gamma}{1-(1-\gamma)^2} = \frac{2}{2-\gamma} \;\xrightarrow[\gamma \to 0]{}\; 1.$$ Numbers: \(\gamma = 0.5 \Rightarrow \sigma_\gamma^2 = 1.33\) (bias +33%); \(\gamma = 0.1 \Rightarrow 1.053\) (+5%); \(\gamma = 0.01 \Rightarrow 1.005\) (+0.5%). The variance bias is \(\gamma/(2-\gamma) \approx \gamma/2\): halving the bias doubles the number of iterations needed to reach a given region — paper [1] of chapter 10 precisely quantifies this trade-off (explicit convergence speed in \(\gamma\), \(d\), and the target error).
Theorem 9.5 — Non-asymptotic guarantees for ULA (statement, [1]-[2]) If \(U\) is \(L\)-smooth and strongly convex, Durmus & Moulines (2017) show that after \(k\) iterations with step \(\gamma\), in Wasserstein distance \(W_2\) (see the Optimal Transport module, chapter 2) and in total variation, $$W_2\big(\mathrm{law}(X_k),\,\pi\big) \;\leq\; \mathcal{O}\!\Big(e^{-k\gamma m/2}\,\sqrt{d/m} + \sqrt{\gamma d/m}\Big) \quad\text{(orders of magnitude, } m = \text{strong convexity constant)},$$ that is, exponential decay toward a bias plateau proportional to \(\sqrt\gamma\); with decreasing steps, ULA converges (ergodicity) but with degraded rates. These bounds, explicit in the dimension \(d\), ground the modern analysis of high-dimensional sampling (ch. 10).
Proposition 9.6 — The score / generative models connection Diffusion-based generative models (see the Multimodal GenAI module, chapter 3; Deep Learning module, chapter 7, for VAE/GAN) learn a family of scores \(s_\theta(x,t) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\) of the noised versions \(p_t\) of the data. Generation by annealed Langevin runs, for decreasing \(t\), $$X_{k+1} = X_k + \tfrac{\varepsilon_t}{2}\,s_\theta(X_k,t) + \sqrt{\varepsilon_t}\,Z_{k+1} \qquad (\text{ULA with potential } -\log p_t),$$ starting from white noise: decreasing temperature = simulated annealing (Prop. 8.6) applied to Langevin dynamics. The network learns the drift of the diffusion; the final randomness \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,Z\) does the rest. The quantities of this chapter — Euler bias, optimal step, noise calibration — are therefore the same tuning knobs as those of diffusion models. 
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(3)

def ula(gamma, n=200_000):             # target N(0,1): U(x) = x^2/2
    x = 3.0
    for k in range(n):
        x = x - gamma*x + np.sqrt(2*gamma)*rng.standard_normal()
        if k >= n//2:                  # ignore burn-in
            yield x

for g in (0.5, 0.1, 0.01):
    v = np.fromiter(ula(g), float)
    print(f"gamma={g:4.2f}  stationary var {v.var():.4f}"
          f"  theory {2/(2-g):.4f}")

def mala(gamma, n=200_000):            # same proposal + MH acceptance
    x, out = 3.0, []
    U, dU = lambda z: z*z/2, lambda z: z
    q = lambda a, b: -(b - a + gamma*dU(a))**2 / (4*gamma)
    for k in range(n):
        y = x - gamma*dU(x) + np.sqrt(2*gamma)*rng.standard_normal()
        loga = U(x) - U(y) + q(x, y) - q(y, x)
        if np.log(rng.random()) < loga: x = y
        if k >= n//2: out.append(x)
    return np.array(out)

print(f"MALA gamma=0.5 : var {mala(0.5).var():.4f} (exact 1)")
Why this chapter is the heart of the module The loop is closed: measure/integration (ch. 1) → expectations and errors (ch. 2-3) → chains and invariance (ch. 4) → simulation (ch. 5) → estimation via trajectories (ch. 6-8) → diffusion dynamics (ch. 9). The score \(\nabla\log\pi\) is at once "the optimal drift for sampling" and "the object learned by generative models": this double status, formalized by the convergence guarantees of the teaching team, is the exact articulation between this module and the Multimodal GenAI and Deep Learning modules. The tuning questions — step, bias, stability — are the same levers there.
