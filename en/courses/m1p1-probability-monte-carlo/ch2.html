2. Convergence: LLN, CLT, delta method

Definition 2.1 — Modes of convergence Let \((X_n)\) be a sequence of r.v. and \(X\) a real r.v.
  • In probability: \(\forall \varepsilon > 0,\; \mathbb{P}(|X_n - X| > \varepsilon) \to 0\);
  • Almost surely: \(\mathbb{P}(\{\omega : X_n(\omega) \to X(\omega)\}) = 1\);
  • In \(L^p\): \(\mathbb{E}|X_n - X|^p \to 0\);
  • In distribution: \(\mathbb{E}[f(X_n)] \to \mathbb{E}[f(X)]\) for every bounded continuous \(f\) — the only mode where \(X_n\) and \(X\) may live on different spaces.
Classic implications: \(L^p\) and a.s. imply in probability; in probability implies in distribution; no other general implication (counterexample: the dyadic "sliding window" \(X_n = \mathbf{1}_{[j/2^k,\,(j+1)/2^k]}\) with \(n = 2^k + j\) on \([0,1]\) equipped with Lebesgue measure converges in probability to 0 but takes the value 1 infinitely often — no almost-sure convergence).
Theorem 2.2 — (Weak) law of large numbers (proved) Let \(X_1, X_2, \dots\) be i.i.d. and square-integrable, with expectation \(\mu\). Then \(\bar{X}_n = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \to \mu\) in probability (and in \(L^2\)).
Linearity (Prop. 1.2): \(\mathbb{E}[\bar X_n] = \mu\). Independence and Fubini (Thm 1.3): \(\mathrm{Var}(\bar X_n) = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_i \mathrm{Var}(X_i) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\). Chebyshev (Prop. 0.2): \(\mathbb{P}(|\bar X_n - \mu| > \varepsilon) \leq \sigma^2/(n\varepsilon^2) \to 0\), and \(\mathbb{E}|\bar X_n - \mu|^2 = \sigma^2/n \to 0\).∎
The strong version (almost sure, under the sole assumption \(\mathbb{E}|X_1| < \infty\)) requires the Borel-Cantelli lemma and Kolmogorov's maximal inequality: admitted here, it justifies the frequentist interpretation of probabilities and the convergence of ergodic chains (ch. 4 and 8).
Theorem 2.3 — Central limit theorem (proved via characteristic functions) Let \(X_1, X_2, \dots\) be i.i.d., \(\mathbb{E}X_1 = \mu\), \(0 < \sigma^2 = \mathrm{Var}(X_1) < \infty\). Then $$\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sigma}\big(\bar X_n - \mu\big) \;\xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{\text{law}}\; \mathcal{N}(0,1).$$
1. Reduction: assume \(\mu = 0\) (replace \(X_i\) by \(X_i - \mu\)). Set \(\varphi(u) = \mathbb{E}[e^{iuX_1}]\), continuous at 0 with \(\varphi(0)=1\). 2. Taylor of \(\varphi\): since \(\mathbb{E}|X_1|^2 < \infty\), we may differentiate twice under the expectation (domination): \(\varphi(u) = 1 - \tfrac{\sigma^2 u^2}{2} + u^2\,\varepsilon(u)\) with \(\varepsilon(u) \to 0\). Setting \(\psi = \log \varphi\) (defined near 0) and using \(\log(1+z) = z + o(z)\): $$\psi(u) = -\frac{\sigma^2 u^2}{2} + u^2\,\tilde\varepsilon(u), \qquad \tilde\varepsilon(u) \to 0.$$ 3. Product: by independence, the characteristic function of \(S_n = \sqrt{n}\,\bar X_n/\sigma\) equals \(\varphi_n(t) = \varphi(t/(\sigma\sqrt n))^n\), hence $$\log \varphi_n(t) = n\,\psi\Big(\frac{t}{\sigma\sqrt n}\Big) = n\Big(-\frac{t^2}{2n} + \frac{t^2}{n}\tilde\varepsilon\big(t/(\sigma\sqrt n)\big)\Big) \xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{} -\frac{t^2}{2}.$$ 4. Conclusion: \(\varphi_n(t) \to e^{-t^2/2}\), which is the characteristic function of \(\mathcal{N}(0,1)\); Lévy's continuity theorem (a pointwise limit of characteristic functions continuous at 0 is a characteristic function, and convergence of characteristic functions is equivalent to convergence in distribution — admitted) concludes the proof.∎
Theorem 2.4 — Delta method (proved) If \(\sqrt{n}(T_n - \theta) \xrightarrow{\text{law}} \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)\) and \(g : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\) is \(\mathcal{C}^1\) in a neighborhood of \(\theta\) with \(g'(\theta) \neq 0\), then $$\sqrt{n}\big(g(T_n) - g(\theta)\big) \xrightarrow{\text{law}} \mathcal{N}\big(0,\; \sigma^2\, g'(\theta)^2\big).$$
Taylor with controlled remainder: \(g(T_n) = g(\theta) + g'(\theta)(T_n - \theta) + R_n\) with \(|R_n| \leq \tfrac12 \sup_{|u - \theta| \leq \delta}|g''(u)|\,(T_n-\theta)^2 = o(|T_n - \theta|)\) on the event \(|T_n - \theta| \leq \delta\). Multiplying by \(\sqrt n\): \(\sqrt n R_n = \sqrt n\, o(|T_n - \theta|) = o(1) \cdot \sqrt n |T_n - \theta|\). Now \(\sqrt n (T_n - \theta)\) converges in distribution, hence is tight (\(\mathcal{O}_\mathbb{P}(1)\)), and \(\mathbb{1}_{|T_n-\theta| > \delta} \to 0\) in probability: the remainder is negligible and \(\sqrt n (g(T_n) - g(\theta)) = g'(\theta)\,\sqrt n (T_n-\theta) + o_\mathbb{P}(1)\). Conclude with Slutsky's lemma.∎
Example 2.5 — Intervals and variance stabilization (numbers)
  • A poll with \(n = 1000\) on a proportion \(p\) has standard error \(\sqrt{p(1-p)/n} \leq \sqrt{1/(4n)} \approx 1.58\,\%\): the "margin of error ±3 points" quoted by polling institutes is a 95% interval (CLT + \(\Phi^{-1}(0.975) = 1.96\)).
  • For \(T_n = \bar X_n\) and \(g = \log\), the limiting variance becomes \(\sigma^2/\theta^2\): it no longer depends on the parameter — this is the principle of variance-stabilizing transformations, used before the computer age to make variances homoscedastic.
  • For the empirical correlation coefficient \(r\) of a Gaussian pair, Fisher's transformation \(g(r) = \tfrac12\log\frac{1+r}{1-r}\) gives a limiting Gaussian distribution with variance \(1/(n-3)\) — a classic example of the delta method in dimension 2 (with the gradient).
