4. Markov chains: stationarity, reversibility, ergodicity

Definition 4.1 — Markov kernel, Markov chain A Markov kernel on a measurable space \((E,\mathcal{E})\) is a map \(P : E \times \mathcal{E} \to [0,1]\) such that \(P(x,\cdot)\) is a probability for every \(x\) and \(P(\cdot,A)\) is measurable for every \(A\). A sequence \((X_k)_{k \geq 0}\) of r.v. is a Markov chain with kernel \(P\) if \(\mathbb{P}(X_{k+1} \in A \mid X_0, \dots, X_k) = P(X_k, A)\) (memory reduces to the present). The \(n\)-step distribution is \(P^n(x,A)\) (composition of kernels, Fubini for kernels — the analogue of matrix multiplication in finite dimension).
Definition 4.2 — Invariance, reversibility, irreducibility, periodicity
  • \(\pi\) is invariant if \(\pi P = \pi\), i.e. \(\int \pi(dx) P(x,A) = \pi(A)\) for every \(A\);
  • \(P\) is \(\pi\)-reversible if \(\pi(dx)P(x,dy) = \pi(dy)P(y,dx)\) (detailed balance); reversibility implies invariance (symmetry of the flow), the converse is false;
  • \(P\) is \(\pi\)-irreducible if for every \(x\) and every \(A\) with \(\pi(A) > 0\), there exists \(n\) (depending on \(x,A\)) such that \(P^n(x,A) > 0\): everything can be reached;
  • the period of \(x\) is the gcd of the \(n\) such that \(P^n(x,\{x\}) > 0\) (in a countable space); \(P\) is aperiodic if all periods equal 1.
Theorem 4.3 — Convergence and ergodicity (statement) Let \(P\) be \(\pi\)-reversible with \(\pi\) a probability, \(\pi\)-irreducible and aperiodic. Then:
  • \(\pi\) is the unique invariant distribution;
  • convergence to equilibrium: \(\|\lambda P^n - \pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}} \to 0\) for every initial distribution \(\lambda\), where \(\|\mu - \nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}} = \sup_A |\mu(A) - \nu(A)|\);
  • ergodic strong law: for \(f\) with \(\pi(|f|) < \infty\), a.s. $$\frac1n \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(X_k) \to \pi(f) = \int f\,d\pi \qquad (X_0 \sim \lambda \text{ arbitrary}).$$
It is this theorem that turns a dependent trajectory into a "usable average sample" — the engine of all the MCMC methods of chapter 8. Positive recurrence (existence of a return time with finite expectation) replaces irreducibility + recurrence in general spaces; complete proofs in Levin-Peres-Wilmer (reference [3]).
Example 4.4 — Two states, explicit computations On \(E = \{1,2\}\), \(P = \begin{pmatrix} 0.7 & 0.3 \\ 0.2 & 0.8 \end{pmatrix}\). Invariance: \(\pi = \pi P\) gives \(0.3\pi_1 = 0.2\pi_2\), i.e. \(\pi = (0.4\,;\,0.6)\). Spectrum: \(1\) and \(0.5\), hence \(P^n = \Pi + 0.5^n\,R\) (spectral decomposition): the distance to equilibrium decreases exactly like \(0.5^n\) — mixing time (defined in ch. 8) \(t_{\mathrm{mix}}(\varepsilon) \approx \lceil \log_2(1/\varepsilon)\rceil\). Check of reversibility: \(\pi_1 P_{12} = 0.4 \times 0.3 = 0.12 = 0.6 \times 0.2 = \pi_2 P_{21}\) ✓.
Example 4.5 — Gaussian AR(1) (general space) \(X_{k+1} = a X_k + \varepsilon_k\), \(|a| < 1\), \(\varepsilon_k\) i.i.d. \(\mathcal{N}(0,\tau^2)\). If \(X_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2/(1-a^2))\) then all the \(X_k\) follow this distribution (stability under Gaussian convolution): it is a Markov chain on \(\mathbb{R}\) with an explicit invariant distribution, reversible (Gaussian kernel symmetric in the gap). It is the exact prototype of a well-tuned random walk Metropolis (ch. 8) and of the Gaussian ULA (ch. 9).
Connection with ML Markov chains appear everywhere: Gibbs samplers for Bayesian inference (see the Refresher Statistics module, chapter 8); latent Markov chains of state-space models; stochastic optimization trajectories; and the diffusion processes of generative models (ch. 9, see the Multimodal GenAI module, chapter 3). The irreducibility/aperiodicity pair respectively guarantees accessibility of the whole space and the absence of locked oscillations.
