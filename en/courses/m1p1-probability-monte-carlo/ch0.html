0. Review: measure, random variables, convergence

L3 review material used
  • Probability spaces: sigma-algebra, probability measure, random variables, pushforward distribution — the vocabulary of the Refresher Statistics module, chapter 1; if this vocabulary is shaky, now is the time to consolidate it;
  • Standard distributions: binomial, Poisson, exponential, Gaussian, gamma — densities, expectations, variances (see the Refresher Statistics module, chapter 3); independence and product measures;
  • Elementary inequalities: Markov, Chebyshev; "workhorse" laws of large numbers and central limit theorem (see the Refresher Statistics module, chapter 4);
  • Real analysis: numerical series, monotone convergence, power series (expansions of \(e^x\), \(\cos\), \(\sin\)), integration by parts, multivariate differential calculus (Jacobian).
Definition 0.1 — Sigma-algebra, measure, probability space A measurable space is a set \(\Omega\) equipped with a sigma-algebra \(\mathcal{F} \subseteq 2^\Omega\) (it contains \(\Omega\) and is stable under complement and countable union). A probability \(\mathbb{P}\) is a measure on \((\Omega, \mathcal{F})\) with total mass 1: \(\mathbb{P}(\Omega)=1\), countably additive. A random variable (r.v.) is a measurable map \(X : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}\) (the preimage of a Borel set lies in \(\mathcal{F}\)); its distribution is the pushforward measure \(\mathbb{P}_X = \mathbb{P} \circ X^{-1}\). This viewpoint — the distribution as a pushforward — is the one that generalizes painlessly to the function spaces of machine learning.
Proposition 0.2 — Markov and Chebyshev (proved) Let \(X\) be a real r.v.
  • Markov: if \(X \geq 0\) and \(a > 0\), \(\mathbb{P}(X \geq a) \leq \mathbb{E}[X]/a\);
  • Chebyshev: if \(\mathrm{Var}(X) < \infty\) and \(t > 0\), $$\mathbb{P}\big(|X - \mathbb{E}X| \geq t\big) \leq \frac{\mathrm{Var}(X)}{t^2}.$$
Markov: \(\mathbb{E}[X] \geq \mathbb{E}[X\,\mathbf{1}_{X \geq a}] \geq a\,\mathbb{P}(X \geq a)\). Chebyshev: apply Markov to \((X - \mathbb{E}X)^2\) at level \(t^2\).∎
Why this module All of probabilistic machine learning — VAEs, diffusion, Bayesian inference, stochastic training — rests on three operations: computing expectations (integration), conditioning (conditional expectation), and sampling (simulation, Monte Carlo, MCMC). This module builds these three pillars under minimal assumptions, then connects them to modern generative algorithms (see the Multimodal GenAI module, chapter 3, for the diffusion part). It completes the "recipe" treatment of the Machine Learning module, chapter 6 (EM), by providing the foundations.
