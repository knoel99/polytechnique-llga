3. Node embeddings

Objective Learn \(z_v \in \mathbb{R}^d\) such that the similarity of the embeddings reflects proximity in the graph: \(\mathrm{sim}(z_u, z_v) \approx P(u \text{ neighbor of } v)\) — an implicit factorization of the adjacency matrix (or of a matrix of derived proximities), in contrast with the GNNs of ch. 5 which condition the embedding on the features \(X\).
DeepWalk (Perozzi et al., 2014) and node2vec (Grover & Leskovec, 2016)
  1. generate random walks from each node (length \(\ell\), \(r\) walks per node); node2vec biases the walk with \((p, q)\) — developed below;
  2. apply word2vec skip-gram to the walks: the "sentences" are sequences of nodes, the window \(b\) defines the (center, context) pairs;
  3. afterwards: any vector method (k-means, classification…).
The random walk defines proximity: two often co-visited nodes get close embeddings — the NetMF theory (Qiu et al., 2018) shows retrospectively that DeepWalk factorizes a deterministic matrix of walks (Theorem 3.3).
Derivation 3.1 — Skip-gram with negative sampling: the loss and its gradient For a (center \(c\), context \(o\)) pair and \(K\) negatives \(v_1, \dots, v_K\) drawn from a noise distribution \(P_n\) (unigram law raised to the power 3/4, as in NLP): $$\mathcal{L}(c, o) = -\log \sigma(z_o^\top z_c) \; - \; \sum_{k=1}^{K} \log \sigma(-z_{v_k}^\top z_c),$$ with \(\sigma(s) = 1/(1 + e^{-s})\). Writing \(s_k = z_{v_k}^\top z_c\) and differentiating \(\frac{d}{ds}[-\log\sigma(s)] = \sigma(s) - 1\): $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_c} = \big(\sigma(z_o^\top z_c) - 1\big)\, z_o \; + \; \sum_{k=1}^K \sigma(s_k)\, z_{v_k}, \qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_o} = \big(\sigma(z_o^\top z_c) - 1\big)\, z_c.$$ Reading: the term \(-z_o\) (since \(\sigma < 1\)) attracts the context toward the center, each negative repels with a weight \(\sigma(s_k) \in ]0,1[\) — strong if the negative is already too close. Stochastic gradient descent: cost per pair \(\mathcal{O}(K d)\), independent of \(n\) — this is what made word2vec (and hence DeepWalk) scalable; the hierarchical softmax trick replaces the \(\mathcal{O}(n)\) of the full softmax by \(\mathcal{O}(\log n)\).
Derivation 3.2 — node2vec: the biases \(p\) and \(q\) via transition probabilities At step \(i\), the walk comes from \(t\), is at \(v\); for the candidate \(x \in \mathcal{N}(v)\): $$\pi_{tx} = \frac{w_{tx}}{Z}\, \alpha_{pq}(t, x), \qquad \alpha_{pq}(t, x) = \begin{cases} 1/p & \text{if } d_{tx} = 0 \\ 1 & \text{if } d_{tx} = 1 \\ 1/q & \text{if } d_{tx} = 2 \end{cases}$$ (\(d_{tx}\) = distance in the graph; \(Z\) normalizes over \(\mathcal{N}(v)\); in a simple graph, \(d_{tx} \leq 2\) for every \(x \in \mathcal{N}(v) \cup \{t\}\) if \(t\) and \(v\) are adjacent). Three regimes:
  • \(p \lt 1\): return toward \(t\) favored — the walk stays in the close neighborhood (BFS): two nodes with analogous structural roles (hubs, bridges) get close embeddings — structural equivalence;
  • \(q \lt 1\): vertices at \(d_{tx} = 2\) are favored — the walk moves away (DFS): two nodes of the same community/cluster are close — homophily;
  • \(p = q = 1\): uniform random walk — DeepWalk is a special case of node2vec.
The qualitative proof: with \(p \to 0\), the probability of returning to \(t\) dominates and the walk samples the 2-hop neighborhood of \(t\) almost uniformly; with \(q \to 0\), the chain avoids \(t\) and behaves like a large-scale exploratory walk — find these behaviors again in exercise 3.
Theorem 3.3 — NetMF: DeepWalk as matrix factorization (Qiu et al., 2018) As the window \(b\) and the number of walks grow, DeepWalk converges to the (truncated, element-wise) factorization of the matrix: $$M = \log\Big( \frac{\mathrm{vol}(G)}{b\, T} \Big( \sum_{r=1}^{T} P^r \Big) D^{-1} \Big), \qquad \mathrm{vol}(G) = \sum_v d_v = 2m,$$ that is, we look for \(Z Z^\top \approx M\); \(T\) plays the role of the effective horizon of the walks. Consequences: (i) a theoretical bridge — DeepWalk, LINE, PTE and node2vec are all factorizations of variants of \(M\) (hence the name "NetMF", Network Embedding as Matrix Factorization); (ii) a deterministic algorithm — factorizing \(M\) directly (truncated SVD) matches or beats DeepWalk without drawing a single walk; (iii) a diagnostic — the target matrix is built on \(P = D^{-1}A\): the embeddings encode the walks, not the features, and remain transductive (a new node requires recomputing everything — direct motivation for the inductive GNNs of ch. 5).
The "word2vec on the graph" intuition — and its converse This is the historical NLP ↔ graphs bridge: a corpus of walks ≈ a corpus of sentences. The teaching team pushed the reverse idea — Graph of Words (ch. 10): representing a document by a graph of co-occurring terms and classifying it with graph tools. Both directions converge today in LLMs on graphs (ch. 11). To go beyond walks (with repetitions) toward paths (simple), see the Path Neural Networks (ch. 10; M2, ch. 1 and 8).
Example 3.4 — Karate Club: complete code Zachary's graph (34 nodes, 78 edges, 2 factions at the club split) is the "MNIST of graphs". 
import numpy as np, networkx as nx
from gensim.models import Word2Vec
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans
from sklearn.metrics import normalized_mutual_info_score

G = nx.karate_club_graph()
y = np.array([0 if G.nodes[v]["club"] == "Mr. Hi" else 1 for v in G])

def biased_walk(G, v, p, q, length):
    walk, prev = [v], None
    for _ in range(length):
        cand = list(G.neighbors(walk[-1]))
        probs = []
        for x in cand:
            d = 0 if x == prev else nx.shortest_path_length(G, prev, x) if prev is not None else 1
            probs.append({0: 1/p, 1: 1.0, 2: 1/q}[d])
        probs = np.array(probs) / sum(probs)
        prev, nxt = walk[-1], np.random.choice(cand, p=probs)
        walk.append(nxt)
    return walk

walks = [ [str(u) for u in biased_walk(G, v, p=1, q=0.25, length=30)]
          for _ in range(80) for v in G.nodes() ]
model = Word2Vec(walks, vector_size=64, window=5, min_count=0, sg=1, epochs=10)
Z = np.array([model.wv[str(v)] for v in sorted(G.nodes())])
km = KMeans(n_clusters=2, n_init=10, random_state=0).fit(Z)
print("NMI :", normalized_mutual_info_score(y, km.labels_))   # ~0.5-0.8 depending on p, q, seed
Exercise 3: vary \((p, q)\) and relate the NMI to the BFS/DFS regime.
