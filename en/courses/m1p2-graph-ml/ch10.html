10. Research of the teaching team

The LLGA master is taught by the DaSciM laboratory (Data Science and Mining, LIX, École Polytechnique): M. Vazirgiannis, J. Lutzeyer and their PhD students. This chapter presents four research directions of the laboratory in direct connection with the program; the papers are to be read and presented as a project — this is the module's "paper" exercise (and the daily bread of M2, ch. 8).

10.1 — Graph of Words: from document to graph (Rousseau & Vazirgiannis; Tixier, Malliaros & Vazirgiannis) The "bag-of-words" representation ignores the order of words; the graph of words partially restores it:
  1. construction: sliding window of size \(w\) (typically 2-10); nodes = unique terms (weighted by frequency); edge \((u, v)\) if \(u\) and \(v\) co-occur in a window, weighted by the number of co-occurrences; a document = one graph;
  2. keywords: extract the "central" nodes of the graph; Rousseau & Vazirgiannis (ECIR 2015) keep the main k-core (the maximal k-core: the largest subgraph with min degree \(\geq k\)), Tixier et al. (EMNLP 2016) classify by degeneracy (the core dominates classification);
  3. document classification: a corpus = a set of graphs → graph classification (ch. 2); moving from bag-of-words → graph-of-words enriches the co-occurrence features and improves the classic text classifiers.
Positioning: this is the symmetric twin of ch. 3 — DeepWalk applies word2vec to graphs, Graph of Words applies graphs to text. Posterity: graph-of-words + graph CNNs for hierarchical classification; topic modeling by bipartite SBM (TopSBM, Gerlach et al., 2018); and the GraphRAG indexes (ch. 11) that index entities and co-occurrences — an enterprise graph of words.
10.2 — Path Neural Networks (Michel, Nikolentzos, Lutzeyer & Vazirgiannis, ICML 2023) Idea: the GNN layer of ch. 5 aggregates walks of length 1 (the neighbors); paths of length \(\ell\) carry more structural information. The path operator: for each node \(v\), consider the set \(\mathcal{P}_v^{(\ell)}\) of simple paths of length \(\ell\) starting from \(v\); each path \(p = (v, u_1, \dots, u_\ell)\) is encoded by an RNN (GRU): $$m_p = \mathrm{GRU}\big(h_{u_\ell}^{(\ell)}, \mathrm{GRU}(\dots \mathrm{GRU}(h_{u_1}^{(0)}, h_v^{(0)})\dots)\big), \qquad h_v^{(\ell)} = \mathrm{AGG}\big(\{m_p : p \in \mathcal{P}_v^{(\ell)}\}\big).$$ Three variants depending on \(\mathcal{P}_v\): shortest paths (PathNN-s), random walks (PathNN-r), paths sampled in the neighborhood (PathNN-n) — sampling bounds the cost, since the number of simple paths grows exponentially. Results: accuracies greater than or equal to the canonical GNNs on the classification benchmarks (nodes and graphs, including ZINC for molecules), with controlled complexity; the expressivity analysis (what the paths see that the walks miss — cycles, distances) is developed in M2, ch. 1 and 8. Positioning in the course: the "natural" generalization of ch. 3 (DeepWalk walks) and of ch. 5 (1-hop neighborhoods).
10.3 — Graph Ordering Attention Networks (Chatzianastasis, Lutzeyer, Dasoulas & Vazirgiannis, AAAI 2023) — J. Lutzeyer's work on ordering and expressivity Invariant aggregators (sum/mean/max) throw away all ordering information — but the local order of the neighbors carries signal (think of a text: the bag of words loses the syntax). GOAT learns a local order:
  1. an attention orders the neighbors of \(v\) (scores learned on \((h_v, h_u)\), sorting);
  2. the ordered sequence is processed by an RNN — a local sequence model, hence strictly more expressive than invariant aggregation;
  3. the GOAT levels (order at increasing radius) compose layer after layer.
The double positioning: in expressivity, GOAT distinguishes graphs that 1-WL confuses (joins M2, ch. 1); in generation, the order choice is THE Gordian knot of autoregressive models (GraphRNN, ch. 9.4; GraphVAE, ch. 9.3) — learning the order rather than suffering it is a transverse contribution. It is also an elegant answer to the GraphSAGE-LSTM question of ch. 5 (the neighborhood there was ordered randomly).
10.4 — Two recent bridges to GenAI: NGG and pseudo-code prompting
  • NGG — Neural Graph Generator (Evdaimon et al., with Nikolentzos & Vazirgiannis, 2024): latent diffusion conditioned on graph features (spectrum, degrees…): a GNN encoder compresses graphs into latent vectors, a diffusion model learns the distribution and the conditioning there; one then generates graphs exhibiting the requested features — ch. 9.5 transposed into latent space, with the spectrum (ch. 4) as descriptor; applications: synthetic data, anonymization, distributional anomaly detection;
  • Pseudo-code prompting (Skianis, Nikolentzos & Vazirgiannis, 2024): make the LLMs reason about graphs (connected components, shortest paths…) by giving them instructions in pseudo-code rather than in natural language — systematic gains across all the LLM sizes tested; detailed in ch. 11.
These four directions (text↔graph, paths, order, generation/diffusion, LLM) cover exactly the map of the master — M2, ch. 8, develops them on the theory side.
