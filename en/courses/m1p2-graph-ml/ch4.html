4. Spectral filters and spectral clustering

Definition 4.1 — Graph Fourier transform Let \(L = U \Lambda U^\top\) (spectral decomposition, \(U\) orthogonal, columns \(u_1, \dots, u_n\)). For a signal \(x \in \mathbb{R}^n\) carried by the nodes: $$\hat x = U^\top x \quad (\text{analysis}), \qquad x = U \hat x \quad (\text{synthesis}).$$ The "frequencies" are the eigenvalues \(\lambda_k\) — small = slow variations over the graph (low frequencies), large = local alternations (high frequencies). This is the convolution of the Signal Processing module, with the Laplacian in place of \(-i\frac{d}{dt}\): \(L\) plays the role of the differential operator there.
Theorem 4.2 — Variational characterization of \(\lambda_2\) (Fiedler) For a connected graph: $$\lambda_2 \;=\; \min_{x \neq 0,\ x \perp \mathbf{1}} \frac{x^\top L x}{x^\top x},$$ attained at the Fiedler vector \(u_2\). The smaller \(\lambda_2\), the more there exists a quasi-constant signal "almost orthogonal" to \(\mathbf{1}\) — that is, two large blocks of weakly connected opposite values.
Write \(x = \sum_i c_i u_i\) (orthonormal basis); \(x \perp \mathbf{1}\) forces \(c_1 = 0\) since \(u_1 = \mathbf{1}/\sqrt{n}\) (the kernel of \(L\) is exactly spanned by \(\mathbf{1}\), Prop. 0.2 of the multiplicity computation). Then \(x^\top L x = \sum_{i \geq 2} \lambda_i c_i^2\) and \(x^\top x = \sum_{i \geq 2} c_i^2\); the quotient is a weighted average of the \(\lambda_i, i \geq 2\), hence lower-bounded by \(\lambda_2\), with equality for \(x = u_2\). ∎
Example 4.3 — Fiedler vector on two linked cliques (exact computation) Two \(K_5\)s (\(\{u, x_1, \dots, x_4\}\) and \(\{v, y_1, \dots, y_4\}\)) linked by the bridge \((u, v)\). By symmetry, look for an antisymmetric eigenvector: \(x_i = a\) on the 4 non-bridge nodes of the left, \(u \mapsto b\), and opposite values on the right. The equations \(L x = \lambda x\) at a non-bridge node (degree 4) and at \(u\) (degree 5) give: $$\lambda a = a - b \qquad \text{and} \qquad \lambda b = 6b - 4a \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 2 = 0.$$ Hence \(\lambda_2 = \frac{7 - \sqrt{41}}{2} \approx 0.298\) and \(b = (1 - \lambda_2)\, a \approx 0.70\, a\); the Fiedler vector equals, up to normalization: $$(\underbrace{1, 1, 1, 1}_{x_i}, \underbrace{0.70}_{u}, \underbrace{-0.70}_{v}, \underbrace{-1, -1, -1, -1}_{y_j})$$ — the sign exactly separates the two communities, and the bridge node takes an intermediate value. This is the picture to remember about spectral clustering.
Theorem 4.4 — Spectral clustering: complete algorithm and justification (RatioCut relaxation) Algorithm (for \(k\) communities):
  1. compute the first \(k\) eigenvectors \(u_1, \dots, u_k\) of \(L\); form \(F = [u_1 | \dots | u_k] \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\);
  2. normalize each row of \(F\);
  3. apply k-means to the \(n\) rows of \(F\) (spectral descriptor).
Justification: the RatioCut of a partition \(\{C_1, \dots, C_k\}\) is \(\mathrm{RatioCut} = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \frac{\mathrm{cut}(C_j, \bar{C}_j)}{|C_j|}\) (cutting a small community is costly — unlike the plain minimization of the cut). Let \(H \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) denote the renormalized indicators \(H_{ij} = \mathbb{1}[v_i \in C_j] / \sqrt{|C_j|}\).
(i) Exactness on the discrete class. \(H^\top H = I\) (orthogonal columns of norm 1). Moreover \(h_j^\top L h_j = \sum_{(u,v) \in E} (h_j(u) - h_j(v))^2 = \frac{1}{|C_j|} \times |\{(u,v) \in E : |\{u, v\} \cap C_j| = 1\}| = \frac{\mathrm{cut}(C_j, \bar{C_j})}{|C_j|}\). So \(\mathrm{tr}(H^\top L H) = \mathrm{RatioCut}\): minimizing RatioCut = minimizing \(\mathrm{tr}(H^\top L H)\) over orthonormal indicators. (ii) Relaxation. We allow any \(F \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) with \(F^\top F = I\); the relaxed problem \(\min_{F^\top F = I} \mathrm{tr}(F^\top L F)\) has as solution the first \(k\) eigenvectors (Ky Fan's theorem: shown by writing \(F^\top L F = \sum_{ij} (F^\top u_i)\,\lambda_i\,(u_j^\top F)\) and exchanging the order of summation, then minimizing the diagonalized coefficients). (iii) Back to the discrete. The rows of \(F\) remain close to the rows of renormalized indicators; k-means groups them. ∎
Normalized Cut variant (Shi & Malik): weighting by the volumes \(d(C_j)\) leads to the generalized problem \(L y = \lambda D y\), solved by the eigenvectors of \(L_{\mathrm{sym}}\) — this is the version used in practice (and by scikit-learn). Links: PCA (Machine Learning module — the matrix \(F\) is the spectral counterpart of the projection); SBM (ch. 9) for the statistical approach; sign ambiguities of the eigenvectors — a real problem for GNNs, solved by SignNet (M2, ch. 3).
Theorem 4.5 — ChebNet: Chebyshev polynomials (Defferrard et al., 2016) Filtering exactly costs \(y = U\, g_\theta(\Lambda)\, U^\top x\) — a full decomposition (\(\mathcal{O}(n^3)\)) specific to each graph. Trick: take \(g_\theta\) a polynomial of degree \(K\) in the Chebyshev basis. Set \(T_0(x) = 1\), \(T_1(x) = x\), \(T_{k+1}(x) = 2x T_k(x) - T_{k-1}(x)\), and \(\tilde\Lambda = \frac{2\Lambda}{\lambda_{\max}} - I\) (rescaling to \([-1, 1]\)); then $$y = \sum_{k=0}^K \theta_k\, T_k(\tilde L)\, x, \qquad \tilde L = \frac{2L}{\lambda_{\max}} - I,$$ without eigenvectors: \(K\)-localized filter, weights \(\theta\) shared by all graphs.
(i) Chebyshev recursion. For \(x = \cos\theta\), \(T_k(x) = \cos(k\theta)\); the addition formula \(\cos((k{+}1)\theta) = 2\cos\theta \cos(k\theta) - \cos((k{-}1)\theta)\) gives the recursion (it propagates to all \(x \in \mathbb{R}\) by polynomial identity). (ii) Functional calculus. Let us show \(T_k(\tilde L) = U T_k(\tilde\Lambda) U^\top\): this is true for the monomials by recursion — \(\tilde L^m = (U \tilde\Lambda U^\top)^m = U \tilde\Lambda^m U^\top\), the inner products \(U^\top U\) canceling — hence for every polynomial. Thus \(y = U\, g_\theta(\tilde\Lambda)\, U^\top x = g_\theta(\tilde L)\, x\): the spectral filter is expressed as a polynomial of \(L\) applied directly to \(x\). (iii) Locality. \(\tilde L x\) at a node only combines \(x_v\) and \(\{x_u : u \in \mathcal{N}(v)\}\); by recursion on the Chebyshev recursion (linear combination of \(\tilde L^k x\), \(k \leq K\)), the output at \(v\) depends only on the \(K\)-neighborhood. ∎
Cost: \(\mathcal{O}(K |E|)\) per application — linear in the size of the graph.
Proposition 4.6 — From spectral to spatial: the equality demonstrated Every filter that is a polynomial of degree \(K\) in \(L\) is exactly a \(K\)-localized operator, and conversely every message passing operator with \(K\) layers whose propagation is a polynomial of \(A\) realizes a polynomial spectral filter.
Forward direction: developed in (iii) above. Conversely: a local layer \(y_v = \sum_{u \in \{v\} \cup \mathcal{N}(v)} c_{uv} x_u\) with uniform weights by symmetry writes in matrix form \(y = \sum_{k \leq 1} c_k\, \Pi_k(A) x\) for polynomials \(\Pi_k\) of degree \(\leq 1\) (the diagonal term is \(\Pi_0\), the edges \(\Pi_1\)); composing \(K\) layers without non-linearity, \(y = \Pi(A) x\) with \(\deg \Pi \leq K\), and since \(L = D - A\) is itself a polynomial in \(A\) (with \(D\) fixed by the graph), \(\Pi(A) = g(L)\) for a polynomial \(g\) of the same degree. The non-linearities between layers break the exact equality — this is precisely the gap between the spectral "CNN on graph" and the spatial GNN: the GNN generalizes the filter by inserting point-wise non-linearities, like a 1D CNN. ∎
