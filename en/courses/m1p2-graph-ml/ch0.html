0. Refresher: graph theory

L3 prerequisites — the "graph" object (cf. Refresher CS)
  • Definitions: \(G = (V, E)\); directed/undirected, weighted; degree \(d_v\), neighbors \(\mathcal{N}(v)\), adjacency matrix \(A\), degree matrix \(D = \mathrm{diag}(d_v)\). Paths, connectivity, components, cycles, DAGs.
  • Euler and Königsberg (1736): crossing the 7 bridges is impossible because more than two vertices have odd degree — the first theorem of graph theory; the abstraction (nodes + edges) founded the discipline.
  • Linear algebra: \((A^k)_{ij}\) counts the paths of length \(k\); eigenvalues of \(A\) = spectrum of the graph; symmetric matrices diagonalizable in an orthonormal basis (algebra reminder).
  • Stochastic matrices: \(P = D^{-1}A\) = random walk; stationary distribution, PageRank \(\pi \propto (I - \alpha P)^{-1}\mathbf{1}\).
Definition 0.1 — Isomorphism, automorphism, subgraph Two graphs \(G = (V, E)\) and \(G' = (V', E')\) are isomorphic if there exists a bijection \(\pi : V \to V'\) such that \((u, v) \in E \iff (\pi(u), \pi(v)) \in E'\). An automorphism is an isomorphism of \(G\) onto itself; their set forms a group \(\mathrm{Aut}(G)\). A subgraph of \(G\) is formed of \(S \subseteq V\) and a subset of \(E \cap (S \times S)\); the induced subgraph keeps all the internal edges. Deciding isomorphism is a problem known neither NP-complete nor polynomial; the Weisfeiler-Lehman test (M2 module, ch. 1) is its fundamental heuristic — and the expressivity bound of GNNs.
Proposition 0.2 — Connectivity and shortest paths The relation "\(u \sim v\) if there exists a path from \(u\) to \(v\)" is an equivalence relation; its classes are the connected components. Between two vertices of the same component, every path of minimal length (shortest path) is a simple path (without repeated vertex).
Reflexivity (path of length 0), symmetry (undirected graph: reverse the path) and transitivity (concatenate) are immediate. If a path from \(u\) to \(v\) visits a vertex \(w\) twice, we delete the cycle between the two visits: the path strictly shortens. A path of minimal length therefore cannot contain a cycle, so every vertex appears there only once: it is simple, of length \(\leq n - 1\). ∎
Theorem 0.3 (spectral theorem) and counting by the trace Every real symmetric matrix \(S \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}\) writes \(S = U \Lambda U^\top\) with \(U\) orthogonal and \(\Lambda\) real diagonal. For the adjacency of a simple graph, three counting identities follow: $$\mathrm{tr}(A) = 0, \qquad \mathrm{tr}(A^2) = 2|E|, \qquad \mathrm{tr}(A^3) = 6\,|\{\text{triangles}\}|.$$
\(\mathrm{tr}(A^2) = \sum_i (A^2)_{ii} = \sum_i d_i\), each edge contributing to two degrees. \((A^3)_{ii}\) counts the closed paths of length 3 through \(i\): these are exactly the triangles containing \(i\), each traversed in both directions from \(i\). Each triangle \(\{i, j, k\}\) thus produces 2 closed paths for each of its 3 vertices, i.e. 6 countings; conversely a closed path of length 3 \(i \to j \to k \to i\) visits three pairwise distinct, pairwise adjacent vertices. ∎
Immediate application in learning: counting triangles (motifs) by matrix products — the basic ingredient of the structural features of ch. 9 and of the expressivity of the M2 module (ch. 1).
Definition 0.4 — The Laplacian of a graph $$L = D - A, \qquad \text{or normalized: } L_{\mathrm{sym}} = I - D^{-1/2} A\, D^{-1/2}.$$ For every vector \(x \in \mathbb{R}^n\) (\(n = |V|\)): $$x^\top L x = \sum_{(u,v) \in E} (x_u - x_v)^2 \ \geq\ 0$$ — \(L\) is positive semi-definite; its eigenvalues \(0 = \lambda_1 \leq \lambda_2 \leq \dots \leq \lambda_n\) encode connectivity; \(\lambda_2\) (the spectral gap, Fiedler 1973) measures the difficulty of cutting the graph, and the number of zero eigenvalues equals the number of connected components.
\(x^\top D x = \sum_v d_v x_v^2\) and \(x^\top A x = 2 \sum_{(u,v) \in E} x_u x_v\) (symmetric matrix); hence \(x^\top L x = \sum_{(u,v) \in E} (x_u^2 + x_v^2 - 2 x_u x_v)\). For the multiplicity of 0: reordering the vertices by components makes \(A\) (and \(L\)) block-diagonal; each block \(L_C\) has the indicator \(\mathbf{1}_C\) as kernel, since \(x^\top L_C x = 0\) forces \(x\) constant on each connected component (the sum of squares cancels edge by edge, and connectivity propagates the constant — Prop. 0.2). ∎
Example 0.5 — The spectrum tells the story Path of \(n\) vertices: \(\lambda_k = 2(1 - \cos\frac{k\pi}{n})\); complete graph: \(0\) then \(n\) (multiplicity \(n-1\)); cycle: eigenvalues of \(2\cos\). A "two-community" graph has a small \(\lambda_2\); the Fiedler eigenvector separates the communities — this is the heart of spectral clustering, developed in ch. 4 (direct bridge with the PCA of the Machine Learning module).
Proposition 0.6 — Random walks: stationarity and ergodicity Let \(G\) be connected undirected, \(P = D^{-1}A\), \(m = |E|\). The distribution \(\pi_v = d_v / (2m)\) is stationary: \(\pi^\top P = \pi^\top\). If moreover \(G\) is not bipartite, the chain is irreducible and aperiodic, hence ergodic: $$P^t(x, \cdot) \xrightarrow[t \to \infty]{} \pi \qquad \text{whatever the starting point } x.$$
Detailed balance: \(\pi_u P_{uv} = \frac{d_u}{2m} \cdot \frac{\mathbb{1}[(u,v) \in E]}{d_u} = \frac{\mathbb{1}[(u,v) \in E]}{2m}\), a quantity symmetric in \(u, v\); summing over \(u\), \((\pi^\top P)_v = \pi_v\). Irreducibility: connectivity makes every vertex reachable. Periodicity: on a bipartite graph, every walk has a fixed parity (\(P^t(x, x) = 0\) for odd \(t\)); conversely, every edge \((u,v)\) of a non-bipartite graph belongs to an odd cycle, providing returns of periods \(d(u,v)+1\) and \(d(u,v)-1\), coprime — period 1. The convergence theorem for Markov chains (cf. Refresher Statistics) concludes. ∎
Practical consequence: the walks of DeepWalk (ch. 3) "mix" the distribution toward \(\pi \propto\) degrees — hence the bias of embeddings toward hubs, partly corrected by node2vec and by NetMF.
Definition 0.7 — Modularity (Newman & Girvan, 2004) For a partition \(\mathcal{C} = \{C_1, \dots, C_k\}\) of the vertices: $$Q(\mathcal{C}) = \frac{1}{2m} \sum_{i,j} \Big[ A_{ij} - \frac{d_i\, d_j}{2m} \Big]\, \delta(c_i, c_j),$$ where \(c_i\) is the community of \(i\) and \(\delta\) the indicator. \(Q\) compares the observed density of intra-community edges to the expected one under the configuration model (edges redistributed while preserving degrees, term \(d_i d_j / 2m\)). Maximization sketch: maximizing \(Q\) exactly is NP-hard; Louvain does it greedily — iteratively merging the (community, neighbor) pairs that most increase \(\Delta Q = \frac{1}{2m}[\Sigma_{in} - \frac{(\Sigma_{tot} + d_i)^2}{4m}]\dots\), then contracting — hence hierarchies of communities used as-is by GraphRAG (ch. 11). Known limit: resolution — \(Q\) cannot "see" communities smaller than ~\(\sqrt{2m}\) edges; SBM (ch. 9) offers a better-founded statistical alternative.
