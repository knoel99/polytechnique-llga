Introduction and overview

ModuleGraph Machine and Deep Learning for Generative AI
CodeCSC_52072_EP
Team and workloadInstructors: Johannes Lutzeyer & Michalis Vazirgiannis (École Polytechnique) · 36 h · 4.5 ECTS · from graph theory foundations to GNNs, graph generation and GraphRAG — the module that gives the master's program its name, and the foundation of the M2 module "Advanced Graph Neural Networks".
Status in the programMandatoryCore of the master

Social networks, molecules, knowledge graphs, citations, infrastructure: a vast share of the world's data is relational, hence naturally represented by a graph. This course goes from graph theory foundations to graph neural networks (GNNs), graph generation and the combination with LLMs (GraphRAG). It is the module that gives the master's program its name: the central course of the program, whose concepts will reappear in almost every M2 module.

What you will be able to do

  • Model an applied problem as a graph and identify the associated learning task (node, edge, or whole graph).
  • Encode the structure of a graph into node embeddings usable by a classical model.
  • Understand spectral filters and spectral clustering via the graph Laplacian.
  • Build and train a GNN by message passing.
  • Solve link prediction and use graph auto-encoders.
  • Scale a GNN to massive graphs (neighborhood sampling, mini-batches).
  • Generate realistic graphs with generative models.
  • Combine graphs and LLMs: GraphRAG and hybrid approaches.

Where this course fits in the program

Mandatory module of period 2 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), taught by Johannes Lutzeyer and Michalis Vazirgiannis: it is the heart of the master's program, the one that structures the "graph" axis around which the advanced M2 modules are organized.

  • Prerequisites — Machine Learning and Deep Learning (the attention mechanism is presented there and reused here), as well as the graph algorithmic basics of the Refresher in Computer Science (BFS, Dijkstra); the refresher section goes over the graph theory useful for the course.
  • Downstream modules — it is the direct foundation of Advanced Graph Neural Networks (M2) and Graph Generative Models and Applications (M2); its GraphRAG chapter prepares the Large Language Models module (M2), and it also feeds Explainability, Security, Privacy of LLMs (M2).

Course outline

  • Refresher: graph theory — vocabulary, traversals and measures put back in place before learning on them.
  • Why graphs? — the motivation: relational, non-Euclidean data, everywhere in industry and science.
  • Learning tasks on graphs — classifying a node, an edge, a whole graph: the taxonomy of the field.
  • Node embeddings — encoding structure into vectors, with the constraints that guide them.
  • Spectral filters and spectral clustering — the Laplacian, its eigenvalues, and the partition that follows.
  • GNNs and message passing — the central building block: propagating information along the edges, layer after layer.
  • Link prediction and graph auto-encoders — predicting missing edges and compressing graphs.
  • GNNs at scale — training on graphs that fit neither in memory nor in a single batch.
  • Applications and hands-on — going from equations to libraries on real use cases.
  • Graph generation — producing new and realistic graphs: the entry into relational generative AI.
  • Research of the teaching team — a tour of the research work of Lutzeyer and Vazirgiannis.
  • Graphs × LLMs: GraphRAG and the hybrid — making a knowledge graph and a large language model cooperate.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Redo the refresher section even if you "know" graphs: BFS and Dijkstra are covered again because the rest of the course relies on them without warning.
  • Implement a message passing from scratch on a small hand-drawn graph before using a library: it is ten lines that light up the whole field.
  • Follow the spectral thread: Laplacian, spectral clustering then GNNs are the same object in three costumes — this thread also prepares the M2 module on geometric data.
  • Visualize node embeddings (dimensionality reduction) to check your intuition on each dataset.
  • Read the GraphRAG chapter in parallel with the Introduction to Text Mining and NLP module, taught in the same period: the two shed light on each other.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next; they build the foundation reused by all the downstream M2 modules.
The LLGA positioning This module is the "graph" half of the master's program, the one that gives the track its meaning: the GraphRAG of the last chapter is exactly the junction point with the LLM axis, extended in M2 by Advanced Graph Neural Networks, Graph Generative Models and Applications and Large Language Models. Fraud detection, recommendation, chemistry, cybersecurity, enterprise knowledge graphs: the industrial and research career paths of graph learning are among the most promising of the discipline.
