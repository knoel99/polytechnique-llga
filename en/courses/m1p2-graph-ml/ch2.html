Definition 2.1 — Permutation invariance and equivariance A graph has neither a fixed size nor a canonical order of the nodes: the model must be permutation-invariant (graph output): \(f(P X, P A P^\top) = f(X, A)\), or permutation-equivariant (per-node output): \(f(PX, PAP^\top) = P f(X, A)\), for every permutation matrix \(P\). CNNs (grid), RNNs (sequence) violate these constraints — hence dedicated architectures (ch. 5) and the whole expressivity theory of the M2 module (ch. 1).
Transductive vs inductive — a distinction that structures the whole module
  • Transductive: training and testing on the same graph (embeddings ch. 3); impossible to predict for a node arriving after training;
  • Inductive: the model (its weights) reapplies to new graphs (GraphSAGE, GNNs ch. 5) — indispensable in production;
  • Structural semi-supervised: on Cora (2,708 nodes), 140 labels suffice — the structure relays the information (homophily hypothesis: neighbors share the label; when it fails, one needs "virtual" edges or heterophilous models).
