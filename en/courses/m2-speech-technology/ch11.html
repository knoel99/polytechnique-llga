11. Speech LLMs and multilingualism

Definition 11.1 — Tokenizing audio: neural codecs and RVQ A neural codec (EnCodec: Défossez et al., 2022) compresses speech with a convolutional auto-encoder constrained by a residual vector quantization (RVQ): the continuous latent is approximated by a sum of vectors from successive codebooks — each frame (at 24 kHz: one frame \(\approx\) 320 samples, ~75 Hz) becomes a stack of ~2-32 discrete indices. Result: speech becomes a text of tokens — consumable by a transformer. The hierarchy of AudioLM (Borsos et al., 2023) orders these tokens: first semantic (discretized units of a self-supervised model — wav2vec 2/HuBERT — that capture the content), then coarse then fine acoustic (timbre, environment): generating in this order guarantees the content before the texture. It is the architecture of dGSLM and Moshi (ch. 10).
Theorem 11.2 — Speech LLMs: three audio grafts onto an LLM
  1. Audio-in: the speech encoder (Whisper, HuBERT) is projected into the LLM's space by an adapter (SALMONN, SpeechGPT, and all multimodal assistants 2025+) — the model listens (implicit transcription, paralinguistics: emotion, speaker) and answers in text; it is the "audio-in" family of ch. 8 of the LLMs module (M2), same machinery as vision;
  2. Audio-out: the LLM generates acoustic tokens (VALL-E: AR over EnCodec with a voice prompt; AudioLM units at the output); the cloning of ch. 9 becomes a skill of the language model;
  3. Audio-in-out (speech-to-speech): Moshi (ch. 10): the text is the inner monologue guiding the audio tokens in both directions — the LLM is the pipeline. SpeechT5 (2022) had laid down the first unified text/speech architecture (T5 encoder-decoder + shared codebook); 2024-2026 generalize: one model, all the module's tasks (ASR, TTS, speech translation, editing, voice to voice).
Proposition 11.3 — Multilingualism: 7,000 languages, 100 covered The bottleneck of low-resource languages: labeling is expensive, writing is sometimes lacking. MMS (Meta, 2023 — "Scaling Speech Technology to 1000+ Languages"): a single wav2vec 2 model covers 1,107 languages in ASR and TTS (4,017 in language identification), learned on public religious readings (the paradox: the bible is the most translated corpus in the world) — a jump by a factor of 10-40 in coverage. Common Voice (Mozilla): open crowd-sourcing (including French — regional accents: lab 1 draws from it). The specificities of French return (ch. 4, ex. 4.3): number normalization (the date sub-test: "en 1996"), acronyms, loanwords. For the engineer: zero-shot (Whisper/MMS) first, light adaptation next (LoRA on the encoder — LLMs module M2 ch. 6), targeted data last; and measure per language — a multilingual "average" WER masks local collapses.
Conclusion of the module — the fusion of the three worlds Signal (M1), language (LLMs M2), conversation (this module): speech is no longer an interface in front of language, it becomes one of its dialects — discrete tokens like any others, generatable, guidable, controllable. The control of the Control in Generative AI module (M2, ch. 2: the taxonomy) now applies word for word: hard constraints = SSML and prosody grammars; guidance = style tokens and cloning by prompt; watermarking = voice provenance (ch. 9); evaluation = WER/MOS/latency and trade-off curves. Wherever you set foot in 2026 — research or product — the final question is the same: what must be controlled, and at what price?
