9. Voice conversion and cloning: ethics and watermarking

Definition 9.1 — Conversion, adaptation, zero-shot cloning Voice conversion: re-emit what a source voice says with the timbre of a target voice (content preserved, speaker changed). Zero-shot cloning: synthesize any text in a given voice from an audio prompt of a few seconds (VALL-E, Voicebox: conditioning by reference, ch. 7). Technically: a speaker embedding (learned by speaker verification — the GE2E loss of Wan et al., 2018, aligns the embeddings of the same voice); YourTTS (ICML 2022) adds it to VITS (the TTS of ch. 7 becomes multi-speaker in one training); VALL-E (Microsoft, 2023) treats cloning as language: AR over the EnCodec acoustic tokens (ch. 11), the 3 s prompt serving as prefix — TTS is absorbed by the LLM paradigm.
Theorem 9.2 — Ethics: the law follows (slowly), the engineer decides (fast) Cloning from 3 s of reference makes possible: voice fraud (calls to the "boss"), audio disinformation, impersonation. Frameworks: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act, art. 50) imposes transparency — systems generating or manipulating image/voice must mark their outputs machine-readably; in France, the voice is protected as an attribute of personality (civil case law on imitation). For the engineer, the operational answer is technical: consent (voice datasets under a clear license — lab 2 uses only authorized voices), marking (watermarking, below), detection (provenance: cf. Explainability module ch. 9 for text, the logic is identical). The exam question: "why is TTS watermarking easier than text watermarking?" — because the output space is continuous and the channel capacity is immense (ch. 6): one can hide bits without perceptible loss... but the channel degrades under compressions.
Theorem 9.3 — Audio watermarking: from spread spectrum to networks Classical: spread-spectrum tattooing — add a low-energy pseudo-random noise (key \(k\)), masked by the psychoacoustic threshold effect, in a time-frequency domain (STFT — Signal Processing M1 modules ch. 6-7: time-frequency analysis and random signals are proven there); detection by correlation with the key — the exact structure of the textual green/red watermark (ch. 6 of the Control module): a pseudo-random key, a hypothesis test, a strength/quality trade-off. Neural: WavMark (2023) hides ~32 bits per second of speech with a learned encoder/detector, robust to re-encodings; AudioSeal (Meta, ICASSP 2024) adds localization: a sample-level detector indicates which segments are synthetic (adversarial generator/detector, perceptual loss); marking during generation (in-generation) applies the watermark in the TTS itself — inseparable from the output. Evaluation protocol: ROC curves detection rate / false positives, under attack (mp3, re-voicing, reverberation) — the robustness budget of the Control module ch. 10, word for word.
Example 9.4 — Voice fraud in 3 steps (and its 3 countermeasures) (1) 3 s of public voice \(\to\) zero-shot cloning; (2) urgency script generated by an LLM (referral to LLMs M2); (3) vishing call. Countermeasures: (i) channel: automatic call-back on a known number (the most robust control is organizational); (ii) challenge posed by the interlocutor (off-script question — conversational TTS answer these better and better, cf. ch. 10); (iii) provenance: watermark verified at reception if both phones support it. Moral of the module: no single layer suffices — defense in depth.
