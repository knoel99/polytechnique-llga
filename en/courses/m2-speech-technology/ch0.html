0. Recap: the speech signal and its representations

Prerequisites (Signal Processing M1 module) — what is assumed known
  • Sampling (ch. 4): Shannon's theorem; speech is sampled at 16 kHz (ASR) or 24 kHz (synthesis), useful telephone band 300-3400 Hz, formants up to ~8 kHz;
  • Time-frequency analysis (ch. 6): STFT, Gabor windows, time-frequency trade-off — the spectrogram is the input of almost all the models in this module (cf. Signal Processing module ch. 6);
  • Mel spectrogram and MFCC (ch. 8): 25 ms frames every 10 ms, Hann window, Mel filter bank (perceptual scale linear below 1 kHz then logarithmic: \(m = 2595\log_{10}(1 + f/700)\)), 20-80 coefficients, possibly DCT (MFCC);
  • Random signals (ch. 7): power spectral density, periodogram — used by energy-based VADs (ch. 10) and spread-spectrum watermarking (ch. 9);
  • Recurrent networks and transformers (Deep Learning M1): LSTM, attention \(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})V\) — the ASR/TTS encoder is a transformer (ch. 3-4);
  • Diffusion and VAE (Multimodal GenAI M1 ch. 1 and 3): ELBO, DDPM, noise prediction — the generative TTS models (ch. 7) descend directly from them.
Definition 0.1 — ASR and TTS, formalized Recognition (ASR): estimate the transcription \(y\) (sequence of tokens — characters, phonemes, BPE) of an acoustic frame \(x_{1:T}\) (Mel features, typically \(T \approx 100\) frames for 10 s of speech): model \(p_\theta(y \mid x_{1:T})\). Synthesis (TTS): the inverse, \(p_\theta(x \mid y)\) — a one-to-many problem: a text can be said in infinitely many ways (voice, rate, emotion), which will structure all of ch. 7-8 (TTS is a generation problem, ASR a decision problem). The reference ASR error is the WER: $$\mathrm{WER} = \frac{S + D + I}{N},$$ substitutions/deletions/insertions obtained by word-level Levenshtein alignment between hypothesis and reference (lab 1).
The module's line — and why "conversational" The course follows the stack of a voice assistant: understand (ch. 1-5), speak (ch. 6-9), converse (ch. 10-11). The technical thread: two breaks occurred — (i) the abandonment of explicit alignment (HMM → CTC → attention → discrete tokens); (ii) the fusion of the three ASR+LLM+TTS building blocks into a single multimodal model (speech LLMs, ch. 11) that speaks and listens — where full-duplex conversation (ch. 10) becomes computable. Speech regains its status there: the fastest (3-4 words/s produced, 5-8 understood) and most natural interface.
