6. Vocoders: from WaveNet to HiFi-GAN

Theorem 6.1 — WaveNet (van den Oord et al., 2016): autoregression over the samples The vocoder models the waveform directly: $$p(x) = \prod_{t=1}^{T_{\mathrm{ech}}} p(x_t \mid x_{<t},\, c),$$ with \(T_{\mathrm{ech}}\) = 24,000 samples per second, \(x_t\) \(\mu\)-law quantized over 256 levels, \(c\) the conditioning (Mel, speaker). Architecture: dilated causal convolutions — layer \(k\) looks \(2^k\) samples back (dilations 1, 2, 4, ..., 512, stacked): the receptive field grows exponentially with depth (\(\approx 2^L\) for \(L\) layers), where a non-dilated convolution would require \(2^L\) layers. Quality: close to human speech in subjective tests (MOS above 4, versus ~4.5 for the ground truth); fatal flaw: generating one second of speech takes 24,000 sequential steps — orders of magnitude slower than real time, unusable in production.
Proposition 6.2 — HiFi-GAN (Kong et al., NeurIPS 2020): speed through the adversary Complete reversal: the generator is parallel feed-forward (upsampling by transposed convolutions from the 80-bin Mel to the waveform, then multi-receptive-field dilated convolutions for phase coherence); training is adversarial (GAN recap: Multimodal GenAI M1 module ch. 2) with two families of discriminators — multi-period (they rearrange the waveform into matrices of periods 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 to trap periodic artifacts: the "buzz") and multi-scale — plus an L1 loss on the reconstructed Mel to anchor the content. Result: WaveNet quality, parallel generation — orders of magnitude faster than real time on GPU, the industry's default vocoder. To remember from the chapter: the pairing of mixing duration (autoregressive over 24 kHz) against parallel architecture + perceptual loss dictates all of modern TTS.
The Mel as a contractual interface The two stages of the TTS (ch. 7) communicate via the Mel spectrogram: 80 bins, one frame roughly every 12 ms (~ 80 Hz), logarithmic scale — the vocoder's specification sheet. Changing the definition (sampling frequency, hop, n_bins) breaks the model/vocoder compatibility — the first experiment of lab 2. And the Mel is not invertible (phase lost): the whole difficulty of the vocoder is to re-invent a coherent phase — hence the period discriminators.
