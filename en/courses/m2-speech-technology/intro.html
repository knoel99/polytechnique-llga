Introduction and overview

ModuleSpeech Technology for Conversational AI

Voice is the most natural interface there is: a complete conversational assistant must understand speech (ASR), reason (LLM) and answer with a natural voice (TTS), all continuously and at low latency. This module builds this loop end to end: from the signal and its spectrograms to the GMM-HMM pipeline, the derived CTC loss, the encoder-decoder architectures (LAS, Conformer, RNN-T) and Whisper; then the vocoders from WaveNet to HiFi-GAN, neural TTS (Tacotron 2, VITS, diffusion), expressivity and cloning — with its ethics and its watermarking — up to full-duplex systems and speech LLMs. In the LLGA master, it is the direct bridge between M1 signal processing and M2 language models.

What you will be able to do

  • Choose and compute the representations of the speech signal (Mel spectrogram, MFCC) according to the use;
  • Explain the classical GMM-HMM pipeline and what caused its replacement;
  • Derive the CTC loss (sum over alignments, forward-backward) and implement it;
  • Compare LAS, Conformer and RNN-T according to the quality/latency trade-off, and explain Whisper's multi-task robustness;
  • Size a streaming system: chunking, voice activity detection, latency vs quality;
  • Explain the modern TTS chain: vocoders (WaveNet to HiFi-GAN), Tacotron 2, VITS, diffusion and flow approaches;
  • Control the expressivity of a synthesis (prosody, style tokens, SSML) and master the ethical issues of voice cloning;
  • Describe the complete conversational loop (VAD, turn-taking, full-duplex) and how multilingual speech LLMs work.

Where this course fits in the program

An M2 module offered in period 2, outside the recommended track: it is the master's "multimodal" module devoted to the audio modality, designed as the joint production deployment of two prerequisites. Prerequisites: Signal Processing (windowing, Fourier transform, spectrograms — revisited in ch. 0) and Large Language Models, whose multimodal chapter (ch. 8) announces the speech LLMs covered here at the end of the course.

Downstream: the module gives its voice component to the conversational assistant — the LLGA's application through-line, which crosses the agents of Advanced Topics in Large Language Models (ch. 8) and the production constraints of ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles (P99 latency, costs); it also extends the watermarking of the Control in Generative AI module to the audio side.

Course outline

  • Recap: the speech signal and its representations — windowing, Mel spectrograms and MFCC: the raw material of the whole course.
  • Classical ASR: the GMM-HMM pipeline — the historical architecture, still useful to understand what neural methods replaced.
  • CTC: the loss, derived — learning without frame/phoneme alignment: the complete derivation of the loss that opened up deep ASR.
  • Encoder-decoder: LAS, Conformer, RNN-T — three architectural answers to the quality/latency trade-off.
  • Whisper: robustness and multi-task — what very large-scale supervised learning changes (and costs).
  • Streaming: chunking, VAD, latency vs quality — going from file to stream: split, detect speech, own the latency.
  • Vocoders: from WaveNet to HiFi-GAN — generating the waveform itself, and the path from slow autoregressive models to fast GANs.
  • Neural TTS: Tacotron 2, VITS, diffusion and flow — modern synthesis from text to spectrogram then to signal.
  • Expressivity: prosody, style tokens, SSML — not only saying, but how to say it.
  • Voice conversion and cloning: ethics and watermarking — the promises of cloning, its misuse, and how to mark generated audio.
  • The conversational loop: VAD, turn-taking, full-duplex — listening, waiting one's turn, interrupting oneself: real-time orchestration.
  • Speech LLMs and multilingualism — the speech signal fed directly into the language model: architectures and issues for low-resource languages.
  • Exercises / Labs — align a CTC, measure a WER, synthesize and watermark a voice clip.
  • References — the founding papers of neural ASR and TTS.

How to study this module

  • Listen to every example audio of the course (originals, degraded, synthesized): the ear is here a measuring instrument to train as much as the WER.
  • Write the CTC forward-backward yourself on a mini-example by hand: it is the obligatory step to never again confuse alignment and decoding.
  • For each architecture, place it on the "latency/quality" axis and note what is computed online versus offline: it is the module's reading grid.
  • Wire up a mini "microphone → ASR → response → TTS → speaker" loop early, even a basic one, then improve it chapter after chapter.
  • Read ch. 9 (cloning, ethics) with the eye of a future professional: questions of consent and audio watermarking will arise in your projects from the internship onward.
The LLGA perspective With speech LLMs, voice has become a first-class modality for language models: the LLGA master, which trains at the crossroads of LLMs and their applications, offers you here the complete chain — from spectrogram to speaking turn — rather than a simple overview. Conversational assistants (telephony, accessibility, embedded voice interfaces) are one of the most in-demand career paths in the field, and the module crosses all the master's skills: signal, LLMs, real-time streaming, ethics and control.
