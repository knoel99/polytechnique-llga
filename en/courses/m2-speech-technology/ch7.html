7. Neural TTS: Tacotron 2, VITS, diffusion and flow

Theorem 7.1 — Tacotron 2 (Shen et al., ICASSP 2018): the two-stage scheme Stage 1 (acrobatic): an attentional encoder-decoder reads the text (graphemes) and writes the Mel frame by frame — the location-sensitive attention learns the text→acoustic alignment there (same mechanism as LAS in ch. 3, applied in reverse); training by teacher forcing on the reference Mels. Stage 2: a WaveNet (ch. 6) turns the Mel into a wave. Historical quality: natural on read sentences. Two chronic diseases: attention failure (skipped or stuttered words on long or out-of-distribution sentences) and abusive determinization — one text, a single voice/rate (the one-to-many of Definition 0.1 denied by training toward the average Mel: smoothed, metallic output).
Proposition 7.2 — VITS (Kim et al., ICML 2021): one-stage, stochastic, end-to-end Three ideas that answer Tacotron's diseases. (i) VAE: a variational encoder encodes the target speech into \(z\); the decoder (HiFi-GAN) writes the wave from \(z\) — the noise of the prior explores the multiplicity of ways of saying; (ii) normalizing flow: a flow (exact change of variables: cf. Graph Generative Models M2 module ch. 4 for the proof) aligns the distribution of the text-conditioned latent with the prior, which makes training end-to-end (exact likelihood + adversary); (iii) implicit monotone alignment (MAS: search for the optimal monotone alignment between text and Mel, refined at each step). Result: a single text→wave network, parallel, stochastic — the first "industrializable" generation of TTS (YourTTS, ch. 9, derives from it).
Theorem 7.3 — Iterative generation arrives: Grad-TTS then Voicebox Grad-TTS (Popov et al., ICML 2021): an encoder predicts, for each phone, the target mean \(\mu(x)\) of a Gaussian in Mel space; a diffusion decoder (DDPM — cf. Multimodal GenAI M1 module ch. 3) turns a noise \(\mathcal N(0, I)\) into a Mel around \(\mu\), guided by the noise prediction \(\hat\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \mu)\); a duration predictor handles the number of frames per phone. Quality grows with the number of steps... like latency: the step/quality curve is the trade-off curve of the Control module (M2) ch. 9, in TTS version. Voicebox (Le et al., 2023): conditional flow matching in infilling mode — one masks a region of the Mel and regenerates only it, the context remaining fixed: the same machinery as DiGress's inpainting (Graph Generative Models M2 module ch. 5) transported into acoustics. Immediate applications: editing a word in a sentence while keeping the voice, de-noising, voice translation... and zero-shot cloning from a few seconds of reference. Non-autoregressive and parallel: the dominant family of research TTS 2024-2026.

Example 7.4 — Latency budget of a conversational TTS Goal: < 300 ms between the end of the LLM's decision and the first sound. An AR Tacotron-like: the first sentence must be synthesized before the first sound (if one wants the intonation) — 1-2 s of lead time required: prohibitive. VITS: the first chunk of Mel (a few frames) suffices — ~100 ms; Voicebox: one must bound the number of sampling steps: 4-10 steps (instead of 50-100) with distillation, otherwise quality drops (the curve of thm. 7.3). Conclusion: the full-duplex of ch. 10 was only possible after the non-autoregressive revolution — architecture and conversation are coupled constraints.
