10. Research of the teaching team

Karim Lounici — research directions Karim Lounici (CMAP, École Polytechnique) works on the statistical theory of high-dimensional learning: sparse estimation, oracle bounds for the Lasso and its extensions, aggregation of estimators. The four papers below structure chapters 8 and 9 of this module.
Paper 1 — "Sup-norm convergence rate and sign concentration property of Lasso and Dantzig estimators" K. Lounici, Electronic Journal of Statistics 2, 2008 — arXiv:0801.4610. After Knight and Fu (consistency in estimation), this paper establishes the rate of the Lasso and of the Dantzig selector in sup norm: \(\|\hat\beta - \beta^\star\|_\infty\) decreases exactly at \(\sigma\sqrt{\log p/n}\) (an optimal \(1/\sqrt{n}\) factor), and deduces sign concentration: under a \(\beta_{\min}\) condition and "columns in general position", the support and the signs are exactly recovered with probability tending to 1. It is the natural complement of Theorem 8.4 (prediction → selection).
Paper 2 — "Oracle inequalities and optimal inference under group sparsity" K. Lounici, M. Pontil, S. van de Geer, A. B. Tsybakov, Annals of Statistics 39(4), 2011 — arXiv:1007.1771. The group Lasso penalizes by blocks \(\sum_g \sqrt{d_g}\|b_g\|_2\). The authors prove oracle inequalities for the prediction error and in \(\ell_2\) under a group-wise restricted eigenvalue condition, showing the gain from grouping (the "effective dimension" becomes the number of active groups); above all, they construct simultaneous inference procedures (confidence bands for the components) achieving the minimal price \(s \log p/n\): the first stone of high-dimensional inference of the "debiasing" type.
Paper 3 — "Generalized mirror averaging and D-convex aggregation" K. Lounici, Mathematical Methods of Statistics 16(3), 2007. Standard exponential-weights aggregation requires convex sets; this paper extends mirror averaging (aggregation by mirror averages, i.e. mirror descent in the geometry of the simplex) to the \(D\)-convex framework, with oracle inequalities for linear aggregation — the \(\log M/n\) regime of Theorem 9.3(ii), in a more general geometry (in the lineage of Juditsky-Nemirovski and Rigollet-Tsybakov).
Paper 4 — "PAC-Bayesian bounds for sparse regression estimation with exponential weights" P. Alquier, K. Lounici, Electronic Journal of Statistics 5, 2011 — HAL version. Sparse priors (decreasing masses on supports according to their size) + exponential weights: PAC-Bayesian oracle bounds for high-dimensional regression without a restrictive design condition, with the right \(\log p\). The exact bridge between the PAC-Bayesian note of chapter 9 and the sparsity of chapter 8: aggregation replaces convex penalization.
