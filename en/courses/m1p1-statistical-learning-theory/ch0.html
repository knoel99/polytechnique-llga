0. Review: probability, estimation, convexity

L3 review material used
  • Probability: modes of convergence, law of large numbers, central limit theorem (see the Refresher Statistics module, chapter 4; the proved version of these theorems appears in chapter 2 of the Probability Theory for ML module); Markov and Chebyshev inequalities; conditional expectation; binomial and Gaussian distributions;
  • Estimation: bias and variance of an estimator, quadratic error, maximum likelihood (see the Refresher Statistics module, chapter 5), confidence intervals and elementary tests (chapter 6);
  • Linear regression: least-squares estimator, normal equations, Gauss-Markov theorem (chapter 7; and the Machine Learning module, chapter 2, for ridge regularization);
  • Convex analysis: convex sets and functions, strong convexity, subgradients (the \(\ell_1\) norm is not differentiable at 0 — this is the nerve of the Lasso in chapter 8), directional derivatives.
Proposition 0.1 — Union bound (proved) and first use For events \(A_1, \dots, A_M\): \(\mathbb{P}\big(\bigcup_{j=1}^M A_j\big) \leq \sum_{j=1}^M \mathbb{P}(A_j)\).
By induction on \(M\) starting from \(\mathbb{P}(A \cup B) = \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B) - \mathbb{P}(A \cap B) \leq \mathbb{P}(A) + \mathbb{P}(B)\).∎
This trivial lemma does half the work of this course: controlling a supremum over \(M\) functions means controlling each one (Hoeffding, ch. 2) then taking \(\delta/M\) in each event — the price to pay is a logarithm, \(\log M\), omnipresent in high-dimensional statistics (where \(M\) will be the number \(p\) of variables, or the number of dichotomies \((2n)^d\) of a class of Vapnik-Chervonenkis dimension \(d\), ch. 3-4).
Position in the curriculum This module is the theoretical counterpart of the Machine Learning module: the statistical framework is laid out there in chapter 1 and "generalization theory" is surveyed in chapter 7 — we take up these two points and prove them. It also prepares chapter 2 of the Machine Learning module (Lasso), the choice of hyperparameters by cross-validation (Machine Learning module, chapter 9), and provides the concentration background used everywhere else (Deep Learning and Emerging ML modules).
