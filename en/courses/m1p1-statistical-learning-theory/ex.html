Exercises

  1. Sample size (Hoeffding). A fixed classifier has a 0-1 loss. How many examples to guarantee \(|\hat L - L| \leq 0.02\) with probability \(0.99\)? And if the same guarantee is wanted simultaneously for \(M = 100\) classifiers? Solution. \(n \geq \log(2/\delta)/(2\varepsilon^2) = \log 200/0.0008 = 5.298/0.0008 \approx 6623\). For \(M\) classifiers, union bound: replace \(\delta\) by \(\delta/M = 10^{-4}\): \(n \geq \log(2\times10^4)/0.0008 = 9.903/0.0008 \approx 12\,379\) — the price of "simultaneous" is a factor \(\log M\), not \(M\).
  2. VC dimension of intervals. Show that the class of intervals \(\{x \mapsto \mathbf{1}_{x \in [a,b]}\}\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) has \(\mathrm{VC} = 2\). Solution. Two points \(x_1 < x_2\) are shattered: the four labelings are obtained with \([a,b]\) avoiding everything, containing only \(x_1\), only \(x_2\), or both. Three points \(x_1 < x_2 < x_3\): the labeling \(\{x_1, x_3\}\) without \(x_2\) is impossible (an interval containing the extremes contains the middle): no shattering, \(\mathrm{VC} = 2\). (Bonus question: unions of \(k\) intervals → \(2k\), by the same parity argument on the components.)
  3. Sauer in small dimensions. Verify \(s(N,2) = 1 + N + N(N-1)/2\) and compute \(s(10,2)\); show that the class of thresholds (Example 1.4) achieves exactly \(N+1\) dichotomies, and conclude that the Sauer bound is not always tight. Solution. \(\sum_{k\leq2}\binom{N}{k} = 1 + N + N(N-1)/2\); \(s(10,2) = 1 + 10 + 45 = 56\). Thresholds produce \(N+1\) dichotomies on \(N\) sorted points (cut after the \(k\)-th): \(11\) for \(N=10\), far below \(56\) — the upper bound is generic, not attained by every class of dimension 2.
  4. Symmetrization to complete (Prop. 5.2). Redo the proof and justify: (a) the use of Jensen to swap \(\sup\) and \(\mathbb{E}_{S'}\); (b) the legality of introducing the signs \(\sigma_i\); (c) the final sub-additivity. Solution. (a) \(S \mapsto \sup_f \mathbb{E}_{S'}(\dots)\): for each \(S\), the sup of an affine function of the distribution of \(S'\) bounds the expectation of the sup... more precisely \(\sup_f \mathbb{E}[g_f] \leq \mathbb{E}[\sup_f g_f]\) since for every \(f_0\), \(\mathbb{E}g_{f_0} \leq \mathbb{E}\sup_f g_f\) (inequalities pass to the expectation, then take the supremum on the left). (b) Conditionally on \((X_i, X'_i)\), the quantity \(\sigma_i(f(X'_i) - f(X_i))\) has the same distribution as \(f(X'_i) - f(X_i)\) because \(\sigma_i\) is symmetric and independent — one may therefore insert \(\sigma_i\) without changing the distribution of the supremum. (c) \(\sup_f (A_f + B_f) \leq \sup_f A_f + \sup_f(-B_f)\) for any choice of decomposition, applied to \(A_f = \frac1n\sum\sigma_if(X'_i)\), \(B_f = -\frac1n\sum\sigma_if(X_i)\).
  5. Rademacher of constants (exact computation). Compute \(\hat{\mathcal{R}}_S(\{x \mapsto c : c \in [0,1]\})\) and its equivalent as \(n \to \infty\). Solution. \(\sup_{c \in [0,1]} \frac1n|c\sum_i\sigma_i| = \frac1n \max\big(\sum_i\sigma_i, 0\big)\). By symmetry of the signs, \(\mathbb{E}\max(\Sigma, 0) = \frac12\mathbb{E}|\Sigma|\) with \(\Sigma = \sum_i\sigma_i\); by the CLT (Probability Theory for ML module, chapter 2), \(\mathbb{E}|\Sigma| \sim \sqrt{2n/\pi}\), hence \(\hat{\mathcal{R}}_S \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}} \approx \frac{0.399}{\sqrt n}\): even a "one-dimensional" class has nonzero complexity, of order exactly \(1/\sqrt n\).
  6. Le Cam for the Bernoulli mean (quadratic loss). Show that the quadratic minimax risk for estimating \(p\) from \(n\) Bernoulli draws satisfies \(\mathcal{M}_n \geq 1/(64n)\). Hint. Two parameters \(p_{1,2} = 1/2 \pm s\), quadratic loss, separation \(\Delta = 2s\); test \(A = \{|\hat p - p_1| \leq s\}\) and bound the binomial KL.
    On \(A^c\): \((\hat p - p_1)^2 \geq s^2\) contributes \(s^2\,\mathbb{P}_1(A^c)\); on \(A\): \(|\hat p - p_2| \geq s\) contributes \(s^2\,\mathbb{P}_2(A)\). Summing then averaging over the two problems: \(\mathcal{M}_n \geq \frac{s^2}{2}\big(\mathbb{P}_1(A^c) + \mathbb{P}_2(A)\big) \geq \frac{s^2}{2}(1-\delta)\). KL of the product distributions: \(n\,\mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{Bern}(p_1)\|\mathrm{Bern}(p_2)) \approx n \times \frac{(2s)^2}{2p(1-p)} = 8ns^2\) (to first order at \(p = 1/2\)). Taking \(s = 1/(4\sqrt n)\): \(\mathrm{KL} \approx 1/2\), Pinsker gives \(\delta \leq 1/2\), hence \(\mathcal{M}_n \geq \frac{s^2}{4} = \frac{1}{64n}\) — the \(1/n\) order is what matters; the empirical mean achieves \(p(1-p)/n \leq 1/(4n)\): the parametric rate is optimal.
  7. Compatibility under orthonormal design. \(n = p\), \(X\) orthonormal (\(X^\top X = n I\), normalized columns). (a) Show that the compatibility condition holds with \(\kappa = 1\). (b) Show that the Lasso reduces to soft-thresholding of the correlations \(z = X^\top Y/n\): \(\hat\beta_j = \mathrm{sign}(z_j)(|z_j| - \lambda)_+\). (c) Deduce \(\|\hat\beta - \beta^\star\|_2^2 \leq \sum_j \min(z_j^2, \lambda^2)\) and comment on the \((s\log p)/n\) rate. Solution (sketch). (a) \(\|Xh\|^2/n = \|h\|_2^2 \geq \|h_S\|_2^2\) (the cone restricts nothing). (b) The objective function separates coordinate by coordinate: \(\frac12(z_j - b_j)^2 + \lambda|b_j|\), whose minimizer is the soft-thresholding. (c) Each "signal" coordinate loses at most \(\lambda^2\), each "noise" coordinate at most \(z_j^2 \leq \lambda^2\) on the event of Prop. 8.2: \(\|\hat\beta - \beta^\star\|_2^2 \lesssim s\lambda^2 + (p - s)\mathbb{E}[z^2 \wedge \lambda^2] \lesssim \sigma^2 s\log p/n\) (each \(z_j\) Gaussian with variance \(\sigma^2/n\), tails bounded via the maximum, Prop. 2.6).
  8. Convex aggregation (Thm 9.3(i)) to prove step by step. Redo the three steps (convexity, uniformization, assembly) and write each inequality with its exact constants; where does the proof use (i) the uniform prior, (ii) the convexity of the loss, (iii) independence? Hint. Follow the proof of the theorem; for the uniformization step, write the Hoeffding bound for each \(j\), take \(\delta/(2M)\) and sum.
    (i) The uniform prior gives \(\pi\) charging the best \(j\): the bound depends only on \(\log M\), the number of candidates; a non-uniform prior would replace \(\log M\) by \(\log(1/\rho_{j^\star})\). (ii) Convexity allows \(L(\tilde f) \leq \sum_j\pi_j L(f_j)\); without convexity, one would be limited to selection (MS). (iii) Independence between the observations is used exactly in Hoeffding; it is also what makes \(\hat L(f_j)\) computable and concentrated — the same role as everywhere in the course since Prop. 1.2.
