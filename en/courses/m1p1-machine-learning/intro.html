Introduction and overview

ModuleMachine Learning — SynapseS 2026-2027 sheet: "Programme d'approfondissement — Bases de l'Apprentissage Automatique" (occurrence: "Foundations of Machine Learning")
CodeMDC_51006_EP
Team and workloadCourse coordinators: Erwan Le Pennec & Jesse Read (École Polytechnique, CMAP) · 36 h in person (2026-2027 catalog, max enrollment 144) · 4.5 ECTS · language of instruction: English · the methodological foundation of the program: supervised and unsupervised learning, from least squares to boosting, together with the accompanying generalization theory.
Status in the programMandatory M1 · S1 / period 1 (fall semester, from 17/09/26 to 18/12/26)

A mandatory module of the program, this course lays the common methodological foundation: what does it mean to learn from data, and under what conditions does it work. You will cover supervised learning from least squares to boosting, unsupervised learning, and the generalization theory that explains when a model will avoid overfitting. In companies as in research, this foundation is what distinguishes a practitioner who understands their models from a library user: all the downstream LLGA modules — Deep Learning, NLP, Graph ML, RL — reuse its concepts and vocabulary.

What you will be able to do

  • Frame a learning problem: risk, empirical risk, hypotheses, bias-variance trade-off.
  • Implement and tune linear regression, ridge/Lasso regularization, logistic regression and SVMs, justifying every choice.
  • Use kernels to move to the nonlinear setting and understand what a "reproducing kernel Hilbert space" brings in practice.
  • Choose between trees, random forests and boosting (gradient boosting) according to the nature of the data and the interpretability constraint.
  • Conduct a complete unsupervised analysis: k-means, Gaussian mixture and EM, PCA and dimensionality reduction.
  • State and exploit generalization bounds to argue for a model's performance on new data.
  • Build a rigorous evaluation pipeline: splits, cross-validation, adapted metrics, scaling up.
  • Read an academic or industrial ML paper and identify the underlying statistical framework.

Official objectives and syllabus (2026-2027 catalog)

Official objectives (paraphrased from the description published on SynapseS): machine learning is the discipline that designs algorithms allowing computers to learn from data, automatically extract complex patterns from them and derive intelligent decisions from them. The course focuses on the methodology underlying supervised and unsupervised learning, with a particular emphasis on the mathematical formulation of the algorithms and on how they can be implemented and used in practice; to this end it presents the necessary tools from optimization theory. Proofs are not done in lecture: some are done in labs, others deferred to a document; every session ends with a numerical lab in Python. Official assessment: a final exam. The detailed session-by-session program is not published on the sheet ("not communicated"); the following table maps the elements of the official description to our chapters.

Where this course fits in the program

This mandatory module of Year 1 · Period 1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS) is the hinge of the program: it assumes the two refresher modules are completed, Refresher in Statistics (optimization, concentration, estimation) and Refresher in Computer Science (Python, NumPy, complexity), whose opening chapter "Review" makes the direct link. The official 2026-2027 sheet publishes no formal prerequisites ("not communicated"), but the emphasis on mathematical formulation and Python labs presupposes precisely these two foundations; the course unit is moreover shared with the engineering cycle (X), the MJH, MPRI and CCSN master's programs and the other MScT tracks ViCAI and TRAI — hence a course tailored to advanced scientific profiles.

Downstream, it feeds everything else in the program. Deep Learning immediately follows by revisiting the perceptron and gradient descent; Emerging Subjects in ML reuses SGD and generalization theory for distributed learning; Statistical Learning Theory deepens chapter 7 up to VC bounds and Rademacher. The chapter on evaluation, big data and pipelines directly prepares the projects and applied modules of Period 2, from NLP to Graph ML.

The chapter "Research of the teaching team" presents the research of Erwan Le Pennec and Jesse Read (CMAP): it is a gateway to internships and possible PhD topics from the end of M1.

Course outline

  • Review: optimization and concentration: gradient, convexity, concentration inequalities — the tools taken up from the refresher and immediately mobilized.
  • The statistical framework of learning: risk, empirical minimization, bias-variance — the constitution of the problem.
  • Linear regression and regularization: the reference model and the first answer to overfitting.
  • Classification: logistic and SVM: margins, losses, decision boundaries.
  • Kernels and RKHS: linearizing the nonlinear via the kernel embedding.
  • Trees, forests, boosting: the still-essential family on tabular data, in companies as in competitions.
  • Unsupervised: k-means, EM, PCA: segmenting and compressing without labels.
  • Generalization theory: why (and when) the empirical error announces the true error.
  • Research of the teaching team: research at CMAP, from forests to ensembles, and possible internship topics.
  • Evaluation, big data and pipelines: operational rigor — metrics, validation, scaling up.

How to study this module

  • First secure the review: the opening chapter revisits the optimization and concentration of the Refresher in Statistics module — if an exercise there resists you, handle it before moving on.
  • Follow the chapter order: every model family (linear, kernels, trees) is taken up again by generalization theory, which only makes sense after them.
  • Reimplement at least once each familiar model (ridge regression, k-means, a tree) in NumPy before using scikit-learn.
  • Do the paper exercises on bounds and bias-variance: these are the most discriminating questions in the assessments.
  • Build a complete end-to-end mini-pipeline on a real dataset: this is the spirit of the final chapter and what recruiters expect.
  • Keep the chapter "Research of the teaching team" as an orientation signal: it announces both Statistical Learning Theory and internship topics.
A word from the teaching team LLGA trains practitioners and researchers who cross large language models with knowledge graphs. Whichever side, the founding question remains that of this module: under what conditions does a model learned on past data generalize to new data? Everything else in the program — transformers, GNNs, systems — is nothing but answers to this question.
