11. Research of the teaching team

Mathieu Desbrun — from animation to discrete geometry Before becoming one of the fathers of discrete differential geometry, M. Desbrun (Caltech, then Inria associate researcher, co-head of the course) left his mark on physical animation:
  • Smoothed Particles: a new paradigm for animating highly deformable bodies (with M.-P. Cani, Eurographics Workshop on Computer Animation and Simulation, 1996) — paper on HAL. The "smoothed" particles carry a density: the object's surface is the isosurface of this density, so that merging, fragmentation and flows emerge without re-topologizing (chapter 4);
  • Discrete Exterior Calculus (with A. Hirani, M. Leok, J. Marsden, 2005) — arXiv math/0508341, and Discrete Differential Forms for Computational Modeling (with E. Kanso, Y. Tong) — Springer chapter: an exact discrete structure for the differential operators (curl, divergence, Laplacian) on meshes, which preserves continuous identities — the theoretical foundation of shell, fluid and elastic simulations on surfaces;
  • The SIGGRAPH Asia course Digital Geometry Processing with Discrete Exterior Calculus (with K. Crane, F. de Goes, P. Schröder, 2013) — ACM DL — and the open version DDG (course notes) are the reference for the master's geometry labs (M2 Geometric DL module).
Marie-Paule Cani — implicit surfaces and animated modeling Professor at École Polytechnique (and Inria researcher, French Academy of Sciences), M.-P. Cani built much of the theory of animated implicit surfaces:
  • Active Implicit Surface for Animation (with M. Desbrun, Graphics Interface, 1998) — paper on HAL: the surface is an isosurface of a density field whose source particles move; the field follows the physics — automatic union and merging, ideal for viscous fluids and soft flesh; the natural continuation of the duo's smoothed particles;
  • Interactive Sculpting with Implicit Surfaces (Shape Modeling International, 2002) — abstract and links: two schools of digital sculpting (sampled fields versus CSG constructions) and their control/quality trade-offs;
  • Her profile page People of ACM summarizes the research program: high-level models for geometry and animation (implicit and developable surfaces, frame-based deformations) — exactly the building blocks of chapters 4, 6 and 7 of this module.
What this work changes for the course The Desbrun–Cani duo's smoothed particles are the direct ancestor of the SPH simulators of modern DCCs (Blender, Houdini); their animated implicit surfaces will motivate the "reconstruction" chapter of the M1P1 module Digital Representation and Analysis of Shapes (isosurfaces, marching cubes); Desbrun's DEC resurfaces in the M2 module Geometric Deep Learning (discrete operators on meshes). Geometry and animation are but a single discipline seen from two sides.
