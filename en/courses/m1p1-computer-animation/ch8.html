8. Inverse kinematics

Definition 8.1 — Forward and inverse problems Forward (FK): angles \(\theta \in \mathbb{R}^n\) ⟹ positions of the effectors by composition \(T(\theta) = T_1(\theta_1)\cdots T_n(\theta_n)\). Inverse (IK): find \(\theta\) such that an effector reaches a target \(\mathbf{p}^\ast\) (and possibly an orientation). The problem is nonlinear, possibly without a solution (target out of reach), with multiple solutions (redundancy) — an entire family if \(n > 6\) for an effector of 6-DoF pose.
Proposition 8.2 — Jacobian method The effector error \(\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{p}^\ast - \mathbf{p}(\theta)\) is linearized: \(d\mathbf{p} = J(\theta)\,d\theta\), where column \(i\) of \(J\) equals \(\mathbf{a}_i \times (\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{q}_i)\) (joint axis \(\mathbf{a}_i\), center \(\mathbf{q}_i\)). The iterations $$\Delta\theta = J^+\mathbf{e} \quad \text{with} \quad J^+ = J^\top (J J^\top + \lambda I)^{-1} \ \ (\text{damped least squares})$$ converge locally to a minimum of \(\|\mathbf{e}\|^2\); the damping \(\lambda > 0\) avoids jumps near singularities (where \(\det JJ^\top \to 0\)).
Example 8.3 — Planar two-segment arm, closed-form solution Lengths \(L_1 = L_2 = 1\), target \((x, y) = (1.2\,;\,0.6)\). Distance to the origin \(d = \sqrt{1.44 + 0.36} = \sqrt{1.8} \approx 1.342 \leq L_1 + L_2\) ⟹ two solutions. Law of cosines: \(\cos\theta_2 = \dfrac{d^2 - L_1^2 - L_2^2}{2L_1L_2} = \dfrac{1.8 - 2}{2} = -0.1\) ⟹ \(\theta_2 = \pm 95.7°\) (elbow up/down), then \(\theta_1 = \mathrm{atan2}(y, x) - \mathrm{atan2}(L_2\sin\theta_2,\, L_1 + L_2\cos\theta_2)\) ⟹ \(\theta_1 \approx -21.3°\) for the elbow-down solution (\(\theta_2 = +95.7°\)); FK check: \(x = \cos(-21.3°) + \cos(74.4°) = 0.932 + 0.268 = 1.20\) ✓ and \(y = -0.363 + 0.963 = 0.60\) ✓. A target at \(d > 2\) is not reachable: the damped IK then stops at the boundary of the reachable space, arm extended.
Definition 8.4 — CCD and FABRIK
  • CCD (Cyclic Coordinate Descent): correct one joint at a time, from the effector toward the root — each step is an elementary 2D rotation; trivial to implement, widely used for foliage and plants;
  • FABRIK: geometric resolution by back-and-forth (target positions propagated along the chain, length constraints reimposed); converges in few iterations, handles multi-parent chains (hands on an object) naturally;
  • Analytic multi-body IK (foot on the ground): character engines combine closed-form 2-3 segment solutions and Jacobian relaxation for the rest.
IK and data: framing the solution space Redundancy (infinitely many \(\theta\) for the same target) makes IK an ill-posed problem: the choice of the minimum is a matter of prior. Data-driven approaches (chapter 10) replace the "articular energy" prior with real poses: IK in the latent space of a learned model of motion. It is also the natural bridge to crowds (chapter 9), where thousands of agents economize by reusing IK solutions.
