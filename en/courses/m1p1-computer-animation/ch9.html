9. Crowds and behaviors

Definition 9.1 — Boids (Reynolds, 1987) Each agent (boid) is a particle endowed with three local behavioral forces (perception neighborhood):
  • separation: \(\mathbf{f}_s = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}} \dfrac{\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j}{\|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|^2}\) — anti-collision;
  • alignment: \(\mathbf{f}_a = \overline{\mathbf{v}}_{\mathcal{N}} - \mathbf{v}_i\) — same direction as the neighborhood;
  • cohesion: \(\mathbf{f}_c = \overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{N}} - \mathbf{x}_i\) — stay with the group.
Emergence (flocks, schools) comes from local rules without a leader or a plan; cost \(O(n \cdot k)\) with \(k\) neighbors, neighborhood found by spatial grid or kd-tree — cf. Refresher CS module, spatial data structures chapter.
Definition 9.2 — Social forces (Helbing–Molnár) For human crowds, each agent has a desired velocity \(\mathbf{v}_i^0\) (toward its goal, via a navigation graph) and undergoes $$m_i\dot{\mathbf{v}}_i = \underbrace{m_i\tfrac{1}{\tau}\big(\mathbf{v}_i^0 - \mathbf{v}_i\big)}_{\text{goal attainment}} + \underbrace{\sum_{j\neq i} \mathbf{f}_{ij}^{soc}}_{\text{psych. repulsion}} + \underbrace{\mathbf{f}_{i}^{murs}}_{\text{walls}}, \qquad \mathbf{f}_{ij}^{soc} = A\,e^{-\|\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i\|/B}\,\mathbf{n}_{ij}.$$ Typical parameters \(A = 2 \times 10^3\) N, \(B = 0.08\) m, \(\tau = 0.5\) s. Documented emergent phenomena: formation of opposing lanes in corridors, arches in front of doors, "faster-is-slower" (pushing more slows down evacuation).
Example 9.3 — Two flows, one door, one jam 60 agents, half heading right, half heading left, a door of width \(1.2\) m in a wall. Measured flow as a function of "impatience" (gain on \(\|\mathbf{v}^0\|\)): at \(v^0 = 1\) m/s, flow of 1.1 agents/s, orderly queues; at \(v^0 = 2.5\) m/s, unstable arches, flow drops to 0.6 agents/s. Approximate analytical check: flow \(\approx \rho\,v\,w_{eff}\) with \(w_{eff}\) the effective width; the arch blocks the section (\(w_{eff}\) drops by half), density does not compensate — the qualitative summary of "faster-is-slower". An animation engine uses this to regulate background crowds (level of detail: full simulation < 30 m, sprites beyond).
Navigation: graph, grid, fields The goal \(\mathbf{v}^0\) comes from a navigation layer: waypoint graph (A*, Dijkstra — cf. Refresher CS module, graphs chapter), navigation mesh (navmesh) with hierarchical search, or potential fields precomputed by the eikonal equation (fast marching — the same equation as the geodesic distances of the geometry module). The RVO/ORCA techniques (reciprocal velocity obstacles) replace forces with a chosen velocity avoiding collisions over a short horizon: more stable at high density than social forces, the standard of real-time urban simulations.
