6. Collisions and constraints

Definition 6.1 — Detection: broad and narrow phases Broad phase: eliminate distant pairs via axis-aligned bounding volumes (AABB, \(O(1)\) cost per pair, acceleration by sweeping — sweep and prune — or spatial grids); typically \(O(n \log n)\) for n particles. Narrow phase: exact test of the pair (sphere-sphere: distance; sphere-plane: signed distance; box-box: separating axis test; arbitrary convexes: GJK, which maximizes the distance by simplex over the Minkowski difference). In physics-based animation, one also detects between steps (continuous collision: swept segment against triangles, or conservative advancement) to avoid wall tunneling at high velocities.
Theorem 6.2 — Impulse response: formula and consistency Two bodies \(a, b\) of masses \(m_a, m_b\) come into contact at a point with unit normal \(\mathbf{n}\) (from \(a\) to \(b\)), relative velocity \(\mathbf{v}_{rel} = \mathbf{v}_b - \mathbf{v}_a\), coefficient of restitution \(e \in [0, 1]\). The scalar impulse that resolves the contact is $$j = -\,\frac{(1 + e)\,(\mathbf{v}_{rel} \cdot \mathbf{n})}{\dfrac{1}{m_a} + \dfrac{1}{m_b}}, \qquad \mathbf{v}_a^+ = \mathbf{v}_a - \frac{j}{m_a}\mathbf{n}, \quad \mathbf{v}_b^+ = \mathbf{v}_b + \frac{j}{m_b}\mathbf{n}.$$ It conserves momentum and realizes exactly the restitution law \((\mathbf{v}_{rel}^+ \cdot \mathbf{n}) = -e\,(\mathbf{v}_{rel} \cdot \mathbf{n})\).
Write \(\Delta\mathbf{v}_a = -\tfrac{j}{m_a}\mathbf{n}\), \(\Delta\mathbf{v}_b = +\tfrac{j}{m_b}\mathbf{n}\): the total momentum varies by \(m_a \Delta\mathbf{v}_a + m_b \Delta\mathbf{v}_b = -j\mathbf{n} + j\mathbf{n} = 0\). The normal relative velocity evolves as \(\mathbf{v}_{rel}^+ \cdot \mathbf{n} = \mathbf{v}_{rel}\cdot\mathbf{n} + j\big(\tfrac{1}{m_a} + \tfrac{1}{m_b}\big)\). Imposing the restitution law, \(\mathbf{v}_{rel}^+\cdot\mathbf{n} = -e\,\mathbf{v}_{rel}\cdot\mathbf{n}\), gives \((1 + e)\,\mathbf{v}_{rel}\cdot\mathbf{n} + j\big(\tfrac{1}{m_a} + \tfrac{1}{m_b}\big) = 0\), that is, the announced formula. ∎
Example 6.3 — Billiard balls Ball \(a\) (\(m_a = 2\) kg, \(v_a = 3\) m/s) against ball \(b\) (\(m_b = 1\) kg, at rest), head-on impact, \(e = 0.5\), \(\mathbf{n}\) carried by the velocity: \(\mathbf{v}_{rel}\cdot\mathbf{n} = -3\), \(j = \tfrac{1.5 \times 3}{0.5 + 1} = 3\) N·s. Hence \(v_a^+ = 3 - \tfrac{3}{2} = 1.5\) m/s, \(v_b^+ = 0 + 3 = 3\) m/s. Checks: restitution \((3 - 1.5)/3 = 0.5\) ✓; momentum \(2 \times 1.5 + 1 \times 3 = 6 = 2 \times 3\) ✓. Energy: from 9 to \(6.75\) J (2.25 J dissipated in deformation and sound), consistent with \(e < 1\).
Definition 6.4 — Three ways to handle constraints
  • Penalty: a spring of stiffness \(k\) on the interpenetration \(\delta\): simple but stiff (constraint \(h \leq 2/\sqrt{k/m}\), cf. ch. 4); compliant contacts: choose \(k = m/(h^2)\) exactly for the scheme's stiffness;
  • Impulse / projection: solve the contacts in velocities by linear complementarity (LCP): \(\delta \geq 0\) (no interpenetration), \(j \geq 0\), \(\delta \cdot j = 0\): solvable by projected Gauss-Seidel iterations — the approach of game engines;
  • Position Based Dynamics (PBD): directly move positions to satisfy each constraint (distance, volume, bend), then deduce the velocities \(\mathbf{v} = (\mathbf{x}^+ - \mathbf{x}^-)/h\). Stable, fast, artist-controllable (stiffness as an ad hoc parameter): XPBD restores a physically calibrated stiffness via a compliant solver.
Friction, an honest word Coulomb friction (\(\|\mathbf{f}_t\| \leq \mu\,j\)) turns the LCP into a nonlinear complementarity problem, with no guaranteed unique solution: engines cheat through iterated tangential projection. Practical consequence: a "too stiff" PBD sand pile slides; adjusting \(\mu\) and the number of iterations is as much artistic tuning as physics.
