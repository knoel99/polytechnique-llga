0. Refresher: ODEs, Taylor, rotations, energy

L3 prerequisites mobilized
  • Cauchy problem: an ODE \(\dot{\mathbf{y}} = f(t, \mathbf{y})\), \(\mathbf{y}(0) = \mathbf{y}_0\), admits (Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem) a unique local solution as soon as \(f\) is Lipschitz in \(\mathbf{y}\) — all of physical animation is the numerical resolution of such problems;
  • Taylor expansion to order 2 and 3 of a smooth function, Lagrange remainder — it gives the order of integrators (chapter 2);
  • Orthogonal matrices and rotations: \(R \in SO(3)\) if and only if \(R^\top R = I\), \(\det R = 1\); eigenvectors, rotation angle; unit quaternions will be revisited in chapter 5;
  • Real symmetric matrices: diagonalizable in an orthonormal basis, real eigenvalues — the stiffness of a mass-spring system is read in the spectrum of \(M^{-1}K\) (chapters 3 and 4);
  • Mechanical energy: \(E = \frac12 m v^2 + U(x)\), conserved when the forces derive from a potential and there is no friction — an integrator that makes \(E\) grow "blows up", an integrator that dissipates it artificially "damps".
Proposition 0.1 — The harmonic oscillator, the course's compass For \(m = 1\), \(k = \omega^2\), the system \(\ddot{x} = -\omega^2 x\) rewrites in state dimension 1 as: $$\dot{\mathbf{y}} = A\,\mathbf{y}, \qquad \mathbf{y} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ v \end{pmatrix}, \quad A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -\omega^2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \sigma(A) = \{\, i\omega,\ -i\omega \,\}.$$ The exact solution is a uniform rotation of angular frequency \(\omega\) in the plane \((x, v/\omega)\): \(x(t) = x_0\cos\omega t + \tfrac{v_0}{\omega}\sin\omega t\), and the energy \(E = \tfrac12 v^2 + \tfrac12 \omega^2 x^2\) is constant. Any integrator that, on this trivial system, increases \(E\) will diverge on a real system — this is the stability criterion of chapter 3.
Why this refresher structures the whole module A spring (garment, muscle, soft body), a regularized walking step, a penalized contact: almost all forces in animation are stiff and dissipative. The behavior of a numerical scheme on the pair \(\{i\omega, -c \pm i\omega\}\) — pure imaginary and complex with negative real part — predicts its behavior in production. This reading grid will reappear in chapters 3 (stability), 4 (stiffness of springs) and 6 (penalization of contacts).
