7. Skinning: LBS and its artifacts

Definition 7.1 — Skeletal deformation (LBS) A mesh is attached to a skeleton of \(K\) bones. Each bone \(i\) has a rest transformation \(B_i\) (local frame) and a current one \(M_i\). Each vertex \(\mathbf{v}\) carries weights \(\{w_i\}\), \(\sum_i w_i = 1\) (typically 4 bones max, painted or computed by heat diffusion). Linear blend skinning computes $$\mathbf{v}' = \sum_{i} w_i\, M_i\, B_i^{-1}\, \mathbf{v}.$$ The quantity \(B_i^{-1}\mathbf{v}\) places the vertex in the bone's local frame, \(M_i\) puts it back into the animated world; the summation of matrices (hence linear: hence the name) precedes the application to the vertex — this is where the artifacts are born.
Theorem 7.2 — The "candy wrapper" collapse Consider a point at distance \(r\) from the axis of an elbow, with weights \(\tfrac12/\tfrac12\) on the two bones that share the same axis, one rotated by \(\theta\), the other fixed. Then its distance to the axis after LBS is $$r' = \tfrac12\,\|R_\theta\,\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{u}\|\,r = r\,\cos\!\Big(\frac{\theta}{2}\Big), \qquad \mathbf{u} \perp \text{axis},\ \|\mathbf{u}\| = 1.$$ The volume collapses: at \(\theta = 90°\), \(r' = r\cos 45° \approx 0.707\,r\) (50% section loss); at \(\theta = 180°\), \(r' = 0\) — the arm annihilates itself.
Take the \(z\) axis, \(\mathbf{u} = (0,1,0)\). Then \(R_\theta \mathbf{u} = (-\sin\theta, \cos\theta, 0)\) and \(\tfrac12(R_\theta\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{u}) = (-\tfrac{\sin\theta}{2}, \tfrac{1+\cos\theta}{2}, 0)\), of norm \(\tfrac12\sqrt{\sin^2\theta + (1+\cos\theta)^2} = \tfrac12\sqrt{2 + 2\cos\theta} = \sqrt{\tfrac{1+\cos\theta}{2}} = \cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\) (half-angle formula). ∎
Definition 7.3 — Responses: DQS, cages, learning
  • Dual Quaternion Skinning: replace the matrix average by an average of dual quaternions (rigid rotation + translation): the blended transformation remains an isometry ⟹ no more collapse, at the price of a slight bulging of folds and the loss of the expressivity property of weights;
  • Cages and free-form deformations: embed the mesh in a simple shell and deform by harmonic/green coordinates — fine control of folds, higher cost;
  • Learned skinning: predict the weights or the deformation by a neural network (e.g. fold definition from examples): cf. Machine Learning module, neural networks chapter — skinning becomes a regression problem on (pose, mesh) pairs.
Example 7.4 — Assessing the artifact then correcting it Elbow at \(\theta = 90°\), weights \((0.4\,;\,0.6)\): redoing the computation of Theorem 7.2 with non-uniform weights, \(\|\,0.4\,R_\theta\mathbf{u} + 0.6\,\mathbf{u}\|^2 = 0.16 + 0.36 + 0.48\cos\theta = 0.52 + 0\) ⟹ \(r' = r\sqrt{0.52} \approx 0.72\,r\). Classical rigging remedy: distributing the rotation over \(k\) joints (each rotated by \(\theta/k\)) multiplies the collapse factors: for \(k = 3\), \(\cos(\theta/6)^3 = \cos(15°)^3 \approx 0.90\) versus \(\cos(45°) \approx 0.71\) — better, but the artifact remains of the same nature, and only a blended isometry (DQS) eliminates it. Moral: no blend replaces a well-constructed rig, and DQS saves average rigs.
Definition 7.5 — Facial animation: blendshapes A face has no bones: the skin slides over the muscles, and skeletal skinning collapses there. The standard is the blendshape: a neutral mesh \(\mathbf{v}_0\) and sculpted target deformations \(\mathbf{v}_i\) (smile, raised eyebrow, "visemes" of phonemes for dubbing), combined by $$\mathbf{v}' \;=\; \mathbf{v}_0 \;+\; \sum_i \alpha_i\,(\mathbf{v}_i - \mathbf{v}_0), \qquad \alpha_i \in [0, 1]\ \text{(or even beyond, to exaggerate)}.$$ The \(\alpha_i\) come from the performance: marker or video capture, today by regression (a network learned from the image to the coefficients — the same move as the learned skinning of def. 7.3), with the artist's touch-ups on top. In production: blendshapes for the face, LBS/DQS for the body, sculpted fixes at the joints — the vocabulary of real-time avatars and automatic dubbing.
