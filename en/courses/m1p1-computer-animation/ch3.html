3. Stability: the oscillator theorem

Definition 3.1 — Amplification and stability Applied to the linear system \(\dot{\mathbf{y}} = A\mathbf{y}\), the schemes of chapter 2 write \(\mathbf{y}_{n+1} = G\,\mathbf{y}_n\) with \(G\) linear. The scheme is stable on this system if the spectral radius \(\rho(G) \leq 1\) (every component remains bounded), unstable if \(\rho(G) > 1\) (geometric divergence). For EE and EI, \(G = I \pm hA\): stability depends on the product \(h\lambda\) for each eigenvalue \(\lambda\) of \(A\). The set \(\{z \in \mathbb{C} : |1 + z| \leq 1\}\) (resp. \(\{|1 - z|^{-1} \leq 1\}\)) is the stability region of EE (resp. EI).
Theorem 3.2 — Explicit Euler diverges on the oscillator, for every step For the oscillator \(\ddot{x} = -\omega^2 x\) (eigenvalues \(\lambda = \pm i\omega\)), EE gives \(x_{n+1} = x_n + h v_n\), \(v_{n+1} = v_n - h\omega^2 x_n\), that is $$G_{EE} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & h \\ -h\omega^2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(G_{EE} - \lambda I) = (1-\lambda)^2 + h^2\omega^2,$$ whose roots are \(\lambda_\pm = 1 \pm i\,h\omega\). Hence \(\rho(G_{EE}) = \sqrt{1 + h^2\omega^2} > 1\) for every \(h > 0\): the energy grows by \(\rho^2 = 1 + h^2\omega^2\) per step, independently of the fineness of the step.
The characteristic polynomial is computed directly. The roots are obtained by solving \((1 - \lambda)^2 = -(h\omega)^2\), i.e. \(1 - \lambda = \pm i h\omega\). The modulus of \(1 \pm i h\omega\) equals \(\sqrt{1 + h^2\omega^2}\), strictly greater than 1 as soon as \(h\omega \neq 0\). The component of \(\mathbf{y}_0\) on an eigenvector grows like \(\rho^n\): after \(n\) steps, \(\|\mathbf{y}_n\| \gtrsim \rho^n\). ∎
Example 3.3 — A measured explosion With \(\omega = 1\), \(h = 0.1\) (100 steps per period, yet a "fine" step): \(\rho = \sqrt{1.01} \approx 1.005\). The energy grows by 0.5% per step: ×1.64 after 100 steps (1 period!), ×145 after 1000 steps. On screen: a ball bouncing higher and higher without any energy injected — the classic artifact of the intern who codes EE on a spring.
Theorem 3.4 — Implicit Euler is unconditionally stable (A-stable) On the same oscillator, EI writes \(G_{EI} = (I - hA)^{-1}\), with eigenvalues \(\dfrac{1}{1 \mp i h\omega}\), of modulus \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1 + h^2\omega^2}} < 1\): stable for every \(h\), with a numerical damping that grows with \(h\). More generally, \(\rho(G_{EI}) < 1\) as soon as all the eigenvalues of \(A\) have real part \(< 0\) — a property called A-stability (Dahlquist, 1963).
\((I - hA)\mathbf{u} = (1 - h\lambda)\mathbf{u}\) for every eigenvector \(\mathbf{u}\), so the eigenvalues of the inverse are the inverses \((1 - h\lambda)^{-1}\). If \(\lambda = i\omega\), \(|1 - i h\omega| = \sqrt{1 + h^2\omega^2} > 1\) so the inverse is \(< 1\). If \(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda < 0\), then \(|1 - h\lambda|^2 = (1 - h\mathrm{Re}\lambda)^2 + (h\mathrm{Im}\lambda)^2 > 1\), whence the result for every \(h > 0\). ∎
Theorem 3.5 — Symplectic Euler: conditional stability and bounded energy With ES, \(v_{n+1} = v_n - h\omega^2 x_n\), \(x_{n+1} = x_n + h v_{n+1}\): $$G_{ES} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - h^2\omega^2 & h \\ -h\omega^2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det G_{ES} = 1, \qquad \mathrm{tr}\, G_{ES} = 2 - h^2\omega^2.$$ The scheme is stable if and only if \(h\omega \leq 2\). Moreover \(\det G_{ES} = 1\) (conservation of areas in the phase plane): under this condition the energy oscillates around its initial value instead of drifting.
\(\lambda_+ \lambda_- = \det = 1\) and \(\lambda_+ + \lambda_- = \mathrm{tr} = 2 - h^2\omega^2\). Set \(c = 1 - \tfrac{h^2\omega^2}{2} = \tfrac{\mathrm{tr}}{2}\). If \(|c| \leq 1\) (i.e. \(h\omega \leq 2\)), the roots of \(\lambda^2 - 2c\lambda + 1\) are \(\lambda_\pm = c \pm i\sqrt{1 - c^2}\), of modulus \(\sqrt{c^2 + 1 - c^2} = 1\): bounded. If \(h\omega > 2\), then \(c < -1\), the roots are real, \(\lambda_\pm = c \pm \sqrt{c^2 - 1}\), and \(\max|\lambda_\pm| = |c| + \sqrt{c^2 - 1} > 1\) with a sign change (zigzag divergence). Conservation of areas is the determinant: it is the trace of a geometric property (symplecticity) that EE and EI lack. ∎

Stiff systems: why garments are "unstable" A cloth mass-spring network contains very stiff bending springs: its natural frequencies range from \(\omega_{\min}\) to \(\omega_{\max} \gg \omega_{\min}\). The constraint \(h \leq 2/\omega_{\max}\) then imposes a minuscule step dictated by the fastest modes while the visible motion is slow: this is stiffness. Two ways out: the implicit approach (solve a system at every step, chapter 4) or abandoning forces for position constraints (PBD, chapter 6). The link with symplectic schemes and variational methods is a research theme of the teaching team (chapter 11).
