10. Procedural versus data-driven

Definition 10.1 — Three genealogies of animation
  • Artistic: keyframes + interpolation (chapter 1), IK constraints (chapter 8) — maximal control, human cost;
  • Procedural/physical: simulation (chapters 2 to 6), behaviors (chapter 9) — emergence and physical realism, indirect control;
  • Data-driven: learn from motion captures (mocap: optical markers at 100–240 Hz, or IMU) — realism of gesture, generalization limited by the data.
Definition 10.2 — Motion matching Search at every frame, in a mocap database (hours of clips), for the pose/trajectory closest to a query (direction, speed, contact) $$i^\ast = \arg\min_i \big\|\mathbf{c}_i - \mathbf{c}_{goal}\big\|^2_W,$$ then blending toward that clip — the weights \(W\) on (future position, direction, foot speed) are tuned by the designer. The standard of AAA games: no state machine to write, but a galaxy of data to record and a search cost (accelerated by kd-tree) at every frame.
Proposition 10.3 — Learning motion Recurrent networks (or transformers) on mocap windows predict the next pose conditioned on control; phase-functioned networks (Holden et al., 2016) condition an MLP on the phase of the walking cycle; temporal autoregressive networks drift in the long run — hence learning in latent space with reinjected constraints (feet on the ground: IK losses). Differentiable physics (gradients of the solver, cf. chapter 4: \((I - hJ)\Delta\mathbf{v}\) backpropagates) closes the loop: learning controllers through simulation. All these models rest on the same foundations as the Machine Learning module (backpropagation and recurrent network chapters) and prepare the multimodal generative models of the Multimodal GenAI module (chapter on 3D/video generation).

Production: the standard sandwich A modern character superposes the three generations: data-driven locomotion (motion matching), local physical layer (garment, hair, accessory via PBD/XPBD), artistic touch-up by keyframes/IK (hand placed on an object). Skinning (chapter 7) remains the rendering interface between the skeleton (whatever its source) and the mesh; neural deformation networks do not change this architecture — they replace blocks, not the sandwich.
