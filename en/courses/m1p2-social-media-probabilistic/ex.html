Exercises

  1. From binomial to Poisson. Complete the proof of Proposition 1.2 with the bound \(\big(1 - \frac{k}{n}\big)^{n-1} \geq (1 - c/n)^n \to e^{-c}\) (logarithm expansion); show that the variance of \(\mathrm{Bin}(n-1, c/n)\) converges to \(c\) (Refresher Statistics module, ch. 3).
  2. Giant component. Solve \(\rho = 1 - e^{-c\rho}\) numerically for \(c = 1.5\), 2, 4, 10 and check by simulation (nx.gnp_random_graph, 10 repetitions); show the uniqueness of the solution in \((0, 1)\) for \(c > 1\) (study \(f(\rho) = \rho - 1 + e^{-c\rho}\)).
  3. Triangle threshold. Complete the second moment of Theorem 1.6: compute \(\mathbb{E}[T(T-1)]\) by separating pairs of triangles that are disjoint, share a vertex, share an edge; deduce \(\mathrm{Var}(T) = o(\mathbb{E}[T]^2)\) when \(np \to \infty\). What happens for \(K_4\) (threshold \(n^{-2/3}\))?
  4. Preferential attachment. Redo the master equation of Theorem 2.5 with the rule \(\Pi(i) \propto d_i + \alpha\) (attachment with newborns): show \(p_k \sim k^{-\gamma}\) with \(\gamma = 3 + \alpha/m\) (set up the differential equation and differentiate as in the proof).
  5. Watts–Strogatz. Simulate the diameter and average clustering of the rewired ring for \(\beta \in \{10^{-4}, \dots, 1\}\) (log scale); reproduce the original figure; explain why a few \(\Theta(\beta n)\) "long" links per vertex suffice to drop the diameter from \(\Theta(n/K)\) to \(\Theta(\log n)\) while clustering barely moves.
  6. Transmission-recovery race. In continuous SIR, show that on an infected edge, transmission beats recovery with probability \(\frac{\beta}{\beta + \delta}\) (independent exponential races — minimum distribution); deduce the coupling of Theorem 3.4 and recompute \(R_0^{\text{graph}}\) of Theorem 3.2.
  7. Spectral threshold. Complete the proof of Theorem 3.3(i): justify the equality \(\sum_i x_i \sum_{j \sim i} u_j = \rho \sum_i x_i u_i\) (use \((A u)_i = \rho u_i\) component by component); where do we use \(u > 0\) (Perron–Frobenius) in the last step \(\Phi_t \geq (\min_i u_i)\mathbf{1}_{\{\text{not extinct}\}}\)?
  8. Push-pull. Show that pull alone cannot start from a single informed node when \(n\) is large (probability of contacting the informed node = \(1/(n-1)\) per round); write the exact recursion \(\mathbb{E}[k_{t+1} \mid k_t]\) for push alone and verify the ratio \(3/2\) of the doubling phase (Theorem 4.2).
  9. Submodularity. Prove the submodularity of reachability via the live-edge coupling of Theorem 4.4; show that the \((1 - 1/e)\) bound is attained by the set-cover example; implement the CELF greedy algorithm on an SBM (1000 vertices) and compare with 20 random seeders.
  10. Non-backtracking. For a path with \(n\) vertices, compute the spectrum of \(A\) and of \(B\) (tridiagonality); for a \(d\)-regular graph, show that \(\rho(B) = d - 1\) (walks without U-turns on a local tree); implement sparse \(B\) (scipy.sparse) on an SBM with \(q = 2\) blocks and check the Kesten–Stigum criterion of Theorem 5.3 by scanning \((a, b)\).
  11. Paper. Choose a paper from ch. 8 (Ganesh–Massoulié–Towsley 2005; Massoulié 2014; Bordenave–Lelarge–Massoulié 2015; Hall–Massoulié 2023; Even–Koloskova–Massoulié 2024), read it and present it in 10 minutes: model, main theorem, proof idea, algorithm, numerical results, limitations. Grid: one figure re-explained, one experiment reproduced in a TME, one open question.
  12. Power of two choices. Simulate \(n\) balls into \(n\) bins (one choice vs two); plot max load vs \(n\) on a log scale and check the two asymptotics \(\frac{\ln n}{\ln \ln n}\) and \(\frac{\ln \ln n}{\ln 2}\) (Theorem 7.1); discuss the information cost (the bin must know its load) and the passage to the dynamic model (queue arrivals/departures).
