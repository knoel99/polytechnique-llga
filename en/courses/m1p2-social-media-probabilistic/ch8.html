8. Research of the teaching team

The module is taught by Laurent Massoulié, Inria senior researcher (Dragon team, Inria Paris center; he headed the joint Microsoft Research–Inria center and teaches at École Polytechnique). His career covers exactly the syllabus: epidemics and networks, rumors, stochastic block model, and distributed systems (caching, load balancing, decentralized optimization). Five axes, with the founding papers to read as a project (exercise 11).

8.1 — Epidemics and topology (Ganesh, Massoulié, Towsley, INFOCOM 2005) The Effect of Network Topology on the Spread of Epidemics (> 1,000 citations) founded epidemic control through structure: Theorem 3.3 of the module is its core — fast extinction as soon as \(\beta/\delta < 1/\rho(A)\), proved via the spectral supermartingale we sketched, and long survival in the supercritical regime. Legacy: the entire literature on spectral epidemic thresholds (NIMFA, targeted vaccination), and malware/worm slowdown policies. Position in the course: it is the quantitative answer to "does the shape of the network change the epidemic?" — yes, spectrally.
8.2 — The SBM detection threshold (Massoulié, STOC 2014) Community Detection Thresholds and the Weak Ramanujan Property. Result: above the Kesten–Stigum threshold, random graphs "resemble" Ramanujan graphs (their nontrivial eigenvalues lie in the disk of radius \(\sqrt d\)) — the weak Ramanujan property — and a spectral algorithm exploits it to detect communities down to the information threshold. This is the first proof of the conjecture of Decelle et al. on the feasibility side (independently of Mossel–Neeman–Sly), and the answer to question 5.3 of the module. Legacy: the founding slab of detection below the threshold, extended by 8.3.
8.3 — The non-backtracking spectrum (Bordenave, Lelarge, Massoulié, FOCS 2015 / Ann. Probab. 2018) Non-backtracking Spectrum of Random Graphs: Community Detection and Non-regular Ramanujan Graphs. Theorem 5.5 of the module: the spectrum of the non-backtracking matrix of an SBM is confined in a disk of radius \(\sqrt d\) — bulk — except for the informative eigenvalues, which escape it exactly at the Kesten–Stigum threshold. Double payoff: (i) the "eigenvectors of \(B\) + k-means" algorithm detects down to the threshold (in practice: it is the one of TME 9); (ii) random graphs (even irregular ones) are asymptotically Ramanujan — a pure graph theory result that closes an open question. It is also the theoretical justification of non-backtracking operators in learning on graphs (Graph ML module, ch. 3-4; Advanced GNN module, ch. 3).
8.4 — Distributed systems: caching, balancing, bandwidth Three works connected to ch. 7:
  • Bandwidth sharing — Massoulié, Roberts (INFOCOM 1999; IEEE/ACM ToN 2002): convergence of decentralized protocols (TCP-AIMD) to proportional fairness (7.3), the foundation of network stability — ~1,000 citations.
  • Distributed caching over heterogeneous mobile networks — Ioannidis, Massoulié, Chaintreau (SIGMETRICS 2010; Queueing Systems 2012): users cache contents and exchange them when they meet; which decentralized policy maximizes throughput? The model is that of the epidemics of ch. 3 — a usable rumor to save bandwidth.
  • Bipartite graph structures for efficient balancing of heterogeneous loads — Leconte, Lelarge, Massoulié (ACM, 2012): balancing heterogeneous loads as a matching on a random bipartite graph — the bridge between power-of-two-choices (7.1) and graph structures (ch. 2).
8.5 — Today: graph alignment and distributed learning
  • Graph alignment — Hall, Massoulié, Partial Recovery in the Graph Alignment Problem (Operations Research 71(1), 2023): two correlated graphs (two platforms), can we recover the vertex correspondence? Partial recovery guarantees depending on sparsity and correlation — the theory of de-anonymization (ch. 6), with the privacy limit that goes with it.
  • Asynchronous SGD on graphs — Even, Koloskova, Massoulié, Asynchronous SGD on Graphs (AISTATS 2024): a unified framework for decentralized and federated optimization via gossip — chapter 7 of the module projected onto today's federated learning.
  • Collective exploration — Cosson, Massoulié, Collective Tree Exploration via Potential Function Method (ITCS 2024): cooperating robots explore an unknown tree — distributed algorithms viewed through online analysis.
Guiding thread (for the exam) A single pattern runs through the whole body of work: take a system (network, platform, swarm), model it by a random process on a graph, identify a spectral or branching threshold, and derive from it a decentralized algorithm with guarantees. This is exactly the backbone of chapters 1 to 7 of this module — teaching one's own research field.
