0. Compact refresher: the P1 foundation

Reminders — the P1 Deep Learning module, in eight points (each point refers to the corresponding chapter of the Deep Learning module)
  • Neuron and network (ch. 1): \(h = \sigma(Wx)\); depth = composition; initialization and normalization to keep the variance stable across layers.
  • Backpropagation (ch. 2): chain rule on the computation graph; the cost of a backward pass ≈ 2× that of the forward pass (remember: this is a complexity fact, not a software approximation — see ch. 1 here).
  • Optimization (ch. 3): SGD, momentum, Adam/AdamW; warmup and decay — the analytical detail is in the Optimization for AI module, ch. 2–5 and 10.
  • CNN (ch. 4): shared weights, receptive fields; cost dominated by the convolutions.
  • Transformers (ch. 6): multi-head attention \(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d})V\), 4\(d\) MLP, residual connections; this is the architecture of all the case studies in this module.
  • Generative models (ch. 7): AE/VAE/GAN — their sampling cost will be revisited in ch. 6.
  • Transfer learning (ch. 8): fine-tuning vs pre-training — the dividing line of budgets (ch. 1 and 7 here).
  • Training in practice (ch. 10): mixed precision, distributed data parallel, monitoring — ch. 5 here provides the theory.
The pedagogical contract of the P2 variant The P1 course answers "how does it work?"; this variant answers "how much does it cost, and how far can one go?". Three questions organize everything: (Q1) count — predict the FLOPs and memory of a training run before launching it (ch. 1, 7); (Q2) parallelize — decompose the compute time over several machines, and know what theory forbids (ch. 2–5); (Q3) compress — reduce the inference cost at nearly equal quality (ch. 6). The instructor is a theorist of distributed optimization (ch. 8): this module is the place in the master's program where one proves lower bounds, not just algorithms.
