4. Communication: all-reduce and friends

The real bottleneck of distributed training is almost never compute: it is the exchange of gradients. A single operator to truly know — the all-reduce — and two reductions: bandwidth or latency.

Definition 4.1 — All-reduce \(P\) machines each hold a vector \(g_i\) of \(S\) bytes; all must receive \(\bar g = \frac1P\sum_i g_i\) (the gradient average in DP). Homogeneous network: latency \(\alpha\) per message, bandwidth \(b\) bytes/s.
Theorem 4.2 — Ring all-reduce: bandwidth-optimal (proved) Arranging the \(P\) machines in a ring and cutting the vector into \(P\) chunks of \(S/P\) bytes, the all-reduce proceeds in two phases (reduce-scatter then all-gather) of \(P-1\) steps each: $$T_\mathrm{ring} \;=\; 2\,(P-1)\,\alpha \;+\; \frac{2(P-1)}{P}\cdot\frac{S}{b} \;\xrightarrow[P \to \infty]{}\; 2\alpha P + \frac{2S}{b}.$$ The bandwidth term becomes independent of \(P\) — each machine never sends more than \(\approx 2S/P\) ... that is, at most \(2S(P-1)/P < 2S\) bytes in total: optimal up to constants.
Reduce-scatter. At step \(t\), machine \(i\) sends a chunk (already partially summed) to \(i+1\) and receives from \(i-1\) a different chunk which it adds to its own: after \(P-1\) steps, machine \(i\) holds the complete sum of chunk \(i\) — each machine has sent \((P-1)\cdot S/P\) bytes. All-gather. Same round in broadcast direction: the complete sums circulate, \((P-1)\cdot S/P\) bytes sent per machine. Total per machine: \(2(P-1)S/P\) bytes at rate \(b\), that is \(2(P-1)S/(Pb)\) — and the critical path is indeed the sequence of \(2(P-1)\) steps of latency \(\alpha\). ∎
Proposition 4.3 — Tree all-reduce: latency-optimal In a binary tree: reduction in \(\log_2 P\) steps then broadcast in \(\log_2 P\) steps, but each step transmits the entire vector: \(T_\mathrm{tree} = 2\log_2 P\,(\alpha + S/b)\). For small \(S\) (short gradients, many small tensors): the tree wins (latency); for large \(S\) (LLM gradients): the ring wins (bandwidth). Real libraries (NCCL) choose dynamically — and compose partial rings over the actual topology (intra NVLink, inter IB).
Three wars against \(\gamma\): overlap, bucketing, compression
  • Overlap: while the GPU computes layer \(k+1\), the gradient of layer \(k\) is already on the network (async by buckets); provided \(T_\mathrm{comm} \leq T_\mathrm{calc}\), communication disappears from the critical path — the raison d'être of PyTorch DDP's "bucketing".
  • Gradient compression: quantizing the gradients (8 bits, even 1 bit) before exchange divides \(S\) by 2–16; error feedback (compensating the quantization error at the next step) preserves convergence — at the price of a delicate variance/theory trade-off (see the Emerging ML module, ch. 2–3 for the federated case, where communication is the limiting factor).
  • Locality: reducing the number of communications (local steps, gossip) is exactly the strategy dictated by the bound of ch. 2: fewer rounds, but more steps — the optimum of Scaman et al. is a precise point on this frontier.
