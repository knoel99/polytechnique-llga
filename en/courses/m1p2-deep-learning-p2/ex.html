Exercises

  1. Counting. Prove the "per token" formula \(24d^2 + 4nd\) of a transformer block (prop. 1.2), then verify theorem 1.3 on a GPT-2 small (\(N \approx 124\)M, 12 blocks, \(d = 768\)): compute \(C\) for \(D = 3\)B tokens and compare with a torch.profiler (or flops-profiler) profile within ±10%. Expected solution: \(6ND \approx 2{,}2\times10^{21}\) FLOPs.
  2. Pipeline bubble. Re-prove theorem 3.3 (draw the 1F1B Gantt chart for \(p=4\), \(m=8\)). Then: what minimal \(m\) for an efficiency ≥ 90% with \(p = 16\) stages? Solution: \(m/(m+15) \geq 0{,}9 \Rightarrow m \geq 135\).
  3. All-reduce. Prove theorem 4.2 on the reduce-scatter (induction on the steps); then plot \(T_\mathrm{ring}\) and \(T_\mathrm{tree}\) (prop. 4.3) for \(S = 1\) GB, \(\alpha = 2\,\mu s\), \(b = 25\) GB/s, \(P = 2 \dots 512\) on a log scale. Crossover? Which mode for a bf16 LLM-13B gradient? Partial solution: the ring wins as soon as \(S/P \gg \alpha b\).
  4. Checkpointing. A model with 30 blocks, activations 1.2 GB per block, 10 GB of memory available for activations. (a) Verify that full-recompute does not fit; (b) apply theorem 5.1: how many segments, what memory, what time overhead? (c) Generalize: for a memory budget \(M\), show that the time overhead is \(\Theta(L\,a/M)\) recomputed activations — and not \(+\tfrac13\) — when \(M \ll \sqrt{L}\,a\).
  5. Inference memory wall. (a) Recover the KV cache formula (def. 6.1) for a model with grouped-query attention (GQA: \(g\) head groups instead of \(h\)) — what saving? (b) Compute the peak throughput of a 13B fp16 on an A100 (2 TB/s) and on a 1 TB/s GPU; (c) show that int4 exactly doubles this ceiling (and why batching changes nothing there). Solution: (a) factor \(g/h\); (b) ≈ 77 then 38 tok/s; (c) in int4, the weights go from \(2N\) to \(N/2\) bytes — the wall thus exactly doubles.
  6. Roofline. A kernel reads \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\) and \(w \in \mathbb{R}^n\) and computes \(Xw\) (\(2mn\) FLOPs, \((mn + n + m)\times4\) bytes). (a) Intensity \(I(m)\) as \(m \to \infty\) (at fixed \(n=4096\))? (b) On an A100 (312 TFLOP/s, 2 TB/s), for which \(m\) is the GEMV memory-bound? (c) Deduce why autoregressive decoding is memory-bound and what batching changes there (hint: \(I\) grows with batch size — but not the wall per token).
  7. Complete budget (the capstone lab). Take up the table 7.3 with \(N = 13\)B, \(D = 300\)B tokens (a "Chinchilla-lite" budget: comment on the \(D/N\) ratio), on 64 H100 GPUs (≈ 990 TFLOP/s bf16, 40% MFU, 700 W). Compute: FLOPs, GPU-hours, calendar time, states memory (ZeRO-3 over 8 nodes?), energy, FR/world emissions. Conclude with three sentences of architecture recommendation. Partial answers: \(2{,}3\times10^{22}\) FLOPs; ≈ 16,500 GPU·h; ≈ 11 days; 208 GB of states → ZeRO mandatory.
  8. Paper + measurement. Choose: Megatron (TP), ZeRO, or FlashAttention. (a) Summarize the mechanism in 10 lines with the module's formulas; (b) measure an effect on a small real setup (2 GPUs suffice): DP scaling 1→2→4 on a model that fits in memory (verify the \(\frac{2(P-1)}{P}\frac{S}{b}\) term of theorem 4.2 with torch.distributed and a barrier-timer); (c) present in 10 minutes: key figure, one reproduced number, one limitation. Grid: accuracy of the quoted formulas, honesty of the measurement (error bars!), relevance of the chosen limitation.
