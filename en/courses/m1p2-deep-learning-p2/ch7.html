7. Real systems: roofline, GPU, and the complete budget of a 7B LLM

Definition 7.1 — Roofline model (why a "312 TFLOP/s" GPU delivers 45%) An operation has an arithmetic intensity \(I = \mathrm{FLOPs}/\mathrm{bytes\ read+written}\). The achievable throughput is $$P_\mathrm{achievable} \;=\; \min\big(P_\mathrm{peak},\; B_\mathrm{mem}\cdot I\big)$$ with \(P_\mathrm{peak}\) the compute throughput and \(B_\mathrm{mem}\) the memory bandwidth. A huge GEMM (large \(I\)): compute-bound; an activation, a normalization, a tokenizer, token-by-token generation (low \(I\)): memory-bound — increasing the GPU's FLOP/s changes nothing there. The 35–50% MFU of LLMs is precisely the share of layers actually in the compute regime; FlashAttention (prop. 6.2) wins by reducing bytes, not FLOPs — a pure roofline move.
Proposition 7.2 — Amdahl's law (proved) — the ceiling of all parallelism If a fraction \(s\) of the work is sequential (inevitably so), the speedup on \(P\) processors is capped: $$A(P) \;=\; \frac{1}{s + (1-s)/P} \;\leq\; \frac{1}{s}.$$
Sequential time \(T_1 = T_s + T_p\) with \(s = T_s/T_1\). On \(P\) processors, the parallel part divides at best by \(P\): \(T_P = T_s + T_p/P\), hence \(A(P) = T_1/T_P = 1/(s + (1-s)/P)\), increasing in \(P\) and bounded above by \(1/s\). ∎

The system's keyboard: what to know about a GPU Memory hierarchy (registers → SRAM ~20 MB → HBM ~80 GB → NVLink → IB); each level ~10× slower; winning kernels are those that maximize locality (tiling, fusion — FlashAttention again); "tensor cores" do bf16 GEMMs in 16×16×16 blocks — hence the importance of dimensions that are multiples of powers of 2, and why padding genuinely wastes.
