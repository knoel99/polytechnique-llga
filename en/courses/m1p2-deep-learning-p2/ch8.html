8. Research of the instructor

Team update (2026-2027 catalog): the APM_52183_EP course unit “Apprentissage profond de la théorie à la pratique” is officially led by Alain Durmus (CMAP, École Polytechnique), with C. Battiloro, P. Bianchi, C. Bonet and B. Bonev on the instructional engineering team. The works presented below are those of Kevin Scaman (CMAP; previously MSR–INRIA), author of the lower-bound results underlying chapter 2: they are cited as classic references of the field, not as the works of the current official team. Three papers to know:

Optimality of distributed optimization (the heart of ch. 2)
  • K. Scaman, F. Bach, S. Bubeck, Y. T. Lee, L. Massoulié, Optimal Algorithms for Smooth and Strongly Convex Distributed Optimization in Networks, ICML 2017 — the paper establishing the lower bound of theorem 2.2 and the optimal algorithm (Nesterov × Chebyshev gossip): the optimal time depends on the product condition number × topology. This is the first complete characterization of the compute/communication trade-off over networks.
  • K. Scaman, F. Bach, S. Bubeck, L. Massoulié, Optimal Convergence Rates for Convex Distributed Optimization in Networks, JMLR 2019 — the journal version, extended to the non-strongly convex case: the two regimes of chapter 2 are characterized there optimally.
Complexity of neural networks: Lipschitz regularity
  • A. Virmaux, K. Scaman, Lipschitz regularity of deep neural networks: analysis and efficient estimation, NeurIPS 2018 — proving that computing exactly the Lipschitz constant of a deep network is NP-hard, and designing the estimator (SeqLip, layer-wise power iteration) that became standard. The link with this module: the propagation bound of prop. 6.2/Responsible AI 3.2 involves precisely these \(\mathrm{Lip}_m\) constants — and the paper says how far one can (not) compute them; on the robustness side, the Lipschitz constant is the certificate of adversarial guarantees (see the Emerging ML module, ch. 1).
Reading thread L1: read the ICML 2017 focusing on (i) the construction of the lower bound (theorem 2.2 above); L2: re-prove the pipeline bubble (3.3) on paper; L3: profile a mini distributed training run (exercise 8) and empirically verify the ring all-reduce formula 4.2. This is the theory → system → measurement chain that defines this module.
