This module takes up the foundation of the P1 Deep Learning course and rereads it through the lens of cost: how many FLOPs, how many bytes, what communications — and what the complexity lower bounds say about what cannot be done better. Around this guiding thread, training parallelization, memory efficiency and inference efficiency become quantitative problems, down to the actual behavior of a 7B LLM on GPU. This is the course where deep learning meets systems.

What you will be able to do

  • Count the FLOPs and memory of a model, and connect these counts to scaling laws.
  • Explain the theoretical complexity bounds of training and what they rule out.
  • Choose a parallelism strategy — data, tensor, pipeline — and justify it quantitatively.
  • Analyze collective communications (all-reduce and friends) and their effective cost.
  • Reduce the memory footprint of a training run with checkpointing and ZeRO.
  • Make inference efficient and know at what performance price.
  • Read a roofline and predict the behavior of a real system, from GPU to 7B LLM.

Official objectives and syllabus (2026-2027 catalog)

Officially, the APM_52183_EP course unit offers an in-depth presentation of the practical and theoretical aspects of deep learning: on the one hand the mastery of Python / PyTorch implementation (implementation, debugging, visualization), on the other the analysis of foundations (automatic differentiation, universality results, non-convex optimization, equivariant models). The stated objective is twofold: give an overview of the deep learning landscape and its applications, and make students able to train by themselves a deep network of several million parameters. Official prerequisites: basics in Machine Learning and coding in Python.

Complement to the official syllabus Chapters 1 to 7 of this module deepen a “cost and systems” angle (FLOPs and memory counting, data/tensor/pipeline parallelism, collective communications, checkpointing and ZeRO, efficient inference, roofline) that does not appear as such in the official syllabus; conversely, official themes 2, 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 are not developed in it. These materials therefore complement the official syllabus on the systems side — they do not replace it.

Where this course fits in the program

An option of period 2 of M1, outside the recommended track: this is the P2 variant of the P1 course, Deep Learning, whose foundation it assumes (or an equivalent); chapter 0 offers compact reminders of it. Official prerequisites: basics in Machine Learning and coding in Python — exactly what chapter 0 brings back up to level before the cost chapters. Downstream, the module directly prepares ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles (M2), where these cost accounts become deployment decisions, and Advanced Topics in Large Language Models (M2), whose large-scale analyses build on the same scaling laws and budgets.

Course outline

  • Compact refresher: the P1 foundation — the essentials of the P1 Deep Learning course, restated in cost vocabulary.
  • Counting: FLOPs, memory, scaling laws — the module's basic discipline: quantify before asserting.
  • Theoretical complexity of training — the bounds that say what no trick will ever beat.
  • Parallelism: data, tensor, pipeline — the three main ways of distributing a training run, and their trade-offs.
  • Communication: all-reduce and friends — the collectives that connect the workers, and their share of total time.
  • Memory efficiency: checkpointing, ZeRO — fitting models and states that do not fit.
  • Efficient inference — cutting the cost of the trained model at the moment it serves.
  • Real systems: roofline, GPU, 7B LLM — confronting theoretical accounts with hardware, on a textbook case.
  • Research of the instructor — the research papers of Kevin Scaman, read with the module's tools.
  • Exercises — counts, bounds and parallelism choices to defend.
  • References — the systems and theory papers underlying each chapter.

How to study this module

  • Work through chapter 0 seriously, even if the P1 course is fresh: the cost vocabulary is introduced there.
  • Quantify everything: for every architecture encountered elsewhere, practice estimating FLOPs and memory before looking up the answer.
  • Never separate theory from hardware: a complexity bound is read facing a roofline.
  • Recompute the systems chapter's examples on paper, then check against GPU orders of magnitude.
  • Cross-reference with the optimization module of the same period: convergence rates ground the training analyses there.
  • Do the exercises by estimating first, computing second: the order of magnitude first is the target skill.
The module in the LLGA master's program The LLMs that structure the M2 of the master's program are first and foremost objects of cost: counting them, parallelizing them and compressing them is the condition for deploying them. This module provides this language of budgets, common to the systems courses and the M2 LLM courses — and directly marketable in careers in large-scale ML engineering as in research on distributed learning.
