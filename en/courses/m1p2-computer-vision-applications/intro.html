An onboard camera, a drone, a LiDAR sensor: computer vision is leaving academic datasets for real systems, with their latency, memory and robustness constraints. This module covers the complete application pipeline: object detection, segmentation (up to SAM), multi-object tracking, 3D vision (stereo and LiDAR), dataset construction, industrial case studies and embedded deployment. It is the "systems and applications" counterpart of the P1 vision course, resolutely geared toward industrial careers.

What you will be able to do

  • Compare the two paradigms of object detection and choose a detector under the application constraint.
  • Rigorously evaluate a detector with mAP and read a public benchmark without being misled.
  • Distinguish semantic, instance and panoptic segmentation, and use a generic segmentation model such as SAM.
  • Implement multi-object tracking on a video sequence.
  • Exploit stereo and LiDAR for applied 3D perception.
  • Build and annotate a vision dataset, and measure its biases.
  • Deploy a model under constraint: ONNX export, quantization, edge execution.
  • Analyze the ethics, robustness and limits of an embedded vision system.

Where this course fits in the program

Module of period 2 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), mandatory according to the MSc&T brochure (outside the recommended track in these materials); no dedicated SynapseS course description in the 2026-2027 catalog (period and assessment modalities: not communicated). It is the module that turns vision skills into deployment skills.

  • Prerequisites — Image Analysis and Computer Vision (P1), whose applied extension it is, and Deep Learning for CNNs and training; the review section covers convolution, CNNs and bounding boxes.
  • Downstream modules — the deployment chapter (ONNX, quantization, edge) directly prepares ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles (M2), and the industrial case studies anticipate the careers targeted by the master's program; vision systems also feed the multimodal pipelines of the Multimodal Generative AI module (same period).

Course outline

  • Review: convolution, CNNs, boxes — the deep vision building blocks revisited before the application tasks.
  • Object detection: the two paradigms — one-stage and two-stage detectors, and their speed-accuracy trade-offs.
  • Evaluation (mAP) and modern detectors — measuring honestly first, then the panorama of recent architectures.
  • Segmentation: semantic, instance, panoptic, SAM — the three granularities of segmentation and the era of foundation models.
  • Multi-object tracking — following targets across a video, identities included.
  • Applied 3D vision: stereo and LiDAR — recovering depth for embedded applications.
  • Data, benchmarks and annotation — building the dataset and the protocol before chasing the model.
  • Industrial case studies — real deployments, with their constraints and lessons learned.
  • Deployment: ONNX, quantization, edge — fitting a model into an embedded compute budget.
  • Ethics, robustness and limits — biases, attacks and failure cases: what a deployed system must own up to.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Redo the review section if the P1 vision course is no longer fresh: detection and segmentation reuse convolution and boxes without revisiting them.
  • For every task, start from the metric before the model: understanding mAP or IoU first avoids 90 % of benchmark misinterpretations.
  • Reproduce a small end-to-end experiment on a public dataset: training, evaluation, then export — that is the heart of the module.
  • Concretely try ONNX export and quantization on a small model: the deployment chapter is understood with hands on the keyboard.
  • Prepare questions for the industrial case studies: that is where constraints, trade-offs and real careers play out.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next; they calibrate your expectations of what a deployed system can actually do.
The LLGA positioning This module gives the master's program its "perception systems" building block: vision is the sensory input of multimodal pipelines and multimodal LLMs, and its embedded deployment foreshadows the practices of the ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles module (M2). Inspection, autonomous vehicles, robotics, imaging: the course's case studies are a dress rehearsal for the industrial careers targeted by the program.
