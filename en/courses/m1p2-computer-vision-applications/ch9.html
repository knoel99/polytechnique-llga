9. Ethics, robustness and limits

9.1. Adversarial examples

Small imperceptible perturbations change the prediction. The simplest attack — FGSM — moves the input one step in the direction of the loss gradient: $$x_{\mathrm{adv}} = x + \varepsilon\, \mathrm{sign}\!\left(\nabla_x \mathcal{L}(\theta, x, y)\right),$$ with \(\varepsilon\) on the order of 2–8/255. In detection, stickers glued to a sign can make an object disappear from the output; defenses range from adversarial training (min-max over perturbations) to bound-based certification — an overview is given in the Machine Learning module; the engineer mainly remembers: a model embedded in a product can be targeted, and robustness is tested (attack toolboxes) before deployment.

9.2. Bias and fairness of vision systems

  • representation bias: mostly North American/European datasets → performance drops in other contexts (streets, clothing, objects); the "Gender Shades" report documented accuracy gaps in detection by skin tone; in practice: break the evaluation down by subgroup (cf. Machine Learning module);
  • surveillance and biometrics: mass facial recognition is restricted/banned in several jurisdictions; the European AI regulation (AI Act, 2024) classifies remote biometric identification among high-risk or prohibited uses; anonymize before storage;
  • consent: scraped data (LAION etc.) and image rights; prefer clearly licensed datasets and document provenance (data cards).

9.3. Fundamental limits to know

Note — What models do not "understand" Compositionality: a detector can see "a person" and "a bicycle" without inferring "a person on a bicycle" — relations require other formalisms (scene graphs; cf. Graph ML module). Multimodal hallucination: captioners describe absent objects (cf. Multimodal GenAI module, chapter 8). Out of distribution: snow, night, a new sensor — the displayed confidence is not calibrated outside the training distribution. Causality: vision correlates, it does not explain — a decision system must separate perception from decision (autonomous driving, chapter 7).
