4. Multi-object tracking

Definition 4.1 — Multi-object tracking (MOT) Given a video stream, associate across frames the detections of the same physical object under a stable track identifier, while managing appearances, disappearances, occlusions and crossings. The dominant paradigm is tracking-by-detection: a detector (chapters 1–2) runs at every frame; the tracker reduces to the association problem between current detections and existing tracks.

4.1. SORT: Kalman + Hungarian matching

SORT (2016) is the minimal tracker:

  1. each track carries a constant-velocity Kalman filter (state \([x, y, s, \dot x, \dot y, \dot s]\); the Kalman filter is recalled below);
  2. prediction: the boxes are extrapolated to the current frame;
  3. association: solve the bipartite coupling between predictions and detections with cost \(1 - \mathrm{IoU}\) (Hungarian algorithm);
  4. the formed pairs update the filters (correction); tracks left unmatched for \(T_{\text{max}}\) consecutive frames are destroyed; orphan detections create new tracks.
Reminder — the Kalman filter in two equations
Linear-Gaussian state model \(x_t = F x_{t-1} + w\), observation model \(z_t = H x_t + v\), white noises with covariances \(Q, R\): $$\hat x_{t|t-1} = F \hat x_{t-1|t-1}, \quad P_{t|t-1} = F P_{t-1|t-1} F^\top + Q \quad (\text{prediction}),$$ $$K_t = P_{t|t-1} H^\top (H P_{t|t-1} H^\top + R)^{-1}, \quad \hat x_{t|t} = \hat x_{t|t-1} + K_t (z_t - H \hat x_{t|t-1}) \quad (\text{correction}).$$ The gain \(K_t\) weighs the prediction (motion) and the observation (detection) according to their uncertainties — cf. the Signal Processing module for the Bayesian interpretation.

4.2. DeepSORT: adding appearance

In a crowd, two pedestrians who cross paths swap their boxes: IoU alone re-associates poorly. DeepSORT (2017) associates in two stages: (i) cascade matching favoring recently seen tracks, with a cost combining IoU and an appearance embedding distance — a small ReID (re-identification) network learns a descriptor \(e_i \in \mathbb{R}^{128}\) invariant to pose/scale; (ii) IoU matching (as in SORT) for the rest. The "appearance memory" (a gallery of the last descriptors per track) resists long occlusions.

4.3. ByteTrack: don't throw away low-score detections

ByteTrack (2022) starts from a simple observation: during a partial occlusion, the detector often emits a correct but low-confidence detection — which classical pipelines discard at the threshold. The algorithm sketch:

  1. split the detections into high score (\(\geq \tau_{\mathrm{high}}\), e.g. 0.5) and low score (\(\tau_{\mathrm{low}} \leq s < \tau_{\mathrm{high}}\));
  2. first matching: active tracks vs high-score detections, Hungarian coupling on a combined distance (IoU + appearance);
  3. second matching: tracks still orphaned vs low-score detections, coupling on IoU alone — the low-confidence boxes "carry" the track through the occlusion;
  4. tracks still orphaned are put on hold then destroyed; orphan high-score detections create tracks (never the low-score ones).

Result: notable MOTA/IDF1 gains without any new detector — association is a lever as profitable as perception.

4.4. Measuring a tracker

Definition 4.2 — MOTA and IDF1 $$\mathrm{MOTA} = 1 - \frac{|\mathrm{FN}| + |\mathrm{FP}| + |\mathrm{ID\ switches}|} {\sum_t |\text{ground truths}_t|}, \qquad \mathrm{IDF1} = \frac{2\,\mathrm{IDTP}}{2\,\mathrm{IDTP} + \mathrm{IDFP} + \mathrm{IDFN}},$$ where ID switches count identity flips (a tracker that "swaps" two people loses IDF1 even if its MOTA is good). MOTA mostly measures detection; IDF1 mostly measures identity; HOTA (2020) rebalances detection and association.
Note — In production Tracking imposes strong system constraints: per-frame latency (the tracker must fit in the budget, cf. chapter 8); robustness to detector dropouts (a missed frame); re-identification after leaving the field of view; and, in multi-camera settings, cross-camera association (pure ReID, with no IoU available). The two key building blocks — Hungarian coupling and the Kalman filter — are L3 classics; the modernity lies in the learned descriptors.
