5. Applied 3D vision: stereo and LiDAR

5.1. The pinhole model (reminder)

L3 reminder — perspective projection
A pinhole camera projects a 3D point \(X = (X, Y, Z)\) (camera frame, \(Z\) pointing forward) to \((u, v)\) in the image: $$\lambda \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = K \,[\,R \mid t\,] \begin{pmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad K = \begin{pmatrix} f_x & 0 & c_x \\ 0 & f_y & c_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},$$ with \(K\) the intrinsics (focal lengths in pixels, optical center), \([R|t]\) the extrinsics (camera pose); \(\lambda = Z\). Calibration (OpenCV) estimates \(K\) and the distortion (radial-tangential model) — cf. the P1 course for the complete geometry (Hartley & Zisserman).

5.2. Stereo: disparity as a depth measurement

Definition 5.1 — Disparity For a rectified stereo pair (coplanar images, horizontal epipolar lines, common focal length \(f\), baseline \(B\)), a 3D point projects to \((u_L, v)\) on the left and \((u_R, v)\) on the right; the disparity is \(d = u_L - u_R \geq 0\), measured in pixels.
Proposition 5.2 — Depth from disparity In the rectified setting, the depth of the point is $$Z = \frac{f \, B}{d}.$$
The 3D point \((X, Y, Z)\) projects on the left to \(u_L = fX/Z\) and on the right (optical center shifted by \(B\) along \(x\)) to \(u_R = f(X - B)/Z\). Taking the difference, \(u_L - u_R = fB/Z\), i.e. \(d = fB / Z\) and hence \(Z = fB / d\). ∎
Practical consequence: the depth uncertainty \(\partial Z / \partial d = -fB/d^2 = -Z^2/(fB)\) grows quadratically with distance — stereo is precise up close, coarse far away; increasing \(B\) or \(f\) improves range at the price of the visible field and harder matchings.

The classical stereo pipeline: (i) rectification; (ii) cost volume — for every left pixel and every candidate disparity \(d\), a matching cost (census, SSD of descriptors) — this is an \(H \times W \times D_{\max}\) volume; (iii) aggregation and regularization: SGM (Hirschmüller, 2008) aggregates costs along several directions (per-path dynamic programming) with discontinuity penalties — the industrial standard (baked into GPUs!); (iv) disparity map. Deep methods (PSM-Net and successors) learn the cost volume: 3D convolutions on descriptors stacked by disparity — exactly the same volume construction that the Multimodal GenAI module, chapter 3, exploits for other data.

5.3. LiDAR: point clouds

LiDAR emits pulses and measures time of flight: the output is a 3D point cloud (\(x, y, z\), reflectivity), ~100k–2M points/s, accurate in distance (~2 cm), sparse and irregular — unstructured: grid convolutions do not apply directly. Families of methods:

  • PointNet (2017): per point, a shared MLP; aggregation by global max; then an MLP. Permutation invariance comes from the max: the global descriptor does not depend on the order of the points — cf. the proposition below;
  • Voxels / pillars: discretize space (VoxelNet) or only the ground plane (PointPillars: vertical columns), aggregate the point features there, then run a 2D/3D CNN on the resulting grid;
  • Center detection: CenterPoint projects everything into bird's eye view (BEV) and detects objects by their center (heatmap + size/rotation regression) — the anchor-free formulation of chapter 2, transposed to a 2D "top-down view".
Proposition 5.3 — The global max is permutation invariant Let \(h(X) = \mathrm{max}_{i=1..n} \mathrm{MLP}(x_i)\) be PointNet's global descriptor. For every permutation \(\pi\) of \(\{1, \dots, n\}\): \(h(x_{\pi(1)}, \dots, x_{\pi(n)}) = h(x_1, \dots, x_n)\).
The MLP is applied pointwise: the set \(\{\mathrm{MLP}(x_i)\}\) is unchanged by permutation (only the enumeration order changes). The per-coordinate maximum of a set does not depend on the enumeration order, hence the equality. Note that a sum or an average would have the same property; the max is preferred because it selects one "witness" per coordinate and avoids the information smoothing of averages. ∎

5.4. Multi-sensor fusion in bird's eye view

Cameras provide semantics (classes, traffic lights, signs) but only indirect depth; LiDAR provides geometry but poor semantics. Modern architectures fuse in BEV: every sensor is brought into a common top-down frame where detection, tracking and trajectory prediction combine. BEVFormer (2022): image features are "queried" in BEV space by spatial cross-attention (every BEV cell queries the pixels along its view pillar), with temporal attention toward the previous BEV map — tracking becomes implicit.
