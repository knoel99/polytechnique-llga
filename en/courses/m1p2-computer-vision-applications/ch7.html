7. Industrial case studies

7.1. Industrial inspection (quality control)

Inspecting parts (scratches, cracks, contamination) on a production line: controlled lighting (strobe, dark background), imposed cadence (decisions per second), and — the major specificity — very few defect examples. Hence the move to unsupervised methods: learn normality (autoencoders, feature distances; cf. Deep Learning module, chapter 7, for autoencoders) and raise an alert on deviation; the MVTec AD dataset is the reference benchmark (15 categories: screws, textiles, wood, bottles…, with pixel-level defect masks). Metrics: image-level detection (AUROC) and defect segmentation (pixel AUROC, PRO). Constraints: an error is asymmetrically costly (false rejection = yield loss; missed defect = product recall); the threshold is tuned on business cost, not on 0.5.

Example 7.1 — Tuning a threshold on costs Let an anomaly score \(s \in [0,1]\); false rejection costs €1 (manual re-inspection), missed defect costs €100 (product recall). At the operating point (FP rate \(= 4\,\%\) on healthy parts, FN rate \(= 2\,\%\) on defective parts, real defect rate 0.5 %): expected cost for 10,000 parts $$10\,000 \times \big[0.995 \times 0.04 \times 1 + 0.005 \times 0.02 \times 100\big] = 10\,000 \times (0.0398 + 0.01) \approx 498\ \text{euros}.$$ Lowering the threshold (more sensitive) increases FPs but reduces FNs: redo the computation and choose the threshold at the cost minimum — the right engineering metric is a cost, not an accuracy.

7.2. Autonomous driving

The typical perception pipeline of a SAE level 2+ to 4 vehicle:

  1. multi-camera detection/segmentation (chapters 1–3);
  2. camera/LiDAR/radar fusion in BEV (chapter 5);
  3. tracking and prediction of agents (chapter 4 + trajectory models);
  4. planning (out of scope — cf. the Reinforcement Learning module for the MDP formalization);
  5. system monitoring: redundancy, diagnostics, degraded mode.

Engineering lessons: corner cases dominate the cost (roadworks, flashing lights, overturned trailers); closed benchmarks (nuScenes) are saturated and correlate poorly with real kilometers; safety imposes latency guarantees (chapter 8) and traceability (version of the model, the dataset, the threshold).

7.3. Medical imaging

Organ/lesion segmentation (U-Net is still the backbone, chapter 3), cell counting, fracture detection. Specificities: rare and costly data (clinicians), DICOM formats (metadata, windowing), strict clinical validation, regulation (medical CE marking); the metric is per-structure Dice and inter-observer consistency is the reachable ceiling — a Dice above human agreement is suspicious; the models are assistance tools (CAD — computer-aided diagnosis), not decision makers.

7.4. Other domains (summary)

  • retail: planograms (products on shelves), customer flows (anonymous counting);
  • agriculture: weed detection for targeted spraying (drone, embedded);
  • sports: player/ball tracking (chapter 4 at scale), tactical statistics;
  • satellite: building/road detection, deforestation masks — with domain randomization and multispectral imaging as a bonus.
