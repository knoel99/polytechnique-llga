6.2. The cost of annotation

A COCO box costs from a few seconds to a minute; a polygon mask, much more; a 3D LiDAR box at 25 Hz over 20 objects, several minutes with dedicated tooling. Three industrial levers:

  • assisted annotation: a pretrained model proposes, the human corrects — the SA-1B loop of chapter 3 is its large-scale version; an additional benefit: active learning only has annotated the examples where the model is uncertain;
  • synthetic data: procedural 3D rendering with domain randomization (random textures, lighting to force invariance); the P2 module "Image Synthesis" (brochure) covers rendering; sim2real remains the hard part;
  • augmentation: geometric (cropping, rotation, mosaic) and photometric (color, blur, noise); caution: geometric transformations must transform the boxes/masks and the metadata (rotation → bounding boxes of the rotated masks).

6.3. Evaluation pitfalls

Note — The three classic pitfalls (1) Data leakage: near-duplicate images between train and test (video sequences); always split by video/scene, not by image. (2) Long-tail imbalance: the global mAP masks rare classes — look at the per-class APs; the training data must be resampled or reweighted (cf. focal loss, chapter 1). (3) Domain shift: a COCO model collapses on your production cameras (angle, compression, weather); plan from the protocol stage for a "shadow" set of unseen real data, and drift monitoring in operation (chapter 8).
