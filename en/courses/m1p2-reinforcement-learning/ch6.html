6. Bandits and exploration

Definition 6.1 — K-armed bandit An MDP with a single state: at each step choose an arm \(a\) and receive \(r \sim \nu_a\) (independent of the past). Objective: minimize the cumulative regret $$R_T = T\,\mu^\star - \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^T \mu_{A_t}\Big] = \sum_{a : \Delta_a > 0} \Delta_a\, \mathbb{E}[N_a(T)], \qquad \Delta_a = \mu^\star - \mu_a,$$ where \(N_a(T)\) counts the pulls of arm \(a\). The bandit isolates the exploration/exploitation trade-off without state generalization cost — and it is already a hard sequential problem: every datum received depends on the past action.
Theorem 6.2 — UCB1: logarithmic regret, proof sketch UCB1 (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi & Fischer, 2002): play each arm once, then at each step $$A_t = \arg\max_a \Big[\hat\mu_a(t) + \sqrt{\tfrac{2 \log t}{N_a(t)}}\Big]$$ ("optimism in the face of uncertainty": the bonus decreases in \(1/\sqrt{N_a}\)). For rewards in \([0,1]\): $$R_T \;\leq\; \sum_{a \neq a^\star} \frac{8 \log T}{\Delta_a} + \Big(1 + \frac{\pi^2}{3}\Big) \sum_a \Delta_a.$$
Step 1 (concentration, Hoeffding). For an arm \(a\) pulled \(n\) times, \(\mathbb{P}\big(\hat\mu_a \leq \mu_a - u\big) \leq e^{-2 n u^2}\) — the empirical means concentrate around the true mean. Step 2 (optimism is reliable). With \(u = \sqrt{2\log t / N_a}\), the event "\(\hat\mu_a + u < \mu_a\)" (pessimistic index) has probability \(\leq t^{-4}\); union bound over \(t\) (the series \(\sum t^{-4}\) is finite, hence the \(\pi^2/3\) term): outside rare events, the index always upper-bounds the true mean, so the optimal arm has an index \(\geq \mu^\star\) and a suboptimal arm can only be chosen if its estimate is too high. Step 3 (counting bad choices). If \(a \neq a^\star\) is chosen at time \(t\), then (indices compared) \(\hat\mu_a + \sqrt{2\log t / N_a} \geq \hat\mu_{a^\star} + \sqrt{2\log t/N_{a^\star}} \geq \mu^\star\), hence \(\hat\mu_a \geq \mu^\star - \sqrt{2\log t/N_a}\) — but \(\hat\mu_a \leq \mu_a + \sqrt{2\log t/N_a}\) outside rare events; combining, \(N_a(t) \leq 8 \log T / \Delta_a^2\) before the arm is definitively dominated. Step 4 (regret). Inject into \(R_T = \sum_a \Delta_a \mathbb{E}[N_a(T)]\). ∎
Theorem 6.3 — Lai-Robbins lower bound (1985) For every consistent strategy (regret sub-polynomial in \(T\) on all instances): $$\liminf_{T \to \infty} \frac{R_T}{\log T} \;\geq\; \sum_{a : \Delta_a > 0} \frac{\Delta_a}{\mathrm{KL}(\nu_a \,\|\, \nu_{a^\star})}.$$ Interpretation: distinguishing \(\nu_a\) from \(\nu_{a^\star}\) requires \(\Omega(\mathrm{KL}^{-1} \log T)\) samples — information-theoretically. UCB1 loses at most a constant factor against this bound; optimized indices (KL-UCB) and Thompson sampling attain it asymptotically. Regret in \(\Theta(\log T)\) is therefore the law of the problem.
Algorithm 6.4 — Thompson sampling: derived posteriors Maintain a posterior over each \(\mu_a\); at each step, sample \(\tilde\mu_a\) from each posterior and play \(\arg\max_a \tilde\mu_a\). Pulls of a dominated arm naturally become rare: exploration emerges from the algorithm.
Bernoulli case: Beta conjugation. Prior \(\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)\) of density \(\propto \theta^{\alpha-1}(1-\theta)^{\beta-1}\), likelihood \(\theta^{S}(1-\theta)^{F}\) (\(S\) successes, \(F\) failures): the posterior is \(\propto \theta^{\alpha + S - 1}(1-\theta)^{\beta + F - 1}\), that is, $$\mu_a \mid \text{data} \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\alpha + S_a,\ \beta + F_a)$$ — the update is a simple counting (uniform prior: \(\alpha = \beta = 1\)). Gaussian case: completing the square. Rewards \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_a, \sigma^2)\), prior \(\mu_a \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau^2)\). The posterior is Gaussian: $$v_n = \Big(\frac{1}{\tau^2} + \frac{n}{\sigma^2}\Big)^{-1}, \qquad m_n = v_n\, \frac{n\, \bar r}{\sigma^2}, \qquad \mu_a \mid \text{data} \sim \mathcal{N}(m_n, v_n)\,: $$ precisions (inverses of the variances) add up — one recovers the empirical estimator in the limit \(n \to \infty\). ∎
Thompson sampling attains the Lai-Robbins bound (asymptotically, exponential families) and empirically dominates UCB — it is the industrial choice (recommendation, A/B tests). Chapelle & Li (2011) for the reference empirical study.
Algorithm 6.5 — Linear contextual bandits: LinUCB Context \(x_t \in \mathbb{R}^d\) (user, article), expected reward linear per action: \(\mathbb{E}[r \mid x, a] = x_{t,a}^\top \theta^\star\). Ridge estimation: $$A_t = \lambda I + \sum_{\tau < t} x_{\tau} x_{\tau}^\top, \qquad b_t = \sum_{\tau < t} x_\tau\, r_\tau, \qquad \hat\theta_t = A_t^{-1} b_t,$$ and index rule (confidence ellipsoid): $$a_t = \arg\max_a\ x_{t,a}^\top \hat\theta_t + \alpha \sqrt{x_{t,a}^\top A_t^{-1} x_{t,a}}.$$ The bonus \(\sqrt{x^\top A^{-1} x}\) is the directional uncertainty (small in often-observed directions); regret \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})\) (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011). LinUCB (Li et al., 2010) deployed this idea at scale for news article recommendation — the context = user/article features, exactly the schema of modern recommender systems (network variants: NeuralTS, etc.).

6.1. Exploring beyond bandits

  • Three families of exploration: \(\varepsilon\)-greedy (blind, learns nothing from the structure); optimism (UCB, count-based: bonus \(\beta/\sqrt{N(s,a)}\) — explore what is uncertain); posterior (Thompson: explore what is plausible). In MDPs, optimism yields the UCT/Monte-Carlo Tree Search algorithms (the heart of AlphaGo).
  • Intrinsic exploration in high dimension: when the count \(N(s,a)\) is empty (states almost all distinct — images), replace novelty by a prediction error: RND (Burda et al., 2019, sketch): a random frozen target network \(f\) and a predictor \(g\) trained to imitate \(f\) on visited states; the intrinsic reward \(r_{\mathrm{int}}(s) = \|f(s) - g(s)\|_2^2\) is large on new states (never seen by \(g\)) and fades with familiarity — a similarity-based generalization of count-based exploration;
  • Exploration in LLMs: RLHF and LLM evaluation (best-of-n, jury voting) are bandit problems (arms = answers, context = prompt); RLVR (RL from verifiable rewards) uses exploration for reasoning-chain search (verifiable reward = binomial oracle); SAC-like policy entropy and sampling temperature play the role of the temperature \(\alpha\) — chapter taken up again in the M2 Large Language Models module.
