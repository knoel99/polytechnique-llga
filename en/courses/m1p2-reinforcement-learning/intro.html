Reinforcement teaches an agent to act in an environment so as to maximize cumulative reward: this is the framework of Markov decision processes, from dynamic programming to Q-learning, from policy gradients to deep RL. Where supervised learning learns to predict, here one learns to decide, without a supervisor, by trial and error. This mandatory module of P2 is strategic in the master's program: LLM alignment (RLHF, PPO, DPO, GRPO) and modern autonomous agents build directly on these methods.

What you will be able to do

  • Formalize a sequential problem as a Markov decision process: states, actions, transitions, rewards, policy.
  • Solve a known MDP by dynamic programming (value iteration, policy iteration).
  • Implement model-free learning methods: TD, SARSA, Q-learning.
  • Write policy gradient algorithms and actor-critic variants.
  • Carry out deep RL: DQN, Rainbow, DDPG/SAC, and offline learning (offline RL).
  • Master the exploration/exploitation trade-off, in particular in the bandit setting.
  • Analyze autonomous agents in the era of LLMs: tool use, LLM agents, multi-agent systems.

Official objectives and syllabus (2026-2027 catalog)

The official course description (X3A session "Reinforcement Learning and Autonomous Agents") presents a course on the development of autonomous agents: systems that perceive, learn and act independently to achieve goals in uncertain and dynamic environments, drawing on probabilistic decision-making, inference and planning. The syllabus makes official the Markov decision processes and their key concepts, deep Q-learning and its variants, the policy gradient and actor-critic architectures, then more specialized methodologies: model-based learning, gradient-free optimization, imitation learning and inverse reinforcement learning, offline and distributional RL, model-based control and decision transformers — with a constant emphasis on modern deep neural architectures, their scaling and the proper formulation of sequential decision problems. Official prerequisites: "a course such as Machine Learning and/or Deep Learning and the concepts associated with these courses, as well as knowledge of Python and its scientific libraries". Assessment: homework (50% in total) and project (50%, oral assessment).

Where this course fits in the program

Mandatory module of period 2 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), of which Jesse Read is the official head. Official prerequisites: a course such as Machine Learning and/or Deep Learning and the concepts associated with these courses, as well as knowledge of Python and its scientific libraries. It constitutes the third pillar of machine learning alongside supervised and unsupervised learning, and directly feeds the second year.

  • Prerequisites — Machine Learning and Deep Learning for models and networks, and Probability Theory for ML: Applications to Monte Carlo Methods and Generative Models for Markov chains; the refresher section covers Markov chains, martingales and dynamic programming.
  • Downstream modules — RL directly prepares the RLHF chapter of the Large Language Models module (M2): PPO, DPO and GRPO are used there without detour; it also sheds light on the autonomous agents of Real-time AI in Video Games: decisive & collaborative actions (same period, real-time decision).

Course outline

  • Refresher: Markov chains, martingales, dynamic programming — the probabilistic foundation on which the whole module rests.
  • Markov decision processes — the formal framework: agent, environment, policy, value function.
  • Dynamic programming — solving a known MDP: value and policy iterations.
  • Learning: TD, SARSA, Q-learning — learning without a model of the environment, by interaction.
  • Policy gradients and actor-critic — differentiating the policy to optimize it directly.
  • Deep RL: DQN, Rainbow, DDPG/SAC, offline RL — scaling up with deep networks, and learning from fixed data.
  • Bandits and exploration — the exploration/exploitation trade-off in the simplest setting, and its general lessons.
  • Autonomous agents in the era of LLMs — tool use, LLM agents and multi-agent: where RL meets language models.
  • Research of the teaching team: Jesse Read — a tour of the instructor's research work.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Assimilate the refresher section before anything else: without Markov chains or dynamic programming, MDPs and TD-learning remain opaque recipes.
  • Implement each algorithm on a small environment (gridworld, bandits): in RL, one only truly understands by coding the agent-environment loop.
  • Always plot learning curves over several random seeds: the variance of runs is in itself a lesson of the module.
  • Classify the algorithms by their assumptions — on-policy or off-policy, online or offline, value or policy: this mental table is worth more than any mnemonic device.
  • Read the section on LLM agents early, even though it comes at the end of the course: it gives a concrete and modern motivation for everything that precedes it.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next; the classic pitfalls (insufficient exploration, divergence of the critic) are encountered there first.
The LLGA positioning Reinforcement learning is the glue between the two axes of the master's program: it is through it that LLMs get aligned (RLHF) and that agents are deployed on graph-structured environments. Careers in sequential decision-making — recommendation, pricing, robotics, games — and research on the alignment of generative models all mobilize these methods, and the Large Language Models module (M2) makes immediate use of them.
