6. Fairness through transport

Definition 6.1 — The framework: sensitive attribute, demographic parity, equalized odds Data \((X, S, Y)\): features, sensitive attribute \(S\) (gender, origin…), label \(Y\); predictor \(\hat Y = h(X)\) (possibly randomized). Two emblematic criteria:
  • Demographic parity (DP): \(\hat Y\) independent of \(S\) — the same positive decision rates per group;
  • Equalized odds (EO, Hardt, Price & Srebro, 2016): \(\hat Y\) independent of \(S\) given \(Y\) — the same true positive and false positive rates per group.
OT enters both scenarios: (i) pre-processing — repair the feature distribution with a Wasserstein barycenter; (ii) post-processing — couple the per-group score distributions. In both cases, the central object is a coupling, i.e. a transport plan as in ch. 1.
Theorem 6.2 — Repair by Wasserstein barycenter (Gordaliza, Del Barrio, Gamboa & Loubes, ICML 2019) Algorithm (full repair, scalar case then multivariate).
  1. Estimate the conditional laws \(\mu_s = \mathcal{L}(X \,|\, S = s)\) by the empirical quantiles \(Q_s = \hat F_s^{-1}\) (sorting, prop. 2.6).
  2. Form the barycenter of the \(\mu_s\) weighted by the proportions \(p_s = \mathbb{P}(S = s)\); in 1D, corollary 2.8 gives its quantile function pointwise: $$Q^\star(u) \;=\; \sum_s\ p_s\, Q_s(u).$$
  3. Repair: replace each observation by its counterpart at the same quantile level in the barycenter: \(\tilde X_i = Q^\star\big(\hat F_s(X_i)\big)\) for \(S_i = s\). In the multivariate case, replace the 1D transport by a Sinkhorn plan between each cloud and a common entropic barycenter (def. 4.3), then use the barycentric projection of the OTDA algorithm (ch. 4).
  4. Retrain the model on the \((\tilde X_i, Y_i)\) without the sensitive attribute.
Full repair guarantees demographic parity. Let \(U_s = \hat F_s(X)\) for \(X \sim \mu_s\): \(U_s\) is (asymptotically) uniform on \([0,1]\), and \(\tilde X = Q^\star(U_s)\). Hence \(\mathcal{L}(\tilde X\,|\,S = s) = \mathcal{L}\big(Q^\star(U)\big)\) is the same law for every \(s\) — the barycenter. Identical conditionals give independence: \(\mathbb{P}(\tilde X \in A, S = s) = \mathbb{P}(\tilde X \in A\,|\,S = s)\, \mathbb{P}(S = s) = \mathbb{P}(\tilde X \in A)\,\mathbb{P}(S = s)\). Any predictor \(h(\tilde X)\) then inherits independence from \(S\): DP, regardless of the downstream classifier.∎
Partial repair: interpolate the quantiles, \(\tilde X^{(\lambda)} = (1-\lambda)\, Q_s(U_s) + \lambda\, Q^\star(U_s)\) with \(\lambda \in [0, 1]\) — \(\lambda = 0\): untouched data; \(\lambda = 1\): full repair. Utility/fairness trade-off: as \(\lambda\) is swept, the demographic parity gap decreases (the conditionals get closer in Wasserstein distance, linearly in \(\lambda\) for the interpolated transport) while accuracy decreases all the faster as \(X\) carries signal correlated with \(S\): the curve (DP gap on the x-axis, accuracy on the y-axis) is typically decreasing with a "knee" — one chooses \(\lambda\) at the elbow, where a lot of equity has been bought for little utility. Proposition 6.3 — Bicausal OT, counterfactuals and optimal EO post-processing by coupling (simple case proved) Bicausal OT. To compare two "twin" individuals differing only in the sensitive attribute, we couple the joint laws \(\mathcal{L}(S, X)\) with a plan \(\pi\) respecting the causal structure \(S \to X\) (bicausal coupling: the copy of \(X\) depends on the copy of \(S\) only through the coupling of the conditionals \(\mathcal{L}(X\,|\,S = s)\) — de Lara, González-Sanz, Asher, Risser & Loubes, JMLR 2024). The counterfactual \(X^\star(s')\) — "the value \(X\) would have taken had \(S\) been \(s'\)" — is the coupled point: constructed by OT between \(\mathcal{L}(X\,|\,S = s)\) and \(\mathcal{L}(X\,|\,S = s')\). Applications: measuring discrimination \(\mathbb{E}\big[c(X, X^\star)\big]\), predicting on the counterfactual ("counterfactual" fairness).
EO post-processing: the optimal coupling is the increasing coupling. Simple setting: \(Y \in \{0,1\}\), real score \(R_s = f(X)\) per group, and a randomized post-processing \(\hat Y_s = g_s(R_s, U)\), \(U \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1]\) common. (i) Such a post-processing is exactly a coupling between the law of \(R_s\,|\,Y = y\) and a target Bernoulli law — the noise \(U\) realizes the plan. (ii) EO requires that the law of \(\hat Y_s\,|\,Y = y\) be the same for both groups: we therefore couple simultaneously the two conditional score laws to a common target. (iii) Among these couplings, the one maximizing utility (accuracy, increasing in the score within each class) is the increasing coupling — quantile matching: the uncrossing argument of the proof of prop. 2.6 applies word for word: uncrossing two pairs (making the coupling more comonotone) cannot decrease a utility that is increasing in both scores. Concretely: bring each score to its rank \(U_s = \hat F_s(R_s)\) (quantile within its group), then apply the same decision rule to the \(U_s\) — the equalization of the conditional laws happens on the quantiles, and choosing the common operating point (TPR, FPR) maximizes the linear utility \(\mathbb{P}(Y{=}1)\,\mathrm{TPR} + \mathbb{P}(Y{=}0)\,(1 - \mathrm{FPR})\) over the feasible set (the intersection of the two regions convexified by randomization — the "mixture of predictors" procedure of Hardt et al., reread as an OT coupling).∎
Limits and debate: incompatible metrics, utility, causal assumptions
  • Theoretical incompatibilities: when the base rates \(\mathbb{P}(Y = 1\,|\,S = s)\) differ across groups, calibration and equalized odds are mutually exclusive (Kleinberg, Mullainathan & Raghavan 2016; Chouldechova 2017): no method — OT included — can satisfy all the criteria; choosing the metric is choosing the trade-off.
  • Utility cost: DP repair can destroy a legitimate signal (if \(S\) genuinely predicts \(Y\), independence forces a loss); "blind" repair is not always fair in the individual sense.
  • Causal assumptions: counterfactuals by bicausal OT assume a structure \(S \to X\) and the absence of unmeasured confounding; two different optimal bicausal couplings (non-uniqueness, cf. example 1.2) can yield different counterfactuals — OT provides a family of models, to be audited (the JMLR 2024 paper proposes statistical tests).
  • Position of the course: OT is not an answer to the question of justice, but a geometric language for quantifying trade-offs: distances between groups, barycenters, optimal couplings — the same objects as in ch. 1-5.

import numpy as np, ot
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
# Adult: X = numerical variables, S = gender, Y = income > 50k

def repair_1d(x, s, lam):
    """Partial quantile repair, feature by feature (th. 6.2)."""
    xR = x.copy()
    qs = {sv: np.sort(x[s == sv]) for sv in np.unique(s)}
    n = min(len(v) for v in qs.values())          # common ranks
    bary = np.mean([v[np.linspace(0, len(v)-1, n).astype(int)]
                    for v in qs.values()], axis=0)  # Q*: average of the quantiles
    for sv in np.unique(s):
        v, idx = np.sort(x[s == sv]), np.argsort(x[s == sv])
        ranks = np.linspace(0, len(v)-1, n)
        rep = (1-lam) * np.interp(ranks, range(len(v)), v) + lam * bary
        xR[s == sv] = np.interp(np.argsort(np.argsort(x[s == sv])),
                                ranks, rep)[np.argsort(idx)]
    return xR
