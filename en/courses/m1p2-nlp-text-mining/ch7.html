7. Transformers for NLP

  • Architecture: carried over from the Deep Learning module ch. 6 (multi-head attention \(\mathrm{softmax}(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})V\), residuals, LayerNorm, encoder/decoder); here we apply it to text — the M2 LLMs course (ch. 2) will revisit the architecture in depth (RoPE, GQA, MoE…);
BERT (Devlin et al., 2019): MLM + NSP in detail Bidirectional encoder, pretrained on two tasks: (1) Masked LM: 15 % of tokens drawn; among them 80 % replaced by [MASK], 10 % by a random word, 10 % unchanged; the original token is predicted (output: per-position classification). The mixing avoids a pretraining/fine-tuning mismatch (the model will never see [MASK] downstream again) and forces a robust representation. (2) Next Sentence Prediction: classify whether two sentences follow each other (the pair [CLS] A [SEP] B) — a weak task, abandoned by RoBERTa in favor of more data. Result: [CLS] = sentence representation; one model, dozens of tasks via a simple head layer.
GPT (Radford et al.) and T5 (Raffel et al., 2019) GPT: causal decoder — \(\mathcal{L}=-\sum_t\log p(x_t\mid x_{<t})\), pretraining = predicting the next token; generative by construction, zero task-specific architecture (this is the family extended in M2). T5: everything in text-to-text — span corruption (masking continuous fragments and regenerating them), every task (classification, translation, QA…) formulated as textual input→output; it unifies MLM and CLM.
Protocol 7.1 — Full fine-tuning (the stable recipe)
  1. Head: a linear layer \(\mathrm{Linear}(d, K)\) on [CLS] (classification) or per position (NER, POS);
  2. Optimizer: AdamW, learning rate \(\{2\times10^{-5}, 3\times10^{-5}, 5\times10^{-5}\}\) — a short grid, fine-tuning is sensitive but flat around the optimum; linear warmup for 10 % then linear decay;
  3. Epochs: 2 to 4 (beyond that: brutal overfitting on small corpora — watch the dev set); batch 16-32;
  4. Small corpora (<1k): freeze the lower layers, layer-wise decay (layer-wise lr decay), or adapters/LoRA (added parameters << 1 % — revisited in M2, ch. 6);
  5. Cost: distillation (DistilBERT ≈ −40 % params, −97 % of training time, ~97 % of the performance), int8 quantization — the performance/cost trade-off is an engineering criterion, deepened in M2 (LLMs ch. 6, ML Ops).
Sentence-BERT (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019): the triplet loss derived To encode sentences in a comparable space (search, clustering, RAG): siamese BERT + pooling (mean), trained with a triplet loss on (anchor \(a\), positive \(p\), negative \(n\)): $$\mathcal{L}=\max\big(0,\ d(a,p)-d(a,n)+\varepsilon\big).$$
If the margin is respected (\(d(a,p)+\varepsilon\le d(a,n)\)), the loss is zero: nothing to learn. Otherwise, the gradient \(\nabla = \nabla d(a,p)-\nabla d(a,n)\) pulls the anchor toward the positive and pushes it away from the negative, with a force proportional to the violation. Crucial point: the quality depends on the negatives (mini-batch: the other pairs provide hard negatives — this is what shapes the space). This is the kind of embeddings that indexes the corpora of RAG systems — detailed in M2 LLMs ch. 7 (dense retrieval, reranking). ∎
French: CamemBERT, FlauBERT, JuriBERT CamemBERT (Martin et al., 2020): RoBERTa retrained on 138 GB of French text — the reference French model; FlauBERT: an academic French BERT; JuriBERT (Douka et al., 2021 — research from this course, ch. 9): an adaptation to legal French, where the vocabulary (doctrine, grievances, appeal) confuses general models. Multilingual: mBERT, XLM-R (100+ languages, shared embeddings — at the price of capacity diluted per language). 
from transformers import pipeline

classifier = pipeline("text-classification",
                      model="distilbert-base-uncased-finetuned-sst-2-english")
print(classifier("Graph RAG is a game changer for enterprise search."))

ner = pipeline("ner", aggregation_strategy="simple")
print(ner("Michalis Vazirgiannis teaches at Ecole Polytechnique."))
