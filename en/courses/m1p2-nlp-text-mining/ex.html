Exercises

  1. TF-IDF on paper. On 3 given documents, compute by hand the TF-IDF matrix (natural log) of the reduced vocabulary, then the cosine similarity of each pair; verify with TfidfVectorizer (watch out for L2 normalization).
    Solution (method): (1) count tf(t,d); (2) df(t) over the 3 documents, then \(\mathrm{idf}=\log(3/\mathrm{df})\) (0 for a term present everywhere — it disappears); (3) multiply; (4) normalize each row \(\tilde d=d/\|d\|_2\), so that \(\cos(d_i,d_j)=\tilde d_i^\top \tilde d_j\) — this is what sklearn returns; (5) classic pitfall: sklearn uses the smoothed IDF \(\log\frac{1+n}{1+\mathrm{df}}+1\); pass smooth_idf=False to recover the formula from the course. ∎
  2. IDF = PMI. Rederive proposition 2.3 in the cases \(\mathrm{df}(t)=n\) (term everywhere) and \(\mathrm{df}(t)=1\) (unique term); deduce why L2 weighting alone is not enough to squash omnipresent words.
    Solution: \(\mathrm{df}=n \Rightarrow \mathrm{idf}=\log 1=0\) — the term discriminates no document (zero PMI); \(\mathrm{df}=1 \Rightarrow \mathrm{idf}=\log n\) — the term perfectly identifies its document. The L2 norm rescales documents, not columns: a frequent word keeps a high tf in all documents — only IDF (a per-column weight) squashes it. ∎
  3. BPE. Redo example 1.4 with the corpus stage×3, stages×2, stand×4 and list the 5 first merges; what happens if we add stand×40?
    Solution (start): initial pairs (s,t)=9, (t,a)=9, (a,g)=5, (a,n)=4… — the initial (s,t)/(t,a) tie is broken arbitrarily; with stand×40, the pair (a,n) reaches 44 and stand becomes a token before the other roots — this is BPE's lesson: the vocabulary encodes the corpus frequencies, not linguistics. ∎
  4. BM25. Show that the tf factor of BM25 is increasing, bounded by \(k_1+1\), and reaches half its value at \(\mathrm{tf}=k_1(1-b+b|d|/\mathrm{avgdl})\); comment on the effect of \(b=0.9\) on a corpus of documents with widely varying lengths.
    Solution: set \(g(x)=\frac{x(k_1+1)}{x+c}\) with \(c=k_1(1-b+b|d|/\mathrm{avgdl})>0\) — derivative \(g'(x)=\frac{c(k_1+1)}{(x+c)^2}>0\), \(g\to k_1+1\), and \(g(x)=\frac{k_1+1}{2} \Leftrightarrow x=c\). High \(b\): long documents see their \(c\) increase → slower saturation and a stronger penalty — suited when length signals richness; dangerous on heterogeneous mixtures of short/long documents. ∎
  5. Naive Bayes. Derive the MLE estimator of \(\mathbb{P}(t \mid c)\), show the problem of zero probabilities and justify Laplace smoothing as the MAP under Dirichlet(1,…,1) (theorem 5.1); implement and compare against MultinomialNB on 20 Newsgroups; test the effect of α on calibration.
    Solution (core): Lagrangians → relative counts; a test word absent from the class → \(\log 0\): the class is eliminated; the uniform prior adds a "pseudo-count" of 1 to every term → the smoothed formula; α>1 squashes differences (less overconfident), α<1 sharpens them (more discriminative, less calibrated). ∎
  6. Skip-gram. Write the negative sampling loss \(\log \sigma(z_c^\top z_t) + \sum_{k}\mathbb{E}_{w_k \sim p_n}\log \sigma(-z_{w_k}^\top z_t)\); compute the gradient in \(z_t\) and interpret it (who is attracted, who is repelled, with what force); implement on a small corpus and check that words with similar contexts have close embeddings.
    Solution: gradient = \(-\,(1-\sigma_c)\,z_c+\sum_k\sigma_k\,z_{w_k}\) (cf. thm 3.1); the positive is attracted with weight \((1-\sigma)\), the negatives repelled with weight \(\sigma\); check: cosine neighborhood of "graphe" ≈ {réseau, nœud, arête}. ∎
  7. Analogies and bias. With pretrained embeddings (GloVe fr), test 10 analogies and measure the success rate; compute the WEAT statistic on two sets of professions × two lists of first names; implement neutralization (projection removal) and measure the effect on both scores.
  8. LDA. Write the joint law of model 4.1, integrate out \(\theta_d\) and \(\varphi_k\) and recover the Gibbs equation of theorem 4.2 in the case \(K=2\), \(\alpha=\beta=1\) (the multivariate betas reduce to generalized factorials); verify numerically the "rich-get-richer" reinforcement on 200 words.
  9. Viterbi. Implement Viterbi for a toy POS tagger (2 labels, 5-word vocabulary); verify optimality against exhaustive enumeration; complexity \(\mathcal{O}(TK^2)\); modify the proof of thm 6.2 for the case of paths with zero weights.
  10. MinHash/LSH. For \(r=5, b=20\), plot \(\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{detect}}(J)=1-(1-J^r)^b\) and locate the "threshold"; which pair \((r,b)\) at \(m=256\) gives a threshold close to \(J=0.7\)?
    Solution: the sigmoid goes from ~0.1 to ~0.9 between \(J\approx0.45\) and \(J\approx0.65\) — threshold \(\approx(1/b)^{1/r}\approx 0.55\); to target 0.7 with \(m=256\): try \(r=7\) (0.7⁷≈0.082) and \(b=256/7\approx36\); keep the rule of thumb: threshold \(\approx (1/b)^{1/r}\), quality increases with \(m\). ∎
  11. Comparison. On a classification task from your own corpus: Naive Bayes / TF-IDF+SVM / fine-tuned CamemBERT — plot accuracy vs training size (50, 200, 1000, all) and comment on the crossover.
  12. Capstone (project). Carry out the project of ch. 8; each stage must report ITS metric (NPMI coherence for LDA, macro-F1 for classification, entity F1 for NER) and an error analysis on 10 examples.
