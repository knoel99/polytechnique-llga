9. Research of the teaching team

This module is backed by a laboratory (DaSciM, LIX, École Polytechnique — M. Vazirgiannis; LIPN, Sorbonne Paris Nord — D. Buscaldi): here is the actual research of the two instructors, each positioned with respect to the chapters of the course. A methodological lesson: every building block of the course (TF-IDF, clustering, graphs, embeddings) is a living research argument.

9.1. Michalis Vazirgiannis: text mining through graphs

  • Graph-of-Word and TW-IDF — F. Rousseau & M. Vazirgiannis, Graph-of-word and TW-IDF: new approach to ad hoc IR, CIKM 2013 (doi); overview: Graph of Words: Boosting Text Mining Tasks with Graphs, WWW 2017 (doi). The idea: represent each document as a graph of words (nodes = terms, edges = cooccurrence within a window), and replace the tf(t,d) of TF-IDF (ch. 2) with the centrality of the term — the sum of the weights of its edges (weighted degree). The weight captures the local cohesion of the term in the discourse, not just its frequency; TW-IDF improves ad hoc retrieval on reference collections. Position: directly extends ch. 2 — "counting becomes a graph score"; it is also the gateway to the Graph ML module (motifs, communities, k-cores).
  • Keywords by degeneracy — Main Core Retention on Graph-of-Words for Single-Document Keyword Extraction, ECIR 2015 (doi); Tixier, Malliaros & Vazirgiannis, A Graph Degeneracy-based Approach to Keyword Extraction, EMNLP 2016 (aclanthology). k-core decomposition of the graph-of-words: the main core (the maximal subgraph in which every word cooccurs with at least k neighbors) isolates the words that carry the document — an unsupervised, noise-robust method that rivals supervised approaches. Position: the "graph" competitor of RAKE/YAKE in ch. 8; GoWvis demo (ACL 2016, aclanthology); real-time version for conversations: EACL 2017 (aclanthology).
  • Clustering — Giatsidis, Malliaros, Thilikos & Vazirgiannis, CoreCluster: A Degeneracy Based Graph Clustering Framework, AAAI 2014 (doi); Skianis, Rousseau & Vazirgiannis, Regularizing Text Categorization with Clusters of Words, EMNLP 2016 (aclanthology): we cluster the words (communities of the cooccurrence graph) and classify the documents on these clusters — a regularization in small dimension, interpretable, that foreshadows the "topics" logic of ch. 4; Position: ch. 4 and ch. 5 (clustering in the service of classification).
  • Word sense induction by neural clustering — Abdine, Kamal Eddine, Buscaldi & Vazirgiannis, Word Sense Induction with Hierarchical Clustering and Mutual Information Maximization, AI Open 2023 (arXiv:2210.05422) — a paper co-signed by the two instructors of the module: clustering the occurrences of a word by their contextual embeddings (BERT), with a mutual information criterion that is maximized — the exact dual of the "coherence" NPMI of ch. 4; Position: a merger of ch. 3 (embeddings) + ch. 4 (topics/clustering) + ch. 7 (BERT).
  • JuriBERT — Douka, Abdine, Vazirgiannis, El Hamdani & Restrepo Amariles, NLLP@EMNLP 2021 (arXiv:2110.01485): adapting the masked LM to legal French — cf. ch. 7; it paves the way for Buscaldi's work in legal IR (below).
Open extension: TF-IDF × LLMs The TF-IDF of ch. 2 selects the terms that identify a document; a current research question is to weight in the same way the examples shown to an LLM (in-context learning, M2 ch. 4) — the loop "classical weights in the service of modern models" starts from TW-IDF. A possible capstone project topic: comparing TF-IDF, graph-based TF-IDF (TW-IDF) and dense embeddings for ordering the examples of a prompt.

9.2. Davide Buscaldi: QA, legal, sentiment, knowledge graphs

  • Question answering by passage retrieval — Gómez-Soriano, Buscaldi et al., A Multilingual Question Answering System using an n-grams based Passage Retrieval Engine, IICAI 2005, and journal version (Answering questions with an n-gram based passage retrieval engine, JIIS 2011): ranking passages by query-passage n-gram overlap (the direct ancestor of the BM25 scoring of ch. 2), evaluated in multilingual settings (CLEF QA) and on spoken questions (QAST). Position: ch. 2 (n-grams, retrieval) and ch. 8 (the retriever before RAG — the "R" of RAG in M2, ch. 7, has this history).
  • Legal information retrieval — Rosso, Correa & Buscaldi, Passage retrieval in legal texts, Journal of Logic and Algebraic Programming 2011 (doi): retrieving the relevant passages in court decisions — long texts, technical vocabulary, cross-references: exactly the "legal French" difficulties of ch. 8, on the retrieval side (JuriBERT, above, handles them on the model side).
  • Sentiment for crisis management — Buscaldi & Hernandez-Farias, Sentiment Analysis on Microblogs for Natural Disasters Management: a Study on the 2014 Genoa Floodings, WWW Companion 2015 (doi): classifying the emotion of tweets during a flood — classification (ch. 5) on short, noisy text, with subtle classes (worry vs alert); natural follow-up: troll detection (TexTrolls, arXiv:1910.01340).
  • Scientific knowledge graphs — Dessì, Osborne, Reforgiato Recupero, Buscaldi et al., AI-KG (ISWC 2020) and CS-KG (ISWC 2022): NLP pipelines (entity extraction, relation extraction — ch. 6) that turn millions of articles into a knowledge graph; Position: the exact outlet of the NER of ch. 6; these graphs feed the GraphRAG of the Graph ML module; the group's LLM-KG article series connects the module to M2.
  • In the LLM era: detection and memorization — with Liyanage & Nazarenko, a benchmark for detecting generated academic text (LREC 2022, arXiv:2202.02013); with Dentan, Shabou & Vanier: Predicting memorization within fine-tuned Large Language Models (ECAI 2025, arXiv:2409.18858) and the multilingual hallucination benchmark MUCH (LREC 2026, arXiv:2511.17081). Position: this research structures the "evaluation and safety" ch. 9 of the M2 LLMs module — the pedagogical continuity of the master's program.
Common thread Vazirgiannis: text as a graph (ch. 2, 4, 8 → Graph ML, GraphRAG); Buscaldi: finding information and making it reliable (ch. 2, 5, 8 → RAG, M2 safety). Their joint paper (WSI) is the crossroads: contextual embeddings + clustering + mutual information — that is, ch. 3, 4 and 7 of this module.
