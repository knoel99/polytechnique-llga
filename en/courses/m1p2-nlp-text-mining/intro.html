Introduction and overview

ModuleIntroduction to Text Mining and NLP
CodeCSC_52082_EP
Team and workloadInstructors: Michalis Vazirgiannis (École Polytechnique) & Davide Buscaldi (Université Sorbonne Paris Nord) · 36 h · 4.5 ECTS · from raw text to transformers: tokenization, TF-IDF and BM25, embeddings, topic models, sequences, fine-tuning, industrial text mining — and the research of the teaching team. Extended in M2 by the Large Language Models module.
Status in the programMandatory

Text is the most widespread form of unstructured data: documents, queries, reviews, conversations, code. This module covers the entire NLP processing pipeline: tokenization and bag of words, TF-IDF and BM25, word embeddings, topic models, text classification, sequences (NER, HMM, CRF), up to transformers and industrial text mining. Mandatory in P2, it is the foundation on which the M2 module Large Language Models will graft large language models.

What you will be able to do

  • Build a complete text processing pipeline, from raw corpus to usable features.
  • Represent documents with bag of words, TF-IDF and BM25, and build a search engine on these foundations.
  • Train and interpret word embeddings.
  • Infer latent topics in a corpus with topic models.
  • Classify texts, from Naive Bayes to transformer fine-tuning.
  • Label sequences (named entity recognition) with HMMs and CRFs.
  • Fine-tune a transformer for a given NLP task.
  • Conduct a text mining project in an industrial context, from data to production deployment.

Where this course fits in the program

Mandatory module of period 2 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), taught by Michalis Vazirgiannis and Davide Buscaldi. It provides the vocabulary and reflexes without which the M2 large language models would remain black boxes.

  • Prerequisites — Machine Learning (Naive Bayes for classification, the EM algorithm reused by topic models) and Deep Learning (the transformer architecture, deepened here for NLP).
  • Downstream modules — it directly prepares Large Language Models (M2), its natural extension; the same processing pipeline applied to speech is found in Speech Technology for Conversational AI (M2); and in the same period, the module Graph Machine and Deep Learning for Generative AI crosses over with this material (a document can become a graph of words, and its embeddings join node embeddings).

Course outline

  • Review: vector spaces and probabilities — the two lenses of the course: representing a document as a vector, reasoning in terms of laws and likelihood.
  • The text processing pipeline — segmentation, cleaning, normalization: the decisive craft before any model.
  • Bag of words, TF-IDF and BM25 — the classical representations and the scoring function that powers information retrieval.
  • Word embeddings — moving from counting to prediction: word vectors that carry meaning.
  • Topic models — discovering the latent topics of a corpus, with EM as the engine.
  • Text classification — from Naive Bayes to neural models: the reference task of NLP.
  • Sequences: NER, HMM, CRF — labeling named entities with structured sequential models.
  • Transformers for NLP — attention, fine-tuning, and the shift toward large pretrained models.
  • Industrial text mining — real-world constraints: volumes, annotation budgets, deployment and monitoring.
  • Research of the teaching team — a tour of the research work of Vazirgiannis and Buscaldi.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Work through the review section: dot products and conditional probabilities are used explicitly in every subsequent chapter.
  • Once, build a bag of words and a TF-IDF score yourself on a mini-corpus: everything that follows is understood by contrast with this baseline.
  • Before each model, write down on paper what it assumes (independence, word order, latency): this is what enables practical choices.
  • Hand-label a few sentences for a NER task: you will measure the cost of annotation, the central constraint of industrial NLP.
  • Read the transformers chapter in parallel with the attention review of the Deep Learning module: the two reinforce each other.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next; they prepare the graded assignments and the bridge to the LLMs module.
The LLGA positioning NLP is the first of the master's two tracks, and this module lays its foundations up to attention: the module Large Language Models (M2) is its direct continuation, while the graph of words makes the junction with the graph track of Graph ML. Information retrieval, monitoring, assistants, industrial classification: text mining skills are among the most sought after on the market as in research.
