Exercises

  1. Algebra and SQL (solved). On the database of Example 1.2, express in algebra then in SQL: (a) the majors of the students enrolled in the DB course; (b) the students enrolled in all courses of at least 4 credits; (c) the courses with no enrollee. Solution. (a) \(\pi_{\texttt{major}}(\texttt{Student} \Join \sigma_{\texttt{code}=DB}(\texttt{Enrolled}))\) — SQL: join + WHERE. (b) division: \(\pi_{\texttt{id}}(\texttt{Enrolled}) \div \pi_{\texttt{code}}(\sigma_{\texttt{credits}\geq4}(\texttt{Course}))\) then join with Student; in SQL, NOT EXISTS (SELECT … FROM Course c WHERE credits >= 4 AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT … FROM Enrolled i WHERE i.id = e.id AND i.code = c.code)) — the double negation of Prop. 2.2, applied to the division of ch. 1. (c) \(\pi_{\texttt{code}}(\texttt{Course}) - \pi_{\texttt{code}}(\texttt{Enrolled})\), SQL: NOT EXISTS or LEFT JOIN … WHERE i.id IS NULL — the anti-join.
  2. Closure and 3NF (solved). \(R(A,B,C,D,E)\), \(F = \{A \to B,\; BC \to D,\; D \to E\}\). (a) Compute the keys. (b) Is the relation in 3NF? (c) Decompose into 3NF without loss and verify each step with Theorem 3.5. Solution. (a) \((AC)^{+}\): \(A\) gives \(B\), \(ABC\) gives \(D\), \(D\) gives \(E\) → everything: \(AC\) is a key (\(A^{+} = AB\), \(C^{+} = C\): neither \(A\) nor \(C\) alone). Prime attributes: \(A, C\). (b) \(D \to E\): \(D\) is not a superkey (\(D^{+} = DE\)) and \(E\) is not prime → not 3NF (2NF yes: no FD from a strict subset of the key). (c) By 3NF synthesis (minimal cover \(\{A\to B, BC\to D, D\to E\}\), one schema per determinant, plus a key): \((AB, BCD, DE, AC)\) — lossless and FD-preserving. The "by hand" decomposition \((ABCD, DE)\) then \((AB, ACD)\) preserves \(\{A\to B\}\) but loses \(BC \to D\) (check with Thm 3.5: the intersection \(B\) determines nothing): synthesis avoids precisely this pitfall.
  3. B+ by hand (solved). Insert in order 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 into a B+ tree of order \(d=1\); give the final tree and its height (same conventions as Example 5.4: when a leaf \([a,b,c]\) splits, the copied separator is \(c\), the first key of the right leaf). Solution. 2 then 4: leaf [2,4]. The 6 overflows [2,4,6] → [2,4] / [6], copy of 6: root [6]. The 8: leaf [6,8]. The 10: [6,8,10] → [6,8] / [10], copy of 10: root [6,10], leaves [2,4] [6,8] [10]. The 12: leaf [10,12]. The 14: [10,12,14] → [10,12] / [14], copy of 14: the root [6,10,14] overflows (3 keys); internal split: the median key 10 is moved to the new root, internal children [6] and [14]. The 14 already placed: final tree 
                  [10]
          ┌──────┴──────┐
        [ 6 ]         [ 14 ]
      ┌──┴──┐        ┌──┴──┐
    [2,4] [6,8]   [10,12] [14]
    Height: 3. Check: all leaves at the same level, 1 ≤ keys ≤ 2 everywhere — the invariants of Definition 5.1 hold at every step.
  4. Serializability (solved). Let \(H = r_1(X)\, r_2(X)\, w_1(X)\, w_2(X)\, r_1(Y)\, w_1(Y)\, c_1\, c_2\). Build \(SG(H)\); is \(H\) serializable? recoverable? What would 2PL do? Solution. Conflicts on X: \(r_1<w_2\) (T1→T2), \(r_2<w_1\) (T2→T1), \(w_1<w_2\) (T1→T2). On Y: T2 does not write Y. Graph: T1↔T2 → cycle → not serializable (Thm 6.2). Recoverable? T2 reads X (original value) then writes X after \(w_1\): T2 reads nothing written by T1 → yes, recoverable — but with a lost update. Under 2PL, \(T_1\) keeping its X lock until its lock point (after \(w_1(Y)\)), \(w_2(X)\) waits: the forbidden interleaving is simply impossible — Theorem 6.5 in action.
  5. External sort. \(N = 2\times10^5\) pages, \(M = 500\). Compute the initial runs, passes, total I/O cost; how long on a disk at 20,000 pages/s? Hint. 400 runs, fan-in 499 → \(\lceil\log_{499} 400\rceil = 1\), \(p = 2\), cost \(2Np = 8\times10^5\) pages → 40 s.
  6. Plan choice. Join \(R \Join S\) with \(B_R = 200\), \(B_S = 10\,000\), \(M = 52\), a selection on \(R\) of selectivity \(1/100\) that can be pushed before the join. Compare: sort-merge on \((R,S)\); hashing; and the order \(S \Join \sigma(R)\) in block nested loops. Hint. \(\sigma(R)\) fits in RAM (2 pages); NLJ with \(R\) filtered on the outside: \(2 + 1\times10\,000\) … the index changes everything: think "small relation in memory = outer loop".
  7. SQLite recursion. Write in recursive SQL: (a) the degree (distance) of separation of each pair of users of a network (table follow(a,b)); (b) the connected components of an undirected graph. Hint. (a) propagate the length by accumulating and take the MIN per pair (window or GROUP BY); (b) propagate the MIN of reached identifiers up to the fixed point.
  8. Vectors: sizing. \(10^7\) embeddings in \(d = 768\) (float32): memory size? Number of multiplications for an exact k-NN? With IVF \(n_{list} = 4096\), \(n_{probe} = 32\) (balanced cells): comparative work performed, and gain? Hint. 30.7 GB; \(7.68\times10^{10}\) products; IVF: \(10^7/4096 \times 32 \approx 78\,000\) vectors probed + distances to the centroids, i.e. ~1% of the exact work, at the price of recall.
  9. Open design. A journalist receives 5 CSV files (elections, demographics, funding, companies, press). Propose an architecture to cross-check and trace the numerical claims of an article: target schema, indexes, provenance, typical queries (reread ch. 10-11).
