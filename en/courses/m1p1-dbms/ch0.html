0. Review: sets, relations, complexity

L3 review material used
  • Sets and Cartesian products: \(A_1 \times \cdots \times A_k\), cardinality of the product \(|A_1 \times A_2| = |A_1|\cdot|A_2|\), subsets, differences; a relation in the database sense is a subset of a Cartesian product;
  • Binary relations: reflexivity, transitivity, transitive closure — the hidden tool behind the theory of functional dependencies (ch. 3) and recursive queries (ch. 2);
  • First-order logic: quantifiers \(\forall, \exists\), negation of quantifiers (\(\neg\forall x\, P \equiv \exists x\, \neg P\)) — SQL is nothing but an evaluable fragment of this logic;
  • Complexity: notations \(O, \Theta, \Omega\), logarithms (\(\log_b x = \ln x / \ln b\)), average complexity of hash tables (L3 algorithms review), sorting and merging;
  • A bit of probability: uniform draws, expectation, the "\(n\) balls into \(m\) bins" paradigm used in ch. 5 (hashing).
Proposition 0.1 — Sizes of products and projections Let \(R \subseteq A \times B\) be a finite binary relation. Then $$|\pi_A(R)| \leq |R| \leq |\pi_A(R)| \cdot |\pi_B(R)|,$$ where \(\pi_A\) denotes the projection (the set of values of \(A\) actually present). The left bound is attained as soon as one value of \(A\) appears in several pairs; the right one is attained for the complete product \(R = \pi_A(R) \times \pi_B(R)\). These bounds, trivial in appearance, are the foundation of all the cardinality estimates of chapter 8: an optimizer never knows the exact content of the tables, only statistics (number of distinct values, histograms), and must bracket the sizes of intermediate results in order to choose an execution plan.
Note — Why this module in an LLGA master's program? Every model lives on data: extracting it, cleaning it, integrating it, indexing it, then serving models in production (vector search). This module covers the layer beneath ML: where the Machine Learning module (ch. 1) assumes clean tables, we explain where they come from. Links given along the way: Graph ML (ch. 9), LLMs (ch. 9-10), NLP (ch. 10).
