10. Research of the instructor

Guokan Shang (lead research scientist, MBZUAI; PhD at the LIX laboratory, École Polytechnique, 2021, under the supervision of M. Vazirgiannis — the LLGA loop) works on the evaluation of generative models, efficient pre-training, multilingual LLMs and dialogue summarization. Three axes presented in detail, then the rest of the filmography, each positioned relative to the chapters.

Axis 1 — Frugal evaluation: FrugalScore (ACL 2022) Kamal Eddine, Shang, Tixier & Vazirgiannis, FrugalScore: Learning Cheaper, Lighter and Faster Evaluation Metrics for Automatic Text Generation — arXiv:2110.08559. Problem: neural generation metrics (BERTScore, MoverScore…) cost one large-model forward per pair — prohibitive in a learning or LLM-judge evaluation loop. Method: distill the reference metric (the scores, not the texts!) into a tiny student (T5-small), with an input representation compatible with the mother metric. Result: correlation nearly preserved for a cost reduced by a factor of up to ~700× (plus pre-trained V2 variants). Position: the "evaluation at an industrializable price" link of ch. 9 — evaluate often, at low cost, before evaluating well (heavy judges) on a subsample.
Axis 2 — Curriculum in pre-training: Beyond Random Sampling (EACL 2026) Zhang, Mohamed, Abdine, Shang & Vazirgiannis, Beyond Random Sampling: Efficient Language Model Pretraining via Curriculum Learning — arXiv:2506.11300. Question: does the order of pre-training data (ch. 3) matter, at constant compute? Method: first systematic large-scale study (several hundred models, from 50M to 1M parameters): curriculum by difficulty (perplexity, quality) easy→hard vs random mixtures. Result: the curriculum improves the loss at fixed budget (better compute efficiency), with granularity choices that matter. Position: the "curriculum & annealing" content of ch. 3 builds on this paper — an example of an engineering question (in what order to feed the model?) turned into measurable science.
Axis 3 — LLMs for low-resource languages: Atlas-Chat & Nile-Chat (2024-2025) Shang et al., Atlas-Chat: Adapting Large Language Models for Low-Resource Moroccan Arabic Dialect — arXiv:2409.17912 (1B/2B/3B models on 223B tokens of dialect; templates, SFT + DPO); Nile-Chat: Egyptian Language Models for Arabic and Latin Scripts — arXiv:2507.04569 (handling arabizi — Arabic in Latin script — at the tokenizer and pre-training level). Position: ch. 1 (multilingual costs, vocabulary) and ch. 4 (SFT/DPO) applied to a real problem: adapting the LLM ecosystem to languages/scripts that dominant vocabularies penalize — the logical continuation of the extra-cost measurements done in the lab.
The rest of the filmography (with positioning)
  • Meeting summarization (the PhD thread): unsupervised abstractive summarization by multi-sentence compression + budgeted submodular maximization (ACL 2018 — the "graphical text mining" of M1 NLP ch. 8); self-attentive abstractive communities (AACL 2020); speaker-change-aware CRF for dialogue acts (COLING 2020 — the CRFs of M1 NLP ch. 6); TACL 2023 survey (arXiv:2208.04163); French corpora FREDSum (political debates) and Claire (spontaneous French dialogue); back to extraction: discourse and extractive summarization (2024);
  • LLM faithfulness and diversity: "LLM as a Broken Telephone" (ACL 2025, arXiv:2502.20258) — iterative generation distorts information (ch. 9); benchmark of linguistic diversity of LLMs (TACL 2025); Markov generation chains (2026, arXiv:2603.11228);
  • Frugal RLVR: "Shorter but not Worse" (2025, arXiv:2511.01937) — regularizing the length of math RLVR reasonings (ch. 5);
  • Graphs & LLMs: graph linearization methods for reasoning with an LLM (ch. 7 — GraphRAG; bridge toward the Graph ML/Advanced GNN modules).
