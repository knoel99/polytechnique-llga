Exercises / Labs

  1. Multilingual tokenization. Compare BPE vs Unigram vs SentencePiece on a multilingual corpus: compression (characters/token), coverage of numbers; measure the French "extra cost" over English for the same text.
    Guidance (expected results): aligned professional translations (fr/en/de) → tokens/word; typically fr ≈ 1.15-1.25× en; German with compounds even more — until frequent compounds become tokens; check that o200k (GPT-4o) narrows the gap vs cl100k. Conclude on the real cost (context, API price) of the same application depending on the language. ∎
  2. RoPE. Prove the relative property of thm 2.3 (\(R(a)^\top R(b) = R(b-a)\)), then check numerically that the attention score is invariant under a common translation of positions; implement context interpolation \(m\to m/L\) and measure perplexity beyond the training length.
    Guidance: 2D rotations = complex numbers — commutativity and \(e^{-ia}e^{ib}=e^{i(b-a)}\); translation invariance follows from the theorem; in practice naive extrapolation degrades PPL, interpolation largely preserves it (this is the basis of extended contexts 32k→128k without full retraining). ∎
  3. KV-cache arithmetic. For Llama-3-8B (32 layers, 8 KV heads, 128 dim) and a 128k-token context: compute the cache memory in fp16, then in int8, then with MQA; what maximum batch fits on an 80 GB GPU (int4 weights ≈ 5 GB)?
    Guidance: 128 KiB/token (prop. 2.4) → 128k tokens = 16 GiB; int8 → 8 GiB; MQA (1 KV head) → 2 GiB; available budget ≈ 80−5 = 75 GiB → batch of ~4 (fp16) to ~37 (MQA int8): GQA/MQA and cache quantization determine the served throughput. ∎
  4. Scaling. From published data (LLaMA 1/2/3, Chinchilla), verify the law \(\mathcal{L} \approx A N^{-\alpha} + B D^{-\beta} + E\) by regression; situate each model relative to the compute optimum (thm 3.1).
  5. MoE. Derive the minimum of the auxiliary loss \(\alpha E\sum_i f_iP_i\) under the constraint \(\sum_i f_i=1\) and show it corresponds to uniform routing; simulate the collapse (biased initialization of the router) without then with the loss.
    Guidance: at router equilibrium, \(P_i=f_i\); minimize \(E\sum f_i^2\) under \(\sum f_i=1\) → Lagrange → uniform \(f_i=1/E\); without the loss, the winning expert attracts tokens (reinforced feedback) and specialization degenerates. ∎
  6. Guided SFT. Fine-tune a small instructed model (1-3B) on 500 (question, answer) pairs from your domain: strict template, masked labels (code of ch. 4), 2-3 epochs; measure the effect of a wrong template at test time (a spectacular drop to witness once and for all).
  7. DPO on paper. Derive the closed form \(\pi^\star\) of the KL-penalized problem (classical calculus of variations), then the DPO loss by re-injecting into Bradley-Terry; check the gradient: it increases the log-ratio margin of the preferred responses.
    Guidance: follow the proof of thm 5.2 step by step — the key argument is that \(\mathcal{J}=-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}e^{r/\beta}/Z)+\beta\log Z\); then \(Z(x)\) disappears in the difference \(r^+-r^-\). The gradient pushes \(\log\pi(y^+)-\log\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y^+)\) up and the \(y^-\) down, modulated by \(\sigma(-\hat r)\) (hard pairs first). ∎
  8. Toy GRPO. On a verifiable task (binary arithmetic), train a mini-model with GRPO (group of 8 responses); plot accuracy and response length — observe the emergence of longer "reasoning"; compare gradient variance with/without normalization by the group standard deviation.
  9. Speculative, on paper. Write out completely the proof of thm 6.3 in the case \(\gamma=2\), then estimate the expected latency gain if \(q=p\) on 80% of the mass but diverges on the rest.
    Guidance: accept token 1 (probability \(\sum_y\min(q,p)\)), then the second conditionally; the expected number of accepted tokens per round is \(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\) (geometric) with \(\alpha\approx0.8\) → ~5 tokens/forward; gain bounded by \(\gamma\) and by the small/large model cost ratio. ∎
  10. RAG vs context length. Query an LLM with 4k/32k/128k of context over a corpus: does the "lost in the middle" effect appear? Compare with RAG; discuss the trade-offs (cost, faithfulness, indexing overhead).
  11. Watermark. With \(\gamma=0.5\) and \(T=400\) tokens, compute \(z\) for a text carrying 230 green tokens; threshold at 5%; how many tokens are needed to detect a +5% green bias?
    Guidance: \(z=(230-200)/10 = 3\) → p ≈ 0.1%: rejected (marked); for a small bias \(\varepsilon\), the expectation of \(z\) grows like \(\varepsilon\sqrt{T/\gamma(1-\gamma)}\) → one needs \(T\sim (1.96/\varepsilon)^2\gamma(1-\gamma)\); with \(\varepsilon=0.05\), ~770 tokens — hence the difficulty on short texts. ∎
  12. Agent project. Extend the final lab: an agent that chooses between vector retrieval and a graph query depending on the question type (relational or factual) — measure the improvement and the extra cost.
