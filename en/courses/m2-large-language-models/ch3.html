3. Pre-training

Pre-training objectives
  • Causal modeling (CLM) — GPT: $$\mathcal{L} = -\sum_t \log p_\theta(x_t \mid x_{<t})$$ — predict the next token; generative by construction;
  • Masked modeling / denoising (MLM) — BERT (NLP ch. 7): mask ~15% and predict (bidirectional); excellent for encoding (classification, retrieval), does not generate;
  • Span corruption / text-to-text — T5: mask spans and regenerate them; everything is formulated as sequence→sequence.

3.1. The data pipeline: quality, filtering, deduplication

  • Curation: web → rule-based filtering (length, code/punctuation ratio, languages), then model-based filtering (quality classifiers trained on reference pages — e.g. distilled judges); controlled mixture (web, code, books, articles, mathematics, synthetic data generated then filtered);
  • MinHash-LSH deduplication (proved in NLP ch. 8.3): k-gram shingles → MinHash signatures (a min-hash collision has probability equal to the Jaccard similarity) → LSH bands/ranks with \(\mathbb{P}_{\text{detect}}(J)=1-(1-J^r)^b\) → remove near-duplicates down to a threshold. At scale: tens of TB of text, approximately linear clustering; measured effect: less memorization, better benchmark (deduplicating is regularizing — LLMs learn duplicates "twice");
  • Mixtures and curriculum: per-domain proportions (annealing: raise quality/lower web at the end of training); easy→hard ordering (by perplexity) improves compute efficiency — demonstrated at scale by the instructor (ch. 10, "Beyond Random Sampling");
  • Multilingual: balancing per language (otherwise the tokenizer and the model drift toward English — cf. ch. 1 and 10).

3.2. Scaling laws: decomposition and the Chinchilla optimum

Combined law (Hoffmann et al. 2022, "Chinchilla") The loss of a model with \(N\) parameters trained on \(D\) tokens follows, over decades of scale: $$L(N,D) = E + \frac{A}{N^{\alpha}} + \frac{B}{D^{\beta}},$$ \(E\) = irreducible entropy of natural text; the other two terms = under-capacity and exhausted data (fitted on ~400 training runs; Kaplan et al. 2020 for the \(L(N)\)-only version). Reading: one must grow N and D together.
Theorem 3.1 — The compute optimum \(D^\star \approx 20\,N\), derived At fixed budget \(C\), with \(\mathrm{FLOPs}(C) \approx 6ND\) (2 per parameter × forward + backward, × D tokens): $$(N^\star, D^\star) = \arg\min_{N,D}\; L(N,D)\quad \text{s.t.}\quad 6ND = C.$$
Lagrangian \(\mathcal{J}=E+AN^{-\alpha}+BD^{-\beta}-\lambda(6ND-C)\): $$\frac{\partial\mathcal{J}}{\partial N}= -\alpha A N^{-\alpha-1}-6\lambda D=0, \qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal{J}}{\partial D}= -\beta B D^{-\beta-1}-6\lambda N=0.$$ Dividing side by side, \(\lambda\) cancels: $$\frac{\alpha A\,N^{-\alpha-1}}{\beta B\,D^{-\beta-1}} = \frac{D}{N} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\alpha A}{\beta B}\,D^{\beta} = N^{\alpha} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{N^{\alpha}}{D^{\beta}} = \frac{\alpha A}{\beta B}.$$ With Chinchilla's fitted constants (\(\alpha\approx0.34\), \(\beta\approx0.28\), \(A\approx406\), \(B\approx411\)): the right-hand side equals \(\approx 1.2\), and the condition together with \(6ND=C\) gives \(N^\star\propto C^{0.46}\), \(D^\star\propto C^{0.54}\) — both grow like \(\sqrt{C}\), and numerically \(D^\star/N^\star\approx 20\) tokens per parameter. GPT-3 (300B tokens for 175B params, ratio 1.7) was therefore "undertrained" at its budget. ∎
Compute and real costs (Llama 3, 2024) Orders of magnitude from the Llama 3 technical report (H100 training): 8B / 15T tokens ≈ 1.5M GPU-hours; 70B ≈ 7M; 405B / 15.6T tokens ≈ 30.8M GPU-hours (≈ 3.5 years of a single H100 × 10⁴). At ~2-3 $/GPU-hour, the 405B costs ~80-90M$ of compute — why one "continues" from checkpoints rather than retraining (ch. 6: distillation, LoRA); and why the post-Chinchilla ratio went up: since inference costs daily, one deliberately overtrains small models (Llama-3-8B: 15T tokens ≈ 1900 tokens per parameter, ~100× Chinchilla) — the optimum depends on what is being optimized: the training cost (Chinchilla) or the total cost including inference.
Lab: training a mini-GPT On a Shakespeare-type corpus (~1 MB), train a ~10M-parameter transformer (nanoGPT): 30 minutes of GPU suffice to generate credible text. Observe: the loss curve, the effect of context, the sampling temperature (ch. 6). Extend: do exercise 4 (check the law \(L(N,D)\) by regression on the published points).
