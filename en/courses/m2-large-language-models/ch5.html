5. RLHF, DPO, GRPO

RLHF — the classic pipeline (Ouyang et al., 2022)
  1. Reward model: train \(r_\phi(x, y)\) on human preferences (response pairs); Bradley-Terry loss: $$\mathcal{L} = -\log \sigma\big(r_\phi(x, y^+) - r_\phi(x, y^-)\big).$$
  2. Policy optimization with PPO (RL module, ch. 4): $$\max_\theta\ \mathbb{E}\big[r_\phi(x, y)\big] - \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_\theta \| \pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}\big)$$ — the KL term prevents reward hacking and out-of-distribution drift.
Proposition 5.1 — Bradley-Terry, derived If humans compare pairs \((y^+,y^-)\) with a probability of preferring \(y^+\) increasing in the "strength" difference, the axiomatics (transitive binary choice, invariance) imposes a logistic model on the difference of latent scores: $$\mathbb{P}(y^+ \succ y^- \mid x) = \sigma\big(r(x,y^+)-r(x,y^-)\big),$$ and the log-likelihood of the observed preferences gives the loss above — only the difference of rewards is identifiable (adding a function of \(x\) changes nothing); hence the need for the KL penalty to pin down the zero.
Derivative in \(r^+\): \(-\big(1-\sigma(r^+-r^-)\big)\) — the penalty is maximal when the pair is misclassified, zero when the margin is amply positive; it is a logistic regression on reward differences. ∎
PPO for LLMs — the concrete protocol Four models in memory: policy \(\pi_\theta\), reference \(\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}\) (frozen, for the KL), reward \(r_\phi\) (frozen), critic (value head). Sensitive hyperparameters: \(\beta\in[0.01,\,0.2]\); low learning rate (≈ 10× smaller than in SFT); PPO clip \(\epsilon\sim0.2\); prompt batch > completion batch. Classic symptoms: KL explodes (β too small); degenerate responses (hacked reward); drifting length (RM verbosity bias). This is chapter 4 of the RL module applied word for word — the prerequisite reading.
Theorem 5.2 — DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023): complete derivation The loss $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\log \sigma\Big(\beta \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y^+ \mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y^+\mid x)} - \beta \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y^- \mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y^-\mid x)}\Big)$$ optimizes the policy directly on preferences — without a reward model or rollouts.
Step 1 (closed-form solution of the KL-penalized problem). For fixed \(x\), maximizing \(\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}[r(y)] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}})\) rewrites as $$\max_\pi \int \pi(y)\Big[r(y) - \beta\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y)}\Big]dy = -\beta\int \pi\log\frac{\pi(y)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y)e^{r(y)/\beta}}\,dy,$$ which is \(-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi\,\big\|\,\tfrac{1}{Z}\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}e^{r/\beta}\big)+\beta\log Z\); the KL is minimal at zero, so \(\;\pi^\star(y\mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)}\,\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y\mid x)\,e^{r(x,y)/\beta}\). Step 2 (implicit reward). Inverting: \(r(x,y) = \beta\log\frac{\pi^\star(y|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y|x)} + \beta\log Z(x)\). Step 3 (injection into Bradley-Terry). In \(\sigma(r^+-r^-)\), the term \(Z(x)\) — the same for \(y^+\) and \(y^-\) — cancels: only the difference of log-ratios matters; maximizing the likelihood of the preferences gives \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}\) above (with \(\theta\) in place of \(\pi^\star\)). Gradient: \(-\beta\,\sigma(-\hat r)\,\nabla_\theta\big[\log\pi(y^+|x)-\log\pi(y^-|x)\big]\) with implicit reward \(\hat r = \beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^+)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y^+)} -\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y^-)}{\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}}(y^-)}\): the log-ratio margin of the preferred responses is increased, all the more strongly as the pair is misclassified. ∎
Theorem 5.3 — GRPO (Shao et al., DeepSeekMath 2024): derivation Generate a group of \(G\) responses \(\{y_i\}\) per prompt; rewards \(\{r_i\}\); advantages within the group: $$A_i = \frac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(r_{1..G})}{\mathrm{std}(r_{1..G})},\qquad \nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{G}\sum_i \frac{1}{|y_i|}\sum_t \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(y_{i,t}\mid x)\,A_i\Big] - \beta\,\nabla_\theta \widehat{\mathrm{KL}},$$ with \(\widehat{\mathrm{KL}}\) an unbiased, low-variance estimator \(\;\mathbb{E}\big[\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\pi_\theta}-\log\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\pi_\theta}-1\big]\).
Variance. REINFORCE: \(\nabla\mathbb{E}[r]=\mathbb{E}[r\,\nabla\log\pi]\) is unbiased for any baseline independent of the action: \(\mathbb{E}[b\,\nabla\log\pi]=b\,\nabla\!\int\pi=0\); subtracting \(b=\mathrm{mean}(r)\) divides the variance (better than \(b=0\) or a learned baseline); normalizing by the standard deviation makes the gradient scale invariant across prompts (easy ones: small spreads → amplified; hard ones: damped). No more critic. Unlike PPO, the baseline comes from the group itself — the value network (and its memory/training) disappears; it is the Monte-Carlo estimation of \(A(s,a)=Q(s,a)-V(s)\) by multiple sampling. ∎
RLVR and reasoning (2024-2026) When quality is verifiable (a passing unit test, an exact mathematical answer), the reward no longer needs to be learned: RL with Verifiable Rewards. GRPO is the engine of DeepSeek-R1: by optimizing verifiable correctness, long chains of thought emerge (self-verification, backtracking, "aha" moments) — the "o1" style. Self-consistency at inference (Wang et al., 2023): sample \(n\) reasonings, answer by majority vote — accuracy climbing at \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\)-ish as long as errors are weakly correlated. Costs: 10× more tokens; recent works regularize length (ch. 10: "Shorter but not Worse"). Exploration (bandits, RL module ch. 6) becomes central: the GRPO group is structured exploration.
Overview SFT (imitate) → RLHF (optimize preferences) → DPO (preferences without RL) → RLVR/GRPO (optimize verifiable truth). A "modern" LLM chains: base → SFT → rejection sampling → DPO/GRPO; this is exactly the plan of the official course ("post-training methods"). In the lab: toy DPO (exercise 7) then GRPO (exercise 8).
