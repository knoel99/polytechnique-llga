Introduction and overview

ModuleLarge Language Models
CodeCSC_53432_EP

This module is the M2's LLM pillar: it covers the entire large language model pipeline — tokenization, architecture, pre-training and scaling laws, post-training (SFT, RLHF, DPO, GRPO), inference, RAG, agents and multimodality, up to evaluation and safety. The theory + labs format takes you from LLM user to developer who understands the pipeline, adapts it and deploys it. Most of the other M2 modules plug into it. Official 24 h volume: this material deepens that description, chapter by chapter, and explicitly flags its additions beyond the official syllabus as deepenings.

What you will be able to do

  • Explain the complete pipeline for building an LLM, from tokenization to post-training.
  • Implement alignment methods — SFT, RLHF, DPO, GRPO — and discuss their trade-offs.
  • Run an LLM in inference and master its costs and limits.
  • Build an in-depth RAG, beyond the basic tutorial.
  • Explore agents, multimodality and the perspectives they open.
  • Evaluate the performance and safety of an LLM with rigorous protocols.
  • Find your way in the literature and the instructor's research work.

Official objectives and syllabus

Official description (master's wiki): the SynapseS 2026-2027 sheet of this course unit is not yet published (the S3/S4 tracks of the catalog are currently empty); the description confirmed below is that of the wiki, which specifies the volume (24 h), credits (2 ECTS) and instructor. An immersion into LLMs, mixing essential theory and hands-on work to develop both practical skills and conceptual understanding — with a view to the LLM development and deployment professions: the syllabus starts with a brief panorama of historical NLP techniques, then addresses the transformer architecture (attention mechanism, tokenization) — the heart of modern LLMs —, the pre-training objectives (masked/denoising and causal language modeling, foundations of BERT, GPT and T5), the post-training techniques that refine a pre-trained model — instruction tuning (SFT), reinforcement learning from human feedback (PPO/DPO) and from verifiable rewards (GRPO) — and, finally, applications and future directions: RAG, agents, multimodality, alternative architectures. Where this course fits in the program

An M2 module (period 1), 24 h for 2 ECTS, M2 badge. Three M1 courses prepare it: Introduction to Text Mining and NLP for text processing, Deep Learning for the transformer architecture, and Reinforcement Learning and Autonomous Agents whose PPO sheds light on the RLHF and GRPO of the alignment chapter. Downstream, it is the gateway to Advanced Topics in Large Language Models, Explainability, Security, Privacy of LLMs, ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles and Speech Technology for Conversational AI.

Course outline

  • Background: from word2vec to transformers — the trajectory leading from word embeddings to the architecture at the center of the module.
  • Tokenization — how text becomes a sequence of tokens, and why this choice matters so much.
  • The architecture in depth — the transformer dissected: attention, blocks, variants and implementation details.
  • Pre-training — objectives, data and costs of the model's founding stage.
  • Post-training: SFT and alignment — going from the raw language model to the usable assistant.
  • RLHF, DPO, GRPO — reinforcement learning methods and their alternatives for aligning models.
  • Running an LLM: inference — serving a model: constraints, optimizations and costs.
  • RAG in depth — plugging models into external knowledge, seriously.
  • Agents, multimodality and perspectives — LLMs that act, see and listen.
  • Evaluation and safety of LLMs — measuring what models really do, and containing their risks.
  • Research of the instructor — the papers of Guokan Shang, read with the module's grid.
  • Exercises / Labs — hands-on sessions that follow each stage of the pipeline.
  • References — the papers and sources of each chapter.

How to study this module

  • Do the labs alongside the chapters: the LLM pipeline is understood hands-on.
  • Keep the full pipeline in mind — tokenization, pre-training, alignment, inference — and place each new development in it.
  • Review the M1 reinforcement learning module before the RLHF chapter: PPO is assumed known there.
  • Read the key papers as you go: each chapter has one or two that sum it up.
  • For each method, ask what it costs in compute and data: that is often what decides in practice.
  • Test evaluation concepts on your own model outputs: measuring is harder than it looks.
