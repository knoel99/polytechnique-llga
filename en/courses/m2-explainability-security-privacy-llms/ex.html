Exercises / Labs

  1. Probes and selectivity (ch. 1). Train linear probes (language, sentiment, POS category) on every layer of a GPT-2; plot the accuracy per layer; add the control tasks of Hewitt & Liang and report the selectivity; conclusion on "is the information linearly encoded?".
  2. Home-grown tuned lens (ch. 2). Implement the logit lens on GPT-2 (final LayerNorm + unembedding at every layer); train the affine transformations of the tuned lens per layer (regression toward the final distribution); visualize the trajectory of the top token on 50 examples — identify the "late switches".
  3. Attention under control (ch. 2). Reproduce the experiment of Jain & Wallace on a small model: show that an adversarial (permuted) attention distribution preserves the prediction; then show with patching that an identified induction head is causally necessary for pattern completion. Two conclusions, one method.
  4. SAE on a toy model (ch. 3). Train the toy autoencoder with 5 features in 2 dimensions (variable rarities); observe the phase transitions; then train an SAE (\(m = 8d\)) on the activations of a layer of a small LM; annotate the 20 most active features and attempt a steering (amplify a feature during generation).
  5. Indirect injection on a toy RAG (ch. 5-6 — central lab). Build the "master's documents" RAG; slip in a booby-trapped page (instruction hidden in white-on-white); demonstrate the exfiltration to a simulated "mail tool"; harden by layers (delimiters, injection classifier, permissions, human confirmation); report the ASR before/after for paraphrased attacks (adaptive!).
  6. Canaries and extraction (ch. 7). Place canaries (random phone numbers) in a fine-tuning corpus with \(k = 1, 10, 100\) repetitions; measure the exposure (prop. 7.2); launch the extraction attack by compression ratio; repeat after deduplication — conclude.
  7. MIA on fine-tuning (ch. 8). Fine-tune a small LM on a restricted corpus (members) with a disjoint corpus (non-members); plot the ROC of the MIA by perplexity; repeat with DP-SGD (Opacus) at \(\varepsilon \in \{3, 8\}\); plot utility vs attack advantage — the trade-off curve of Result 8.5.
  8. End-to-end watermark (ch. 9). Implement the green-list scheme (hashing of the previous token, \(\gamma = 0.5\), \(\delta = 2\)) on an open LM; verify the calibration of the z-test on unmarked texts (Q-Q plot); measure the power at \(T \in \{50, 200, 500\}\); attack with light then heavy paraphrase; discuss the detectability/quality trade-off (perplexity before/after).
  9. Attributions (ch. 4). Compute the IG of a RAG answer with respect to its context tokens; verify the completeness (thm. 4.3) numerically (sum of the attributions vs logit gap); compare two baselines (pad vs empty sentence) and comment on the stability.
  10. Red team (ch. 10). Write a red teaming protocol for the application of lab 5: policy, attack taxonomy, 30 attacks (including 10 adaptive), severity grid; execute, report ASR per family + blast radius; turn the 5 most effective attacks into CI tests.
  11. Regulatory framework (ch. 10). Classify three scenarios (internal HR assistant, public health chatbot, journalistic content generation) in the AI Act risk pyramid; list for each the concrete obligations and the ML Ops artifact that attests to them (registry? eval? monitoring?) — prop. 10.2 in practice.
