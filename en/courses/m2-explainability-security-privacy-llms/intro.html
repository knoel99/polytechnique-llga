Introduction and overview

ModuleExplainability, Security, Privacy of LLMs
CodeM2 · P2 (brochure) — official code not published (course description not published)
Team and workloadInstructor: master's team · ~24 h · opening the black box (probing, logit lens, sparse autoencoders), attacking it (jailbreaks, prompt injections, poisoning, extraction) and protecting it (defenses in depth, differential privacy, watermarking, red teaming), up to the regulatory framework.
Status in the programM2Outside the recommended track · master's teaching team — M2 program being finalized

Deploying an LLM amounts to asking three questions about one and the same black box: why does it say this (interpretability), how can it be made to derail (security), and what does it leak from its training data (privacy). This module opens the box on these three fronts: probing and layer reading, superposition and sparse autoencoders, output explainability (SHAP, LIME), taxonomy of attacks and defenses in depth, memorization, membership inference and DP, anonymization and watermarking, up to red teaming and the AI Act framework. In the LLGA master, it is the synthesis module that makes an LLM deployable — auditable, defended and compliant.

What you will be able to do

  • Design probing experiments and causality tests to know what a layer actually encodes;
  • Read the layers of a transformer with the logit lens, and honestly discuss the limits of attention weights;
  • Explain the superposition phenomenon and use sparse autoencoders to extract interpretable features;
  • Explain an individual output by SHAP, LIME or gradients, knowing the pitfalls of each method;
  • Map the attacks on an LLM: jailbreaks, prompt injections, poisoning, extraction;
  • Design a defense in depth (filtering, alignment, isolation) without believing in the single defense;
  • Audit a model's memorization, conduct a membership inference attack and apply DP;
  • Anonymize, watermark, and put an LLM through a red teaming exercise aligned with the AI Act.

Where this course fits in the program

An M2 module offered in period 2, outside the recommended track: it is the convergence point of the master's three "trust" foundations, applied to the central object of the LLGA. Prerequisites: Large Language Models (transformer architecture, training, RLHF — without which one does not know what to probe); and the differential privacy of Emerging ML (\(\varepsilon\)-DP definition, Laplace noise), revisited in ch. 8. The module also crosses two sibling courses: the attack taxonomy of Fundamentals of Security and Robustness for AI and the mechanisms of Privacy and Uncertainty Quantification are assumed to be understood in broad outline by those who have taken these modules.

Downstream: the module equips responsible deployment — the guarantees, audit and documentation that the AI Act requires for high-risk models — and feeds both the end-of-studies internship (industries audit their LLMs) and research pursuits on model safety (alignment, interpretability).

Course outline

  • Recap: the black box and its three questions — reposition the attention architecture and training of LLMs before opening them.
  • Internal interpretability: probing and control — probe the representations, then prove their role by intervening on them.
  • Reading the layers: logit lens, attention and its limits — project the intermediate layers onto the vocabulary, and do not over-interpret attention.
  • Superposition and sparse autoencoders — why a model encodes more concepts than dimensions, and how to separate them.
  • Explaining an output: SHAP, LIME, gradients — attribute a prediction to input tokens, with the guarantees and illusions of each method.
  • Security: taxonomy of attacks — jailbreaks, prompt injections, poisoning, extraction: the adversarial menagerie of LLMs.
  • Defenses in depth — filtering, alignment guardrails, isolation: layering imperfect defenses that work together.
  • Privacy I: memorization and extraction — measure what an LLM gives back verbatim from its corpus, and how to extract it.
  • Privacy II: membership inference and DP — infer the presence of an individual in the data, and answer with differential privacy.
  • Anonymization and watermarking — clean the corpora and prove the origin of a generation.
  • Evaluating safety: red teaming, AI Act — organize the attack on one's own system and document regulatory compliance.
  • Exercises / Labs — probing, attacking and defending a small LLM, membership inference attack.
  • References — the interpretability, security and privacy papers that set the state of the art.

How to study this module

  • Open a small open-weight model yourself from the first week (small transformer, accessible layers): all the interpretability chapters are understood by handling, not by reading.
  • For each explanation method, first look for the counter-example where it deceives: methodical distrust is the skill of the module.
  • Alternate roles in pairs during the labs: attacker then defender — it is red teaming training, not a classic tutorial.
  • Keep a "safety" logbook of an LLM you use: every hallucination, leak or jailbreak observed becomes an example for ch. 10.
  • Read the AI Act text cited in reference with the risk categories in mind: the regulatory vocabulary will become your professional vocabulary.
The LLGA perspective The LLGA master trains you to build LLM-based applications; this module trains you to own them — before a client, a regulator, an ethics committee. Interpretability, security and privacy are treated here as a single discipline because that is how they present themselves in practice: a system that leaks is also an attackable system, and an uninterpretable system cannot be defended. It is also a breeding ground of booming research topics (mechanistic interpretability, alignment), at the confluence of LLMs and the formal guarantees that structure research at the laboratory.
