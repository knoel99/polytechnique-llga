0. Review: Fourier series in the L² sense, Fubini

L3 background used
  • The Hilbert space \(L^2\): hermitian inner product \(\langle f, g\rangle = \int f(t)\,\overline{g(t)}\,dt\), Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, orthogonal projection onto a finite-dimensional subspace — Fourier = a change of basis in an infinite orthonormal basis;
  • Fubini's theorem: if \(\iint |f(x,y)|\,dx\,dy < \infty\) (or for positive functions), the order of integration is free — it justifies all the convolution computations of chapter 3;
  • Integration by parts and changes of variable;
  • Complex geometric sequences, which give the orthogonality of the exponentials.
Proposition 0.1 — The exponentials form an orthogonal system On \([0, T_0]\), set \(e_k(t) = e^{ik\omega_0 t}\) with \(\omega_0 = 2\pi/T_0\). Then $$\langle e_k, e_\ell\rangle = \int_0^{T_0} e^{i(k-\ell)\omega_0 t}\,dt = \begin{cases} T_0 & \text{if } k = \ell,\\[2pt] \dfrac{e^{i(k-\ell)\omega_0 T_0} - 1}{i(k-\ell)\omega_0} = 0 & \text{if } k \neq \ell,\end{cases}$$ since \(e^{i(k-\ell)\omega_0 T_0} = e^{2i\pi(k-\ell)} = 1\). The \(e_k / \sqrt{T_0}\) therefore form an orthonormal system of \(L^2([0,T_0])\).
Definition 0.2 — The Dirichlet and Fejér kernels The partial sum of order \(N\) of a Fourier series can be written as a convolution with the Dirichlet kernel $$D_N(\theta) = \sum_{k=-N}^{N} e^{ik\theta} = \frac{\sin\big((N+\tfrac12)\theta\big)}{\sin(\theta/2)},$$ while the Cesàro mean of the partial sums (arithmetic mean \(S_0, \dots, S_{N-1}\)) is written with the Fejér kernel \(F_N = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n<N} D_n\), which has the decisive property of being positive (\(F_N \geq 0\)) and of mass 1.
Theorem 0.3 — Fejér convergence, density, completeness
  • Fejér: for \(f\) continuous and \(T_0\)-periodic, \(\sigma_N f = f * F_N \to f\) uniformly. The proof uses \(F_N \geq 0\), \(\int F_N = 1\) and the concentration of the mass of \(F_N\) near 0.
  • Consequence (density): the trigonometric polynomials \(\sum_{|k| \leq N} c_k e^{ik\omega_0 t}\) are dense in \(L^2([0,T_0])\).
  • Consequence (Hilbert basis): the orthonormal system \(\{e_k/\sqrt{T_0}\}\) is complete: it is a Hilbert basis of \(L^2\). The whole theory of Fourier series "in the \(L^2\) sense" follows from it.
Dirichlet versus Fejér — the message to remember The partial sums themselves (Dirichlet kernel, which changes sign) do not converge uniformly for every continuous function: they "oscillate" near discontinuities (Gibbs phenomenon, quantified in ch. 2). The Cesàro average smooths these oscillations. Moral: in \(L^2\), everything converges nicely; uniformly, one must regularize (averaging, windows) — exactly the role of the Hann windows of ch. 5.
