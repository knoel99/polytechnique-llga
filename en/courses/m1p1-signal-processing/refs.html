References

  • A. Oppenheim, A. Willsky, Signals and Systems, 2nd ed., Prentice Hall, 1997 — the reference textbook of the course: ch. 1-2 (LTI, convolution), ch. 3 (Fourier series), ch. 4 (Fourier transform), ch. 7 (sampling).
  • S. Smith, The Scientist and Engineer's Guide to Digital Signal Processing, 1997 — free online, very intuitive: ch. 6 (convolution), ch. 8-10 (DFT and properties), ch. 16 (sinc-window filters), ch. 18 (convolution via FFT).
  • S. Mallat, A Wavelet Tour of Signal Processing, 3rd ed., Academic Press, 2008 — the time-frequency/wavelet reference (ch. 2 Fourier, ch. 4 time-frequency, ch. 7 wavelet bases) and the "sparse" viewpoint that influenced deep learning.
  • P. Stoica, R. Moses, Spectral Analysis of Signals, Pearson, 2005 — chapters 2-3 (non-parametric methods): periodogram, Bartlett/Welch, the substance of ch. 7.
  • F. J. Harris, "On the Use of Windows for Harmonic Analysis with the Discrete Fourier Transform", Proceedings of the IEEE 66(1), 1978 — the source of the numbers of prop. 5.4.
  • J. O. Smith, Mathematics of the DFT / Introduction to Digital Filters — free (CCRMA, Stanford).
  • R. Flamary et al., POT: Python Optimal Transport, JMLR 2021 — pythonot.github.io: for the spectral distances of ch. 8-9 and the bridge to the M1P2 module.
  • E. Tanguy, R. Flamary, J. Delon, Reconstructing discrete measures from projections — the sliced OT / projections bridge of ch. 9.
  • 3Blue1Brown, But what is a Fourier series? — visualization recommended before lab 1.
