5. DFT, FFT and digital filtering

Definition 5.1 — The DFT For \(x_0, \dots, x_{N-1}\): $$X_k = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x_n\, e^{-i 2\pi k n / N}, \qquad k = 0, \dots, N-1.$$ The DFT samples the transform of the implicitly periodized signal (\(k \leftrightarrow \nu_k = k f_s/N\)). Two links to know:
  • DFT ↔ Fourier series: if \(x\) is \(T_0\)-periodic sampled with \(N\) points per period and \(f_s > 2B\): \(X_k = N\,c_k\) (for \(k < N/2\), and \(X_{N-k} = N c_{-k}\));
  • DFT ↔ Fourier transform: for a segment of duration \(T = NT_e\) of an aperiodic signal: \(\hat x(\nu_k) \approx T_e\,X_k\) (the so-called "rectangular" approximation, with the spectral leakage of prop. 5.4).
Proposition 5.2 — Circular convolution (proved) Let \(x \circledast h\) be the circular convolution (indices modulo \(N\)): \(y_n = \sum_{m=0}^{N-1} x_m\,h_{(n-m) \bmod N}\). Then \(\mathrm{DFT}(x \circledast h)_k = X_k\,H_k\).
\(Y_k = \sum_n \sum_m x_m h_{(n-m) \bmod N}\,\omega^{kn}\) with \(\omega = e^{-2\pi i/N}\). For \(m\) fixed, \(n \mapsto p = (n-m) \bmod N\) is a bijection of \(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\) and \(\omega^{kn} = \omega^{k(m+p)}\) (since \(\omega^{kN} = 1\)). Hence \(Y_k = \sum_m x_m\omega^{km}\sum_p h_p\omega^{kp} = X_k H_k\). The linear convolution is obtained by zero-padding both signals to \(N' \geq n + m - 1\) points: the "circular" wrap-around then no longer has any effect (the overflowing indices fall on zeros).∎
Theorem 5.3 — Radix-2 FFT (proved: the butterfly recurrence) If \(N = 2^q\), the DFT is computed in \(T(N) = \mathcal{O}(N \log N)\) operations.
Separate the even and odd sums: $$X_k = \underbrace{\sum_{m=0}^{N/2-1} x_{2m}\,\omega^{2mk}}_{E_k} +\ \omega^{k}\,\underbrace{\sum_{m=0}^{N/2-1} x_{2m+1}\,\omega^{2mk}}_{O_k}.$$ Now \(\omega^2 = e^{-4\pi i/N}\) is an \(N/2\)-th root of unity: \(E\) and \(O\) are two DFTs of size \(N/2\), periodic with period \(N/2\); and \(\omega^{k + N/2} = -\omega^k\). Hence the butterfly: $$X_k = E_k + \omega^k O_k, \qquad X_{k+N/2} = E_k - \omega^k O_k, \qquad k = 0,\dots,\tfrac{N}{2}-1,$$ which produces the \(N\) outputs from the \(N\) inputs in \(O(N)\) multiply-adds once \(E, O\) are known. Recurrence \(T(N) = 2T(N/2) + cN = cN\log_2 N\). For \(N = 2^{20}\) (one second of audio): \(\approx 2\times10^7\) units versus \(10^{12}\) for the definition — a factor of 50,000.∎
Proposition 5.4 — Windows: quantified lobes Analyzing a segment of signal = multiplying it by a window \(w\) ⇒ convolving the spectrum with \(\hat w\) (prop. 3.2): a pure line becomes the profile \(\hat w\). Characteristics (window of length \(M\), bin \(= f_s/M\), classical values of Harris 1978):
WindowMain lobe1st side lobeDecay
rectangular±1 bin−13 dB−6 dB/octave
Hann±2 bins−31 dB−18 dB/octave
Hamming±2 bins−41 dB−6 dB/octave
Blackman±3 bins−58 dB−18 dB/octave
Universal trade-off: a narrow window (low side lobes) ⇒ a wide main lobe (resolution lost). The rectangular one "leaks" at −13 dB: a strong line masks a weak neighboring line at −60 dB; Hann is the default choice.
Digital filters: FIR and IIR FIR (finite impulse response): \(y_n = \sum_{k=0}^{M} b_k x_{n-k}\) — always stable (Σ|b| finite: th. 1.3), linear phase if the \(b_k\) are symmetric, designed by windowing; IIR (infinite): \(y_n = \sum b_k x_{n-k} - \sum_{j\geq1} a_j y_{n-j}\), transfer function \(H(z) = B(z)/A(z)\) — more selective at equal coefficient counts, but stability must be checked.
Theorem 5.5 — Stability of an IIR: the poles (proved) For a rational IIR, BIBO stable ⇔ all the poles of \(H(z)\) lie in the open disk \(|z| < 1\).
The impulse response of a system with (simple) poles \(p_1, \dots, p_r\) is a combination of \(\mathcal{O}(n^s)\,p_j^n\): each mode contributes \(\sum_n |p_j|^n\), a geometric series convergent ⇔ \(|p_j| < 1\). A pole on the unit disk ⇒ a marginal or divergent mode; outside ⇒ a divergent exponential (the accumulator \(h = \mathbf{1}\), pole at 1, illustrates this).∎
Example 5.6 — Designing a low-pass FIR by windowing, step by step Objective: \(H_d(\nu) = \mathbf{1}_{|\nu| \leq \nu_c}\) (ν normalized by \(f_s\)).
  1. Ideal response in time: \(h_d[n] = 2\nu_c\,\mathrm{sinc}(2\nu_c n)\) (inverse FT of the rectangle, cf. proof of th. 4.1) — infinite and non-causal.
  2. Delay of \(M/2\) for causality (and symmetry ⇒ linear phase).
  3. Windowing: \(h[n] = h_d[n - M/2]\,w[n - M/2]\), \(n = 0,\dots,M\).
  4. Analysis: \(H = H_d * \hat w\); the transition band equals the width of the main lobe of \(\hat w\) — rule of thumb \(M \cdot \Delta\nu \approx 4\) (Hann), \(\approx 6\) (Blackman); the in-band ripple equals the level of the side lobes (prop. 5.4).
The effective frequency response is the DTFT of \(h\), \(H(\nu) = \sum_n h[n]e^{-2\pi i\nu n}\), evaluated by zero-padding the FFT (trick: a DFT with \(N_{\text{fft}} \gg M\) points interpolates the DTFT). A finer alternative: frequency-domain least squares — minimize \(\sum_{\omega \in \Omega} |H_b(\omega) - D(\omega)|^2\) over a frequency grid, a quadratic problem in \(b\), hence a linear system (Parks-McClellan equiripple method: the same spirit in infinite norm). For IIRs, one projects an analog prototype via the bilinear transform \(s \mapsto \frac{2}{T_e}\frac{1 - z^{-1}}{1 + z^{-1}}\) (Butterworth, Chebyshev…) while keeping the poles inside the disk — the "smooth" filter of example 5.7.
