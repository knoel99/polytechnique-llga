Exercises

  1. Square wave series (with solution). Compute the \(c_k\) of the odd square wave \(f = \mathrm{sign}(\sin)\), check the decay in \(1/k\) and illustrate the Gibbs phenomenon (partial sums at 5, 21, 101 harmonics). Solution. \(f\) odd and real ⇒ \(c_k\) purely imaginary. For \(k \neq 0\) even, the integral of \(\sin(k\omega_0 t)\) over the two half-periods cancels. For \(k\) odd: \(c_k = \frac{1}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0}f e^{-ik\omega_0t}dt = \frac{-2i}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0/2}\sin(k\omega_0 t)dt = \frac{-2i}{T_0}\cdot\frac{2\,(1-(-1)^k)}{2k\omega_0} = \frac{-2i}{\pi k}\), that is \(|c_k| = 2/(\pi|k|)\) — consistent with lemma 2.3: the period carries two jumps (of \(+2\) at \(0\), of \(-2\) at \(T_0/2\)), whose contributions \(\frac{2}{2\pi i k}\) and \(\frac{-2\,(-1)^k}{2\pi i k}\) add up to \(\frac{2(1-(-1)^k)}{2\pi i k} = \frac{-2i}{\pi k}\) for \(k\) odd and \(0\) for \(k\) even. Recombining: \(f(t) = \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{m\geq0}\frac{\sin((2m+1)\omega_0t)}{2m+1}\).
  2. Numerical Parseval. Check Parseval's identity on a sampled periodic signal (compare \(\sum|x_n|^2/N\) and \(\sum|X_k|^2/N^2\)), then explain why the DFT preserves the energy up to a factor \(N\) (a Vandermonde matrix, unitary up to a factor).
  3. Quantitative Gibbs (with solution). Show that for the square wave of exercise 1, the maximum of the partial sum \(S_N\) converges to \(1 + \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{\sin u}{u}du - 1 \approx 1.0895\), i.e. an overshoot of about 9% of the jump. Solution (sketch). Near the jump at 0, \(S_N f(t) = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\omega_0 N t}\frac{\sin u}{u}\,du\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\) (substitution in the Dirichlet kernel \(D_N\), prop. 0.2: the partial sum is written \(S_Nf(t) = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{N\omega_0t}\mathrm{sinc}\) — oscillations concentrated on a width \(O(1/N)\)). The maximum of the sinc integral is reached at the first zero of the numerator, \(u = \pi\), and equals \(\mathrm{Si}(\pi) \approx 1.852\) versus \(\mathrm{Si}(\infty) = \pi/2\). The relative overshoot is \(\frac{\mathrm{Si}(\pi) - \pi/2}{\pi/2} \approx 17.9\,\%\) of the half-jump, i.e. \(\approx 8.95\,\%\) of the full jump — independent of \(N\); only the width \(\sim 1/N\) shrinks.
  4. Gaussian (with solution). Re-prove prop. 3.3 by completing the square: show that \(\int e^{-\pi(t + i\nu)^2}dt = \int e^{-\pi t^2}dt = 1\) (move the contour, or differentiate \(\int e^{-\pi(t+i\nu)^2}dt\) with respect to \(\nu\) and conclude), and deduce the two half-widths (standard deviations) \(\sigma_t\), \(\sigma_\nu\) and their product. Solution (key). \(-\pi t^2 - 2\pi i\nu t = -\pi(t+i\nu)^2 - \pi\nu^2\) ⇒ \(\hat g = e^{-\pi\nu^2}\cdot\int e^{-\pi u^2}du\) with \(u = t + i\nu\) on the horizontal segment — the integral does not depend on \(\nu\) (holomorphy + decay at the ends). For \(g = e^{-\pi t^2}\): \(\sigma_t^2 = 1/(2\pi)\), \(\sigma_\nu^2 = 1/(2\pi)\), product \(= 1/(4\pi)\): the Gaussian saturates Heisenberg-Gabor (th. 3.4).
  5. Heisenberg (with solution). Check that equality \(\sigma_t\sigma_\nu = 1/(4\pi)\) requires \(x' = \lambda t x\) in the proof of th. 3.4, solve this differential equation and conclude that only the Gaussians (translated, modulated, dilated) saturate the inequality. Solution (key). Solve \(x'(t) = \lambda t\,x(t)\): \(x(t) = C e^{\lambda t^2/2}\) with \(\lambda < 0\) for integrability; \(x \in L^2\) ⇔ \(\lambda = -2\pi/\sigma^2\)… then a translation in time (center \(t_0\)) and a modulation (center \(\nu_0\)), which do not change the standard deviations.
  6. Aliasing. Generate a chirp \(f(t) = 100 + 400t\) Hz sampled at \(f_s = 500\) Hz; compute the spectrogram and interpret the "ghost" trace that goes back down; what apparent frequency does \(f = 350\) Hz correspond to? (prop. 4.2: \(|350 - 500| = 150\) Hz, and the trace rises up to 250 Hz before going back down.)
  7. Homemade FFT. Implement the butterfly recursion of th. 5.3 (even/odd recursive function), validate it against np.fft.fft, and plot the execution time as a function of \(q = \log_2 N\) for \(N = 2^q\), \(q = 4,\dots,18\); check the \(\log\)·\(N\) slope.
  8. Convolution via FFT. Implement the linear convolution via zero-padding + FFT (prop. 5.2), measure the times against np.convolve for \(n = m = 2^{14}\), and explain the crossing of the complexities.
  9. Hann filter. Low-pass FIR design by windowing (example 5.6): ideal response \(H(\nu) = \mathbf{1}_{|\nu| \leq \nu_c}\), multiplication by a Hann window, inverse transform. Plot the frequency response for \(M = 31\) and comment on the transition/aliasing trade-off; compare with Blackman at the same \(M\).
  10. Line detection (with solution). A signal contains \(x(t) = \sin(2\pi\cdot50t) + A\sin(2\pi\cdot80t)\) + white noise, with \(A = 0.01\) (a line at −40 dB). Show that the raw periodogram does not detect the line at 80 Hz (th. 7.5), then make it emerge with Welch (Hann, segments of 1024). Solution (key). Periodogram: leakage of the rectangular window at −13 dB (prop. 5.4) + 100% variance ⇒ the line at −40 dB is drowned. Welch: Hann window (first lobe −31 dB, decay −18 dB/octave) ⇒ the leakage of the 50 Hz line falls below −60 dB; \(L \approx 2N/1024\) segments ⇒ the standard deviation of the estimator divided by \(\sqrt{L}\): the line at 80 Hz exceeds the detection threshold. Code: scipy.signal.welch as in ch. 7.
  11. Mel bank (with solution). Implement a bank of 26 triangular Mel filters between 0 and \(f_s/2\) (formulas of ch. 8), apply it to a spectrogram, then the DCT; check that a vowel and a fricative have very different MFCCs. Solution (key). Uniform edges in Mel \(m_i = i\,2595\log_{10}(1+f_{max}/700)/26\), converted to Hz by the inverse formula, triangles of normalized area (the highest ones are wider in Hz); energies \(E_m = \sum_\nu W_m(\nu)|S(\nu)|^2\), log, DCT-II: the vowels (formants, low-Mel energy) concentrate on the first MFCCs, the fricatives (high frequencies) on the following ones.
  12. Project — denoising and spectral loss. Denoise a recording (white noise added): spectrogram → time-frequency masking (attenuate the cells below an energy threshold) → reconstruction by ISTFT; SNR before/after, musical artifacts. Then compare the distributions of original/denoised spectrograms by Gaussian \(W_2\) (formula of ch. 8, POT: ot.gaussian.wass2) — direct preparation of the M1P2 module.
