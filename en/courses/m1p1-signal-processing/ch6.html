6. Time-frequency analysis and wavelets

Definition 6.1 — STFT and spectrogram $$\mathrm{STFT}_x(t, \nu) = \int x(\tau)\, w(\tau - t)\, e^{-i2\pi\nu\tau}\, d\tau,$$ with \(w\) a short window (Hann). The spectrogram \(|\mathrm{STFT}_x|^2\) describes the energy across time and frequency: it is the image of sound that a convolutional network "sees" (ch. 8).
Proposition 6.2 — Exact resolution of the STFT At each instant, the STFT is the FT of the windowed segment \(x\cdot w(\cdot - t)\): its frequency resolution is the width of the main lobe of \(\hat w\) (prop. 5.4); its time resolution is the duration \(T\) of the window. For Hann: main lobe \(\pm 2/T\) in frequency, hence $$\Delta t = T, \qquad \Delta\nu \approx \frac{2}{T} \ \ (\text{Hann}), \qquad \Delta t\,\Delta\nu \approx 2,$$ to be compared with the floor \(1/(4\pi) \approx 0.08\) (standard deviations) of th. 3.4: the STFT works at a resolution fixed in advance, far from the floor, but with time-frequency cells all identical. Long window ⇒ good frequency resolution, poor time resolution — and conversely: the trade-off is irreversible for a given window.
Gabor frames: sampling the STFT A continuously stored STFT is redundant; one samples it on a grid \((t,\nu) = (m\,a,\ n\,b)\). Results (admitted): if \(ab < 1\) (a fine enough grid), the family of translated-modulated windows \(w_{m,n}\) forms a Gabor frame: stable reconstruction by a dual window; at the critical density \(ab = 1\), no well-localized orthonormal basis exists (the Balian-Low theorem): one must accept redundancy (or change the geometry: wavelets below). This is exactly the structure of spectrograms: frames every 10 ms, \(N\) frequencies — a frame, not a basis.
Definition 6.3 — Wavelets: analysis at variable scale Mother wavelet \(\psi\) (zero mean, localized); \(\psi_{a,b}(t) = \frac1{\sqrt a}\psi\big(\frac{t-b}{a}\big)\); the CWT samples \((a,b)\), often in octaves \(a = 2^{-j}\) (scalogram, \(Q = \Delta\nu/\nu\) constant): good time localization at high frequency, frequency localization at low frequency — suited to sounds (closely spaced harmonics at the bottom of the spectrum, brief transients at the top), where the STFT is uniform.
Example 6.4 — Haar, a complete example down to reconstruction The Haar wavelet: \(\varphi = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1)}\) (scale), \(\psi = \mathbf{1}_{[0,1/2)} - \mathbf{1}_{[1/2,1)}\) (detail). Signal \(x = (9, 7, 3, 5)\):
  • level 1: averages \(a_1 = (8, 4)\), details \(d_1 = (1, -1)\);
  • level 2: \(a_2 = (6)\), \(d_2 = (2)\);
  • coding: \(x \leftrightarrow (a_2, d_2, d_1) = (6, 2, 1, -1)\); reconstruction: \(9 = 6+2+1\), \(7 = 6+2-1\), \(3 = 6-2+1\), \(5 = 6-2-1\).
The multiresolution equations: \(\varphi(t) = \sqrt2\sum_k h_k\varphi(2t-k)\), \(\psi(t) = \sqrt2\sum_k g_k\varphi(2t-k)\); for Haar, \(h = (\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2})\), \(g = (\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2})\) — \(h\) = averaging filter (low-pass), \(g\) = differentiator (high-pass). The Daubechies \(D_N\) compactify this scheme with \(2N\) coefficients and \(N\) vanishing moments: on a piecewise regular signal, the detail coefficients decay like \(2^{-Nj}\) — this is what makes the coding sparse (a polynomial of degree < N has exactly zero details: Haar = \(D_1\) annihilates constants).
DWT = a filter bank, and the JPEG 2000 link The DWT is implemented without any integral: a cascade of low-pass (\(h\)) / high-pass (\(g\)) filterings followed by decimation by a factor 2, iterated on the low branch; the "quadrature mirror" conditions on \((h,g)\) cancel the aliasing introduced by the decimations. JPEG 2000 codes the subbands of a biorthogonal DWT (5/3 lossless, 9/7 lossy): an image = a 2D signal, and compression exploits lemma 2.3 (regularity ⇒ small coefficients). Same bank, from denoising to compression: the theory does not change.
