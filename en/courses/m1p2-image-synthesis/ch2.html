2. The rendering equation

Definition 2.1 — The light transport equation (LTE) At a surface point \(x\) with normal \(\mathbf{n}\), the outgoing radiance \(L_o(x, \omega_o)\) in a direction \(\omega_o\) of the hemisphere equals (Kajiya, 1986): $$L_o(x, \omega_o) = L_e(x, \omega_o) + \int_{\Omega^+} L_i(x, \omega_i)\, f_r(x, \omega_i \to \omega_o)\, \cos\theta_i\, d\omega_i,$$ with \(L_e\) the self-emission, \(L_i\) the incident radiance, \(f_r\) the BRDF (chapter 3) and \(\cos\theta_i = \omega_i \cdot \mathbf{n}\) the projection factor. Writing \(L_i(x, \omega) = L_o(r(x, -\omega), \omega)\) (the ray traced backwards), the equation becomes an integral over surfaces: it is a Fredholm equation of the second kind, $$L = L_e + T\,L, \qquad T\ \text{the transport operator (one bounce)}.$$
Theorem 2.2 — Bounce series (Neumann) and convergence If the materials have bounded albedo \(\rho = \sup \int_{\Omega^+} f_r \cos\theta_i\, d\omega_i < 1\), then \(T\) is a contraction in the sup norm and the unique solution of the LTE is the series $$L = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} T^k L_e = \underbrace{L_e}_{\text{emission}} + \underbrace{T L_e}_{1^{\text{st}}\ \text{bounce}} + \underbrace{T^2 L_e}_{2^{\text{nd}}\ \text{bounce}} + \dots$$ with truncation error \(\|T^{k+1} L\|_\infty \leq \rho^{k+1} \|L\|_\infty\) — geometric decay of the weight of the bounces.
For two functions \(f, g\) on rays, \(\|T f\|_\infty \leq \sup_x \int_{\Omega^+} f_r \cos\theta_i d\omega_i \cdot \|f\|_\infty \leq \rho \|f\|_\infty\). By induction \(\|T^k L_e\|_\infty \leq \rho^k \|L_e\|_\infty\): the series converges in norm (geometric series of ratio \(\rho < 1\)), and \(L = \sum T^k L_e\) satisfies \(L = L_e + T L\) by summing. Uniqueness follows from contractivity: if \(L, L'\) are solutions, \(\|L - L'\|_\infty = \|T(L - L')\|_\infty \leq \rho \|L - L'\|_\infty\) forces \(L = L'\). ∎
Example 2.3 — Two surfaces face to face: the series in action Two diffuse planes of albedo \(\rho = 0.5\), one emitting \(L_e = 1\) toward the other. Radiance leaving the receiving plane: \(0.5\) (1st bounce) + \(0.25\) (2nd: one extra round trip) + \(0.125 + \dots = 1\). With \(\rho = 0.9\) (white paint): \(0.9 + 0.81 + \dots\) — the 10th bounce still carries \(0.9^{10} \approx 35\)% of the emission: interreflections dominate direct lighting in a white room (a white alcove looks brighter than the wall alone — this is the "color bleeding" of global illumination).
Alternative forms Depending on the context the LTE is rewritten: as a surface integral (integrate \(L_i\) over all visible surfaces, with the visibility factor \(V(x \leftrightarrow y)\) and the geometry \(G = \cos\theta_x \cos\theta_y / \|x - y\|^2\)) — useful for sampling emissive surfaces (next event estimation, chapter 6); or as a flight equation (volume rendering, chapter 9: the emission-scattering term \(\sigma_s \int p\, L\) replaces the BRDF). Changing the formulation = changing the sampling strategy.
