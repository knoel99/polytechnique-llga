Exercises

  1. Guided radiance. (a) Take up the proof of Theorem 1.2 and redo it with a sketch with explicit dimensions (\(dA_1 = 1\) cm², \(dA_2 = 4\) cm², \(r = 0.5\) m, incidences 30° and 0°): verify \(L_1 = L_2\) numerically. (b) Deduce the irradiance of a point 1 m then 3 m from an isotropic 10 W point source — explain the \(1/r^2\) without invoking radiance.
  2. Bounce series. Two planes facing each other with albedo \(\rho = 0.9\), one emissive (\(L_e = 1\)). (a) Compute the equilibrium radiance (geometric series). (b) How many bounces to capture 99% of the energy? 99.9%? (c) What is the link with the default path depth (3–4) of real-time engines?
  3. Guided Lambert. (a) Redo the proof of Theorem 3.2 in detail (computation of \(\int_{\Omega^+} \cos\theta\, d\omega = \pi\)). (b) Check the energy conservation of Phong with \(k_d = 0.7\), \(k_s = 0.3\), \(n = 30\) (numerical integration over the hemisphere); what happens for \(n = 1\)? (c) Sample the GGX BRDF by the normal distribution method (draw \(\mathbf{h}\) according to \(D(\mathbf{h})\cos\theta_h\), bounce) — check unbiasedness by comparison with a reference integral.
  4. Fresnel. Plot Schlick's \(F(\theta)\) for \(F_0 = 0.04\) and for gold \((1.00,\,0.71\,0.29)\) over \([0°, 90°]\); recover the values of Example 3.4; explain why wet grass looks shiny (dielectric water layer over a diffuse surface).
  5. Guided camera. (a) Implement pixel ⟶ ray (Definition 4.1) and check Example 4.3. (b) For \(f_x = 1000\) px and a 1920×1080 image, compute the horizontal and vertical fields of view. (c) Add a thin lens (sample a disk of radius \(r\) in the lens plane, aim at the focal point) and show that the circle of confusion grows linearly with \(r\).
  6. Rasterization. (a) Rasterize by hand the triangle of Example 5.4 over the grid of pixels with integer centers — list of covered pixels and barycentrics. (b) Build an example with two triangles where painter's sorting fails and the z-buffer does not. (c) Prove that the "top-left" rule avoids holes and double counting between adjacent triangles.
  7. Guided Monte Carlo. (a) Re-derive the variance computation of Theorem 6.3 (uniform sampling: \(\pi^2/3\)). (b) Implement the two estimators of \(\int_{\Omega^+}\cos\theta\, d\omega\) and plot the error as a function of \(N\) (log scale); check the \(-1/2\) slopes. (c) Generalize: for \(f(\theta) = \cos^k\theta\), what is the zero-variance density? and a realistic approximate density?
  8. BVH/SAH. (a) Build by hand the median BVH of 8 triangles in \([0,1]^3\) (2 levels); count the boxes traversed by a diagonal ray. (b) Implement the SAH split (12 candidates per axis) and compare the total traversal cost with (a) on a scene of 1000 random triangles. (c) What becomes the BVH if all the triangles move? (refit versus rebuild — measure both.)
  9. Volume rendering. (a) Check Example 9.5 against the exact integral (piecewise-constant densities, analytic integration of \(\int T \sigma c\)). (b) Implement alpha compositing over 64 randomly drawn samples (NeRF quadrature): does the bias vanish on average? (c) Simulate a "3D Gaussian" (\(\sigma(x) = \sigma_0 e^{-\|x - c\|^2/2s^2}\)) and render a cloud — why must the number of samples per ray grow with \(1/s\)?
  10. Project — extend the mini tracer. (a) Mirror/glass materials (reflection + Snell refraction, Fresnel scheme); (b) explicit Russian roulette (termination \(q = 1 - \rho\), reweighting) and measurement: average depth, per-pixel variance; (c) depth of field; (d) guided bilateral denoising with the buffers (normal, depth) and equal-time comparison against more spp. Conclusion: what budget for what quality?
