6. Monte Carlo path tracing

Definition 6.1 — The rendering estimator Path tracing estimates the LTE (chapter 2) by sampling paths \(\mathbf{x} = (x_0, \dots, x_k)\) from the eye toward the sources, with density \(p(\mathbf{x})\), via $$\hat{L} = \frac{L_e\, f_r\, \cos\theta\, f_r \cos\theta \cdots}{p(\mathbf{x})} \quad \text{(product of transport factors / probability of the path)}.$$ A path is built bounce after bounce: at each bounce, draw an outgoing direction according to a density \(p(\omega)\) (hemispherical uniform, or proportional to \(\cos\theta/\pi\) — cosine sampling), accumulate the contributions, terminate with Russian roulette (Definition 6.4). It is an unbiased estimator of the complete bounce series — in contrast with deterministic methods (radiosity, photon mapping: biased but less noisy).
Theorem 6.2 — Unbiased, and variance in \(\sigma/\sqrt{N}\) (i) \(\mathbb{E}\big[f(X)/p(X)\big] = \int_D f\) as soon as \(p > 0\) on the support of \(f\). (ii) For \(N\) i.i.d. samples, the mean estimator \(\hat{I}_N\) satisfies \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat{I}_N) = \mathrm{Var}(f(X)/p(X))/N\) and \(\hat{I}_N \to I\) almost surely. To divide the typical error by 2, you need 4 times more samples — the curse of Monte Carlo (and its salvation: the \(-1/2\) exponent is independent of the dimension, hence the triumph of MC in rendering, with its infinite dimension of paths).
(i) \(\mathbb{E}[f/p] = \int (f/p)\, p = \int f\). (ii) the variance of an independent sum adding up, \(\mathrm{Var}(\hat I_N) = \frac{1}{N^2} \sum \mathrm{Var} = \mathrm{Var}/N\) — and the strong law of large numbers gives the convergence. For the typical error, the CLT gives \(\hat I_N - I \approx \mathcal{N}(0, \mathrm{Var}/N)\): standard deviation in \(\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}/N}\). ∎
Theorem 6.3 — Importance sampling: the zero-variance optimum Among the densities \(p\), the variance of \(f(X)/p(X)\) is minimal (zero) for \(p^\ast \propto |f|\); in practice one chooses \(p\) close to \(f\) where \(f\) is large. The module's perfect example: for \(I = \int_{\Omega^+} \cos\theta\, d\omega = \pi\) (point-to-point form factor), uniform sampling (\(p = 1/2\pi\)) gives the estimator \(2\pi\cos\theta\), of variance \(\pi^2/3\); cosine sampling (\(p = \cos\theta/\pi\)) gives the constant estimator \(\pi\) — exactly zero variance.
\(p^\ast = |f|/\int|f|\): then \(f/p^\ast = \pm\int|f|\) is constant ⟹ zero variance. For the uniform sampling computation: \(\mathbb{E}[X^2] = \int (2\pi\cos\theta)^2 \frac{d\omega}{2\pi} = 2\pi \int_{\Omega^+}\cos^2\theta\, d\omega = 2\pi \cdot \frac{2\pi}{3} = \frac{4\pi^2}{3}\), and \(\mathbb{E}[X]^2 = \pi^2\), hence \(\mathrm{Var} = \frac{4\pi^2}{3} - \pi^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3}\) (using \(\int_{\Omega^+}\cos^2\theta\,d\omega = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^2\theta \sin\theta\, d\theta\, d\varphi = \frac{2\pi}{3}\)). For the cosine: \(f/p = \cos\theta / (\cos\theta/\pi) = \pi\) for every draw. ∎
Definition 6.4 — The three ingredients of a real path tracer
  • Next Event Estimation (NEE): at each bounce, connect the point directly to the emissive sources (shadow ray) instead of waiting for a path to hit them by chance — multiple importance sampling (MIS, Veach): combine the "BRDF" strategy and the "light" strategy with balance weights;
  • Russian roulette: terminate the path with probability \(q\) (the albedo of the point), weighting the survivors by \(1/(1-q)\): unbiased, expected length bounded by the reflectance — the geometric series of Theorem 2.2, sampled;
  • Sampling of extended lights: a \(1\,\mathrm{m}^2\) panel seen from \(3\) m at oblique incidence occupies only a few thousandths of a steradian — draw points on the source (weighted by its area and the distance) rather than directions; this is the factor of 100 between a render "noisy in 10 minutes" and "clean in 10 seconds".
Connections with the rest of the curriculum Estimating the LTE is a high-dimensional integration problem — exactly the setting of Wasserstein distances and their sampling estimators (cf. Optimal Transport module: importance sampling, concentration, curse of dimensionality); MIS is a special case of combining estimators (variance minimization under an unbiasedness constraint). The same proofs serve rendering and learning; only the integrand changes.
