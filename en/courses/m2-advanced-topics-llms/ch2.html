2. Long context: RoPE, ring attention, RAG or LC?

Today's readings J. Su et al., RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), 2021 (arXiv:2104.09864); S. Chen et al., Extending Context Window via Positional Interpolation, 2023 (arXiv:2306.15595); B. Peng et al., YaRN, 2023 (arXiv:2309.00071); H. Liu et al., Ring Attention with Blockwise Transformers for Near-Infinite Context, NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2310.01889); N. Liu et al., Lost in the Middle, 2023 (arXiv:2307.03172); Z. Li et al., Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs?, EMNLP 2024 industry (arXiv:2407.16833); P. Xu et al., Retrieval meets Long Context LLMs, ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2310.03025).

2.1. RoPE: position by rotation

Definition 2.1 — RoPE (Su et al., 2021) Each even coordinate pair \((2i, 2i{+}1)\) of the vector is viewed as a complex number to which a rotation of angle \(m\theta_i\) is applied, with \(m\) the position of the token and \(\theta_i = b^{-2i/d}\) (geometric bases): $$q_m = R(m\Theta)\, q, \qquad k_n = R(n\Theta)\, k, \qquad \Theta = \mathrm{diag}(\theta_0, \dots).$$ The attention dot product becomes a function of the relative position — and the high frequencies \(\theta_i\) resolve the local, the low frequencies the global.
Proposition 2.2 — RoPE encodes only relative position \(\langle R(m\Theta)\,q,\; R(n\Theta)\,k \rangle\) depends only on \(m - n\) (and on \(q, k\)). The proof is one line of trigonometry: per coordinate pairs, $$\langle R(m\theta)q,\, R(n\theta)k\rangle = \langle R((m-n)\theta)\, q,\; k \rangle,$$ since \(R(m\theta)^\top R(n\theta) = R((n-m)\theta)\) (composition of the rotations — they commute in the plane).
Plane rotations are orthogonal: \(\langle R(m\theta)q, R(n\theta)k\rangle = q^\top R(m\theta)^\top R(n\theta)k = q^\top R((n - m)\theta)k\). Summing over the coordinate pairs (each pair with its own frequency \(\theta_i\)), the attention score is a function of \(m - n\) only. Engineering consequence: extending the context amounts to re-parameterizing the angles, not to relearning positional embeddings. ∎

2.2. Extending the window: interpolation and YaRN

Positional interpolation (Chen et al., 2023): trained with angles up to \(L\), one serves \(L' > L\) by linearly compressing the positions \(m \mapsto m \cdot L/L'\) — the model sees angles already covered; a few hundred fine-tuning steps suffice (Llama-2 4k → 32k). YaRN (Peng et al., 2023) refines: interpolate selectively by frequency (the low — global — frequencies are interpolated; the high — local — extrapolated; "NTK-aware") and rescale the attention temperature (\(\times\) constant depending on the extension factor) to compensate for the dilution of the scores; result: an extension that reduces data and fine-tuning steps by roughly 10× compared to PI. Spectral viewpoint (cf. Signal Processing module): handling a larger window = handling a wider frequency spectrum without aliasing.

2.3. Ring attention: cutting the memory cost in a ring

Proposition 2.3 — Ring Attention (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023) Cut the sequence of length \(N\) into \(D\) blocks distributed over \(D\) GPUs; at each step, each GPU computes the attention of its query block against a key/value block received from a neighbor, then passes this block to the next (ring topology) — overlapping communication and computation. After \(D\) steps, each query has seen all the blocks: the attention is exact (not an approximation), and the memory per GPU drops from \(\mathcal{O}(N)\) to \(\mathcal{O}(N/D)\). Demonstration at scale: contexts on the order of 32 million tokens on 32 A100 GPUs — ~27× the memory state of the art of the time.

2.4. RAG or long context? Lost in the middle and the hybrid router

"Lost in the Middle" (N. Liu et al., 2023): when the information is placed in the middle of a long context, performance collapses in a U shape (beginning and end favored) — a 128k window does not "read" its 128k uniformly. The RAG vs LC dossier: Li et al. (2024) compare systematically on long-context tasks: with sufficient resources, long context wins on average (retrieval can miss the needle); but RAG remains much more economical — and their Self-Route (the model estimates whether the query is answerable with the retrieved excerpts; if not, the whole document is sent) achieves LC quality for ~39% less cost. Xu et al. (ICLR 2024) complete the picture: retrieval improves models whatever the window (4k/16k/32k), and a 4k + retrieval approaches a 16k without retrieval — RAG is not dead, it is complementary.

Critical analysis (i) "Needle in a haystack" (finding a sentence planted in a long text) is a retrieval test, not a long-context reasoning test — multi-document aggregation benchmarks break the scoreboard; (ii) the KV cache grows linearly with \(N\) (cf. ML Ops module ch. 3): "free" LC at the API is an economic choice of the provider, not a physical property; (iii) RoPE extensions are measured in perplexity and in downstream tasks — YaRN shows they can diverge; (iv) Self-Route relies on the model's self-assessment (meta-cognition): how good is it on adversarial queries? To be related to ch. 3 (memory) and the ML Ops module ch. 6 (index cost vs context cost). 
# Self-Route self-evaluation (sketch) -- RAG first, LC as fallback
def self_route(question, docs, llm, budget):
    snippets = retrieve(question, docs, k=8)          # classic RAG
    verdict = llm(f"Sauras-tu repondre avec SEULEMENT ces extraits ? "
                  f"(OUI/NON)\n---\n{snippets}", max_tokens=2, temp=0)
    if verdict.strip().upper().startswith("OUI"):
        return llm(f"Extraits :\n{snippets}\nQuestion : {question}")
    return llm(f"Documents complets :\n{concat(docs)}\nQuestion : {question}")
    # cost: ~39% of full LC if the router is well calibrated (Li et al. 2024)
