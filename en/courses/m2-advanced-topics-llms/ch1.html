1. Reasoning: chain-of-thought, RLVR

Today's readings J. Wei et al., Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models, NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv:2201.11903); X. Wang et al., Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models, ICLR 2023 (arXiv:2203.11171); H. Lightman et al., Let's Verify Step by Step, ICLR 2024 (arXiv:2305.20050); DeepSeek-AI, DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning, 2025 (arXiv:2501.12948); N. Lambert et al., Tulu 3, 2024 (arXiv:2411.15124) — which names RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards).

1.1. Chain-of-thought and self-consistency

The prompt "let's think step by step" triggers the generation of an intermediate reasoning chain \(z\) before the answer \(y\) — and the gains emerge with scale (clear from ~1011 parameters in the original study): the model allocates compute tokens to the problem. The formal reading:

Proposition 1.1 — Self-consistency = marginalization (Wang et al., 2023) Generating a chain \(z\) then an answer \(y\) realizes a joint model \(p(y, z \mid x) = p(z\mid x)\,p(y \mid x, z)\). The probability of the correct answer under the full model is the margin $$p(y \mid x) \;=\; \sum_{z} p(y, z \mid x).$$ Self-consistency approximates \(\arg\max_y p(y\mid x)\) by majority vote over \(k\) sampled chains \(z_1, \dots, z_k \sim p(\cdot \mid x)\): a Monte Carlo of "latent reasoning". Typical gains: +10 to +20 points on GSM8K compared to greedy decoding — for an inference cost of ×\(k\).
Sampling \(z_i \sim p(z\mid x)\) then \(y_i \sim p(y \mid x, z_i)\) produces pairs \((y_i, z_i)\) distributed according to the joint; the count \(\frac1k \sum_i \mathbf{1}[y_i = y]\) is an unbiased estimator of \(\sum_z p(z \mid x)\,p(y \mid x, z) = p(y\mid x)\) (the two successive samplings compose exactly the joint). The empirical argmax converges to the true argmax as \(k \to \infty\) (concentration of frequencies) — it is a special case of the Monte Carlo marginalization seen in the ML module, with the particularity that the latent space is text. ∎

1.2. Process vs outcome rewards

Training a reward model on the final answer (ORM, outcome) is noisy: an answer that is right by chance receives the same reward as a clean reasoning. Lightman et al. build PRM800K (800,000 step-by-step labels given by humans) and train a PRM (process reward model): at equal labeling budget, the PRM selects better solutions on MATH than the ORM — supervision of the process is more informative than that of the outcome. Immediate limit: step-by-step labeling is expensive and subjective; hence the idea of automating verification...

1.3. RLVR: verifiable rewards and the emergence of reasoning

Definition 1.2 — RLVR Instead of a learned reward model (subject to reward hacking), the reward is a verifiable rule: does the mathematical answer match the ground truth? does the code pass the tests? is the format correct? Training (GRPO — cf. LLMs module ch. 5; PPO — cf. RL module ch. 4) maximizes $$\mathcal{J}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x, y)\sim \pi_\theta} \bigl[\, r_{\text{verif}}(x, y) \,\bigr]$$ over domains where truth is decidable. Tulu 3 (Lambert et al., 2024) makes it the third stage of open post-training (SFT → DPO → RLVR).
Pivot result — DeepSeek-R1 (2025) R1-Zero: purely RL (no initial SFT) with rule-based rewards on math/code/format; the model develops by itself reasoning behaviors — verification, backtracking, reflection ("aha moment"), lengthening chains — measurable by the growing generation length over the course of training. R1: adding cold-start data + multiple stages (SFT → RL → re-SFT) stabilizes and aligns, up to rivaling the best closed reasoning models on math/code benchmarks. Associated publication in Nature (2025) — read both: the technical account and the scientific letter do not tell exactly the same story.
Critical analysis (a whole session) (i) Generalization: RLVR only improves domains close to pre-training + verifiable; transfer to "general" reasoning is debated. (ii) Benchmarks: MATH/AIME are saturated and contaminable (ch. 9); are the advances measured on the scale of their own pollution? (iii) Residual reward hacking: even "hard" rules are bypassable (misleading format, weak tests); (iv) Costs: chains of tens of thousands of tokens — reasoning is a product that is compute-bound at inference (cf. ML Ops module ch. 4, green ops ch. 10); (v) Alternative reading: does R1-Zero select capabilities already present (pre-training) rather than create them? The distillation experiments (small models trained on R1's traces outperforming their direct RL cousins) lean toward the first reading. Exercise: defend both theses with the figures of the paper.
