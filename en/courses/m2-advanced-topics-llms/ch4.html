4. Mixture-of-Experts

Today's readings N. Shazeer et al., Outrageously Large Neural Networks: The Sparsely-Gated Mixture-of-Experts Layer, ICLR 2017 (arXiv:1701.06538); W. Fedus, B. Zoph, N. Shazeer, Switch Transformers, JMLR 2022 (arXiv:2101.03961); A. Jiang et al., Mixtral of Experts, 2024 (arXiv:2401.04088); DeepSeek-AI, DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report, 2024 (arXiv:2412.19437).
Definition 4.1 — Sparse MoE layer Each feed-forward block is replaced by \(N\) experts \(E_1, \dots, E_N\) and a router: $$y \;=\; \sum_{i \in \mathrm{TopK}(g(x))} g_i(x)\, E_i(x), \qquad g(x) = \mathrm{softmax}\bigl(W_g\,x\bigr),$$ only the \(k\) strongest experts are computed (k = 1-2 typically). The total parameters grow (capacity), the active parameters per token remain bounded (cost): Mixtral 8×7B = ~47B parameters, ~13B active; DeepSeek-V3 = 671B of which ~37B active.

4.1. Balancing the experts: the auxiliary loss

Proposition 4.2 — Auxiliary load-balancing loss (Switch) With \(f_i\) = fraction of tokens routed to expert \(i\) and \(P_i\) = average router probability mass on \(i\): $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}} \;=\; \alpha\, N \sum_{i=1}^{N} f_i\, P_i, \qquad \sum_i f_i = \sum_i P_i = 1,$$ whose minimum \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}} = \alpha\) is reached at uniform routing \(f = P = \mathbf{1}/N\). The loss penalizes the degenerate routing where a few experts capture everything — otherwise the router gradient collapses (the unpicked experts receive no gradient: the rich get richer).
At the uniform point, \(\sum_i f_iP_i = N \cdot \frac1N \cdot \frac1N = \frac1N\), so \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}} = \alpha N \cdot \frac1N = \alpha\). To show that this is indeed a minimum in the targeted regime, we use Chebyshev's sum inequality: if the sequences \((f_i)\) and \((P_i)\) are similarly ordered (co-monotone — the likely experts are also the most loaded, exactly the degenerate regime that we want to penalize), then $$\frac1N \sum_i f_i P_i \;\geq\; \Bigl(\frac1N \sum_i f_i\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac1N \sum_i P_i\Bigr) = \frac{1}{N^2}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \sum_i f_i P_i \;\geq\; \frac1N,$$ with equality if and only if one of the sequences is constant. Hence \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{aux}} \geq \alpha\), equality at uniform. Finally, the dynamic effect: \(\partial (f_iP_i)/\partial P_i = f_i \geq 0\) — the gradient pulls \(P_i\) down proportionally to the load \(f_i\): a negative feedback loop against concentration. ∎
Critical analysis — read the hidden assumption The lower bound is only proved for co-monotone \((f, P)\): an anti-monotone pair (a very likely but lightly loaded expert) could have a product below \(1/N\) — the loss is not a symmetric divergence in the strict sense; it is a load proxy whose gradient dynamics does most of the work. This is exactly the kind of detail a critical paper reading must catch: what quantity does the loss actually measure, under what assumption, and what does its gradient do? DeepSeek-V3 goes as far as replacing the loop with auxiliary-loss-free balancing: a per-expert bias term, dynamically adjusted to the observed load — direct control rather than a differentiable penalty, with reported quality gains. Less elegant, more effective: up for debate.

4.2. What the experts learn

The Mixtral analysis (the "routing" figure) shows syntactic and domain-level specialization (code, math, languages) rather than fine-grained topics — and substantial redundancy across experts: the MoE mostly democratizes capacity, not the division of labor. DeepSeek-V3 pushes two structural innovations: fine-grained experts (more numerous, smaller) + shared experts (always active, capturing cross-cutting knowledge — reducing the redundancy), and FP8 training at scale (14.8T tokens) — the technical report is a systems-engineering course in itself (expert-parallel communication, cf. Emerging ML module ch. 2 on distributed computing).

Critique in practice — what are the "active parameters" for? Comparing a 47B/13B-active MoE to a 13B dense model is misleading: the MoE keeps 47B in memory (cf. ML Ops module ch. 3: the KV cache and the weights reside in full in VRAM) but ~13B of compute per token. MoE wins provided large batches (amortizing memory through throughput): excellent for shared serving, debatable for single-user. The LLGA reading grid: every architecture must be evaluated at full cost (memory, compute, communication) — not in advertising "parameters".
