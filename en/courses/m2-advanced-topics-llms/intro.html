Status in the programM2Outside the recommended track · master's teaching team — M2 program being finalized

LLM research moves so fast that a lecture course would be outdated before the end of the semester: this module therefore takes the form of a seminar, where each chapter organizes the critical reading of a pivot paper — reasoning (chain-of-thought, RLVR), long context, memory, mixture-of-experts, alternative architectures (Mamba, RWKV), inference efficiency, agents and evaluation. The goal is not to pile up results, but to make you able to read a new paper, spot its fragile assumptions and situate its contributions. In the LLGA master, it is the hinge module between the training and the research frontier — and the only one that literally carries the "LLMs × graphs" crossroads that gives the master its name.

What you will be able to do

  • Read an LLM research paper critically: claims, evidence, protocol, what the authors do not say;
  • Explain model reasoning: chain-of-thought, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR);
  • Analyze long-context techniques (RoPE, ring attention) and arbitrate between long context and RAG;
  • Compare agent memories: episodic, vector stores, GraphRAG;
  • Explain how mixture-of-experts architectures and alternatives to transformers (SSM, Mamba, RWKV) work and why they matter;
  • Speed up inference with speculative decoding and quantization, quantifying the trade-offs;
  • Analyze the LLMs × graphs crossroads and agent architectures (planning, tools, multi-agents);
  • Evaluate an LLM without deceiving oneself: benchmark contamination, LLM judge bias, open problems.

Where this course fits in the program

An M2 module offered in period 2, outside the recommended track: a research seminar, it assumes the foundations are laid and works through readings and discussions rather than ex cathedra lectures. Prerequisites: Large Language Models (architecture, training, alignment — the foundation that every chapter goes beyond) and Advanced Deep Learning, whose architectures and efficiency analyses are reused continuously.

Downstream: it is the direct preparation for the research internship and the PhD — each chapter ends with open questions that are as many internship topics — and for R&D jobs, where the competitive edge is played on a six-month head start in reading the literature.

Course outline

  • Recap and how to use the seminar — the LLM foundation recapped, and the method: how one reads, discusses and presents a paper here.
  • Reasoning: chain-of-thought, RLVR — have the model unfold a reasoning, then train it on verifiable rewards.
  • Long context: RoPE, ring attention, RAG or LC? — extend the window, and the real industrial question: put everything in context or retrieve?
  • Memory: episodic, vector stores, GraphRAG — give memory beyond the window, from vector storage to knowledge graphs.
  • Mixture-of-Experts — immense models of which only a few experts activate: training, routing, savings.
  • Alternative architectures: SSM, Mamba, RWKV — thinking beyond attention: promises and measured realities.
  • Efficiency: speculative decoding, quantization — serve faster and cheaper, with their effects on quality.
  • LLMs × graphs: the LLGA crossroads — reasoning on structures, GraphRAG, graph foundation models: the master's signature.
  • Agents: planning, tools, multi-agents — models that plan, call tools and collaborate.
  • Evaluation: contamination, judge bias — why leaderboards are wrong, and how to evaluate honestly.
  • Open problems — the map of research fronts as it takes shape this year.
  • Exercises / Labs — minimal reproductions: RoPE, speculative decoding, a small contamination-free evaluation.
  • References — the pivot papers of each chapter, with their rebuttals.

How to study this module

  • Read each paper before the session, in a first twenty-minute pass (abstract, figures, protocol), then a second targeted pass: the seminar only works if everyone has read.
  • For each paper, write down the sentence "the central claim is X, proven by Y, fragile because Z": it is the module's single exercise, do it every week.
  • Present at the board when it is your turn: explaining RoPE or speculative decoding without slides is the best rehearsal for a PhD interview.
  • Systematically cross with the other modules: long context dialogues with the RAG Ops of ML Ops, agents with Speech Technology, ch. 7 with your graph courses.
  • Follow two or three preprint sources after each chapter: the module above all teaches you to stay up to date by yourself.
The LLGA perspective Chapter 7 is called "LLMs × graphs: the LLGA crossroads": this module is the place in the master where the motto becomes a research object. The fronts it covers — reasoning, memory, agents, evaluation — are exactly those where the crossroads of language models with relational data produces the most open questions. If you are aiming for a PhD, an R&D internship or a startup at the frontier, this is where you will learn the rarest gesture: read fast, judge surely, and identify the problem that no one has yet posed well.
