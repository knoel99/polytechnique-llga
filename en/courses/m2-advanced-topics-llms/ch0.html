0. Recap and how to use the seminar

Prerequisites (M1/M2 modules)
  • LLMs module (M2): architecture (ch. 2), pre-training and scaling laws (ch. 3), SFT/RLHF/DPO/GRPO (ch. 4-5), inference (ch. 6), RAG (ch. 7), agents (ch. 8), evaluation (ch. 9) — this seminar assumes all of this acquired and pushes each subject one notch further;
  • RL (M1): policy gradients and PPO (ch. 4), deep RL (ch. 5), agents in the era of LLMs (ch. 7) — the engine of reasoning by RLVR (ch. 1);
  • Graph ML (M1): message passing (ch. 5), GraphRAG (ch. 11) — the crossroads of ch. 7;
  • RLHF and theory: rewards, optimization (ML / Optimization); concentration (Refresher Statistics) for ch. 9.
Definition 0.1 — Seminar format One session = one pivot paper (read by everyone before), dissected in class (method, equations, experiments), then subjected to a structured critical analysis: what exact question? what method? what evidence (and counter-evidence)? what do the figures say in detail? what limits — theoretical, experimental, operational? what remains to be done? The reading note (lab 1) is the module's permanent deliverable.
Common reading grid For each paper: (1) central claim in one sentence; (2) evidence: benchmarks, baselines, ablations, significance; (3) costs — compute, data, energy (cf. ML Ops module ch. 4 and 10); (4) reproducibility — code, data, seeds, potential contamination (ch. 9); (5) LLGA positioning: what link to graphs, RAG, agents? This grid is also that of your M2 internship report.
