Exercises

  1. Randomization by hand. Data 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, split 3+3. Enumerate the 20 allocations, compute all values of \(\hat d\), recover \(p = 0.10\) and the exact distribution (stem-and-leaf display). Which split gives exactly \(\hat d = 2.33\)?
  2. Power. (a) Redo the computation of 2.5 for \(\delta/\sigma = 0.4\) and 85 % power. (b) A two-sided test has \(\alpha = 5\,\%\) and \(n = 50\) per group: plot \(\pi(\delta)\) for \(\delta/\sigma \in [0; 1]\) and read off the smallest detectable effect size with 80 % power. (c) For proportions (\(p_A = 0.10\), expected effect +1 point), adapt the formula by replacing \(2\sigma^2\) with \(p_A(1-p_A) + p_B(1-p_B)\) and compute \(n\).
  3. Simpson. Verify all the cells of table 3.2 (82.8 %, etc.), then recompute the overall rates by standardizing on A's profile and on B's; comment on the sensitivity to the profile choice.
  4. DiD. With table 4.4: compute the naive before/after comparison, the cross-sectional after comparison, and the DiD; check that the DiD equals the interaction coefficient of the model \(y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \text{NJ} + \beta_2 \text{After} + \beta_3 (\text{NJ} \times \text{After}) + e\). Simulate 500 restaurants with common AR(1) shocks and show that naive standard errors are 3–5× too small without clustering (Bertrand et al. 2004).
  5. BH in practice. (a) Apply Bonferroni, Holm and BH to the p-values of example 6.4 for \(q = \alpha = 0.10\) and \(q = 0.01\). (b) Simulation: generate \(m = 1000\) p-values (\(m_0 = 900\) uniform, 100 false nulls with p ~ Beta(1, 8)), repeat 500 times, estimate the empirical FWER and FDR of each procedure and verify the calibration \(\mathrm{FDR} \approx q\, m_0/m\).
  6. Regression. On the cars dataset (or an equivalent): fit \(d \sim s\), plot the 4 diagnostics, apply Box-Cox, refit; identify the point with the largest Cook's distance, redo without it; compare the classical and HC1 standard errors (statsmodels).
  7. Peeking. Simulate 1000 A/B tests under \(H_0\) (two Bernoulli \(p = 0.10\), \(n = 10^5\) per arm): (a) a single look at midpoint — false positive rate; (b) daily looks for 28 days with stopping if p < 0.05 — compare with the 22.6 % of the union bound and with the bound of Johari et al.; (c) implement a Gaussian mSPRT and verify its "always" validity.
  8. Bayesian A/B. Take up the code of ch. 8: (a) analyze the sensitivity to the Beta(1,1) vs Beta(10,10) prior; (b) turn the decision rule into "expected loss < cost of a rollout"; (c) add the per-segment analysis (mobile/desktop) with BH correction of the interaction tests; (d) relate to the frequentist Wald CI — when do they diverge?
  9. RCBD. Add to the data of 9.1 a moderate block × variety (interaction) effect, redo the ANOVA, test the interaction; build the same trial under a completely randomized design (same values, assignment blind to the blocks) and show the loss of F. Write the simultaneous (Tukey) confidence interval for the 6 comparisons.
  10. Communication. You have \(\hat d = 3.1\), 95 % CI = [−3.5; 9.7], p = 0.041, \(n = 80\). Write in 5 lines: (a) a misleading "press" summary; (b) an honest summary; (c) list the three graphical manipulations that would reverse the reader's impression, and their fixes.
  11. Project. INRAE or open data from a blocked trial (or kaggle: agricultural crop yield): from plan to conclusions — map the potential biases, justify the plan, ANOVA + diagnostics, bootstrap of the contrasts, multiple-comparison correction, final one-page report with honest visualizations and limits. Grading: 40 % plan, 40 % correct analysis, 20 % communication.
