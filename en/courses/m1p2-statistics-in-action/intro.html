How to run a statistical study end to end on real data: plan the trial, randomize, compute the required power, hunt biases, correct multiple tests, bootstrap, then communicate honest results? This module teaches the statistician's craft rather than the theory of tests: every section starts from a concrete situation, from the agricultural trial to the A/B test. This methodological rigor is a valuable differentiator in a machine learning master's program, where the data collection plan often makes the difference between a result and an illusion.

What you will be able to do

  • Run the complete cycle of a statistical study, from the initial question to communicable conclusions.
  • Design an experimental plan: randomization, blocks, and a power computation suited to the objective.
  • Identify the biases of observational studies and the classic pitfalls of inference.
  • Apply the basics of causal inference: potential outcomes and difference-in-differences (DiD).
  • Practice regression for real: diagnostics, transformations, reasoned decisions.
  • Correct multiple tests (Bonferroni, Benjamini-Hochberg) according to the goal.
  • Build intervals and tests by bootstrap on arbitrary data.
  • Run a modern A/B test: sequential, targeted effects — and report the results honestly.

Official objectives and syllabus (2026-2027 catalog)

According to the APM_52066_EP — "Statistique en Action" description: the official objective is to show students how statistics are used in practice to answer a precise question, by introducing a series of important model-based approaches. Students learn to select and use the appropriate strategic methodologies, on concrete examples of real datasets from different fields — medicine, genomics, ecology, among others. All analyses are carried out in R; knowledge of R programming is not required (only basic scripting). The detailed session-by-session syllabus is not published on the description (not communicated); the course unit does however appear in the 2026-2027 catalog via the "P2 – Applied Mathematics" track of the engineering cycle, without appearing in the SynapseS composition of the LLGA track S2.

Where this course fits in the program

Module of period 2 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), offered as an option: the methodological "reality check" of the master's program, taught by Zacharie Naulet (École Polytechnique & INRAE, MaIAGE team). Official prerequisites: not communicated (the "Prerequisites" tab of the SynapseS description is empty); the description only specifies that no knowledge of R is required, basic scripting being sufficient.

  • Prerequisites — the background of Refresher in Statistics (tests, intervals, estimation) and the framework of Machine Learning; the review section reactivates the "survival kit" actually used afterwards.
  • Downstream modules — the culture of intervals, bootstrap and uncertainty built here directly feeds Privacy and Uncertainty Quantification (M2), which deepens the uncertainty quantification of models.

Course outline

  • Review: the statistician's survival kit — the only results from the statistics course that are really used, every week.
  • The life cycle of a statistical study — from question to report: the map of the craft.
  • Experimental designs: randomization, blocks, power — deciding before collecting how many and how.
  • Observational versus experimental: biases — why we do not always randomize, and what that costs.
  • Causal inference: potential outcomes and DiD — approaching a causal effect with imperfect data.
  • Regression in practice: diagnostics and transformations — using, checking and repairing a regression model.
  • Multiple testing: Bonferroni and Benjamini-Hochberg — what to do when testing many hypotheses at once.
  • The bootstrap in practice — intervals without closed formulas, on almost any statistic.
  • A/B testing: sequential and targeted effects — the randomized trial as digital platforms practice it.
  • Case study: agricultural trials and blocks — the historical origin of experimental designs, on a complete case.
  • Communicating: intervals, p-values, honest visualizations — making results readable without distorting them.
  • Research of the teaching team — a tour of the research work of Zacharie Naulet.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Validate the "survival kit" of the review section: it, and not an advanced chapter, determines your ease throughout the module.
  • Work through each section on a real dataset: this course's specificity is that obstacles come from the data, not from formulas.
  • Simulate: the power of a test, the inflation of false positives under multiple testing and the coverage of the bootstrap are understood in a few lines of code.
  • Before every analysis, write the plan — hypotheses, sample size, criterion: the reflex this module seeks to instill.
  • Apply the same rigor to your machine learning models: evaluating a model is a statistical study in its own right.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next; the final case study brings them all together.
The LLGA positioning In a master's program oriented toward LLMs and graphs, this module brings the discipline that makes everything else credible: A/B tests and rigorous evaluation are the daily life of the product teams deploying models, and the culture of uncertainty continues in M2 in Privacy and Uncertainty Quantification. It is also a sought-after profile in research — at the interface with applied sciences, as at INRAE — and in industrial data science wherever decisions have consequences.
