11. Research of the teaching team

Zacharie Naulet is a junior professor (INRAE, MaIAGE team — Mathematics and Computer Science applied to Food and the Environment) and teaches at École Polytechnique; his PhD (Université Paris-Saclay) was on nonparametric Bayesian methods for signal and image processing. His themes — nonparametric Bayesian statistics, exchangeability, hidden state space models, applications to biological data and networks — directly feed several chapters of this module: the bootstrap (ch. 7), multiple testing and the estimation of \(\pi_0\) (ch. 6), network models (ch. 8–9).

Example 11.1 — Exchangeability and de Finetti: the backbone De Finetti's theorem (1931) states that an infinite sequence of exchangeable variables is conditionally i.i.d. with respect to a random law: this is the foundation of Bayesian inference. The quantitative question — how fast does exchangeability approach i.i.d.? — was solved by Dolera & Favaro ("De Finetti's theorem: rate of convergence in Kolmogorov distance", Bernoulli 2020; arXiv:1802.02244) — work by colleagues in the field, cited here as a standard, and not by the instructor. Z. Naulet's work extends the de Finetti program on two fronts:
  • exchangeable relational data: V. Veitch, E. Sharma, Z. Naulet, D. Roy, "Exchangeable modelling of relational data…" — train/test splitting and sparse Poisson factorization for graphs (a bridge with the Graph ML module); Z. Naulet, J. Rousseau, F. Caron, "Asymptotic Analysis of Statistical Estimators related to MultiGraphex Processes under Misspecification" — what becomes of the estimators when the graphex model is wrong, a statistician-in-action question;
  • species sampling: C. Balocchi, S. Favaro, Z. Naulet, "Bayesian Nonparametric Inference for 'Species-sampling' Problems" (Statistical Science): how many species — genetic variants, words, users — have we not seen? This is the "missing mass" that also controls the adaptive FDR of ch. 6 (with S. Favaro: "Optimal estimation of high-order missing masses…", IEEE Trans. IT; "Near-optimal estimation of the unseen under regularly varying tail populations", Bernoulli 2023).
Example 11.2 — From the bootstrap to networks, and the bridge to privacy
  • Bootstrap for graphs: Z. Naulet, D. Roy, E. Sharma, V. Veitch, "Bootstrap estimators for the tail-index and for the count statistics of graphex processes" — resampling networks to estimate uncertainty (ch. 7, research version);
  • hidden models: K. Abraham, E. Gassiat, Z. Naulet, "Frontiers to the learning of nonparametric hidden Markov models"; E. Gassiat, I. Kaddouri, Z. Naulet, "Clustering risk in Non-parametric Hidden Markov and I.I.D. Models"; quantum homodyne tomography with E. Barat (arXiv:1610.01895);
  • disclosure risk: F. Camerlenghi, S. Favaro, Z. Naulet, F. Panero, "Optimal disclosure risk assessment" (Annals of Statistics, 2021): how far can data be published without re-identifying individuals? — the gateway to the M2 module "Privacy and Uncertainty Quantification";
  • nonparametric estimation: "Adaptive Bayesian density estimation in sup-norm"; "Tails assumptions and posterior concentration rates for mixtures of Gaussians" (with J. Rousseau); risk of the minimum-norm estimator in spike covariance (with Y. Mahdaviyeh).
Other recent work: change-point detection in preferential attachment graphs (with I. Kaddouri and E. Gassiat), robust community detection (SubSearch, with L. Bianco and C. Keribin).
Common thread This work shares a signature: guarantees (minimaxity, concentration rates, exact risk) for usable procedures on real data — exactly the ambition of this module at the M1 level, and its M2 extension (privacy, uncertainty, calibration).
