9. Case study: agricultural trials and blocks

Agricultural experimentation is the cradle of experimental design: Fisher at Rothamsted in the 1920s invented randomization, blocks and analysis of variance precisely because plots are heterogeneous — the same problem as your users or your patients. It is also the daily ground of INRAE, the instructor's institution: multi-year multi-location variety trials, complete blocks and alpha-lattices (Patterson & Williams, 1976) to screen hundreds of varieties.

Observed \(F_{3,12}\) \(= 30\) against a threshold of \(F_{0.95;\,3,12} = 3.49\) — highly significant varietal differences. Pairwise comparisons: standard deviation of a difference of means \(\sqrt{2 \times 1.0/5} = 0.63\), LSD at the 5 % level: \(2.179 \times 0.63 = 1.38\) — A (53.0) > B (52.6) > C = D (48.6); A against B is not separated (0.4 < 1.38). The gain from blocking: without blocks, the block variability would join the residual: \((480 + 12)/16 = 30.75\) instead of \(1.0\) — the same trial under a completely randomized design would have an \(F\) on the order of 1: the trial would have seen nothing. This is literally Fisher's lesson: planning well beats modeling well after the fact.
Natural continuation Multi-environment trials: genotype × environment interactions, AMMI/GGE models (the PCA of the Machine Learning module ch. 6 applied to interactions); resolvable incomplete blocks (alpha-lattices) when \(K >\) block size; spatial analysis (fertility trend by rows/columns). The INRAE thread: dozens of trials, thousands of plots, one imperative — randomize, block, estimate the uncertainty, communicate honestly (ch. 10).
