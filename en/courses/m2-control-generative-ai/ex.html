Exercises / Labs

  1. Lab: constrained decoding on regex (the module's lab). (a) Build the DFA of ^\d{2}/\d{2}/\d{4}$ by hand (states, co-accessible pruning), table \(\mathrm{Adm}(q)\) on a mini-vocabulary including the multi-character tokens "12/", "19"; (b) implement DfaMask (code of ch. 3) with a small GPT's tokenizer; (c) plug in as a HuggingFace LogitsProcessor and generate 1,000 dates; (d) measure the pass rate with/without mask and the time overhead compared to free generation.
    Guidance (expected results): without constraint, the pass rate of a small model on the exact requested date plateaus (2026-08-26, 26/08/2026 formats mixed); with mask: 100% — including false but syntactically valid dates (31/02/2026): semantics requires one more layer (bounds validation), the conclusion of ch. 10 experienced in the lab. Overhead: ~0% (the mask is negligible next to the transformer pass); the real cost is the precomputation of the index, paid once. ∎
  2. The z-test in full. With \(\gamma = 0.5\), \(\delta = 2\): (a) simulate 10,000 human texts (iid uniform tokens) and empirically verify \(\mathbb{P}(z > 4) \approx 3 \times 10^{-5}\); (b) estimate \(p_\delta\) empirically by simulating the biased softmax at temperature 1; (c) plot the power of the test as a function of \(T \in [20, 500]\); (d) derive the minimal \(T\) for power 0.9 (compare with example 6.3); (e) repeat with \(\gamma = 0.25\) and conclude on the tuning.
    Guidance: (a) the distribution of \(z\) sticks to \(\mathcal N(0,1)\) from \(T \geq 50\) (De Moivre-Laplace); (b) \(p_\delta\) depends on the standard deviation of the logits; with logits \(\mathcal N(0,1)\), \(p_\delta \approx 0.75\); (c) sigmoidal power in \(\sqrt{T}\); (d) \(T \approx 30\)-50 for \((0.5, 2)\); (e) \(\gamma = 0.25\) divides the variance under H0 (\(\times 0.75\)) and concentrates the signal — slightly better at fixed \(\delta\), but lower text quality: the triple trade-off of ch. 6 is verified numerically. ∎
  3. Steering step by step. On GPT-2 small: build \(v = h^{(l)}(\texttt{Love}) - h^{(l)}(\texttt{Hate})\) for \(l \in \{2, 5, 8, 10\}\); sweep \(\alpha \in \{-2, -1, -0.5, 0, 0.5, 1, 2\}\); measure (i) frequency of "marriage" words, (ii) perplexity, (iii) intra-batch diversity (self-BLEU).
    Guidance: the topic effect is clear and quasi-linear in \(\alpha\) at small \(\alpha\) (prop. 4.3), perplexity moves little up to \(\alpha \approx 2\) then derails; diversity drops as \(|\alpha|\) grows — the same fidelity/diversity curve as CFG (ch. 9), again. Discuss: why does steering "succeed" on broad latent concepts and fail to impose a precise format (answer: full support — ch. 2). ∎
  4. Theoretical fidelity/diversity frontier. Prove the entropy bound 9.2 (Gibbs), then generalize example 9.4: arbitrary initial law, score \(c\), plot \((\mathrm{Fid}(w), H(w))\) for \(w \in [0, 10]\) and verify that the curve converges to the corner \((\max c, 0)\). What happens to the curve if \(c\) is noisy?
    Guidance: normalize \(p_w \propto p\, e^{w c}\) (exponential family); \(H(w)\) decreases (derive \(dH/dw = -w\,\mathrm{Var}_w(c)\) — a classic identity: the derivative of the log-partition gives the variance, that of the entropy its weighted variance); with noise, a plateau appears before the collapse: the analyst chooses the elbow. ∎
  5. Zero-convolution: the gradient under the magnifier. For the ControlNet branch of theorem 7.2 at initialization: (a) show that the output is exactly zero; (b) compute \(\partial y / \partial W_2\) and show it is nonzero; (c) show that \(\partial y / \partial \Theta_c = 0\) at initialization and explain why this is not a deadlock; (d) compare with random initialization (branching \(F_c\) without \(Z\)): simulate 10 descent steps on a toy task and compare the initial losses.
    Guidance: (a) \(Z_2 = W_2 h = 0\); (b) \(\partial y/\partial W_2 = h_c \neq 0\) (activations of the copy initialized at \(\Theta\)); (c) chain rule through \(W_2 = 0\), unlocked as soon as \(W_2\)'s first update happens; (d) the variant without zero-conv starts from a high loss and destroys the frozen model's output — the experiment reproduces the motivation figure of the ControlNet paper. ∎
  6. Attacking your own watermark (bonus). After lab 2: paraphrase the marked texts (via an LLM, instruction "rewrite with the same ideas"), measure the drop in \(z\) as a function of the rate of replaced tokens, and estimate the rate needed to fall below \(z = 2\). Conclude on the sentence "a watermark is a marker, not a proof".
    Guidance: each green→red replacement shifts \(\mathbb E[z]\) by about \((p_\delta - \gamma)\big/\sqrt{T\gamma(1-\gamma)}\); going from \(\mathbb E[z] = 7\) to 2 requires rewriting the majority of tokens — the text that survives the attack is so transformed that content traceability (plagiarism) becomes the issue again, not that of the machine. Loop closed with ch. 10. ∎
