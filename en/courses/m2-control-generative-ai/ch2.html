2. Taxonomy: four families of control

Definition 2.1 — The four families Let \(c\) be the control signal (instruction, target property, admissible language).
  1. Conditioning: \(c\) enters the model (prompt, prefix, fused embedding); the law becomes \(p_\theta(\cdot \mid x, c)\). Zero inference cost, no guarantee — soft control;
  2. Guidance: one biases the sampling toward \(c\) without relearning \(p_\theta\) — guided score \(\hat\epsilon_u + w(\hat\epsilon_c - \hat\epsilon_u)\) in diffusion (ch. 7), activation steering for LLMs (ch. 4), gradient guidance of flows (ch. 8); the strength \(w\) tunes fidelity at the price of diversity;
  3. Hard constraints: one restricts the support — automaton-constrained decoding (ch. 3), valence masks (ch. 8), inpainting. A 100% guarantee; risk: a renormalized law, possibly impoverished;
  4. Post-processing / filtering: generate, check \(c\), reject (best-of-\(n\), resampling, correction); exact but costly when \(c\) is rare.
Theorem 2.2 — The mask is an exact conditioning (renormalization) Let \(S \subseteq \mathcal{Y}^*\) be a set of admissible outputs and \(p_\theta\) a stepwise law (allowed at each step: a subset of tokens). If at each step one masks the tokens that make \(S\) unreachable, the effective law is exactly $$\tilde p_\theta(y \mid x) = \frac{p_\theta(y \mid x)\, \mathbf{1}[y \in S]} {\sum_{y' \in S} p_\theta(y' \mid x)} = p_\theta(y \mid x,\ y \in S).$$
By recursion over the steps: at step \(t\), the mask allows exactly the tokens \(v\) such that there exists a suffix \(s\) with \(y_{<t} v s \in S\). The probability of an admissible prefix is therefore \(p_\theta(\text{prefix}) \cdot Z_{t}^{-1}\) where \(Z_t\) is the mass of allowed continuations — the normalization factor telescopes from step to step and, at the final step, the total mass over \(S\) equals 1: the conditional law is exact. Precious corollary: if the model estimates correctly the ratios of admissible outputs (the training data are already in \(S\) up to a few exceptions), then \(\tilde p_\theta \approx p_\theta(\cdot \mid S) \approx p_\star\) — the mask is nearly free in bias and massive in guarantee. This is the analysis made in ch. 3 of the Graph Generative Models module for valences; it holds for any language. ∎
Proposition 2.3 — Rejection costs \(1/p_\star(S)\) Filtering by rejection (drawing iid until landing in \(S\)) also realizes \(p_\theta(\cdot \mid S)\), but the number of draws follows a geometric law of expectation \(1/p_\theta(S \mid x)\).
Each draw is an independent Bernoulli trial with success probability \(q = p_\theta(S \mid x)\); the waiting time for the first success is geometric, \(\mathbb{E}[N] = \sum_{k \geq 1} k(1-q)^{k-1}q = 1/q\). For complex unconstrained JSON, \(q\) can be \(10^{-2}\) (100 draws on average); for a valid molecule without constraint, GraphAF measures 68% (ch. 8); for a sentence satisfying both ten rare constraints, \(q\) collapses exponentially — hence the structural superiority of the mask, which pays only a single pass. ∎

Hard or soft: how to choose? A favorite exam question. If failure is catastrophic (the parser breaks, the molecule is invalid): hard constraint. If failure is gradual (tone, style, relevance): guidance, with the strength tuned by the fidelity/diversity curve of ch. 9. In practice one stacks: format constraint (hard) + alignment (RLHF) + watermark (provenance) — the three layers of 2026 production systems, which ch. 10 shows fall… together if one is not careful.
