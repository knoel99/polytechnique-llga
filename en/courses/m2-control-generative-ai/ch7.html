7. Controlling diffusions: CFG, ControlNet, inversion

Control of diffusion models was introduced in M1 (Multimodal GenAI module ch. 4); here we give the short proof (the part we had not done), then the two industrial standards of spatial control (ControlNet) and editing (inversion).

7.1. CFG: the short proof

Theorem 7.1 — Classifier guidance and CFG are the same exponential reweighting Let \(\hat\epsilon_c, \hat\epsilon_u\) denote the noises predicted with/without conditioning, and \(\hat\epsilon_w = \hat\epsilon_u + w\,(\hat\epsilon_c - \hat\epsilon_u)\) the CFG prediction. Since \(\hat\epsilon(x_t, t) = -\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\, \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t)\) (score parametrization), the CFG combination realizes $$\nabla_{x_t} \log p_w(x_t) \;=\; \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t) + w\, \nabla_{x_t} \log p(c \mid x_t) \;=\; \nabla_{x_t} \log \big[p(x_t \mid c)^w \, p(x_t)^{1-w}\big],$$ that is, sampling from the reweighted law \(p_w \propto p(x_t|c)^w p(x_t)^{1-w}\) — exactly the family of theorem 5.1 (RLHF) with \(w \leftrightarrow 1/\beta\).
Linearity of the score: \(\hat\epsilon_w = (1 - w)\hat\epsilon_u + w \hat\epsilon_c\) (rewrite the combination); each term is proportional to the gradient of the corresponding log-density, so the sum is the gradient of the sum of the logs. For the conditional term, Bayes' rule \(p(x_t \mid c) \propto p(c \mid x_t)\, p(x_t)\) gives \(\nabla \log p(x_t|c)^w p(x_t)^{1-w} = w\nabla\log p(c|x_t) + \nabla \log p(x_t)\) — which is precisely the formula of classifier guidance with weight \(w\): CFG obtains it without a classifier, by training the same network with conditioning dropout. At \(w = 1\): pure conditional model; \(w > 1\): over-guidance (fidelity \(\uparrow\), diversity \(\downarrow\), color saturation — the classic visual symptom); \(w < 1\): interpolation toward the unconditional. ∎

7.2. ControlNet: spatial control, without breaking the pre-training

Theorem 7.2 — ControlNet architecture (Zhang, Rao & Agrawala, ICCV 2023) Let \(F(x ; \Theta)\) be a frozen UNet block (operation on the activation \(x\) of layer \(i\)). ControlNet branches off a trainable copy initialized at \(\Theta\) and locks the output with two null convolutions (zero-convolutions): $$y \;=\; F(x;\Theta) \;+\; Z_2\Big(F_c\big(Z_1(c;\Theta_{z_1});\ \Theta_c\big);\ \Theta_{z_2}\Big),$$ where \(c\) is the control map (Canny edges, depth, skeletal pose…); \(Z_i\) is a \(1\times1\) convolution with zero weights at initialization.
Identity property at the start. At \(t = 0\): \(Z_2(\cdot) = W_2 \, h = 0\) since \(W_2 = 0\) — the branch outputs exactly zero, and the composed model is identically the pre-trained model: fine-tuning starts from the initial quality, without "catastrophic shift". The gradient does not vanish. The derivative of the output with respect to \(W_2\) equals \(\partial y / \partial W_2 = h_c \neq 0\) (the copy \(F_c\), initialized at \(\Theta\), produces nonzero activations): the first update pulls \(W_2\) away from zero, which then unlocks the gradients toward \(F_c\) and \(Z_1\) (which were zero as long as \(W_2 = 0\), by the chain rule). Compare the naive alternative (branching \(F_c\) directly): at step 0, uncontrolled random noise is added to the activations of the frozen UNet and destroys the coherence of the output for hundreds of steps. ∎
Why a "copy" and not a "new network" The copy inherits the pre-trained features: control learns the correspondence control map → existing rich features, not vision from scratch — hence the data efficiency (a few thousand pairs suffice for a new type of control). This is a general pattern of the module: condition around a frozen model and protect the interface with an identity initialization (the mask of ch. 3 is the exact analogue for discrete slots: allow only what preserves the invariant).

7.3. Editing a real image: inversion and null-text

Proposition 7.3 — DDIM inversion and its failure under CFG DDIM defines a deterministic step linking \(x_t\) and \(x_{t-1}\) via the prediction \(\hat x_0(x_t, c)\); inversion traverses the relation backwards (reminder: Multimodal GenAI module ch. 4): $$x_{t+1} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t+1}}\, \hat x_0(x_t, c) + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t+1}}\; \hat\epsilon_c(x_t, c).$$ With CFG, the applied "noise" depends on a \(w\)-combination of the conditional/unconditional predictions; inversion becomes an implicit equation (the step depends on \(x_t\) through two networks): error accumulates, the reconstruction drifts. Null-text inversion (Mokady et al., CVPR 2023): keep the inversion trajectory as an anchor and optimize, step by step, the null condition embedding \(c_\varnothing^{(t)}\) to minimize \(\|\hat x_0\big(x_t; c, c_\varnothing^{(t)}\big) - x_0\|^2\) — a few dozen gradient steps per image; editing (Prompt-to-Prompt: substituting the cross-attention maps; InstructPix2Pix: learning editing on synthetic data) then operates along this faithful trajectory.
Example 7.4 — CFG by hand (pen-and-paper, in the graph module's format) Vocabulary of 3 artistic "directions", logits of step \(t\) without conditioning: \((\text{painting}: 2.0,\ \text{photo}: 1.5,\ \text{3D}: 0.5)\); with condition "watercolor": \((3.0,\ 0.5,\ 0.5)\). At \(w = 3\): guided logits \(= (1-w)\,u + w\,c = (-4.0,\ -1.0,\ 0.5)\)… in the equivalent additive form \(u + w(c - u) = (5.0,\ -1.0,\ 0.5)\) — softmax \(\approx (0.95, 0.007,\ 0.04)\): the target style crushes the distribution, exactly like the molecular CFG of example 7.3 of the Graph Generative Models module. \(w = 8\) → a single mode survives: the curve of ch. 9, readable on a single step.
