1. Why control: safety, fidelity, intellectual property

A generator is a law over an immense space. Asking it politely (a prompt) only constrains this law statistically: the exact format, the safety of the content, the traceability of the origin are guaranteed by no theorem. This chapter motivates each family of needs through its characteristic failures.

Definition 1.1 — Controlling a generator Let \(p_\theta(\cdot \mid x)\) be a conditional law over outputs \(y \in \mathcal{Y}^*\) (token sequences, images, graphs). Controlling means replacing \(p_\theta\) with a law \(\tilde p\) that (i) lives on a specified subset \(S \subseteq \mathcal{Y}^*\) (support constraint: a language, a JSON schema, a chemical valence); or (ii) maximizes in expectation a property \(c\) (fidelity constraint: following the instruction, the style, the target); or (iii) carries a verifiable mark of origin (provenance constraint: watermarking). Chapters 3 to 8 realize (i)-(ii)-(iii) through different mechanisms; ch. 2 will organize them.
Example 1.2 — Three real failures, three needs
  1. Format safety: one asks the LLM for a JSON of the form {"user": ...}; it replies with a trailing comma, a typographic apostrophe, or worse a preamble "Here is the requested JSON". Any downstream parser breaks. The need: \(S =\) the strict JSON language, a 100% guarantee — that is ch. 3.
  2. Fidelity: one asks for a neutral summary in formal French; the model slips into casual style one time out of ten. The prompt guarantees nothing; the need is statistical — soft steering of the law: ch. 4 and 5.
  3. Provenance: a text circulates: human or machine? The classifier is unreliable; the need is cryptographic: a statistical mark planted at generation, testable — ch. 6.
Theorem 1.3 — Intellectual property and law as drivers of control Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 ("AI Act", art. 50) imposes provenance transparency: providers of generative systems must mark their outputs in a machine-readable way and make them detectable as artificially generated. Concretely: watermarking (ch. 6) is no longer an academic exercise but a compliance requirement; likewise for audio marking (cf. Signal Processing module) and images. This legal framework motivates the control/robustness articulation (ch. 10): an attackable mark is not a proof.
Control cuts across modalities The module's guiding thread: the same four mechanisms (condition, guide, constrain the support, filter a posteriori) apply to text, images, and graphs. The tools differ (automata for text; ControlNet and CFG for images; valence masks for graphs), the theory does not. The table in ch. 2 sets the grid; each chapter fills it in for one modality.
