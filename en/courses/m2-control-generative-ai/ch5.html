5. RLHF: control in behavior space

RLHF was presented as alignment (cf. LLMs M2 module ch. 5 — we do not redo its practice); here we reread it as a control mechanism, and we prove that it is — mathematically — the twin of classifier-free guidance in ch. 7. This double reading is the module's synthesis point.

Theorem 5.1 — RLHF = exponential guidance of the output law (closed-form solution proved) The alignment program with KL penalty reads $$\pi^* = \arg\max_{\pi}\ \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}\big[r(y, x)\big] \;-\; \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot \mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\big).$$ Its unique solution is $$\pi^*(y \mid x) \;=\; \frac{1}{Z(x)}\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y \mid x)\, \exp\big(r(y, x)/\beta\big), \qquad Z(x) = \sum_{y'} \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y' \mid x) e^{r(y',x)/\beta}.$$
One maximizes over the law \(\pi\) (an infinite-dimensional variable). Set \(J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_\pi[r] - \beta\,\mathbb{E}_\pi[\log \frac{\pi}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}]\). First order: for every perturbation \(\pi + \varepsilon(\nu - \pi)\) (\(\nu\) any law), \(\frac{d}{d\varepsilon} J\big|_{\varepsilon=0} = \mathbb{E}_{\nu}[r - \beta\log\frac{\pi}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \beta - c] = 0\) for all \(\nu\), where \(c\) is the multiplier of the constraint \(\sum_y \pi(y) = 1\). This forces \(r(y,x) - \beta \log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y|x)}\) constant in \(y\): \(\log \frac{\pi}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} = r/\beta + \mathrm{const}\), giving the result (the constant is fixed by normalization; the strict concavity of the KL gives uniqueness). Control reading: the aligned policy is the reference law reweighted by an exponential factor in the reward, exactly as CFG reweights \(p(x_t)\) by \(p(c \mid x_t)^w\) (ch. 7); \(\beta\) (the inverse of the reward temperature) plays the role of the guidance strength \(w\), and the KL is the budget of allowed deviation — high fidelity to \(r\), low diversity if \(\beta\) is small. ∎
Proposition 5.2 — DPO: the same solution, without a reward model By rewriting \(r = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} + \beta\log Z\), the preference between \(y_w \succ y_l\) is expressed directly in policies: the DPO loss $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\log \sigma\Big(\beta \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l|x)}\Big)$$ is the likelihood of the preferences under the closed form of theorem 5.1: DPO learns the same exponential guidance by direct regression on pairs. GRPO (group-relative reinforcement) is its extension via within-group comparisons — developed in detail in ch. 5 of the LLMs module (M2), which should be consulted for the actual training.
Reward hacking: when control misses its target The reward is a spec; the model optimizes the spec, not the intention. The mechanism (well documented): the closed-form solution 5.1 concentrates mass on the peaks of \(r\) — including artifactual peaks (long answers, sycophancy, formats favored by the reward model). The KL brakes (budget!), without forbidding: with KL budget zero, \(\pi^* = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\); as \(\beta \to 0\), collapse onto the argmax of \(r\). Same structure as the CFG fidelity/diversity trade-off — ch. 9 for the measurement, ch. 10 for the attack (exploiting the rewarder's artifacts is a control hijack).
