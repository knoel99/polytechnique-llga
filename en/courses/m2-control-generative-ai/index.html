Knowing how to generate is not knowing how to steer: a generative model produces texts, images, or graphs, but it is control — decoding within a grammar, steering activations, aligning behaviors, proving an origin — that turns a curious generator into a reliable tool. This module builds a taxonomy of the four families of control then rolls it out end to end: constrained decoding, steering, RLHF, watermarking, control of diffusions and graph generators, up to the evaluation of the fidelity/diversity trade-off and its robustness to attacks. In the LLGA master, this is the cross-cutting course that connects LLMs, images, and graphs through a single question: how can one impose a constraint without killing the generation? Official course description not published (the 2026-2027 SynapseS S3/S4 catalog is empty): these course materials expand on the brochure's announcement, and their own additions are flagged as going beyond the syllabus.

1. Why control: safety, fidelity, intellectual property

A generator is a law over an immense space. Asking it politely (a prompt) only constrains this law statistically: the exact format, the safety of the content, the traceability of the origin are guaranteed by no theorem. This chapter motivates each family of needs through its characteristic failures.

Definition 1.1 — Controlling a generator Let \(p_\theta(\cdot \mid x)\) be a conditional law over outputs \(y \in \mathcal{Y}^*\) (token sequences, images, graphs). Controlling means replacing \(p_\theta\) with a law \(\tilde p\) that (i) lives on a specified subset \(S \subseteq \mathcal{Y}^*\) (support constraint: a language, a JSON schema, a chemical valence); or (ii) maximizes in expectation a property \(c\) (fidelity constraint: following the instruction, the style, the target); or (iii) carries a verifiable mark of origin (provenance constraint: watermarking). Chapters 3 to 8 realize (i)-(ii)-(iii) through different mechanisms; ch. 2 will organize them.
Example 1.2 — Three real failures, three needs
  1. Format safety: one asks the LLM for a JSON of the form {"user": ...}; it replies with a trailing comma, a typographic apostrophe, or worse a preamble "Here is the requested JSON". Any downstream parser breaks. The need: \(S =\) the strict JSON language, a 100% guarantee — that is ch. 3.
  2. Fidelity: one asks for a neutral summary in formal French; the model slips into casual style one time out of ten. The prompt guarantees nothing; the need is statistical — soft steering of the law: ch. 4 and 5.
  3. Provenance: a text circulates: human or machine? The classifier is unreliable; the need is cryptographic: a statistical mark planted at generation, testable — ch. 6.
Theorem 1.3 — Intellectual property and law as drivers of control Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 ("AI Act", art. 50) imposes provenance transparency: providers of generative systems must mark their outputs in a machine-readable way and make them detectable as artificially generated. Concretely: watermarking (ch. 6) is no longer an academic exercise but a compliance requirement; likewise for audio marking (cf. Signal Processing module) and images. This legal framework motivates the control/robustness articulation (ch. 10): an attackable mark is not a proof.
Control cuts across modalities The module's guiding thread: the same four mechanisms (condition, guide, constrain the support, filter a posteriori) apply to text, images, and graphs. The tools differ (automata for text; ControlNet and CFG for images; valence masks for graphs), the theory does not. The table in ch. 2 sets the grid; each chapter fills it in for one modality.

2. Taxonomy: four families of control

Definition 2.1 — The four families Let \(c\) be the control signal (instruction, target property, admissible language).
  1. Conditioning: \(c\) enters the model (prompt, prefix, fused embedding); the law becomes \(p_\theta(\cdot \mid x, c)\). Zero inference cost, no guarantee — soft control;
  2. Guidance: one biases the sampling toward \(c\) without relearning \(p_\theta\) — guided score \(\hat\epsilon_u + w(\hat\epsilon_c - \hat\epsilon_u)\) in diffusion (ch. 7), activation steering for LLMs (ch. 4), gradient guidance of flows (ch. 8); the strength \(w\) tunes fidelity at the price of diversity;
  3. Hard constraints: one restricts the support — automaton-constrained decoding (ch. 3), valence masks (ch. 8), inpainting. A 100% guarantee; risk: a renormalized law, possibly impoverished;
  4. Post-processing / filtering: generate, check \(c\), reject (best-of-\(n\), resampling, correction); exact but costly when \(c\) is rare.
Theorem 2.2 — The mask is an exact conditioning (renormalization) Let \(S \subseteq \mathcal{Y}^*\) be a set of admissible outputs and \(p_\theta\) a stepwise law (allowed at each step: a subset of tokens). If at each step one masks the tokens that make \(S\) unreachable, the effective law is exactly $$\tilde p_\theta(y \mid x) = \frac{p_\theta(y \mid x)\, \mathbf{1}[y \in S]} {\sum_{y' \in S} p_\theta(y' \mid x)} = p_\theta(y \mid x,\ y \in S).$$
By recursion over the steps: at step \(t\), the mask allows exactly the tokens \(v\) such that there exists a suffix \(s\) with \(y_{<t} v s \in S\). The probability of an admissible prefix is therefore \(p_\theta(\text{prefix}) \cdot Z_{t}^{-1}\) where \(Z_t\) is the mass of allowed continuations — the normalization factor telescopes from step to step and, at the final step, the total mass over \(S\) equals 1: the conditional law is exact. Precious corollary: if the model estimates correctly the ratios of admissible outputs (the training data are already in \(S\) up to a few exceptions), then \(\tilde p_\theta \approx p_\theta(\cdot \mid S) \approx p_\star\) — the mask is nearly free in bias and massive in guarantee. This is the analysis made in ch. 3 of the Graph Generative Models module for valences; it holds for any language. ∎
Proposition 2.3 — Rejection costs \(1/p_\star(S)\) Filtering by rejection (drawing iid until landing in \(S\)) also realizes \(p_\theta(\cdot \mid S)\), but the number of draws follows a geometric law of expectation \(1/p_\theta(S \mid x)\).
Each draw is an independent Bernoulli trial with success probability \(q = p_\theta(S \mid x)\); the waiting time for the first success is geometric, \(\mathbb{E}[N] = \sum_{k \geq 1} k(1-q)^{k-1}q = 1/q\). For complex unconstrained JSON, \(q\) can be \(10^{-2}\) (100 draws on average); for a valid molecule without constraint, GraphAF measures 68% (ch. 8); for a sentence satisfying both ten rare constraints, \(q\) collapses exponentially — hence the structural superiority of the mask, which pays only a single pass. ∎

Hard or soft: how to choose? A favorite exam question. If failure is catastrophic (the parser breaks, the molecule is invalid): hard constraint. If failure is gradual (tone, style, relevance): guidance, with the strength tuned by the fidelity/diversity curve of ch. 9. In practice one stacks: format constraint (hard) + alignment (RLHF) + watermark (provenance) — the three layers of 2026 production systems, which ch. 10 shows fall… together if one is not careful.

3. Constrained decoding: grammars, automata, masks

The technical heart of the module for text: guaranteeing that an output authorized token by token belongs to a formal language — regular expression, context-free grammar (JSON, SQL), or logical constraint. The founding idea (PICARD, then Outlines and XGrammar): decoding is a walk in an automaton, and the LLM is only allowed to follow valid transitions.

3.1. From regex to automaton

Definition 3.1 — DFA and regular language A deterministic finite automaton is a quintuple \(A = (Q, \Sigma, \delta, q_0, F)\): states \(Q\), alphabet \(\Sigma\), transition \(\delta : Q \times \Sigma \to Q\), initial state \(q_0\), accepting states \(F\). One extends \(\delta\) to words by \(\delta^*(q, uv) = \delta^*(\delta^*(q, u), v)\). The recognized language is \(L(A) = \{w \in \Sigma^* : \delta^*(q_0, w) \in F\}\). Construction: regex \(\to\) \(\varepsilon\)-NFA (Thompson, linear in the pattern size), then determinization by subset construction (\(2^{|Q|}\) states at worst, rare in practice). Course example, the ISO date: \d{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])-(0[1-9]|[12]\d|3[01]) — a nontrivial \(F\): "2026-02-31" must be syntaxically accepted (calendar semantics is not a simple regular language: we return to this).
Proposition 3.2 — Prune before masking: co-accessibility For masking, one replaces \(A\) with its co-accessible sub-automaton: one removes the states \(q\) from which no accepting state is reachable (computation: reverse traversal of the transitions from \(F\), \(\mathcal{O}(|Q| + |\delta|)\)). It is this sub-automaton that guarantees that a prefix always remains extensible into a complete word: without pruning, the decoder could lock itself in a dead end (no admissible token, including EOS) and generate indefinitely.

3.2. The masking algorithm, in detail

Theorem 3.3 — Constrained decoding by state→tokens index (Willard & Louf, 2023) Let \(V\) be the tokenizer vocabulary (multi-character tokens!). One precomputes, for each state \(q\) of the pruned DFA, the set of admissible tokens $$\mathrm{Adm}(q) = \big\{ v \in V \ : \ \delta^*(q, \mathrm{str}(v)) \ \text{is defined and co-accessible} \big\},$$ with the table \(\mathrm{next}(q, v) = \delta^*(q, \mathrm{str}(v))\). Constrained decoding replaces each distribution with $$p^{\mathrm{mask}}_\theta(v \mid y_{<t}) \propto p_\theta(v \mid y_{<t})\, \mathbf{1}\big[v \in \mathrm{Adm}(q_t)\big], \qquad q_{t+1} = \mathrm{next}(q_t, v_t),$$ and allows EOS only if \(q_t \in F\). Then every output is in \(L(A)\): the guarantee is structural, by induction over the steps (theorem 2.2).
Complexity. Precomputation: \(|Q| \times |V|\) token reads, each \(\mathcal{O}(|v|)\) transitions — once and for all (and shareable between requests of the same grammar: XGrammar further accelerates by separating context-independent tokens, pre-checked globally, from dependent ones, the only ones resimulated). Online: one lookup per step, \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) — the overhead over the LLM pass (\(\mathcal{O}(n^2)\)) is negligible. BPE subtlety: a token is not a character; "12" and "12/" are two distinct tokens; the definition of \(\mathrm{Adm}\) copes with this because \(\delta^*\) reads the string of the token, character by character — the DFA does not even know it is being run over BPEs. Instructive counterexample: a token can cross the boundary of the language ("}," is valid at the end of JSON, "},x" is not); this is precisely why one must index over tokens and not over characters. ∎
# pedagogical skeleton: automaton-based constrained decoding (cf. lab 1)
import torch

class DfaMask:
    """dfa: transitions[q] = {character: q2}, accepting = {q...} (already pruned)."""

    def __init__(self, dfa, tokenizer):
        self.index = {}                        # q -> {token_id: q2}
        for q in dfa.states:                   # precomputation O(|Q| x |V| x |v|)
            self.index[q] = {}
            for tid, s in tokenizer.vocab.items():
                q2 = dfa.walk(q, s)            # read character by character
                if q2 is not None:             # None = dead prefix
                    self.index[q][tid] = q2

    def mask(self, logits, q):
        mask = torch.full_like(logits, float("-inf"))
        for tid in self.index[q]:
            mask[tid] = 0.0
        return logits + mask                   # -inf = forbidden

Example 3.4 — The ISO date by hand Simplified constraint \d{2}/\d{2}/\d{4}. After "26/0", the state encodes "second digit of the month in progress, 0 already seen"; the tokens "8", "9" are masked (only 1-9 complete a month 01-09), so is "/". Conversely, if the first digit of the month were 1, the second would be restricted to 0-2. Writing the table \(\mathrm{Adm}(q)\) for these states is exercise 1; the classic pitfall: allowing the token "13" as a single BPE block when the state accepts only "1" followed by "0-2" — "13" must be masked as a whole.
Beyond regular languages: grammars and JSON Schema Regular languages cannot express nesting ({"a": {"b": ...}} of arbitrary depth). One needs context-free grammars: PICARD (Scholak et al., EMNLP 2021) constrains a T5 on SQL by incremental parsing (forbidden logits are set to \(-\infty\) as soon as the token makes the derivation dead); XGrammar compiles EBNF/JSON Schema into nearly free online masks (2-10× faster than the previous state of the art). In 2026 industrial practice: every LLM tool call (function calling) goes through a grammar mask — the output is valid JSON by construction. Cf. LLMs module (M2) ch. 8 for the agent layer.

4. Steering: controlling in activation space

Constrained decoding acts on the outputs. Steering acts inside the network: if concepts ("positivity", "gender", "refusal") are encoded by directions of the activation space, then moving them should move the behavior. This is the most fascinating and least guaranteed control of the module.

Definition 4.1 — Steering vector (ActAdd, Turner et al. 2023) Let \(h^{(l)}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d\) be the residual activation of layer \(l\) after reading the prefix \(x\). For two contrasted prompts \(x^+\) ("Love") and \(x^-\) ("Hate"), one defines $$v = h^{(l)}(x^+) - h^{(l)}(x^-) \in \mathbb{R}^d,$$ and, during generation, one replaces \(h^{(l)}(x_t) \leftarrow h^{(l)}(x_t) + \alpha\, v\) at every step (and often on the first token only); \(\alpha \in [-4, 4]\) is the guidance strength. The model, the weights, the prompt: nothing changes — only a vector is added.
Example 4.2 — The founding experiment, described step by step (to replay in lab 3) Model: GPT-2 (124M-1.5B in the paper; the lab uses the small one). (i) compute \(v\) = \(h^{(6)}(\texttt{Love}) - h^{(6)}(\texttt{Hate})\); (ii) generate 200 continuations of the prompt "I want to tell you something" without steering, then with \(\alpha = +1\), then \(\alpha = -1\); (iii) count the marriage-related words ("love, wedding, bride, forever…"): their frequency jumps by a factor of several tens at \(\alpha = +1\), drops below the baseline at \(\alpha = -1\); (iv) measure the perplexity on OpenWebText: nearly unchanged at \(\alpha = \pm 1\), it explodes toward \(|\alpha| \gtrsim 5\) (degenerate outputs); (v) observe the inter-layer disagreement: steering works "better" (strong effect, coherent text) on some layers (middle of the network) than others — one more hyperparameter.
Proposition 4.3 — Why a simple addition shifts the output (first-order analysis) Let \(W_U\) denote the unembedding matrix (output layer). The logit of token \(w\) equals \(\ell_w = \langle W_U^{(w)}, h^{(L)} \rangle\) (final layer, ignoring the softmax). If the steered residual \(h^{(L)} + \alpha v^{(L)}\) stays within the network's linearity domain, then $$\Delta \ell_w \;=\; \alpha\, \langle W_U^{(w)}, v^{(L)} \rangle\ :$$ all tokens aligned with the direction \(v\) are favored proportionally to \(\alpha\) — a global, monotone reweighting, even before any nonlinear effect.
Differentiability: to first order, \(h^{(L)} \mapsto \ell_w\) is affine; \(\Delta \ell_w = \nabla_h \ell_w^\top (\alpha v) = \alpha \langle W_U^{(w)}, v\rangle\). This is exactly the structure of guidance (ch. 2, family 2): each token's logit is shifted by a "compatibility with the direction" score. The difference from the mask: here no logit is sent to \(-\infty\) — the law keeps full support, the control is soft; and the linear approximation becomes false as \(\alpha\) grows, hence the observed degeneration. ∎
The steering family in 2026: reading, writing, weights Read: probing and concept directions (cf. Explainability module ch. 1); write: ActAdd (difference of two activations), contrastive activation addition (differences averaged over pairs of behaviors), inference-time intervention (moving activations toward the truthfulness direction), representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023: extracting via self-interpretation directions of honesty, refusal, bias and subtracting/adding them); on weights: task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023): adding to the weights the difference between a fine-tuned model and the base model — the same "add a direction" gesture moved from one space to another, composable (adding two tasks, canceling one by subtraction). Common limits: empirical choice of layer and \(\alpha\), non-monotone effects, fragile out-of-distribution generalization — and no support guarantee: to truly forbid a format, only ch. 3 applies.

5. RLHF: control in behavior space

RLHF was presented as alignment (cf. LLMs M2 module ch. 5 — we do not redo its practice); here we reread it as a control mechanism, and we prove that it is — mathematically — the twin of classifier-free guidance in ch. 7. This double reading is the module's synthesis point.

Theorem 5.1 — RLHF = exponential guidance of the output law (closed-form solution proved) The alignment program with KL penalty reads $$\pi^* = \arg\max_{\pi}\ \mathbb{E}_{y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}\big[r(y, x)\big] \;-\; \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot \mid x)\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(\cdot \mid x)\big).$$ Its unique solution is $$\pi^*(y \mid x) \;=\; \frac{1}{Z(x)}\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y \mid x)\, \exp\big(r(y, x)/\beta\big), \qquad Z(x) = \sum_{y'} \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y' \mid x) e^{r(y',x)/\beta}.$$
One maximizes over the law \(\pi\) (an infinite-dimensional variable). Set \(J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_\pi[r] - \beta\,\mathbb{E}_\pi[\log \frac{\pi}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}}]\). First order: for every perturbation \(\pi + \varepsilon(\nu - \pi)\) (\(\nu\) any law), \(\frac{d}{d\varepsilon} J\big|_{\varepsilon=0} = \mathbb{E}_{\nu}[r - \beta\log\frac{\pi}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} - \beta - c] = 0\) for all \(\nu\), where \(c\) is the multiplier of the constraint \(\sum_y \pi(y) = 1\). This forces \(r(y,x) - \beta \log\frac{\pi(y|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y|x)}\) constant in \(y\): \(\log \frac{\pi}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} = r/\beta + \mathrm{const}\), giving the result (the constant is fixed by normalization; the strict concavity of the KL gives uniqueness). Control reading: the aligned policy is the reference law reweighted by an exponential factor in the reward, exactly as CFG reweights \(p(x_t)\) by \(p(c \mid x_t)^w\) (ch. 7); \(\beta\) (the inverse of the reward temperature) plays the role of the guidance strength \(w\), and the KL is the budget of allowed deviation — high fidelity to \(r\), low diversity if \(\beta\) is small. ∎
Proposition 5.2 — DPO: the same solution, without a reward model By rewriting \(r = \beta \log \frac{\pi^*}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}} + \beta\log Z\), the preference between \(y_w \succ y_l\) is expressed directly in policies: the DPO loss $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}} = -\log \sigma\Big(\beta \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w|x)} - \beta \log\tfrac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l|x)}\Big)$$ is the likelihood of the preferences under the closed form of theorem 5.1: DPO learns the same exponential guidance by direct regression on pairs. GRPO (group-relative reinforcement) is its extension via within-group comparisons — developed in detail in ch. 5 of the LLMs module (M2), which should be consulted for the actual training.
Reward hacking: when control misses its target The reward is a spec; the model optimizes the spec, not the intention. The mechanism (well documented): the closed-form solution 5.1 concentrates mass on the peaks of \(r\) — including artifactual peaks (long answers, sycophancy, formats favored by the reward model). The KL brakes (budget!), without forbidding: with KL budget zero, \(\pi^* = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\); as \(\beta \to 0\), collapse onto the argmax of \(r\). Same structure as the CFG fidelity/diversity trade-off — ch. 9 for the measurement, ch. 10 for the attack (exploiting the rewarder's artifacts is a control hijack).

6. Watermarking: proving the origin of a text

Provenance constraint (def. 1.1 iii): modify the generation so that outputs carry a secret statistical mark, verifiable without wiggle room. The green/red scheme of Kirchenbauer et al. (ICML 2023) is the most taught because its test can be proved at the M2 level — it is also the bridge with the audio watermarking of the Signal Processing module and ch. 9 of the Explainability module.

Definition 6.1 — Green/red watermark (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023) Key: secret \(k\). At every step: one hashes the previous token with \(k\) (PRF \(h_k\)), the result serves as a pseudo-random seed to partition the vocabulary: a green list \(G_k(y_{t-1})\) of fraction \(\gamma\) (typically \(\gamma = 0.5\)), the rest in the red list. Generation: before the softmax, one adds \(\delta\) to the green logits (typ. \(\delta = 2\)): $$\ell^{(t)}_v \leftarrow \ell^{(t)}_v + \delta \cdot \mathbf{1}\big[v \in G_k(y_{t-1})\big].$$ Detection: knowing \(k\), one recounts the greens.
Theorem 6.2 — The z-test, proved (H0: unmarked text) Let a suspect text of \(T\) tokens, \(s = \sum_{t=1}^T \mathbf{1}[y_t \in G_k(y_{t-1})]\) its number of greens, and the statistic $$z \;=\; \frac{s - \gamma T}{\sqrt{T\, \gamma (1 - \gamma)}}.$$ Under the null hypothesis "text written without knowledge of \(k\)" (hence human, or an unmarked model), \(z\) converges in law to \(\mathcal{N}(0, 1)\) as \(T \to \infty\), and one rejects \(H_0\) at threshold \(\alpha\) if \(z > \Phi^{-1}(1 - \alpha)\).
(i) Law under H0. The seed \(h_k(y_{t-1})\) is pseudo-random: for a text that was not generated according to the lists, the membership of \(y_t\) in the green list is a Bernoulli(\(\gamma\)) draw — independent of \(k\) in practice (this is the cryptographic property of the PRF; a standard heuristic in information theory). Hence \(s \sim \mathrm{Bin}(T, \gamma)\) (neglecting the weak sequential dependence). (ii) Normality. \(\mathbb{E}[s] = \gamma T\), \(\mathrm{Var}(s) = T\gamma(1-\gamma)\); the De Moivre-Laplace theorem (binomial CLT — Refresher Statistics module) gives \(\frac{s - \gamma T}{\sqrt{T\gamma(1-\gamma)}} \Rightarrow \mathcal{N}(0,1)\). (iii) Threshold. P(false positive) \(= \alpha\): with \(z_{\mathrm{threshold}} = 4\), \(\alpha \approx 3.2 \times 10^{-5}\) — a human text is accused once in ~30,000. (iv) Power under H1. For a marked text, each step draws a green with probability \(p_\delta > \gamma\) (the bump \(\delta\) favors greens); \(\mathbb{E}[z \mid H1] \approx (p_\delta - \gamma)\sqrt{T}/\sqrt{\gamma(1-\gamma)}\), which grows in \(\sqrt{T}\): with \(\gamma = 0.5\), \(\delta = 2\), \(p_\delta \approx 0.7\)-0.8 in practice, and \(T = 200\), \(\mathbb{E}[z] \approx 5.7\)-8.5 — detection is nearly certain. ∎
Example 6.3 — The test in numbers (exercise 2 in full) \(T = 200\), \(\gamma = 0.5\). A marked text observes \(s = 150\) (75% greens): \(z = (150 - 100)/\sqrt{200 \times 0.25} = 50/\sqrt{50} \approx 7.07\) — massive rejection of H0. A typical human text would have \(s \in [87, 113]\) at 95% (\(z \in [-1.96, 1.96]\)). Bonus question: how many tokens are needed to distinguish \(\gamma = 0.5\) from \(p_\delta = 0.6\) with power 0.9? Answer: \(T \approx \big(\Phi^{-1}(0.9)\sqrt{0.25} + \Phi^{-1}(0.975)\sqrt{0.24}\big)^2 / 0.01 \approx 340\) — the resolution grows quadratically with the fineness of the signal, the required size linearly with \((p_\delta - \gamma)^{-2}\): this is the scaling law of watermarking.
Proposition 6.4 — The family: distortion, robustness, undetectability Four descendants structure the field. (i) Unigram (Zhao et al., 2023): the green list is fixed (context-independent) — robust to paraphrase (tokens survive reordering), at the price of lower detectability at given \(T\). (ii) Gumbel/Aaronson: the token draw follows an inverse rank sampled by PRF — distortion-free (the per-step marginal law is exactly the model's); the score becomes a sum of logs. (iii) Kuditipudi et al. (2023): marking by a re-parameterizable key sequence, zero distortion and robust to edits; (iv) SynthID-Text (Dathathri et al., Nature 2024): deployed in production at Gemini's scale — a tournament of keys per step, qualitatively preserved, corpus-aggregated test. In contrast: the negative result of Sadasivan et al. (2023) — against a paraphraser good enough, every detector (watermark included) tends toward blindness; and Christ, Gunn & Zamir (2024) prove the theoretical frontier: an "undetectable" watermark cannot beat the quality-detection trade-off of the green/red scheme in general. Ch. 10 returns to this in attack mode.
Tuning: \(\gamma\), \(\delta\), and the triple trade-off Increasing \(\delta\) → easy detection (\(p_\delta \to 1\)) but degraded text (the choice is biased toward greens even when a red was the "right" word); decreasing \(\gamma\) → rare green lists, a sharper signal but a stiffer constraint on quality. In production one targets \(\gamma = 0.25\)-0.5, \(\delta = 1\)-2: imperceptible quality loss (evaluated by perplexity and human tests), \(z > 4\) reached from \(T \approx 50\)-100. The same bipolar tuning signal quality / law fidelity structures audio watermarking (WavMark, AudioSeal — cf. Speech Technology module ch. 9): two modalities, one theory.

7. Controlling diffusions: CFG, ControlNet, inversion

Control of diffusion models was introduced in M1 (Multimodal GenAI module ch. 4); here we give the short proof (the part we had not done), then the two industrial standards of spatial control (ControlNet) and editing (inversion).

7.1. CFG: the short proof

Theorem 7.1 — Classifier guidance and CFG are the same exponential reweighting Let \(\hat\epsilon_c, \hat\epsilon_u\) denote the noises predicted with/without conditioning, and \(\hat\epsilon_w = \hat\epsilon_u + w\,(\hat\epsilon_c - \hat\epsilon_u)\) the CFG prediction. Since \(\hat\epsilon(x_t, t) = -\sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\, \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t)\) (score parametrization), the CFG combination realizes $$\nabla_{x_t} \log p_w(x_t) \;=\; \nabla_{x_t} \log p(x_t) + w\, \nabla_{x_t} \log p(c \mid x_t) \;=\; \nabla_{x_t} \log \big[p(x_t \mid c)^w \, p(x_t)^{1-w}\big],$$ that is, sampling from the reweighted law \(p_w \propto p(x_t|c)^w p(x_t)^{1-w}\) — exactly the family of theorem 5.1 (RLHF) with \(w \leftrightarrow 1/\beta\).
Linearity of the score: \(\hat\epsilon_w = (1 - w)\hat\epsilon_u + w \hat\epsilon_c\) (rewrite the combination); each term is proportional to the gradient of the corresponding log-density, so the sum is the gradient of the sum of the logs. For the conditional term, Bayes' rule \(p(x_t \mid c) \propto p(c \mid x_t)\, p(x_t)\) gives \(\nabla \log p(x_t|c)^w p(x_t)^{1-w} = w\nabla\log p(c|x_t) + \nabla \log p(x_t)\) — which is precisely the formula of classifier guidance with weight \(w\): CFG obtains it without a classifier, by training the same network with conditioning dropout. At \(w = 1\): pure conditional model; \(w > 1\): over-guidance (fidelity \(\uparrow\), diversity \(\downarrow\), color saturation — the classic visual symptom); \(w < 1\): interpolation toward the unconditional. ∎

7.2. ControlNet: spatial control, without breaking the pre-training

Theorem 7.2 — ControlNet architecture (Zhang, Rao & Agrawala, ICCV 2023) Let \(F(x ; \Theta)\) be a frozen UNet block (operation on the activation \(x\) of layer \(i\)). ControlNet branches off a trainable copy initialized at \(\Theta\) and locks the output with two null convolutions (zero-convolutions): $$y \;=\; F(x;\Theta) \;+\; Z_2\Big(F_c\big(Z_1(c;\Theta_{z_1});\ \Theta_c\big);\ \Theta_{z_2}\Big),$$ where \(c\) is the control map (Canny edges, depth, skeletal pose…); \(Z_i\) is a \(1\times1\) convolution with zero weights at initialization.
Identity property at the start. At \(t = 0\): \(Z_2(\cdot) = W_2 \, h = 0\) since \(W_2 = 0\) — the branch outputs exactly zero, and the composed model is identically the pre-trained model: fine-tuning starts from the initial quality, without "catastrophic shift". The gradient does not vanish. The derivative of the output with respect to \(W_2\) equals \(\partial y / \partial W_2 = h_c \neq 0\) (the copy \(F_c\), initialized at \(\Theta\), produces nonzero activations): the first update pulls \(W_2\) away from zero, which then unlocks the gradients toward \(F_c\) and \(Z_1\) (which were zero as long as \(W_2 = 0\), by the chain rule). Compare the naive alternative (branching \(F_c\) directly): at step 0, uncontrolled random noise is added to the activations of the frozen UNet and destroys the coherence of the output for hundreds of steps. ∎
Why a "copy" and not a "new network" The copy inherits the pre-trained features: control learns the correspondence control map → existing rich features, not vision from scratch — hence the data efficiency (a few thousand pairs suffice for a new type of control). This is a general pattern of the module: condition around a frozen model and protect the interface with an identity initialization (the mask of ch. 3 is the exact analogue for discrete slots: allow only what preserves the invariant).

7.3. Editing a real image: inversion and null-text

Proposition 7.3 — DDIM inversion and its failure under CFG DDIM defines a deterministic step linking \(x_t\) and \(x_{t-1}\) via the prediction \(\hat x_0(x_t, c)\); inversion traverses the relation backwards (reminder: Multimodal GenAI module ch. 4): $$x_{t+1} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t+1}}\, \hat x_0(x_t, c) + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t+1}}\; \hat\epsilon_c(x_t, c).$$ With CFG, the applied "noise" depends on a \(w\)-combination of the conditional/unconditional predictions; inversion becomes an implicit equation (the step depends on \(x_t\) through two networks): error accumulates, the reconstruction drifts. Null-text inversion (Mokady et al., CVPR 2023): keep the inversion trajectory as an anchor and optimize, step by step, the null condition embedding \(c_\varnothing^{(t)}\) to minimize \(\|\hat x_0\big(x_t; c, c_\varnothing^{(t)}\big) - x_0\|^2\) — a few dozen gradient steps per image; editing (Prompt-to-Prompt: substituting the cross-attention maps; InstructPix2Pix: learning editing on synthetic data) then operates along this faithful trajectory.
Example 7.4 — CFG by hand (pen-and-paper, in the graph module's format) Vocabulary of 3 artistic "directions", logits of step \(t\) without conditioning: \((\text{painting}: 2.0,\ \text{photo}: 1.5,\ \text{3D}: 0.5)\); with condition "watercolor": \((3.0,\ 0.5,\ 0.5)\). At \(w = 3\): guided logits \(= (1-w)\,u + w\,c = (-4.0,\ -1.0,\ 0.5)\)… in the equivalent additive form \(u + w(c - u) = (5.0,\ -1.0,\ 0.5)\) — softmax \(\approx (0.95, 0.007,\ 0.04)\): the target style crushes the distribution, exactly like the molecular CFG of example 7.3 of the Graph Generative Models module. \(w = 8\) → a single mode survives: the curve of ch. 9, readable on a single step.

8. Controlling graph generators

Theorem 8.1 — Three controls, three mechanisms, one grid
  1. Hard constraints: chemical valence. Each decoding step masks the bonds that would exceed the remaining valence \(r_v\) of atom \(v\) — NeVAE (AAAI 2019) pushes validity above 90% without post-processing (versus a few percent otherwise); GraphAF masks likewise and reaches 100% with rules (68% without). This is word for word theorem 2.2: renormalization over the legal set \(\mathcal{L}\), guaranteed by construction;
  2. Gradient guidance: GraphAF (ICLR 2020) — each decision starts from a \(z_t \sim \mathcal N(0,1)\) passed through a flow; to maximize a property \(Q(G)\) (solubility, docking), one moves \(z_t\) along \(\nabla_{z_t} Q(G_t)\) during generation — the guidance strength is the gradient step, the fidelity/diversity trade-off is measured there (the graph module ch. 4 derives it);
  3. Conditioning + inpainting: DiGress (ICLR 2023) trains the denoiser with a descriptor \(c\) sometimes masked (classifier-free: thm 7.1 transposed) and freezes partial scaffolds; the discrete posterior allows generating around an imposed motif — the exact analogue of the image null-text/inpainting of ch. 7.

9. Evaluating control: fidelity versus diversity

Controlling without measuring is believing. This chapter sets the protocol: two axes (fidelity, diversity), one curve (the strength sweep), one decomposition (precision/recall), and a bound showing that the trade-off is structural, not an engineering defect.

Definition 9.1 — Fidelity and diversity Control fidelity: \(\mathrm{Fid}(S) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathbf{1}[y_i \in S]\) (pass rate; or a continuous metric — property score, similarity to the prompt). Diversity: mean intra-batch distance \(\mathrm{Div} = \frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j} d(y_i, y_j)\) (self-BLEU for text, LPIPS distance for images, Tanimoto for molecules — graph module ch. 6); or entropy of the effective law. Quality in the data-fidelity sense: FID (recall of the Multimodal GenAI module ch. 8), perplexity under a reference model.
Theorem 9.2 — The trade-off is structural (entropy bound) Let \(S \subset \mathcal{Y}^*\) be the admissible set (\(|S| = m\) finite; for sequences, consider outputs of length \(\leq L\) over a vocabulary of size \(k\): \(m \leq k^L\), the whole space). Every law \(\tilde p\) supported on \(S\) satisfies $$H(\tilde p) \;\leq\; \log m \;=\; H_{\max}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq L}) \;-\; \log\frac{k^L}{m}.$$
Entropy is maximal for the uniform law (Gibbs' inequality: for every \(p\) on \(S\), \(\mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, \mathrm{Unif}_S) = \log m - H(p) \geq 0\), with equality iff uniform). Support constraint → entropy ceiling lowered by \(\log(k^L/m)\): every order of magnitude of constraint dividing the number of admissible outputs by \(e\) costs 1 nat of maximal diversity. Guidance acts likewise but smoothly: \(p_w \propto p^{1-w}\mathrm{score}(c)^w\) concentrates mass on the modes compatible with \(c\) — the effective entropy decreases monotonically in the usual cases (theorems 5.1 and 7.1: the KL from the start is exactly the price paid); hence the curve. ∎
Proposition 9.3 — The control curve (protocol) For each strength \(w \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, \dots\}\) (or the watermark's \(\delta\), steering's \(\alpha\), RLHF's \(\beta^{-1}\)): generate a fixed batch (same seeds), measure \((\mathrm{Fid}(w), \mathrm{Div}(w))\), plot the trajectory. Reading: (i) a method dominates if its curve is above/to the right of another's everywhere; (ii) the "elbow" (fidelity stops growing, diversity collapses) is the recommended tuning point; (iii) complete with the precision/recall decomposition (Kynkäänniemi et al., NeurIPS 2019): precision measures the quality of the occupied modes, recall the coverage of the data's modes — over-guidance kills recall (abandoned modes) before precision. Classic anti-protocol: plot only fidelity ("100% valid JSON!") and keep silent that the model now emits only three templates — mode collapse, control version.
Example 9.4 — Toy curve, by hand Law over 4 outputs with probabilities \((0.4,\ 0.3,\ 0.2,\ 0.1)\); exponential control with score \(c = (3,\ 3,\ 0,\ 0)\) at strength \(w\). At \(w=0\): entropy 1.85 nats; \(w=1\): \(\propto (8.0,\ 6.0,\ 0.2,\ 0.1) \to\) probs \((0.56,\ 0.42,\ 0.014,\ 0.007)\), entropy 0.97; \(w=2\): \((7.2,\ 5.4,\ 0.004,\ 0.001)\) — two modes at 99%, entropy 0.69; \(w = 4\) collapse onto mode 1. Mean fidelity to the score: 0.5 → 0.98 → 0.99. The pair \((\mathrm{Fid}, H)\) draws exactly the frontier of theorem 9.2. Generalize in exercise 4.

10. Attacks on control and robustness

Control is a promise; this chapter tests the promise. Four surfaces, in the module's order: the mask, steering/RLHF, the watermark, spatial control.

Theorem 10.1 — Attacking constrained decoding (hostile content inside the format) The mask guarantees \(y \in S\) — syntax, never semantics. An injected prompt can fill a perfectly valid JSON with {"instruction": "ignore the previous rules"}; a constrained tool call can be diverted into hostile parameters (prompt injection in agents: cf. Explainability module ch. 5). Second: the complexity of the grammar is a surface — an ambiguous grammar explodes the number of states (subset construction \(2^{|Q|}\)) and allows a denial of service of the control itself; finally the tokenizer interaction: changing the tokenizer invalidates the state→tokens index (recompilation). Defenses: downstream semantic validation (schema + bounds: \(\delta\)-constraints beyond the regular), state-size limits, server-side pre-compiled grammars (defense-in-depth pattern of the Explainability module ch. 6).
Proposition 10.2 — Attacks on the watermark (and impossibility results)
  1. Edits: paraphrase, synonym substitution, emoji/homoglyph insertion, LLM rewriting — every exchange of a green token for a red one costs \(\approx 1/\sqrt{T\gamma(1-\gamma)}\) in \(z\); to bring \(\mathbb{E}[z] = 6\) below 2 one must rewrite a substantial fraction of the tokens, at the price of quality;
  2. Spoofing: can an attacker without the key mark an innocent text to accuse a provider? Public-key schemes and the reliability analysis (Kirchenbauer et al., ICLR 2024 — "On the Reliability"; already referenced in the LLMs module) quantify these false accusations: key management becomes the real problem (revocation, multi-keys, aggregated tests);
  3. Undetectability: Sadasivan et al. (2023) show that a good enough paraphraser drives every detector toward chance; Christ, Gunn & Zamir (2024) bound what is achievable: robustness is bought only in distortion. Operational conclusion: a watermark is a provenance marker, not a cryptographic proof of origin; stacking it (text + audio + image, multi-key styles) is the realistic strategy.
Attacks on guidance and steering, in brief Steering (ch. 4) has no support guarantee: a behavior removed on a test prompt reappears out of distribution (jailbreaks by rephrasing, other languages, encodings); RLHF is vulnerable to reward hacking (ch. 5) and preference attacks; CFG/ControlNet: adversarial prompts (forbidden contents rephrased), image injections (diverted inpainting, editing of persons — deepfakes), hence mandatory marking (theorem 1.3) and downstream moderation. A vast program crossing the Explainability (LLM security) and Responsible AI modules.
Definition 10.3 — Robustness budget (the quantity to remember) For a fidelity control \(c\) and a family of attacks \(\mathcal A\), one defines the degradation \(\Delta(w) = \mathrm{Fid}(w) - \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \mathcal A}[\mathrm{Fid}(w, a)]\). A robust control has flat \(\Delta\) in \(w\); the evaluation of ch. 9 must therefore be repeated under attack — the complete protocol fits in one sentence: plot the fidelity/diversity curve, with and without an adversary, at fixed quality budget.

    Guidance: normalize \(p_w \propto p\, e^{w c}\) (exponential family); \(H(w)\) decreases (derive \(dH/dw = -w\,\mathrm{Var}_w(c)\) — a classic identity: the derivative of the log-partition gives the variance, that of the entropy its weighted variance); with noise, a plateau appears before the collapse: the analyst chooses the elbow. ∎
