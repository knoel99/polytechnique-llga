Knowing how to generate is not knowing how to steer: a generative model produces texts, images, or graphs, but it is control — decoding within a grammar, steering activations, aligning behaviors, proving an origin — that turns a curious generator into a reliable tool. This module builds a taxonomy of the four families of control then rolls it out end to end: constrained decoding, steering, RLHF, watermarking, control of diffusions and graph generators, up to the evaluation of the fidelity/diversity trade-off and its robustness to attacks. In the LLGA master, this is the cross-cutting course that connects LLMs, images, and graphs through a single question: how can one impose a constraint without killing the generation? Official course description not published (the 2026-2027 SynapseS S3/S4 catalog is empty): these course materials expand on the brochure's announcement, and their own additions are flagged as going beyond the syllabus.

What you will be able to do

  • Argue for the need for control (output safety, fidelity to an instruction, intellectual property) and choose the right family of response;
  • Place any technique in the four-family taxonomy (conditioning, guidance, hard constraints, post-processing);
  • Implement grammar- or automaton-constrained decoding, with the masking algorithm detailed step by step;
  • Install and use steering vectors to orient a generation in activation space;
  • Describe RLHF as a control in behavior space, from the reward model to policy optimization;
  • Verify the presence of a textual watermark with the z-test, and discuss its limits;
  • Steer a diffusion via classifier-free guidance, ControlNet, or inversion;
  • Transpose control to graph generators, and measure the fidelity/diversity trade-off — including under attack.

Where this course fits in the program

M2 module offered in period 1, outside the recommended track: it synthesizes building blocks seen elsewhere in the master rather than introducing a new heavy formalism. Prerequisites: classifier-free guidance and diffusion models from Multimodal Generative AI (ch. 7 builds directly on them), and the decoding then RLHF from Large Language Models (revisited in ch. 0 then deepened in ch. 5).

Downstream modules: the textual watermarking of ch. 6 is taken up and extended on the attestation and anonymization side by Explainability, Security, Privacy of LLMs (ch. 9), which also covers safety-side attacks; Speech Technology for Conversational AI mobilizes the same control reflexes in continuous settings (expressivity, latency, guardrails) in the voice conversational loop.

Course outline

  • Reminders: generating, conditioning, decoding — refreshes sampling, conditioning, and decoding from the prerequisite modules.
  • Why control: safety, fidelity, intellectual property — the three industrial and legal motivations that make control unavoidable.
  • Taxonomy: four families of control — the reading grid that structures the rest of the course.
  • Constrained decoding: grammars, automata, masks — guaranteeing syntactically valid outputs at decoding time, mask by mask.
  • Steering: controlling in activation space — orienting the model during the forward pass, without retraining.
  • RLHF: control in behavior space — what preference alignment can and cannot impose.
  • Watermarking: proving the origin of a text — statistically marking a generation and proving it with a z-test.
  • Controlling diffusions: CFG, ControlNet, inversion — from classifier-free guidance to editing by inversion.
  • Controlling graph generators — imposing constraints and properties where the generated object is discrete and combinatorial.
  • Evaluating control: fidelity versus diversity — measuring what every constraint costs the generated distribution.
  • Attacks on control and robustness — circumventing grammars, steering, and watermarks, and defending these mechanisms.
  • Exercises / Labs — guided implementations: masking automaton, minimal steering, watermark and its test.
  • References — the founding papers of each family of control.

How to study this module

  • Learn the taxonomy of ch. 2 by heart and practice classifying every "controlled generative AI" paper or product you come across: it is the module's durable analysis tool.
  • For constrained decoding, simulate the masking algorithm by hand on a mini-automaton: the difficulty is in the implementation, not the idea.
  • Do the labs in order: steering, watermark, and diffusion are better understood once the decoding foundation is mastered.
  • For each technique, note a "cost" line (compute, quality, diversity) and a "guarantee" line: this module is a course in trade-offs.
  • Read ch. 10 last but reread it twice: attacking control is the best way to understand why it holds.
The LLGA perspective Control is the exact place where the three pillars of the master — LLMs, multimodal diffusion, graphs — speak the same language: same constraint grammars, same fidelity/diversity trade-off, same attacks. By seeing it first as a cross-cutting problem rather than a collection of per-modality recipes, you will acquire the reflex that distinguishes generative AI engineers and researchers: choosing the level of intervention (data, decoding, activations, behaviors) before choosing the tool.
