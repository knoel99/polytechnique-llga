9. Evaluating control: fidelity versus diversity

Controlling without measuring is believing. This chapter sets the protocol: two axes (fidelity, diversity), one curve (the strength sweep), one decomposition (precision/recall), and a bound showing that the trade-off is structural, not an engineering defect.

Definition 9.1 — Fidelity and diversity Control fidelity: \(\mathrm{Fid}(S) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathbf{1}[y_i \in S]\) (pass rate; or a continuous metric — property score, similarity to the prompt). Diversity: mean intra-batch distance \(\mathrm{Div} = \frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j} d(y_i, y_j)\) (self-BLEU for text, LPIPS distance for images, Tanimoto for molecules — graph module ch. 6); or entropy of the effective law. Quality in the data-fidelity sense: FID (recall of the Multimodal GenAI module ch. 8), perplexity under a reference model.
Theorem 9.2 — The trade-off is structural (entropy bound) Let \(S \subset \mathcal{Y}^*\) be the admissible set (\(|S| = m\) finite; for sequences, consider outputs of length \(\leq L\) over a vocabulary of size \(k\): \(m \leq k^L\), the whole space). Every law \(\tilde p\) supported on \(S\) satisfies $$H(\tilde p) \;\leq\; \log m \;=\; H_{\max}(\mathcal{Y}^{\leq L}) \;-\; \log\frac{k^L}{m}.$$
Entropy is maximal for the uniform law (Gibbs' inequality: for every \(p\) on \(S\), \(\mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, \mathrm{Unif}_S) = \log m - H(p) \geq 0\), with equality iff uniform). Support constraint → entropy ceiling lowered by \(\log(k^L/m)\): every order of magnitude of constraint dividing the number of admissible outputs by \(e\) costs 1 nat of maximal diversity. Guidance acts likewise but smoothly: \(p_w \propto p^{1-w}\mathrm{score}(c)^w\) concentrates mass on the modes compatible with \(c\) — the effective entropy decreases monotonically in the usual cases (theorems 5.1 and 7.1: the KL from the start is exactly the price paid); hence the curve. ∎
Proposition 9.3 — The control curve (protocol) For each strength \(w \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, \dots\}\) (or the watermark's \(\delta\), steering's \(\alpha\), RLHF's \(\beta^{-1}\)): generate a fixed batch (same seeds), measure \((\mathrm{Fid}(w), \mathrm{Div}(w))\), plot the trajectory. Reading: (i) a method dominates if its curve is above/to the right of another's everywhere; (ii) the "elbow" (fidelity stops growing, diversity collapses) is the recommended tuning point; (iii) complete with the precision/recall decomposition (Kynkäänniemi et al., NeurIPS 2019): precision measures the quality of the occupied modes, recall the coverage of the data's modes — over-guidance kills recall (abandoned modes) before precision. Classic anti-protocol: plot only fidelity ("100% valid JSON!") and keep silent that the model now emits only three templates — mode collapse, control version.
Example 9.4 — Toy curve, by hand Law over 4 outputs with probabilities \((0.4,\ 0.3,\ 0.2,\ 0.1)\); exponential control with score \(c = (3,\ 3,\ 0,\ 0)\) at strength \(w\). At \(w=0\): entropy 1.85 nats; \(w=1\): \(\propto (8.0,\ 6.0,\ 0.2,\ 0.1) \to\) probs \((0.56,\ 0.42,\ 0.014,\ 0.007)\), entropy 0.97; \(w=2\): \((7.2,\ 5.4,\ 0.004,\ 0.001)\) — two modes at 99%, entropy 0.69; \(w = 4\) collapse onto mode 1. Mean fidelity to the score: 0.5 → 0.98 → 0.99. The pair \((\mathrm{Fid}, H)\) draws exactly the frontier of theorem 9.2. Generalize in exercise 4.
