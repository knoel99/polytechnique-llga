0. Reminders: generating, conditioning, decoding

Prerequisites (M1 and M2 modules) — what is assumed acquired
  • Autoregressive generation (LLMs M2 module, ch. 1-2): factorization \(p_\theta(y \mid x) = \prod_{t} p_\theta(y_t \mid x, y_{<t})\), softmax over the vocabulary (BPE: tokens are subword units, not characters), sampling with temperature, top-\(k\), top-\(p\) (ch. 6 of the same module);
  • Conditioning and alignment (LLMs M2 module, ch. 4-5): prompting, SFT, RLHF/DPO/GRPO — we return to these in ch. 5 as control mechanisms;
  • Diffusion (Multimodal GenAI M1 module, ch. 3-4): DDPM, noise prediction \(\hat\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\), classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance (CFG), DDIM and its inversion — recalled in ch. 7 with proof;
  • VAE and ELBO (Multimodal GenAI ch. 1): the variational bound, the reconstruction/regularization trade-off;
  • Graph generation (Graph ML M1 module ch. 9; Graph Generative Models M2 module): autoregressive (GraphRNN, DeepGMG), VAE (NeVAE and its valence masks), flows (GraphAF), discrete diffusion (DiGress) — ch. 8 takes up their control;
  • Hypothesis testing (Refresher Statistics M1 module): type I/II error, threshold, \(p\)-value, central limit theorem — ch. 6 makes intensive use of it.
The module's storyline The previous modules answered "how to generate?". This one answers "how to generate what we want?". The course's thesis: control is not a patch applied after the fact but a unified family of mechanisms — condition, guide, constrain, filter — that recur identically on text (ch. 3-6), images (ch. 7), and graphs (ch. 8), with a common fidelity/diversity trade-off (ch. 9) and a common attack surface (ch. 10).
