Exercises

  1. 1-WL by hand. Run 1-WL on C6 and 2×C3 and verify indistinguishability (detail Example 1.4); then on the "house" graph (square + roof) versus the pentagon; conclude on the exact family of indistinguishable pairs. Partial solution: house vs pentagon — all nodes of degree 2 except the roof top (degree 3); the pentagon is 2-regular, the house is not → 1-WL distinguishes them from iteration 1 (initial color = degree); generalization: 1-WL fails exactly on certain pairs of regular graphs with the same degree sequence (more finely: same iteration tree; connected 2-regular graphs = cycles, all indistinguishable from one another).
  2. GIN vs GCN. Show that the mean is not injective on multisets (give the counterexamples {a, a, a} vs {a} and {a, a, b, b} vs {a, b}) and that sum+MLP is (discrete features); check experimentally: train GIN and GCN on a 1-WL-indistinguishable pair and measure. Partial solution: mean: {a, a, a} → a and {a} → a (collision); discrete sum: encode a by \(\log 2\), b by \(\log 3\) — \(\{a,a,b,b\} \mapsto 2\log 2 + 2\log 3 = \log 36\) and \(\{a, b\} \mapsto \log 6\) — no collision by uniqueness of factorization (th. 1.3); in practice, the mean does better on continuous real features — discuss why (estimation variance).
  3. Minimal 2-GNN. Implement a 2-GNN on pairs (representation \(h_{ij}\), messages between adjacent pairs) and verify that it separates C6 and 2C3 (disjoint readout outputs) where GIN fails. Hint: start with hashed colors (exact 2-WL) before learning.
  4. Over-squashing. Build two "communities" connected by a single bottleneck node; measure the classification performance of a GCN as a function of the distance to the bottleneck; compare GPS (ch. 4) and GCN + virtual edges (DIGL); plot \(\lambda_{\max}\) before/after rewiring. Partial solution (expected): gradient of accuracy with distance for the GCN; GPS nearly uniform; after DIGL, \(\lambda_{\max}\) drops by several percent and the gap narrows — the \(\lambda_{\max}\) vs accuracy curve is the graph to produce.
  5. LapEig. Compute the first 2 nontrivial eigenvectors of the Laplacian of the karate club graph; project the nodes; verify that the visualized separation ≈ the actual partition; rerun 5 times and observe the sign flips; implement the SignNet correction (ch. 3.1) and measure stability (distance between the encodings of two runs). Partial solution: \(\rho(V) = \mathrm{MLP}(\sum_i \phi(v_i) + \phi(-v_i))\) — the sum of the two parities is invariant under flips; compare with the baseline "sign fixed by the first positive component" (unstable at nodes of near-zero value).
  6. RWSE by hand and in code. Recover the computations of Example 3.2 (triangle vs hexagon), then compute the RWSEs (\(K = 4\)) of all nodes of the karate club graph; show that two 1-WL-equivalent nodes of a regular graph get the same RWSE — and explain why this is both a feature (invariance under automorphism) and a bug (expressive power).
  7. Graphormer-PE. Implement the attention bias by shortest-path distance (distance matrix by BFS; learned bias table — skeleton in ch. 4.1); train on ogbg-molhiv and compare GCN / GIN / Graphormer-mini; report means ± standard deviations over 5 seeds. Partial solution: BFS \(\mathcal{O}(n(|V| + |E|))\) per graph — precompute; clamp distances to 5 (Pareto law of molecular SPDs); expected: a small but systematic gain of the SPD bias over GCN.
  8. Temporal. On a standard TGN dataset (Wikipedia/Reddit): train a TGN (pytorch_geometric.nn.models.TGNMemory library or the paper's code); ablation: remove memory (keep the TGAT embedding); plot the AUC of future link prediction as a function of the horizon \(\Delta t\). Partial solution (expected): memory dominates on recent interactions; without memory, the AUC drops by several points at small \(\Delta t\) (the information of the last events is no longer compressed).
  9. Attack. Implement an attack by removing/adding \(k\) edges targeting a node (gradient over nonexistent edges — ch. 7.1, linearized surrogate); measure the prediction flip rate vs budget; test a defense (RGCN or training on perturbed graphs). Partial solution: score = |gradient of the attack loss per edge|; take the top-k; the surrogate suffices (transfer); with RGCN, the required budget roughly doubles — never zero; conclude on certification (ch. 7.3).
  10. Edge-DP. Prove that randomized response (flipping each edge with probability \(p\)) is \((\varepsilon, 0)\)-DP with \(\varepsilon = \log \frac{p}{1-p}\) (analyze the two output mechanisms per edge); deduce the equivalent Laplace noise for an edge count. Solution: for two graphs neighboring in one edge, the likelihood ratio of any output is bounded by \(\frac{p}{1-p}\) (worst case: the flipped edge); hence the \(\varepsilon\); counting: each edge contributes Bernoulli(\(1-p\)) vs Bernoulli(\(p\)), the sum of \(m\) Bernoullis has binomial noise — approximated by Laplace of variance \(m\,p(1-p)\), i.e. scale \(\sqrt{m\,p(1-p)}\).
  11. Research project. Reproduce an experiment from one of (PathNN / GOAT / GPS / TGN / NGG) on OGB or TU; report: protocol, seeds, comparison to the published numbers, failure analysis — the M2's "paper" exercise; for PathNN: compare the three variants (s/r/n) on a molecular dataset and explain the observed hierarchy via ch. 8.1.
