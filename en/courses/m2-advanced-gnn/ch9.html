9. LLGA bridges: 3D, algorithmic reasoning, GNN × LLM

9.1 — 3D molecules: EGNN and E(n) equivariance (Satorras, Hoogeboom & Welling, 2021) The positions \(x_i \in \mathbb{R}^3\) make the molecular graph geometric; physics is invariant under rotations/translations (E(3)); a GNN that respects this symmetry by construction learns better with less data (Bronstein et al., the Geometric DL "charter"). EGNN: $$m_{ij} = \phi_e\big(h_i, h_j, \|x_i - x_j\|^2, e_{ij}\big),$$ $$x_i^{(\ell+1)} = x_i^{(\ell)} + C \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} (x_i^{(\ell)} - x_j^{(\ell)})\, \phi_x(m_{ij}),$$ $$h_i^{(\ell+1)} = \phi_h\big(h_i^{(\ell)}, \textstyle\sum_{j} m_{ij}\big).$$ Equivariance proof (sketch): under \(x \mapsto Rx + t\) (\(R\) orthogonal), the distances \(\|x_i - x_j\|\) are invariant, hence so are the \(m_{ij}\); the position step \(\sum_j (x_i - x_j)\phi_x\) transforms into \(R \sum_j (x_i - x_j)\phi_x\) — exact equivariance; the factor \(C\) bounds the step. Applications: 3D property prediction, learned molecular dynamics, 3D generation (EDM — equivariant diffusion; the M2 Geometric DL module generalizes to groups; M1 ch. 9 covers the 2D version).
9.2 — Neural algorithmic reasoning (Veličković et al.) Learning to execute algorithms with GNNs: encode inputs as a graph (sorting: a list = a comparison graph; shortest paths: the graph itself), execute via message passing layers, decode outputs — the black-box replaces the code. The CLRS benchmark (Veličković et al., ICML 2023) tests faithful execution of classical algorithms (CLRS = Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, Stein); results: GNN processors generalize to unseen sizes better than transformers on certain families (structural induction); the LLGA lesson: a learned algorithm is differentiable (it can be inserted into an end-to-end trained pipeline: routing, scheduling, matching) and approximately robust (noisy inputs); at the price of exactness — the trade-off to master. Loop with Refresher CS: what more can a "learned algorithm" do than an algorithm? Answer: train on data, slot into gradient descent — and the pseudo-code prompting of ch. 8.4 is the dual path (the LLM simulates the execution; NAR embodies it).
9.3 — GNN × LLM: injected encoders and benchmarks Three injection architectures (M1, ch. 11.2, deepened):
  • structural tokens: the outputs of a GNN (node/graph embeddings) are projected into the LLM's embedding space as soft tokens; the LLM reads structure as a language;
  • structural recall: the GNN (or the graph traversal) retrieves the relevant facts, the LLM writes — RAG becomes "graph-RAG"; in both cases, the question of the alignment of the spaces arises (contrastive: bring the GNN embedding closer to the text describing the same node);
  • LLM annotator: the LLM enriches node features (summaries, types) for the GNN — the graph becomes text-attributed (the "TAG" regime); benchmarks: NLGraph (serialized reasoning — M1, ch. 11.2), Talk like a Graph (encodings: none dominates everywhere), and the industrial GraphRAG suites.
Industrial GraphRAG (summary; details in M1, ch. 11): LLM extraction → entity graph → Leiden communities + summaries → local/global queries; the M2's role: the quality of the graph (robust communities — ch. 7; KGC completion — M1 ch. 6) is the system's bottleneck; the rare specialty the master builds: both GNN theory and the LLM pipeline.
9.4 — What the master expects of you here Knowing how to choose: MPNN vs transformer (ch. 4), with or without PE (ch. 3), deep or rewired (ch. 2), certified or not (ch. 7) — and knowing how to prove the theorems of ch. 1, reproduce the experiments of ch. 8, and read a paper of the field in one hour. This is the "entry-level research / advanced R&D" level.
