6. Scale: sampling, systems

What is assumed known (M1, ch. 7)
NeighborSampler (fanouts, expansive neighborhood \(1 + s_L(1 + s_{L-1}(\dots))\)), Cluster-GCN (METIS partitioning, partition bias), GraphSAINT (subgraphs + bias-free coverage normalization), and the bias/variance/memory triangle. Here: the systems layer and experimental hygiene.
  • Multi-GPU and partitioning: beyond one GPU, partition the graph (METIS/vertex-cut) across several machines; boundary edges are duplicated (shadow nodes) — the distributed training of the Emerging ML module applies almost as is (parameter server vs all-reduce); PinSage (M1, ch. 6) is the industrial reference;
  • Caching and pipelining: neighborhood sampling is a system problem (I/O, reuse of subgraphs across batches — hence PinSage's importance sampling that freezes the entry points); the frameworks: DGL, PyG + torch_geometric.sampler, Quiver;
  • Summary of complexities:
    MethodMemory/batchBiasNotes
    full-batch\(\mathcal{O}(|V| + |E|)\)nonebreaks beyond ~1M nodes
    NeighborSampler\(\mathcal{O}(\prod s_l)\)varianceinductive, standard
    Cluster-GCN\(\mathcal{O}(|E|/k)\)partitiondeep possible
    GraphSAINT\(\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{G}_s|)\)correcteddense batches
  • Benchmarks and hygiene: OGB (ogbn-arxiv 169k; ogbn-products 2.4M; PCQM4M 3.7M molecules), public leaderboards; the "Benchmarking Graph Neural Networks" paper (Dwivedi et al., 2020/2023) sets the protocol: same parameter budgets, same splits, multiple seeds with confidence intervals — many "SOTAs" die by this yardstick; Bayesian hyperparameter search is the rule (gains < 0.5 point demanding a strict protocol);
  • Good research practice: always publish the variance, test significance (paired tests over seeds), and check for leakage (temporal splits for dynamic graphs — ch. 5).
