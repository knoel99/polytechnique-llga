8. Applications: drugs, materials, synthetic networks

Generating graphs is not an end in itself: it is the operational arm of inverse design — specify what one wants (a property, a structure, a statistic) and let the model explore the space \(\mathcal{G}\) on our behalf. This chapter visits the module's three application territories, each time with the same three questions: which hard constraints? which objectives? which evaluation?

Definition 8.1 — Inverse design, Pareto, scalarization We have predictors \(Q_1, \dots, Q_m : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{R}\) (solubility, synthesizability, predicted activity...). Inverse design seeks to maximize a vector objective \(Q = (Q_1, \dots, Q_m)\): in general no \(G\) optimizes all the \(Q_i\) at once (solubility against permeability, activity against toxicity). One defines Pareto dominance: \(G \succ G'\) if \(Q_i(G) \geq Q_i(G')\) for all \(i\), with at least one strict inequality; the non-dominated ones form the Pareto frontier. Two usual scalarizations bring the problem back to a scalar objective: $$J(G) = \sum_{i} w_i\, Q_i(G) \quad\text{(weighted)}, \qquad J_{\times}(G) = \prod_{i} \pi_i\big(Q_i(G)\big)^{w_i} \quad\text{(geometric)},$$ where \(\pi_i : \mathbb{R} \to [0,1]\) maps each property into \([0,1]\) (sigmoids, normalization); the geometric form (that of GuacaMol's MPO tasks) penalizes heavily any collapsed objective: a product does not forgive a zero. The role of the generator: proposing candidates where direct combinatorial optimization (\(10^{500}\) adjacencies, ch. 1) is hopeless — the gradient guidance of ch. 4, the CFG of ch. 5 and the reinforcement-learning optimization of ch. 7 are the three interfaces between \(p_\theta\) and \(J\).
Theorem 8.2 — Drug discovery: where does the generator plug in? The industrial pipeline, simplified but faithful:
  1. Target and hypothesis: a protein involved in a disease;
  2. Proposal: the generator samples \(G \sim p_\theta(\cdot \mid c)\) (c = target descriptor, properties) or optimizes \(J\) in the latent space — GraphAF guidance (ch. 4), DiGress CFG (ch. 5), RL policies on SMILES (industrial pipelines of the REINVENT type);
  3. Filtering: validity (valence, ch. 3), \(QED\) — the "drug-likeness" score on \([0,1]\) (Bickerton et al., 2012) —, synthesizability \(SA\), logP, diversity (ch. 6);
  4. Costly evaluation: docking (predicted interaction energy), ADMET models (absorption, toxicity) — each step costs orders of magnitude more than the previous one;
  5. Synthesis and trials: the real bottleneck — hence the operational metric: the quality of the top-k (the k candidates actually synthesized), not the batch average.
An often-ignored consequence: optimizing the mean of \(J\) over 104 molecules can be worse than optimizing the maximum — the useful generator is the one that explores the corners of the Pareto frontier; this is exactly the fidelity/diversity curve of the Control in Generative AI module (ch. 9), reread from the application side.
Molecular benchmarks: ZINC, MOSES, GuacaMol
  • ZINC (Sterling & Irwin, 2015): the database of purchasable compounds — the reservoir; the subset ZINC-250k (≈ 250,000 lead-like molecules, popularized by grammar VAEs) is the standard training set of chapters 3-5 (NeVAE, GraphAF train and evaluate on it);
  • MOSES (Polykovskiy et al., 2020): ~1.9M ZINC molecules with scaffold split (the test scaffolds are unknown at training time) and standardized metrics: validity, uniqueness, novelty, FCD, scaffold similarity — the "distribution-learning" protocol of ch. 6;
  • GuacaMol (Brown et al., 2019): two families of tasks — distribution-learning (reproduce a library: FCD, KL divergences on physico-chemical properties, validity/novelty) and goal-directed (rediscover a given molecule, similarity, isomers, and the MPO tasks: maximize the geometric product of properties — Definition 8.1 in action).
The common trap: metric learning (ch. 6, point 4); on goal-directed tasks, a model can memorize the targets — hence the evaluation on targets kept secret. These benchmarks are to molecular generation what CIFAR/ImageNet are to vision (cf. Deep Learning module): convenient stops, not the destination.
Theorem 8.3 — Materials: the periodic constraint (CDVAE, MatterGen) A crystal is not a molecule: it is a triple \((\Lambda, X, L)\) — lattice \(\Lambda\) (three cell vectors), positions \(X\) of \(n\) atoms in the cell, the invariance being the group of integer translations: every quantity (distances, energies, densities) is computed modulo the periodic images. Two models bound the domain:
  1. CDVAE (Xie et al., ICLR 2022): a VAE whose decoder denoises by diffusion; the initialization places the atoms far from any equilibrium and the diffusion makes them "fall" toward realistic bonds — the physical inductive bias (stability is a local energy minimum) is inscribed in the dynamics: the exact analogue of the valence mask of ch. 3, moved into the space of positions;
  2. MatterGen (Zeni et al., Nature 2025): joint diffusion on atom types, coordinates and lattice — with conditioning by adapter: the base model (learned on the whole periodic table) is frozen, a small per-task module redirects the denoising toward the target property (composition, symmetry, magnetism...) — the industrial analogue of the classifier-free guidance of ch. 5: one large model, a thousand cheap adaptations.
Evaluation specific to materials: stable fraction (predicted energy close to the convex hull of known phases), novelty against crystallographic databases (the analogue of the novelty of ch. 6), symmetry validity (space groups). The module's lesson repeats: periodicity is a hard constraint — it is architectural (equivariance to translations, cf. Geometric Deep Learning M2 module), never a post-processing.
Proposition 8.4 — Synthetic networks: generating to protect Sharing a real graph (contacts, transactions, calls) means exposing individuals: the structure itself re-identifies — knowing the neighbors of a node suffices to find it again in most real networks, and anonymizations by masking are systematically broken. Generating a synthetic network that reproduces the useful statistics (degrees, clustering, motifs — the features of ch. 6) without copying the individuals is the alternative; NetGAN (Bojchevski et al., ICML 2018) opened the way: learn the distribution of the graph's biased random walks (a GAN on node sequences), then reconstruct the adjacency from the co-occurrences of the walks — the graph is never copied, only its "walk signature" is.
The fidelity/confidentiality trade-off, quantified. Fidelity is measured by MMD on degrees/clustering/orbits (ch. 6); confidentiality by node-level membership inference tests (the graph counterpart of the attacks of the Security and Robustness M2 module): a generator that memorizes (overfitting on a single graph) spits out exact subgraphs — detectable, real damage. Mastering the trade-off goes through (i) the regularization of \(p_\theta\) (limiting memorization as one limits overfitting — cf. Machine Learning module ch. 7); (ii) noise at the node/edge level (the analogue of the Gaussian mechanism of DP — cf. Emerging ML ch. 5 and Privacy and Uncertainty M2 modules); (iii) evaluation on both sides: publishing a synthetic network without an attack test is a rerun of the "100% validity" of ch. 6 — half a proof. ∎
Example 8.5 — Pareto by hand (pen and paper) Four candidates, two normalized objectives (\(Q_1\) = predicted activity, \(Q_2\) = synthesizability): \(A = (0.9;\ 0.2)\), \(B = (0.7;\ 0.7)\), \(C = (0.6;\ 0.9)\), \(D = (0.2;\ 0.95)\). Dominance: does \(B\) dominate \(D\)? \(0.7 > 0.2\) but \(0.7 < 0.95\) — no; step by step, all four are on the frontier. Weighted scalarization \(J = w Q_1 + (1-w) Q_2\): at \(w = 0.3\), \(C\) wins (\(J = 0.81\)) ahead of \(D\) (0.725) and \(B\) (0.70); at \(w = 0.6\), \(C\) prevails again (0.72) against \(B\) (0.70) and \(A\) (0.62); \(A\) overtakes \(C\) only for \(w \geq 0.7\) — the ranking pivots with the weights: report the frontier, never an isolated argmax. Geometric version \(J_\times = Q_1 Q_2\): \(C\) (0.54) and \(B\) (0.49) dominate, \(A\) (0.18) and \(D\) (0.19) are eliminated — unbalanced profiles are crushed: the signature of MPO.
