3. VAEs: GraphVAE, NeVAE and valence constraints

3.1. The graph VAE: ELBO and one-shot decoder

Theorem 3.1 — GraphVAE (Simonovsky & Komodakis, 2018) and the matching problem GNN encoder \(q_\phi(z \mid G) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(G), \mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2_\phi(G)))\), one-shot decoder: \(p_\theta(G \mid z) = p(n \mid z) \cdot p_\theta(\hat A, \hat X \mid z)\) with continuous adjacency \(\hat A \in [0,1]^{n\times n}\) (independent sigmoids) and features \(\hat X\). The likelihood is written against the data via a correspondence matrix (Hungarian matching, an \(\mathcal{O}(n^3)\) algorithm): the decoder does not emit in a canonical order, its nodes must be matched to those of \(G\) — the price of one-shot decoding. The ELBO: $$\log p_\theta(G) \geq \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z \mid G)}[\log p_\theta(G \mid z)] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid G)\,\|\,\mathcal{N}(0, I)\big),$$ proven in the M1 (Multimodal GenAI module ch. 1); here the reconstruction term is not a simple sum of Bernoullis because of the matching — it is lower-bounded by the optimal matching (sum of the log-probabilities over the retained pairs), which preserves the validity of the ELBO.

3.2. NeVAE in detail: valence as the architect

Theorem 3.2 — NeVAE (Samanta et al., AAAI 2019): granular VAE with constrained decoding Four ingredients, to be known precisely:
  1. Sparse encoder: one embedding per atom \(z_i \in \mathbb{R}^d\), MPNN messages between neighboring atoms only — \(\mathcal{O}(n + m)\) memory instead of \(\mathcal{O}(n^2)\): the molecular graph is sparse, exploiting this divides memory by \(\Theta(n)\); the posterior law is a product of local Gaussians \(q(z_i \mid G) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_i, \sigma_i^2)\);
  2. Remaining valence as state: let \(r_v^{(t)} \in \{0, \dots, 4\}\) denote the available valence of atom \(v\) at step \(t\) (4 for C, 2 for O, 1 for H...). Each decoding head is conditioned on this state: $$p\big(\text{bond }(u, v, b)\big) = \mathrm{softmax}\big(f\,[\,z_u, z_v, r_u, r_v\,]\big),$$ where the mask forbids any \(b\) with \(b > r_u\) or \(b > r_v\) — a saturated carbon (r = 0) can no longer receive a bond, by construction;
  3. Aromaticity: aromatic rings are not alternating single/double bonds but a dedicated type; specific heads sample ring membership — otherwise the model massively underestimates benzene cores;
  4. Noise correction: during training, if a sampled bond violates valence, it is resampled among the legal ones before the loss is computed — the penalty on forbidden bonds is softened and the gradient does not break; measured result: chemical validity > 90% without RDKit sanitization, versus a few percent for unconstrained GraphVAE.
Proposition 3.3 — Why is the valence mask a good statistical idea? Masking the forbidden outputs amounts to replacing \(p_\theta\) by its renormalization on the legal subset \(\mathcal{L}\): $$\tilde p_\theta(y \mid x) = \frac{p_\theta(y \mid x)\,\mathbf{1}[y \in \mathcal{L}]} {\sum_{y' \in \mathcal{L}} p_\theta(y' \mid x)}.$$
Two effects. (i) Support correction: the forbidden mass (typically \(\varepsilon\) per step, but \(1 - (1-\varepsilon)^T \to 1\) over \(T\) steps) is redistributed; the support of \(\tilde p\) is included in the valid molecules by construction, which no post-processing guarantees (post-processing repairs, it does not guarantee that the repaired law is close to the target law). (ii) Bias: if the model estimates the ratios of the legal options well, \(\tilde p_\theta \approx p_\star(\cdot \mid \mathcal{L})\) — now molecular datasets are (almost) entirely in \(\mathcal{L}\), so \(p_\star(\cdot \mid \mathcal{L}) \approx p_\star\): the mask is nearly free in bias and massive in validity gain. The same analysis holds for all the decoding masks of the Control in Generative AI module (ch. 3). ∎
From GraphVAE to NeVAE: the general lesson Injecting domain knowledge into the architecture (valence, aromaticity) beats post-processing; this lesson will repeat: DiGress auxiliary losses (ch. 5), chemically valid fragments of junction trees (ch. 2), valence constraints of the Control module (ch. 8). Trade-off: the model becomes specific to one domain (organic chemistry) — for general graphs (social networks), the "hard constraint" analogue does not exist, and that is precisely one of the open problems of ch. 10.
Example 3.4 — NeVAE decoder step by step (by hand) Prefix: C—C—O (remaining valences C: 3, C: 2, O: 1). Next step: choose the next atom and its bond. Heads: \(p(\text{atom}) = (\text{C}: 0.6,\ \text{O}: 0.3,\ \text{N}: 0.1)\); suppose C is drawn, attached to the O (the only usable \(r_v \neq 0\) with high probability); bond masked to \(\{0, 1\}\) (O has \(r=1\)); after fixing C—O, the O has \(r = 0\) — it is closed forever. To be written as a table over 4-5 steps: each row = a draw, the applied mask, the new \(r_v\); exercise 2 schedules it.
