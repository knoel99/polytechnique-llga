4. Normalizing flows: change of variables and GraphAF

4.1. The change of variables, proven

Theorem 4.1 — Additive/affine flows: exact likelihood Let \(f_\theta : \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d\) be a diffeomorphism (a differentiable bijection with differentiable inverse) and \(z \sim p_Z\) (e.g. \(\mathcal{N}(0, I)\)). Then \(y = f_\theta(z)\) has density $$p_Y(y) = p_Z\big(f_\theta^{-1}(y)\big)\,\Big|\det \frac{\partial f_\theta^{-1}}{\partial y}(y)\Big| = p_Z(z)\,\Big|\det \frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial z}(z)\Big|^{-1}.$$
First point: for a diffeomorphism \(f\) and a measurable set \(B\), $$\mathbb{P}(Y \in B) = \mathbb{P}(z \in f^{-1}(B)) = \int_{f^{-1}(B)} p_Z(z)\,dz = \int_B p_Z\big(f^{-1}(y)\big)\Big|\det J_{f^{-1}}(y)\Big|\,dy$$ by the change of variables of multivariate integration (the volume element is multiplied by the Jacobian in absolute value — cf. Refresher Statistics module, Jacobians chapter); identify the integrand with \(p_Y(y)\). Second point: \(J_{f^{-1}}(y) = \big[J_f(z)\big]^{-1}\) (derivative of an inverse), and \(\det(M^{-1}) = 1/\det(M)\). ∎
Proposition 4.2 — Composition and triangular Jacobians (i) \(\det(f \circ g)' = \det(f') \cdot \det(g')\) (product rule for determinants, \(\det(AB) = \det A \det B\)): a flow is a composition \(f = f_L \circ \dots \circ f_1\) and the log-Jacobian adds up. (ii) If each \(f_\ell\) alters only one coordinate conditionally on the previous ones (coupling: \(y_a = x_a\), \(y_b = s(x_a) \odot x_b + t(x_a)\)), its Jacobian is triangular — the determinant is the product of the diagonal elements \(\prod_i s_i(x_a)\), computable in \(\mathcal{O}(d)\) instead of \(\mathcal{O}(d^3)\).
The Jacobian of a coupling has blocks \(\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ \ast & \mathrm{diag}(s) \end{pmatrix}\) — block-triangular, determinant \(\prod_i s_i\). Limiting case: autoregressive masks (MADE): \(y_i = \tau(x_i\,;h_i(x_{<i}))\) give a strictly lower triangular Jacobian with diagonal \(\partial y_i/\partial x_i\) — this is exactly the building block of GraphAF below. ∎

4.2. GraphAF: the autoregressive flow for graphs

Theorem 4.3 — GraphAF (Shi et al., ICLR 2020): exact likelihood + guidable sampling GraphAF combines the sequential factorization of ch. 2 with the flow of ch. 4.1: each decision (new atom, new bond) starts from a source \(z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1)\) transformed by a conditional flow: $$x_t = f_\theta\big(z_t\,;\ s_t\big), \qquad s_t = \mathrm{GNN}\big(G_{t-1}, \text{candidates}\big),$$ where \(s_t\) is the state of the partial graph (MPNN embeddings of the candidate nodes), and the density of the decision is read exactly via Theorem 4.1: $$\log p_\theta(G) = \sum_t \Big[\log p_Z(z_t) - \log\big|\det J_{f_\theta}(z_t\,;\ s_t)\Big|\Big].$$ Key results: (i) exact likelihood (unlike VAEs); (ii) 68% of valid molecules without chemical rules, 100% with the valence mask of ch. 3 — the mask and the flow are orthogonal; (iii) sampling remains sequential (\(\Theta(n^2)\) steps), BUT each step is a draw from a discretized continuous law — hence gradient guidance: to maximize a property \(Q(G)\) (solubility, docking), one moves \(z_t\) along of \(\nabla_{z_t} Q\big(G_t\big)\) during generation: guidance compatible with the exact likelihood — impossible with a VAE whose latent is not in bijective correspondence with the data.
Discrete flows: the cardinal difficulty Theorem 4.1 requires a continuous space (Jacobian!). Two answers: (i) GraphAF stays continuous upstream and discretizes downstream (the flow lives on \(z\)); (ii) discrete flips (Tran et al., 2019; Hoogeboom et al., 2021): on categorical variables, replace the Jacobian with a learned correction term — the discrete analogue of the change of variables; GraphDF (Cai et al., 2020) applies them to graphs; each flip is a bijection on a finite set, the "density" becomes a mass law and the log-likelihood remains exact. To be known to exist; the module's lab implements only the (continuous) GraphAF route, simpler and sufficient for the properties. 
# GraphAF skeleton (pedagogical): one conditional flow step
import torch, torch.nn as nn

class CouplingStep(nn.Module):
    """y_b = s(h) * x_b + t(h); diagonal Jacobian = prod(s)."""
    def __init__(self, d):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Linear(64, 2 * d)      # GNN state (64) -> (s, t)
    def forward(self, x_b, h):
        s, t = self.net(h).chunk(2, dim=-1)
        s = torch.sigmoid(s + 2.0)           # s > 0: positive det, stable
        y = s * x_b + t
        return y, s.log().sum(-1)            # y and log|det J|

# generation of an atom/bond decision:
#   h = GNN(G_t); z ~ N(0,1); x, ld = flow(z, h)
#   atom = argmax or sample of softmax(x)  (+ valence mask!)
#   log p(x) = log N(z) - ld   (change of variables theorem)

  • LLGA link: the internal GNN of GraphAF is an MPNN — replacing it by a PathNN (ch. 9) or a graph transformer (cf. Advanced GNN module ch. 4) is an open research question, at the boundary of the two M2 modules.
