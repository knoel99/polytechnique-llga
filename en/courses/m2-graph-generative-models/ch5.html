5. Discrete diffusion: DiGress in detail

Continuous diffusion (Multimodal GenAI module ch. 3) noises with Gaussians — pointless for categorical atom types. DiGress (Vignac et al., ICLR 2023) transposes the machinery to the categorical, via the D3PM framework (Austin et al., 2021): noising = Markov chains on the categories, denoising = closed-form Bayesian posterior. Goal of this chapter: master all the equations.

Definition 5.1 — Forward process by transition matrices A type \(x\) (atom among \(K\)) is represented as a one-hot \(X \in \{0,1\}^K\). The noising is a Markov chain independent per node and per edge: $$q(X_t \mid X_{t-1}) = \mathrm{Cat}\big(X_t;\ Q_t X_{t-1}\big),$$ where \(Q_t \in [0,1]^{K \times K}\) is stochastic (rows summing to 1): row \(k\) is the law of the new type given the old one \(k\). The usual cases: uniform \(Q_t = (1 - \beta_t) I + \beta_t \frac{1}{K}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\) (with probability \(\beta_t\), forget and redraw uniformly); absorbing (toward a mask state [MASK] — the analogue of BERT's MLM, cf. LLMs module M2 ch. 3); discretized Gaussian (transition toward neighboring types). The composition is exact: \(q(X_t \mid X_s) = \mathrm{Cat}(X_t\,;\ \bar Q_s^t X_s)\) with \(\bar Q_s^t = Q_{s+1} \dots Q_t\).
Proposition 5.2 — Stationary marginal (proven in the uniform case) For the uniform kernel above, \(q(X_t \mid X_0) = \mathrm{Cat}\big((1-\bar\beta_t) X_0 + \bar\beta_t\, \mathbf{u}\big)\) with \(\mathbf{u}\) the uniform law and \(\bar\beta_t = 1 - \prod_{s \leq t}(1 - \beta_s)\): at large \(t = T\), the law is (almost) uniform — independent of \(X_0\), the condition for "total" noising.
By recursion: let \(p_t = q(X_t \mid X_0)\) (row vector); \(p_t^\top = Q_t\, p_{t-1}^\top = \big[(1-\beta_t) I + \frac{\beta_t}{K}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top\big] p_{t-1}^\top = (1-\beta_t)\,p_{t-1}^\top + \beta_t\, \mathbf{u}^\top\) since \(\mathbf{1}^\top p_{t-1}^\top = 1\) (mass law); unrolling, \(p_t^\top = \big(\prod_s (1-\beta_s)\big) X_0^\top + \big(1 - \prod_s(1-\beta_s)\big)\mathbf{u}^\top\). At \(t = T\), the weight of \(X_0\) is \(\prod_s (1-\beta_s) \approx 0\) (schedule \(\beta_s \to 1\)): the dataset marginal law is also accessible — one can make it converge toward the empirical marginal \(\mathbf{m} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_i X_0^{(i)}\) rather than the uniform, by replacing \(\mathbf{u}\) with \(\mathbf{m}\): DiGress uses this option ("no bond" edges dominate, reflecting them in the final law helps the denoising). ∎
Theorem 5.3 — Posterior \(q(X_{t-1} \mid X_t, X_0)\) in closed form (the heart of DiGress) $$q(X_{t-1} = j \mid X_t = i,\ X_0 = k) = \frac{(Q_{t-1})_{k j}\,(\bar Q_{t})_{j i}} {(\bar Q_{t-1})_{k i}},$$ all quantities being rows/columns of the transition matrices — direct computation, no network is involved; the network only serves to estimate \(\hat X_0\).
Bayes on the chain: \(q(X_{t-1} \mid X_t, X_0) \propto q(X_t \mid X_{t-1})\, q(X_{t-1} \mid X_0)\) (Markov: \(X_t \perp X_0 \mid X_{t-1}\)). In one-hot, \(q(X_t = i \mid X_{t-1} = j) = (Q_t)_{j i}\) and \(q(X_{t-1} = j \mid X_0 = k) = (\bar Q_{t-1})_{k j}\); the denominator is the marginal \(q(X_t = i \mid X_0 = k) = \sum_j (Q_t)_{j i} (\bar Q_{t-1})_{k j} = (\bar Q_t)_{k i}\) (matrix product — this is exactly the composition of Definition 5.1). ∎
Theorem 5.4 — DiGress: architecture, losses and topology perturbations
  1. Denoising network: a hybrid GNN (MPNN + global attention layers, graph-transformer style — cf. Advanced GNN module ch. 4) predicts \(\hat X_0, \hat E_0 = \mathrm{softmax}_K\big(\mathrm{GNN}(X_t, E_t)\big)\); training maximizes the likelihood of the clean types at the drawn steps \(t\): $$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{t, G_0}\;\mathbb{E}_{q(X_t, E_t \mid X_0, E_0)} \big[-\log p_\theta(\hat X_0 = X_0) - \log p_\theta(\hat E_0 = E_0)\big]\,;$$
  2. Chemical auxiliary losses: penalties on derived features — valence violations, bond lengths, angles: weighted by \(\lambda\)'s; they do not change the likelihood but steer the denoising toward chemically plausible regions (same lesson as NeVAE ch. 3, transposed);
  3. Noised topology: edges also appear/disappear: at each step, a fraction of edges is redrawn (added/removed) around the density diagonal — without this, the number of edges would be frozen by the conditioning; exchangeability is exact: no node order exists in the process (unlike GraphRNN);
  4. Sampling: from \((X_T, E_T)\) (marginal law), iterate \(\hat X_0 \to q(X_{t-1} \mid X_t, \hat X_0)\) (Theorem 5.3); cost: \(T\) GNN passes (typically \(T\) reduced by discrete DDIM), each \(\mathcal{O}(n^2)\) — parallel over all nodes: the contrast with the \(\Theta(n^2)\) sequential steps of GraphRNN;
  5. Guidance: classifier-free conditioning (recap from the Multimodal GenAI module ch. 4): DiGress trains the network with a conditional descriptor \(c\) sometimes masked; at sampling, \(\hat X_0^{cfg} \propto \hat X_0^{uncond}\cdot\big(\hat X_0^{cond}/\hat X_0^{uncond}\big)^{w}\) — the "guided" sample sequences follow the target property (e.g. \(QED\), logP penalty) at a measured cost in diversity (the trade-off of ch. 6 of the Control in Generative AI module);
  6. Inpainting: freeze a scaffold (known types and edges), noise the rest, generate: the posterior 5.3 is constrained trivially (the frozen positions are not denoised) — conditional generation by partial graph without retraining.
Cousins and descendants EDP-GNN (Niu et al., 2020): continuous score matching on the permuted adjacency (with permutation annealing); GDSS (Jo et al., ICML 2022): a coupled node/edge SDE system, solved as a joint continuous diffusion; EDM/EDT (3D): equivariant positions in addition to types — the geometric entry point (cf. Geometric Deep Learning M2 module for equivariance); NGG (ch. 9): the recipe moved into a latent space of graph features. The diffusion family dominates the 2023-2026 molecular state of the art on the quality/exchangeability trade-off — autoregressive models keep the exact likelihood, flows the gradient guidance: ch. 6 shows how to compare them fairly.
