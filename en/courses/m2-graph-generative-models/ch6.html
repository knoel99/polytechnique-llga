6. Evaluating rigorously: MMD, validity, FCD

A graph generator is not scored image by image but distribution against distribution (M1 recap); at the M2 level, we require: the formulas, their biases, and their attacks. Why so much rigor? because evaluation drives research: a truncated metric produces truncated models (ch. 10).

Definition 6.1 — MMD (Maximum Mean Discrepancy) on graph features For a kernel \(k\) on \(\mathbb{R}^d\) (e.g. Gaussian \(k(u,v) = e^{-\|u-v\|^2/2\sigma^2}\)) and a feature \(\phi : \mathcal{G} \to \mathbb{R}^d\) (degree histogram, clustering coefficients, orbit counts...): $$\mathrm{MMD}^2\big(\{G_i\}_{i\leq n}, \{G'_j\}_{j\leq m}\big) = \Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_i k(\phi(G_i), \cdot) - \frac{1}{m}\sum_j k(\phi(G'_j), \cdot)\Big\|^2_{\mathcal{H}} = \frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,i'} k_{ii'} + \frac{1}{m^2}\sum_{j,j'} k_{jj'} - \frac{2}{nm}\sum_{i,j} k_{ij},$$ computable in \(\mathcal{O}(n^2 + m^2)\); the estimator is unbiased if one removes the diagonals (\(\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\)). In practice: three canonical features (degrees, clustering, orbits) and a Gaussian kernel with median bandwidth.
Proposition 6.2 — Orbits: the right scale of motifs Orbit counts count the occurrences of each orbit of a small graph \(F\) (4-5 nodes: triangles, squares, paws...) modulo automorphisms: the number of orbits of 4-node graphs is 15 (not the number of graphs, 6); each node of \(G\) is classified into the orbit of its pattern neighborhood. Raw subgraph counts (without quotient by \(\mathrm{Aut}\)) over-count: a complete triangle counts 3 times as "closed paths"; the quotient corrects this constant factor per pattern. The theoretical link: these features are exactly empirical \(t(F, \cdot)\)'s of Theorem 1.4 — comparing orbit MMDs = comparing the graphons in the limiting sense (up to motif size).
Theorem 6.3 — FCD: the Fréchet ChemNet distance (Preuer et al., 2018) Let \(e : \text{molecule} \to \mathbb{R}^d\) be the embedding of an activity-predictor network (ChemNet, pre-trained) assumed approximately Gaussian on each family; $$\mathrm{FCD}(\mathcal{D}_{gén}, \mathcal{D}_{réf}) = \|\mu_1 - \mu_2\|^2 + \mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma_1 + \Sigma_2 - 2(\Sigma_1 \Sigma_2)^{1/2}\big),$$ with \((\mu_k, \Sigma_k)\) the means/covariances of the embeddings of the two sets — exactly the \(d^2_{\mathcal{W}_2}\) metric of two Gaussians (the analogue of the FID; Multimodal GenAI module ch. 8); the matrix square root is computed via eigenvalues. Key property: the FCD penalizes invalid molecules (no embedding → infinite distance or fixed penalty): it integrates validity and distributional similarity of properties — why it became the reference metric of molecular benchmarks (GuacaMol, MOSES).
The pitfalls of evaluation (to be known by heart)
  1. Validity ≠ quality: 100% validity is obtained by memorizing the dataset (perfect validity, 0 novelty); always report validity and uniqueness and novelty and FCD;
  2. MMD sensitive to sample size: the bias of the estimator grows like \(\mathcal{O}(1/n)\); comparing two models with different numbers of generated \(n\) is invalid — protocol: same \(n\), same seeds, several trials; significance is tested by bootstrap;
  3. Chosen features = favored families: degrees + clustering ignore the motif-position correlation; a generator can pass the exam on marginal distributions and fail on the joints (molecules whose fragments are right but assembled absurdly); hence the addition of orbits, FCD, and transport distances (Gromov-Wasserstein, Optimal Transport module);
  4. Metric overfitting: optimizing against the FCD (e.g. by guidance) eventually inflates it — honest evaluation keeps one metric kept secret (the held-out principle, cf. healthy benchmarking: Dwivedi et al., JMLR 2023, recalled in the Advanced GNN module ch. 6).
