Exercises / Labs

  1. Guided lab: a complete "home-grown" evaluation (the module's lab, ch. 6). (a) Build three reference families: 500 Erdős-Rényi graphs \(G(n, p{=}0.15)\), 500 two-block SBMs (\(p_{in} = 0.3\), \(p_{out} = 0.05\)), 500 Barabási-Albert (\(m = 2\)), with \(n \sim \mathcal{U}\{20, \dots, 40\}\); (b) implement the features (degree histograms, clustering coefficients, counts of 4 motifs: triangle, path of length 3, star) and the Gaussian-kernel MMD (def. 6.1), unbiased estimator; (c) compute the 3×3 MMDs families against families; (d) redo (c) with \(n = 50\) then \(n = 2{,}000\) samples and plot the bias; (e) bootstrap: 95% intervals on each MMD (200 resamplings); (f) write the table of ch. 6 and conclude: which families does the MMD-spectrum separate, which does it confuse? 
    # lab skeleton (networkx + numpy)
import networkx as nx, numpy as np

def features(gs):                     # gs: list of graphs
    return np.array([[np.mean(list(nx.degree(g))),                  # a mean degree
                      np.mean(list(nx.clustering(g).values())),     # b clustering
                      sum(nx.triangles(g).values()) / g.number_of_nodes()]  # c triangles
                     for g in gs])

def mmd(X, Y, sigma=None):            # Gaussian kernel, median bandwidth
    Z = np.vstack([X, Y]); n, m = len(X), len(Y)
    sigma = sigma or np.median(np.linalg.norm(Z[:, None] - Z[None], axis=-1))
    K = np.exp(-np.linalg.norm(Z[:, None] - Z[None], axis=-1) ** 2 / (2 * sigma ** 2))
    return np.sqrt(K[:n, :n].sum() / (n * (n - 1)) + K[n:, n:].sum() / (m * (m - 1))
                   - 2 * K[:n, n:].mean())                          # diagonals removed
    Guidance (expected results): the MMD clearly separates ER from the other two on degrees (Poisson distribution against heavy tail); SBM and BA are distinguished by clustering (strong in the SBM, weak in BA); with fixed features, the MMD decreases roughly as \(1/\sqrt{n}\) — comparing two models at different sample sizes is invalid (ch. 6, trap 2), the lab measures it. Triangles alone do not separate ER from SBM at equal mean \(p\): always report several features. The bootstrap shows overlapping intervals between close families: an MMD without an interval is not a conclusion. ∎
  2. Valence mask (ch. 3). Alphabet \(\{C, O, N\}\) with valences \(\{4, 2, 3\}\), single bonds only. (a) Write the masked sequential decoder (state = remaining valences \(r_v\), Proposition 3.3); (b) sample 1,000 molecules without the mask (unconstrained heads, logits \(\mathcal{N}(0,1)\)) then with the mask, up to 12 atoms; (c) measure the validity (all valences exactly zero at the end of generation) and compare it with the reasoning of Proposition 3.3.
    Solution: without the mask, each sampled bond violates a valence with increasing probability as generation proceeds (the \(r_v\) are no longer respected); the observed validity is typically a few percent at 12 atoms — and \(1-(1-\varepsilon)^T \to 1\) explains the collapse (prop. 3.3). With the mask: 100% validity by construction (the support is restricted to legal outputs), at the price of a slight bias if the logits are poorly calibrated — the renormalization redistributes the forbidden mass onto the legal ones. The code: maintain \(r_v\), forbid bonds \(b\) with \(b > r_u\) or \(b > r_v\), resample among the legal ones — eight lines, the most profitable investment of the module. ∎
  3. The absorbing posterior, in closed form (ch. 5). For the absorbing kernel (stay in state \(k\) with probability \(\alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t\), be masked otherwise: sink state \([\textsf{MASK}]\)), prove: $$q(X_{t-1} = X_0 \mid X_t = \textsf{MASK},\ X_0) = \frac{A_{t-1}\,\beta_t}{1 - A_t}, \qquad A_t = \prod_{s \leq t} \alpha_s,$$ and check that the remaining mass is on \(X_{t-1} = \textsf{MASK}\).
    Solution: \(X_t = \textsf{MASK}\) occurs with probability \(1 - A_t\) (complement of "never masked up to t"); jointly \(X_{t-1} = X_0, X_t = \textsf{MASK}\) requires "never masked up to \(t-1\) then masked exactly at \(t\)": probability \(A_{t-1}\beta_t\). The quotient gives the formula; the remaining mass equals \((1 - A_{t-1})/(1 - A_t)\) — the two sum to 1 since \(1 - A_t = (1 - A_{t-1}) + A_{t-1}\beta_t\). This is the D3PM-absorbing posterior: when \(X_t\) is masked, \(X_{t-1}\) is never revealed — the network must guess it (\(\hat X_0\)) then sample it with these probabilities: the inpainting machine of ch. 5. ∎
  4. Flow and guidance (ch. 4). (a) For the CouplingStep of ch. 4, show that the log-determinant of the Jacobian equals \(\sum_i \log s_i\) and is computed in \(\mathcal{O}(d)\); (b) derive the guidance step: to maximize \(Q(G_t)\), show that $$\nabla_{z_t} Q\big(G_t\big) = J_{f_\theta}(z_t)^\top\, \nabla_{x_t}\, Q\big(\mathrm{disc}(x_t)\big),$$ where \(\mathrm{disc}\) is the discretization (argmax/sampling); discuss going through the discretization (straight-through gradient / Gumbel-softmax).
    Hints: (a) the Jacobian is block-triangular \(\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ \ast & \mathrm{diag}(s) \end{pmatrix}\) (prop. 4.2): the determinant is the product of the diagonals; (b) chain rule on \(z_t \mapsto x_t = f_\theta(z_t) \mapsto G_t\); since the discretization is not differentiable, one replaces it with a relaxation (temperature softmax) or propagates back the gradient of the continuous predictor: the guidance step moves \(z_t\) without changing the weights — but each displacement pulls the trajectory away from the learned law: too large, it fabricates out-of-distribution graphs that the predictor scores well but that chemistry rejects (the fidelity/diversity trade-off of the Control module ch. 9). ∎
  5. The CFG sweep (ch. 7). Take up example 7.3 (unconditioned logits \((2.0;\ 1.5;\ 0.5)\), conditioned \((3.0;\ 0.5;\ 0.5)\)) and plot \(w \mapsto\) (probability of carbon, entropy) for \(w \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 8\}\). Where is the "elbow"?
    Solution: guided logits \(u + w(c - u) = (2 + w;\ 1.5 - w;\ 0.5)\). \(w = 0\): probs \((0.55;\ 0.33;\ 0.12)\), \(H \approx 1.15\) nats; \(w = 1\): \((0.86;\ 0.07;\ 0.07)\), \(H \approx 0.50\); \(w = 2\): \((0.96;\ 0.01;\ 0.03)\), \(H \approx 0.19\); \(w = 3\): \((0.987;\ 0.002;\ 0.011)\), \(H \approx 0.08\); \(w = 8\): carbon at 99.99%, \(H \approx 0\). Fidelity (probability of carbon) is already nearly maximal at \(w = 2\) while entropy has been divided by six: the elbow is at \(w \approx 2\) — beyond, one pays diversity without gaining the target. The exact curve of Theorem 9.2 of the Control module, on a single step. ∎
  6. Step-function graphon and triangle density (ch. 1 and 6). Let \(W\) be constant by blocks on \([0,1]^2\) with two blocks: density \(p\) in the block 1 (\(x, y \leq \tfrac{1}{2}\)), \(q\) in block 2, \(r\) between blocks. (a) Show $$t(K_3, W) = \tfrac{1}{8}\big(p^3 + q^3 + 3 p r^2 + 3 q r^2\big)$$ (integral over the 8 block combinations); (b) verify numerically with \(p = 0.9\), \(q = 0.6\), \(r = 0.05\) and by sampling a graph with \(n = 100\) nodes (50/50) — why do the two coincide to within \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})\)?
    Solution: each triplet \((a, b, c)\) of blocks contributes \(\tfrac{1}{8} W_{ab} W_{bc} W_{ac}\); \((1,1,1)\) gives \(p^3\), \((2,2,2)\) gives \(q^3\), the three permutations of \((1,1,2)\) give \(p r^2\) each, those of \((2,2,1)\) give \(q r^2\). Numerically \(t = (0.729 + 0.216 + 0.0068 + 0.0045)/8 \approx 0.1195\). The sampling realizes Bernoulli variables with parameters \(W(x_i, x_j)\), iid conditionally on the latents: the empirical triangle density is an average of bounded variables — concentration (Hoeffding) around \(t(K_3, W)\) at rate \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})\): this is the counting lemma of Theorem 1.4, verified hands in the mud. ∎
  7. FCD in dimension 2 (ch. 6). Two Gaussian embedding families of diagonal \(\mathbb{R}^2\): \(\mu_1 = (0, 0)\), \(\Sigma_1 = \mathrm{diag}(1, 4)\); \(\mu_2 = (1, 0)\), \(\Sigma_2 = \mathrm{diag}(4, 9)\). Compute the Fréchet distance; what does it become if the two families have the same mean?
    Solution: for diagonal covariances, \((\Sigma_1 \Sigma_2)^{1/2}\) is diagonal with square roots of the products: \(\sqrt{1 \times 4} = 2\), \(\sqrt{4 \times 9} = 6\). Hence \(\|\mu_1 - \mu_2\|^2 = 1\) and \(\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_1 + \Sigma_2 - 2(\Sigma_1\Sigma_2)^{1/2}) = (1 + 4 + 4 + 9) - 2(2 + 6) = 2\): FCD \(= 3\). With equal means, only the trace remains (here 2): the FCD separates means and covariances — why it detects at once a shift in mean properties (logP...) and an impoverishment of diversity (reduced variances), the two typical failures of generators. ∎
  8. Open: text-to-graph and target features (ch. 7, 9). (a) Ask an LLM to produce NetworkX code generating 500 graphs with a statistical specification (mean degree 4, clustering 0.3, n between 20 and 40) — the "Demystifying" route of ch. 7; (b) evaluate with the MMD of lab 1 against an SBM family calibrated to the same features; (c) critique: did the LLM optimize the features (by reading them in the prompt) or generate a law (unrequested distributional properties)? Compare with the NGG approach (conditioning by features, ch. 9): what additional guarantees does the trained model give? (Synthesis question — defense.)
    Hints: also measure unrequested features (spectrum, diameter, motifs): the LLM/SBM difference there is proof that the LLM satisfies the letter of the specification without capturing its distributional spirit (or the opposite); NGG, for its part, learns the law conditional on the features from real data — the unspecified correlations come from the data, not from the prompt. This is the exact frontier of open problem 10.4. ∎
