1. The problem, formalized

Definition 1.1 — The labeled graph space and its measure A labeled graph is a triple \(G = (X, E, n)\): \(n\) nodes, node types \(X \in \mathcal{X}^n\) (atoms, tags, features), edge types \(E \in \mathcal{E}^{n \times n}\) (single/double/triple bonds...). For fixed \(n\), the space \(\mathcal{G}_n = \mathcal{X}^n \times \mathcal{E}^{n\times n}\) is finite (cardinality \(\sim |\mathcal{E}|^{n^2}\)) but gigantic: with \(|\mathcal{E}| = 4\) and \(n = 30\) atoms, \(4^{900} \approx 10^{542}\) adjacencies — hence the need for a strong structural prior. The total space is the disjoint union \(\mathcal{G} = \bigsqcup_{n \geq 1} \mathcal{G}_n\); a law \(p\) on \(\mathcal{G}\) mixes a law on size \(p(n)\) and conditional laws \(p(G \mid n)\). Generating = being able to sample from \(p_\theta \approx p_\star\), the empirical law of a dataset \(\mathcal{D} = \{G_1, \dots, G_N\}\).
Definition 1.2 — Exchangeability (in-depth recap) A law \(p\) on \(\mathcal{G}\) is exchangeable if for every permutation \(\pi\) of \(\{1..n\}\): $$p\big((X_{\pi(1)},\dots,X_{\pi(n)}),\ (\pi E \pi^\top),\ n\big) = p(X, E, n).$$ The Aldous-Hoover theorem — precisely: Aldous (1981) and Hoover (1979), see Lovász (2012) — states that exchangeable laws are exactly those representable by a graphon: \(x_i \sim \mathcal{U}([0,1])\), \(A_{ij} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(W(x_i,x_j))\). Practical consequence: a correct generator must be permutation-invariant, either by construction (one-shot VAEs, diffusion: exchangeable GNN architectures), or on average (autoregressive models: randomize the construction order, cf. ch. 2).
Proposition 1.3 — Aldous-Hoover, forward direction (guided sketch) If \(p\) is exchangeable, there exists \(W\) such that \(p\) is the law of the graphon model.
Exchangeability \(\Rightarrow\) the law of the adjacencies is invariant under every subgroup of permutations; the De Finetti representation for exchangeable arrays (extension of the De Finetti theorem from exchangeable sequences to symmetric matrices) then gives the existence of iid uniform latent variables \((x_i)\) and a conditioning measurable function \(W\): conditionally on the \(x_i\), the \(A_{ij}\) are independent with laws \(W(x_i, x_j)\). The idea of De Finetti's proof (sequence case): the empirical frequency \(\hat F_k = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{i \leq k} \mathbf{1}[S_i]\) of an event converges a.s. to a random variable \(F\) (bounded martingale, martingale convergence theorem — cf. Refresher Statistics module); exchangeability forces conditional independence given \(F\), and \(F\) plays the role of the latent parameter. The symmetric-array case adds the product structure \([0,1]^2\). ∎
Theorem 1.4 — Counting to generate: subgraph counting determines the graphon Two graphons \(W, W'\) are equal in cut metric \(\delta_\square\) iff they have the same homomorphism densities \(t(F, W)\) for all graphs \(F\): $$t(F, W) = \int_{[0,1]^{|F|}} \prod_{(i,j) \in E(F)} W(x_i, x_j)\, dx_1 \dots dx_{|F|}.$$ Now \(t(F, W)\) is estimated on a large graph by the subgraph density: \(t(F, G_n) \to t(F, W)\) a.s. (counting lemma). This is the theoretical foundation of ch. 6: comparing generators by their motif statistics (triangles, paths, stars) amounts to comparing their limiting graphons — orbit-MMD evaluation is a finite, empirical version of this theorem.
