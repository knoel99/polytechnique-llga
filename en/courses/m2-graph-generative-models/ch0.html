0. Recap: generating graphs in M1

Recap of the Graph ML module (M1 P2, ch. 9) — what is assumed known
  • The problem: sample \(G \sim p_\theta(G)\) where \(p\) is learned on a family of graphs (active molecules, networks...), with three specific difficulties: variable size, discrete outputs, a space quotiented by isomorphism;
  • The limiting model: graphons \(W : [0,1]^2 \to [0,1]\) and the Aldous-Hoover theorem — every sequence of exchangeable graphs admits a limiting graphon in the sense of the cut metric; estimation by step functions;
  • Classical random models: Erdős-Rényi \(G(n,p)\) (iid edges, degrees \(\sim \mathrm{Bin}(n-1,p)\), connectivity thresholds) and the SBM: \(P(A_{ij}=1 \mid z_i=a, z_j=b) = B_{ab}\), likelihood, EM/MCMC inference, the Kesten-Stigum threshold;
  • An overview of the deep families: GraphVAE (one-shot decoder), NeVAE (sequential decoding constrained by valence), GraphRNN (sequentialization by adjacency rows, BFS orders), DiGress (categorical noising \(q(X_t|X_{t-1}) = \mathrm{Cat}(Q_t X_{t-1})\), closed-form Bayesian denoising);
  • First metrics: validity/uniqueness/novelty, MMD on histograms, FCD — the analogue of the image FID (cf. Multimodal GenAI module ch. 8);
  • The GNN foundation: message passing, MPNN, GIN and the 1-WL bound (cf. Advanced GNN module ch. 1) — the encoder of all the models in this module.
The module's approach The M1 told the story of graph generation: each family was presented there with its idea and one equation. The M2 proves it: exact sampling conditions, changes of variables, closed-form posteriors, evaluation biases — then goes beyond the M1 on three axes: (i) flows (ch. 4, absent from the M1); (ii) distributional evaluation pushed to its paradoxes (ch. 6); (iii) conditional generation and text-to-graph with LLMs (ch. 7), the 2026 frontier. The control of generators (hard constraints, guidance) is covered in detail in the Control in Generative AI module (M2, ch. 8) — we lay here the foundations it relies on.
