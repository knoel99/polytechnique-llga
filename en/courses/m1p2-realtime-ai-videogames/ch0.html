0. Refresher: graphs, priority queues, probability

L3 prerequisites mobilized (see Refresher CS, chapters 3–5)
  • Graphs: \(G = (V, E)\), weighted or not; breadth-first search (BFS, unweighted edges), depth-first search, shortest paths — Dijkstra \(O(|E| \log |V|)\) with a binary heap, and A* (Refresher CS, chapter 5);
  • Priority queues / heaps: the data structure of pathfinding algorithms; insertion and extraction of the minimum in \(O(\log n)\);
  • Amortized complexity: average cost per operation over a sequence (the heart of the budget tricks of chapter 1);
  • Discrete probabilities: expectation, binomial law, Hoeffding's inequality \(\Pr(|\bar X - \mu| \geq \varepsilon) \leq 2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}\) — the tool that justifies the Monte Carlo simulations of chapter 5 (see Refresher in Statistics, chapter on concentration).

Why a "games" module in an AI master's program? Because video games are the AI environment with the purest constraints: decide well, in a few milliseconds, with bounded memory, for dozens of agents at once. The same trade-offs reappear in robotics, trading, embedded systems — and games provide reproducible test benches that have produced some of the greatest breakthroughs in AI (chapters 5 and 6). The course's subtitle — decisive & collaborative actions — announces the two axes: deciding (individually, chapters 3 and 5) and acting together (chapters 4 and 7).

Note — Position in the brochure An option of the period 2 elective group (MSc&T brochure); the referenced instructor will be confirmed at the start of the period. The module connects naturally to the Reinforcement Learning and Autonomous Agents course (mandatory): chapter 5 for bandits and MCTS, chapter 6 for deep RL, chapter 7 for autonomous LLM-based agents; the present module provides the real-time engineering these formalisms lack.
