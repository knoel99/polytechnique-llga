Can we publish a model without revealing the individuals it was trained on? Can it say, for this input, not only its prediction but also the degree of confidence one can place in it? This module answers these two questions with the two families of formal guarantees of modern machine learning: differential privacy (mechanisms, composition, DP-SGD, industrial deployments) then uncertainty quantification (calibration, ensembles, conformal prediction, LLM UQ). In the LLGA master, this is the module that turns evaluation by accuracy alone into a demonstrable contract — a foundation for any responsible deployment, from tabular data to large language models. Official course description not published (the 2026-2027 SynapseS S3/S4 catalog is empty): these course materials expand on the brochure's announcement, and their own additions are flagged as going beyond the syllabus.

What you will be able to do

  • State the definition of \(\varepsilon\)-differential privacy and prove that a mechanism satisfies it (Laplace, Gaussian, exponential);
  • Compose mechanisms (sequential and parallel theorems) and tighten the privacy budget with Rényi DP;
  • Choose between local and global DP, and explain precisely what DP-SGD modifies in SGD;
  • Read a reliability diagram and quantify the miscalibration of a classifier (ECE, Brier score);
  • Compare MC dropout and deep ensembles for estimating the uncertainty of a deep network;
  • Implement "split" conformal prediction and prove its marginal coverage guarantee;
  • Produce adaptive intervals via conformalized quantile regression;
  • Audit the uncertainty of an LLM and argue for a decision under uncertainty (alert threshold, abstention).

Where this course fits in the program

M2 module offered in period 1, outside the recommended track: it is aimed at all students who want to add demonstrable guarantees to their profile, whatever their specialty. Prerequisites: the first encounter with differential privacy of chapter 5 of Emerging ML (\(\varepsilon\)-DP definition, Laplace noise, DP-SGD as a conclusion of federated learning) is revisited in ch. 0 then developed; the language of statistical guarantees of Statistics in Action (intervals, tests, bootstrap) is assumed acquired.

Downstream modules: the privacy part of Explainability, Security, Privacy of LLMs (memorization, membership inference, DP of LLMs, ch. 7–8) relies directly on the mechanisms and composition proved here; on the production side, calibration and coverage feed the continuous evaluation and alert decisions of the ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles module.

Course outline

  • Reminders: probability, concentration, DP as seen in M1 — puts the Markov/Hoeffding inequalities and the DP definition back in shape before going further.
  • Differential privacy: the foundation — definitions (neighboring, mechanism), post-processing properties and a first Laplace theorem.
  • Mechanisms: Laplace, Gaussian, exponential — sensitivity, noise calibration and the exponential mechanism for discrete responses.
  • Composition: advanced and Rényi DP — why naive compositions waste the budget, and how Rényi DP tightens it.
  • Local versus global, and DP-SGD — two trust models, then the algorithm that trains networks while preserving privacy.
  • DP in industry and attacks — real deployments, the \(\varepsilon\) budget in practice, and attacks (reconstruction, inference) that motivate the theory.
  • Uncertainty: sources and vocabulary — aleatoric versus epistemic, and why modern networks are poorly calibrated.
  • Calibration: diagrams, ECE, Brier — measuring the gap between stated confidence and observed frequency.
  • Bayesian deep learning: MC dropout versus ensembles — two practical approximations of epistemic uncertainty, with their trade-offs.
  • Conformal prediction: theorem and algorithm — a distribution-free coverage guarantee in a few lines of code.
  • Quantile and conformalized regression — intervals whose width adapts to the query point.
  • UQ for LLMs — verbalized confidence, semantic coherence and the limits of the previous methods on generations.
  • Applications: deciding under uncertainty — assembling the tools to arbitrate abstention, alert and action in a real system.
  • Exercises — from proofs of mechanisms to conformal implementations on real data.
  • References — the primary sources (Dwork, Rényi DP, Angelopoulos) to go to the text.

How to study this module

  • Redo the proofs on scratch paper: every theorem fits on one page, and it is by rewriting them that one retains the exact hypotheses.
  • Implement Laplace noise and "split" conformal yourself (a few dozen lines): the guarantees become tangible when you see the coverage histogram.
  • Whenever an \(\varepsilon\) appears, ask yourself which pair of neighboring datasets it distinguishes — that is the only way to give it meaning.
  • Work through the two parts in order but in parallel in your head: chapters 6–12 replay in UQ the same moves as in privacy (guarantee, budget, utility trade-off).
  • Do the exercises before reading the solutions, and compare your intervals with those of others: coverage is verified, not declared.
The LLGA perspective The singularity of the master is to cross LLMs, graphs, and applications; now privacy and uncertainty are precisely the questions where this intersection remains the most open — DP on graphs (sensitive edges, neighbors), UQ of text and graph generations. Whether you aim for research (PhD, R&D) or data careers in a regulated sector (health, banking, insurance), knowing how to certify a learning system — not merely measure it — is what distinguishes an engineer trained in LLGA.
