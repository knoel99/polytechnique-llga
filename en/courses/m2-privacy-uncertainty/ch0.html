0. Reminders: probability, concentration, DP as seen in M1

Prerequisites (M1 modules)
  • Concentration inequalities (cf. Machine Learning module, ch. 0): Markov \(\mathbb{P}(Z \geq t) \leq \mathbb{E}Z/t\), Chernoff/Hoeffding for sums of Bernoullis — they provide the guarantees of the mechanisms and conformal coverage;
  • Differential privacy, first encounter (cf. Emerging ML module, chapter 5): \(\varepsilon\)-DP definition, Laplace noise, DP-SGD as a conclusion of federated learning — the present module takes up this basis and develops it: mechanisms (ch. 2), fine composition (ch. 3), industrial implementations (ch. 4–5);
  • Generalization theory (cf. Machine Learning module, ch. 7): bias-variance trade-off, error decomposition — the UQ part (ch. 6–12) answers the dual question: not just "what risk on average", but "what guarantee on this prediction";
  • Training of networks (cf. Deep Learning module, ch. 3 and 10): SGD, mini-batches, dropout — DP-SGD (ch. 4) and MC dropout (ch. 8) modify them marginally but change their nature;
  • Statistics of tests and intervals (cf. Refresher Statistics module, ch. 4 and 6; Statistics in Action module, ch. 7 and 10): frequentist guarantees, p-values, bootstrap — the language of the coverage guarantees of ch. 9.
Why these two parts in a single module? Privacy and UQ are the two faces of the "right to error" of learning systems: privacy quantifies what a model can reveal about the training data, UQ quantifies what it can assert about a new input. Both escape evaluation by accuracy alone; both provide formal guarantees (inequalities, theorems) rather than empirical promises; and both converge on LLMs (ch. 11): models large enough to memorize and confident enough to mislead. The thread: make the uncertainties computable, then arbitrate them.
