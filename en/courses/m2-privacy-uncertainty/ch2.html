2. Mechanisms: Laplace, Gaussian, exponential

Theorem 2.1 — The Laplace mechanism (Dwork et al., 2006) Let \(f\) have sensitivity \(\Delta_1\). The mechanism $$M(x) = f(x) + (Z_1, \dots, Z_k), \qquad Z_i \sim \mathrm{Laplace}\big(0, \tfrac{\Delta_1}{\varepsilon}\big)\ \text{i.i.d.}$$ is \(\varepsilon\)-DP. The Laplace law with parameter \(b\) has density \(p(z) = \frac{1}{2b} e^{-|z|/b}\).
For an output \(z \in \mathbb{R}^k\), the ratio of densities equals $$\frac{p_x(z)}{p_{x'}(z)} = \prod_{i=1}^k \exp\!\Big( -\frac{\varepsilon}{\Delta_1}\big( |z_i - f_i(x)| - |z_i - f_i(x')| \big) \Big) = \exp\!\Big( \frac{\varepsilon}{\Delta_1} \sum_i \big( |z_i - f_i(x')| - |z_i - f_i(x)| \big) \Big).$$ By the triangle inequality, \(\sum_i \big( |z_i - f_i(x')| - |z_i - f_i(x)| \big) \leq \sum_i |f_i(x) - f_i(x')| \leq \Delta_1\), hence \(p_x(z)/p_{x'}(z) \leq e^{\varepsilon}\) for all \(z\) — integrating over \(S\) gives the definition (and symmetrically by exchanging \(x, x'\)). ∎
Utility, computed. For a counting query (\(\Delta_1 = 1\)): \(\mathbb{P}(|Z| \geq t) = e^{-\varepsilon t}\). With \(\varepsilon = \ln 20 \approx 3.0\) and \(b = 1/\varepsilon \approx 0.33\): the published answer is shifted by at least 1 unit with probability \(e^{-\varepsilon} = 5\%\), by at least 3 units with probability \(20^{-3} \approx 0.01\%\) — a modest noise at the scale of the single individual one seeks to mask.
Theorem 2.2 — The Gaussian mechanism, analytic form For \(f\) with \(\ell_2\) sensitivity \(\Delta_2\), \(M(x) = f(x) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_k)\) is \((\varepsilon, \delta)\)-DP as soon as $$\sigma \;\geq\; \Delta_2\, \frac{\sqrt{2 \ln(1.25/\delta)}}{\varepsilon} \qquad (\varepsilon \leq 1).$$ Numerically, for \(\varepsilon = 1\), \(\delta = 10^{-6}\): \(\sqrt{2\ln(1.25 \times 10^6)} = \sqrt{28.1} \approx 5.3\), hence \(\sigma \approx 5.3\,\Delta_2\). Why prefer Gaussian over Laplace in ML: (i) Gaussian noise composes very well (Rényi DP, ch. 3); (ii) it is isotropic and gradient-compatible; (iii) the Gaussian tail is lighter for large \(k\). Proof: computation of the ratio of Gaussian densities bounded by \(e^{\varepsilon}\) except on a set of probability \(\delta\) — analysis in Dwork & Roth (2014), lemma A.1 (the factor 1.25 is a convenience constant of the upper bound).
Definition 2.3 — The exponential mechanism (McSherry-Talwar, 2007) For a non-numeric response (choosing a median, an item, a model): a score function \(u(x, h)\) (to be maximized) with sensitivity \(\Delta_u = \max_h \max_{x \sim x'} |u(x, h) - u(x', h)|\), and the output law $$\mathbb{P}(M(x) = h) \;\propto\; \exp\!\Big( \frac{\varepsilon\, u(x, h)}{2\Delta_u} \Big).$$ Utility guarantee: if \(h^* = \arg\max_h u(x, h)\) and \(u(x, h^*) - u(x, h) \geq \mathrm{GAP}\), then \(\mathbb{P}(u(x, M(x)) \leq u(x, h^*) - \mathrm{GAP}) \leq |\mathcal H| \exp(-\varepsilon\, \mathrm{GAP}/2\Delta_u)\) — the gap to the optimum decreases exponentially with \(\varepsilon\) and the gap. This is the mechanism of discrete choices: model selection, private top-k, auction mechanisms.
Example 2.4 — Private median: publishing a robust statistic A "median age" query has unbounded sensitivity (shifting the median by one row can make it jump far if the data are concentrated). The exponential mechanism with \(u(x, h) = -\|x - h\|_0\) (minus the number of individuals on the wrong side) publishes an \(\varepsilon\)-DP median with controlled utility: this is the canonical example of the structural choice of mechanism according to the query — the privacy engineer spends most of their time here, not on the noise itself.
