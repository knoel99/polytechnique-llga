3. Composition: advanced and Rényi DP

Theorem 3.1 — Basic composition, proved If \(M_1\) is \(\varepsilon_1\)-DP and \(M_2\), applied after \(M_1\) and allowed to depend on its output, is \(\varepsilon_2\)-DP, then \((M_1, M_2)\) is \((\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2)\)-DP — adaptive composition accumulates linearly.
By conditioning then post-processing: for measurable \(S\), \(\mathbb{P}(M_2(M_1(x), x) \in S_2 \mid M_1(x) = m) \leq e^{\varepsilon_2} \mathbb{P}(M_2(M_1(x'), x') \in S_2 \mid M_1(x') = m)\) — caution: conditioning only makes sense via the regular version; the clean proof handles densities: \(p_x(m, s) = p_x(m)\, p_x(s \mid m) \leq e^{\varepsilon_1} p_{x'}(m) \cdot e^{\varepsilon_2} p_{x'}(s \mid m) = e^{\varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2} p_{x'}(m, s)\), then one integrates. Adaptivity breaks nothing because \(e^{\varepsilon_2}\) bounds the ratio for every value of the history. ∎
Theorem 3.2 — Advanced composition (Dwork, Rothblum, Vadhan, 2010) The composition of \(k\) adaptive \((\varepsilon_0, \delta_0)\)-DP mechanisms is \((\varepsilon^\*, \, k\delta_0 + \delta')\)-DP for every \(\delta' > 0\) with $$\varepsilon^\* = \sqrt{2 k \ln(1/\delta')}\; \varepsilon_0 \;+\; k\, \varepsilon_0 \big( e^{\varepsilon_0} - 1 \big).$$ In \(\sqrt k\) rather than \(k\) — the idea (sketch: the proof bounds the deviation of the sum of log-ratios via Azuma, then converts a sub-Gaussian concentration into an \((\varepsilon, \delta)\)-type threshold). Numerical. \(k = 100\) releases, \(\varepsilon_0 = 0.01\), \(\delta' = 10^{-5}\): \(\sqrt{200 \times 11.5} \times 0.01 = 0.48\), second term \(100 \times 0.01 \times 0.0101 = 0.010\), hence \(\varepsilon^\* \approx 0.49\) versus \(\varepsilon = k\varepsilon_0 = 1.0\) under basic composition — twice as good; the gain explodes for large \(k\) (ch. 4, DP-SGD: \(k \sim 10^4\) steps).
Definition 3.3 — Rényi DP (Mironov, 2017) The Rényi divergence of order \(\alpha > 1\) between two laws \(P, Q\): $$D_\alpha(P \| Q) = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1} \ln\, \mathbb{E}_{Q}\!\Big[ \Big( \frac{dP}{dQ} \Big)^{\alpha} \Big].$$ \(M\) is \((\alpha, \varepsilon)\)-RDP if \(D_\alpha(M(x) \| M(x')) \leq \varepsilon\) for all neighboring databases. Three computational virtues: (i) Gaussian mechanism: for \(f\) with sensitivity \(\Delta_2\), the noise \(\mathcal N(0, \sigma^2)\) is exactly \(\big(\alpha, \frac{\alpha \Delta_2^2}{2\sigma^2}\big)\)-RDP for every \(\alpha\); (ii) composition = addition at fixed \(\alpha\): \(k\) mechanisms \((\alpha, \varepsilon_i)\)-RDP compose into \((\alpha, \sum_i \varepsilon_i)\)-RDP (proof: conditional Hölder inequality applied to the joint density — the global likelihood ratio raised to the exponent \(\alpha\) factorizes into a product of individual ratios, each bounded by \(e^{\varepsilon_i \alpha}\), and the expectation of the product is bounded term by term); (iii) conversion: \((\alpha, \varepsilon)\)-RDP \(\Rightarrow\) \((\varepsilon + \frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{\alpha - 1}, \delta)\)-DP — one then optimizes \(\alpha\) a posteriori.
Example 3.4 — The RDP account, computed end to end 64 successive releases of the same Gaussian counting query, noise \(\sigma = 8\Delta_2\), target \(\delta = 10^{-6}\). (a) Classical route. Each release: \(\varepsilon_0 = \sqrt{2 \ln(1.25/\delta_0)}\,\Delta/\sigma \approx 5.3/8 = 0.66\). Advanced composition over \(k = 64\): \(\sqrt{128 \times 13.8} \times 0.66 + 64 \times 0.66 \times (e^{0.66} - 1) \approx 27.8 + 39.3 \approx \mathbf{67}\). Ruinous. (b) RDP route. Each release is \((\alpha, \alpha/128)\)-RDP (\(\alpha\Delta^2/2\sigma^2 = \alpha/128\)); the 64 compose into \((\alpha, \alpha/2)\)-RDP. Conversion: \(\varepsilon(\alpha) = \frac{\alpha}{2} + \frac{13.8}{\alpha - 1}\), minimized at \(\alpha - 1 = \sqrt{2 \times 13.8} \approx 5.3\), i.e. \(\varepsilon^\* \approx 3.1 + 2.6 = \mathbf{5.7}\).
Same budget, same \(\delta\) — RDP accounting saves a factor of ~10. This is exactly what the "moments accountants" of DP-SGD do (ch. 4); the later improvement (amplification by subsampling, Wang et al. 2019) adds another significant factor when each step touches only a mini-batch.
The family of generalized DP z-CDP (Bun & Steinke, 2016 — Rényi divergence parameterized by \(\rho = \varepsilon/\alpha\)), f-DP (Dong, Roth, Su, 2019 — binary tests, exact frontier): same program, different representations; the practitioner retains: divergences with exact additivity (Rényi) give the tightest composition bounds, and the interface (Opacus, TF Privacy, OpenDP) performs the conversion to \((\varepsilon, \delta)\) for governance.
