10. Quantile and conformalized regression

Definition 10.1 — Distributional quantile, quantile regression, pinball loss (Koenker & Bassett, 1978) The quantile \(q_\tau(x)\) of \(Y \mid X = x\) satisfies \(\mathbb P(Y \leq q_\tau(x) \mid x) = \tau\). One estimates it by minimizing the pinball loss: $$\ell_\tau(y, \hat q) = \begin{cases} \tau\,(y - \hat q) & y \geq \hat q \\ (1 - \tau)\,(\hat q - y) & y < \hat q \end{cases} \qquad \text{(subgradients } \in [\tau - 1, \tau]\text{)}.$$ This is a convex problem (a linear program for the linear model; gradient descent for networks); training two heads \(\hat q_{\text{lo}} = \hat q_{\alpha/2}\), \(\hat q_{\text{hi}} = \hat q_{1 - \alpha/2}\) gives an adaptive prediction band — wide where the data are dispersed (heteroscedastic aleatoric, ch. 6). Without an exact coverage guarantee, however: the learned quantiles are optimistic on the train set.
Theorem 10.2 — Conformalized Quantile Regression (Romano, Patterson, Candès, 2019) Non-conformity score on calibration: $$s_i = \max\big( \hat q_{\text{lo}}(x_i) - y_i,\; y_i - \hat q_{\text{hi}}(x_i) \big).$$ Taking \(\hat q\) = the \(\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil\)-th smallest score, the band $$C(x) = \big[ \hat q_{\text{lo}}(x) - \hat q,\; \hat q_{\text{hi}}(x) + \hat q \big]$$ has coverage \(\geq 1 - \alpha\) in finite samples (thm 9.2), and inherits the adaptivity of the learned quantiles: the bands vary with \(x\). In short: CQR = quantiles (local shape) + conformal (global guarantee) — the dominant recipe in practice.
Example 10.3 — CQR on a synthetic case, verified \(y = 2x + \sin(2x)\, (1 + 0.5x)\,\varepsilon\) (increasing variance). In the lab: (1) a two-head pinball network (\(\tau \in \{0.05, 0.95\}\)); (2) 1000 calibration points, scores \(s_i\); (3) \(\hat q \approx\) corrected empirical quantile; (4) over 100 repetitions, mean coverage 89.8% (target 90%), bands ~3× narrower than the constant-residual split conformal in calm regions — the "hourglass" plot of the bands flaring at large \(x\) is the visual deliverable of the lab (exercise 7).
