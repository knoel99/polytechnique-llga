Exercises

  1. Laplace mechanism. (a) Re-prove thm 2.1 for \(k = 1\). (b) Counting with ε = ln 20: probability of an error ≥ 2? ≥ 5? (c) 50-bin histogram, total ε = 1; distribute the noise (composition) and compare the mean squared error with the vector Laplace mechanism in a single query.
  2. Composition. (a) Redo the computation 3.4 for \(\sigma = 12\), \(k = 256\), \(\delta = 10^{-5}\). (b) Inverse: what \(\sigma\) for \(k = 10^4\) DP-SGD steps, ε = 2, δ = 10⁻⁵, \(\gamma = 0.01\) (approximation without amplification, then with the \(\gamma^2\) factor)? (c) Show that RDP composition at fixed \(\alpha\) is exact for Gaussians (direct computation of the Rényi divergence of a product).
  3. Local DP. (a) Prove the debiasing formula 4.2 and its variance factor. (b) In RAPPOR with 128 bits, 2 successive RRs: what ε per bit? (c) Sizing: precision of ±2 points on a proportion, \(p = 0.75\) — how many users?
  4. DP-SGD. On MNIST: implement per-example clipping + noise (numpy or Opacus), plot accuracy vs ε for σ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2}, comment on the frontier; ablation on \(C\) (0.1 to 10); verify that without clipping the mechanism is no longer DP.
  5. Calibration. (a) Recompute the ECE 7.2 with 10 equal-width bins and comment on the sensitivity. (b) Implement the reliability diagram. (c) Temperature scaling on CIFAR-10 (ResNet): ECE before/after, NLL before/after, accuracy unchanged? (d) Show that the constant 0.75 beats the classifier of example 7.4 in Brier score but not in log-loss if one of the events becomes certain.
  6. MC dropout versus ensembles. 1D heteroscedastic regression: train (a) one network + MC dropout (\(T = 50\)); (b) 5 networks (deep ensemble); compare NLL, RMSE, coverage of the ±2σ bands; (c) test both out of distribution (extrapolation); (d) compare training and inference times, and conclude in a budget-constrained situation.
  7. Conformal. (a) Re-prove the uniformity of the rank with ties (convention of thm 9.2). (b) Empirically verify the coverage over 1000 repetitions (n = 1000, α = 0.1): histogram of the coverages — what happens if the data are not exchangeable (drift)? (c) Effect of the calibration size: \(n \in \{20, 100, 1000\}\); (d) implement per-class Mondrian and measure the coverage inequality across classes.
  8. CQR. Reproduce example 10.3; compare the mean widths CQR vs constant split conformal; what happens if the two quantile heads cross (lo > hi)? Fix it (monotonicity constraint or sorting).
  9. LLM UQ. (a) On 100 trivia questions: verbalized confidence vs actual frequency — plot the reliability diagram. (b) Self-consistency K = 10: correlation between agreement and correctness. (c) Group the answers semantically (by hand or NLI) and compare lexical vs semantic entropy. (d) At small scale: build a conformal set of answers and measure the coverage.
  10. Decision. Screening: prevalence 5%, costs \(c_{FP} = 1\), \(c_{FN} = 20\). (a) Optimal threshold on calibrated probabilities. (b) Re-derive with probabilities overconfident by 10 points — quantify the cost of miscalibration. (c) Add the abstention option with a delegation cost; plot risk-coverage.
  11. Project. Complete "privacy + UQ" pipeline: a sensitive dataset (e.g. Adult): (1) publish useful statistics under budget ε = 2 (mechanisms, composition); (2) train a private predictive model (DP-SGD); (3) calibrate and conformalize its outputs; (4) write the guarantee sheet (ε, δ, coverage, ECE) and the limitations discussion. Grading: 30% privacy, 30% UQ, 20% empirical validation, 20% sheet and honesty about the limits.
