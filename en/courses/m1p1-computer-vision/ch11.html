11. Research of the instructor: urban LiDAR at IGN

The module is taught by Mathieu Brédif, researcher at IGN (MATIS laboratory, ENSG) and professor at Université Gustave Eiffel. His work traverses exactly the program of the semester: mobile acquisition, registration, semantic analysis of urban clouds, and the handling of LiDAR sampling defects. Verified selection:

  • TerraMobilita/iQmulus urban benchmark. B. Vallet, M. Brédif, A. Serna, B. Marcotegui, N. Paparoditis, "TerraMobilita/iQmulus urban point cloud analysis benchmark", Computers & Graphics 49, 2015 — an annotated dataset acquired by Stéréopolis (IGN's mobile mapping vehicle) in Paris and Rueil-Malmaison, with two evaluation tasks: facade segmentation and urban object classification (see data.ign.fr). The international reference for comparing mobile cloud analysis methods — the "test bench" of ch. 8-10.
  • Semantic segmentation by range images: LU-Net. P. Biasutti, V. Lepetit, M. Brédif, J.-F. Aujol, A. Bugeau, "LU-Net: An Efficient Network for 3D LiDAR Point Cloud Semantic Segmentation Based on End-to-End-Learned 3D Features and U-Net", ICCV Workshops 2019 — the architecture of ch. 9 (U-Net) applied to the range images of ch. 10, with 3D features learned end to end: the "signs, lampposts, facades" class at a fraction of the cost of 3D convolutions.
  • LiDAR disocclusion and visibility. Mobile LiDARs see streets from the street: everything occluded (behind a car, a tree) is missing from the cloud. The series of works "Disocclusion of 3D LiDAR point clouds using range images" (ISPRS Hannover Workshop 2017), "Visibility estimation in point clouds with variable density" (VISAPP 2019), and "Diffusion and inpainting of reflectance and height LiDAR orthoimages" (Computer Vision and Image Understanding 179, 2019), with P. Biasutti, J.-F. Aujol, A. Bugeau — extended as early as ICIP 2016 with M. Bevilacqua ("Visibility estimation and joint inpainting of Lidar depth maps") —, addresses these holes: estimating visibility, inpainting range and ortho-reflectance images — LiDAR processed as an image (ch. 2), with geometry as a constraint.
  • 3D models and historical textures. His recent work projects very many photographs (heritage ones included) onto current urban 3D models — projective "multitexturing" of cities and clouds (with M. S. Gomes de Castro) — an exercise in homographies (ch. 5) and visibility (ch. 10) at the scale of a city. See his full CV on HAL.
Note — The guiding thread One theme connects this work: processing massive 3D data with well-tuned 2D tools — range images, U-Net, inpainting — while respecting the geometry of the sensor. This is exactly the approach of ch. 1 to 10: model the formation (ch. 1), choose the representation (ch. 10), then apply the right machinery (ch. 9). The module's project topics draw on the TerraMobilita benchmark and IGN's open data.
