0. Review: linear algebra, convolution, probability

L3 review material used
  • Linear algebra: eigenvalues, eigenvectors, orthogonal matrices (\(R^\top R = I\), they preserve norms), trace (\(\mathrm{tr}(AB) = \mathrm{tr}(BA)\)), and singular value decomposition (SVD);
  • Differential calculus: partial derivatives, gradient, Taylor expansions to order 1 and 2, chain rule;
  • Convolution and Fourier transform (see the Signal Processing module, chapters 2 and 5): 1D convolution, convolution theorem, Shannon theorem; we extend them to two dimensions;
  • Probability: expectation, covariance, Gaussians, independence; least squares method and normal equations (see the Machine Learning module, chapter 2).
Theorem 0.1 — SVD and best rank-k approximation Every real matrix \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\) can be written \(A = U \Sigma V^\top\) with \(U, V\) orthogonal and \(\Sigma\) diagonal with entries \(\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \dots \geq 0\). Moreover, the best approximation of \(A\) in the Frobenius sense by a matrix of rank \(\leq k\) is \(\sum_{i\leq k} \sigma_i u_i v_i^\top\), with error \(\big(\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2\big)^{1/2}\) (Eckart-Young theorem).
Sketch. The singular vectors are the principal axes of \(AA^\top\) and \(A^\top A\): the \(\sigma_i^2\) are their eigenvalues. The approximation follows from the fact that matrices of rank \(\leq k\) form a closed convex set and that the error decomposes orthogonally: \(\|A - X\|_F^2 = \sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2 + \|X_k - \sum_{i \leq k}\sigma_i u_iv_i^\top\|_F^2\). The SVD will be our permanent tool: least squares (DLT of ch. 5), decomposition of homographies, 3D orientation (Kabsch in ch. 10), surface normals.
Proposition 0.2 — Sums of independent Gaussians If \(X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_X, \sigma_X^2)\) and \(Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_Y, \sigma_Y^2)\) are independent, then \(X + Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_X+\mu_Y, \sigma_X^2 + \sigma_Y^2)\). One-line proof via characteristic functions: \(e^{it\mu_X - t^2\sigma_X^2/2}\cdot e^{it\mu_Y - t^2\sigma_Y^2/2} = e^{it(\mu_X+\mu_Y) - t^2(\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2)/2}\). ∎ Immediate application: convolving an image with a Gaussian kernel amounts to modeling an additive Gaussian noise — hence the central role of the Gaussian filter in ch. 2-4.
