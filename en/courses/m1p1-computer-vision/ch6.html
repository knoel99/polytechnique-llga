6. Stereo vision and epipolar geometry

Two views of a 3D scene, taken from different points: the epipolar constraint says that the correspondent of a pixel lies on a known line in the second image. All of stereo vision — from reconstruction to robotics — follows from this geometric observation.

Definition 6.1 — Epipolar geometry, F and E Consider a pair of cameras \(P_1 = K_1[I \mid 0]\), \(P_2 = K_2[R | t]\). The 3D point \(X\) projects to \(x_1\) and \(x_2\); the epipolar plane passes through \(X\), \(x_1\), \(x_2\), and the two optical centers; its intersection with image plane 2 is the epipolar line of \(x_1\) — every match must live on it. The constraint reads $$\tilde{x}_2^\top F\, \tilde{x}_1 = 0,\qquad F = K_2^{-\top} [t]_\times R\, K_1^{-1},$$ \(F\) is the fundamental matrix (rank 2, 7 dof); its version normalized by the intrinsic matrices, \(E = K_2^\top F\,K_1 = [t]_\times R\), is the essential matrix (5 dof): it depends only on the relative motion.
Proposition 6.2 — The 8-point algorithm Each match \(\tilde{x}_1 \leftrightarrow \tilde{x}_2\) gives one linear equation in the 9 coefficients of \(F\); 8 matches suffice (hence the name). Solve by SVD of the homogeneous system, then project onto the set of rank-2 matrices (replace the smallest singular value by 0: \(F = U\,\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, 0)\,V^\top\)). The normalization of the points (Prop. 5.3) is again crucial here. Next: decomposition of \(E\) (SVD) into 4 candidate \((R, t)\) pairs, ambiguity resolved by the chirality test (the reconstructed points must lie in front of the cameras). This is "calibrated stereo"; the uncalibrated version stops at \(F\).
Theorem 6.3 — Rectified stereo: disparity and depth After rectification (resampling of both images so that the epipolar lines are the horizontal lines — a pair of homographies, ch. 5), a point at depth \(Z\) is seen at abscissae \(x_1, x_2\) with $$d = x_1 - x_2 = \frac{f\,B}{Z}\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; Z = \frac{fB}{d},$$ \(f\): focal length in pixels, \(B\): baseline (distance between cameras), \(d\): disparity.
Elementary triangulation: the 3D point \((X, Y, Z)\) projects to \(x_1 = fX/Z\) in the left camera; the right camera, translated by \(B\) along the common optical axis, sees \(x_2 = f(X - B)/Z\). The difference \(x_1 - x_2 = fB/Z\) no longer depends on \(X\): disparity encodes depth. ∎
Example 6.4 — Orders of magnitude (KITTI style) \(f = 721\) px, \(B = 0.54\) m. A car at \(Z = 39\) m has a disparity \(d = fB/Z = 721\times0.54/39 \approx 10\) px; at 10 m, \(d \approx 39\) px. Error: since \(Z = fB/d\) is non-linear, \(\Delta Z/Z = \Delta d/d\): an uncertainty of ±0.2 px on \(d = 10\) px gives ±2% (±0.8 m); on \(d = 3\) px (130 m), ±7% — stereo sees far away poorly: precision degrades with the square of the distance, hence the value of large baselines or LiDAR (ch. 10).
Note — Modern dense stereo The classic pipeline: per-pixel local cost (SSD/SAD over windows, or the illumination-robust census transform), aggregation and regularization by per-row dynamic programming, or semi-global matching (Hirschmüller, 2008): minimize a global energy by summing costs over 8 directions — the industrial standard for depth maps, up to learned stereo networks (PSM-Net etc.) that process the image pair end to end. Triangulation of an isolated match is solved, for its part, as a linear system: \(\tilde{x} \times (P\tilde{X}) = 0\) provides 2 equations per camera, 4 for the pair — SVD, again.
Definition 6.5 — Structure from motion (SfM) and MultiView Stereo The image pair of the chapter generalizes to \(n\) unordered views:
  • Structure from motion (SfM): recovering both the camera poses and the 3D structure. Incremental pipeline (COLMAP): SIFT matches (ch. 4) filtered by RANSAC on \(F\) then \(E\) (Prop. 6.2) → well-conditioned initial pair, decomposition of \(E\) into \((R, t)\), triangulation → views added one by one by resection (DLT, ch. 5), additional 3D points by triangulation; the whole is refined by bundle adjustment: global minimization of the reprojection error \(\sum_{i,j} \big\| x_{ij} - \pi(P_j X_i) \big\|^2\) over poses and points (Gauss-Newton / Levenberg-Marquardt on the sparse system).
  • MultiView Stereo (MVS): poses known, one densifies — dense correlation (the Note above) applied to several pairs to produce depth maps, then fusion into a global point cloud by visibility and geometric consistency tests; this is modern "photogrammetry", which meets the LiDAR clouds of ch. 10.
SfM and MVS close the loop of the course: features (ch. 4), geometry (ch. 5, 6), and dense correspondences manufacture, from images alone, the 3D scene and the poses — exactly the "3D shape recovery" that the course sheet gives as its objective.
