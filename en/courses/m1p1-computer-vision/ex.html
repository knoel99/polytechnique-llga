Exercises

  1. Camera (solved). Sensor 3000×2000 of 2.2 µm, focal length 24 mm. (a) Horizontal field of view? (b) Focal length in pixels? (c) How many point pairs for a homography, and why 4? (d) Does a pure rotation of the camera give a homography between views (justify)? Solution. (a) \(w = 6.6\) mm, \(\mathrm{FOV} = 2\arctan(3.3/24) \approx 15.6^\circ\). (b) \(f_x = 0.024/2.2\,\text{µm} \approx 10\,900\) px. (c) 4 pairs: \(H\) has 8 dof (9 coefficients − 1 scale), each pair provides 2 independent equations (Prop. 5.3). (d) Yes: Theorem 5.2, case (ii) — it is even the only non-planar case that qualifies.
  2. Convolution (solved). (a) Convolve \([10, 12, 11, 40, 42]\) with the kernel \([0.25,\,0.5,\,0.25]\) (replicated borders). (b) How many multiplications per pixel for a 5×5 Gaussian, direct then separable? (c) Why is a 3×3 box filter not an ideal smoother? Solution. (a) \([10.5,\,11.25,\,18.5,\,33.25,\,41.5]\) — the jump 11→40 is smoothed over two samples. (b) 25 direct, \(2\times5 = 10\) separated (Prop. 2.2), ratio 2.5×. (c) its frequency response is a sinc (Def. 2.1 and the Signal Processing module, ch. 5): negative lobes create contrast reversals at high frequencies — the Gaussian has no rebound.
  3. Lucas-Kanade (solved). Window of 4 pixels, measurements \((I_x, I_y\,;\,I_t)\): (2, 1; 6), (4, 2; 10), (3, 1; 7), (5, 2; 12). Solve for the flow; compute the eigenvalues of the tensor; is the flow reliable? Propose two remedies. Solution. \(M = \begin{pmatrix}54 & 23\\ 23 & 10\end{pmatrix}\), right-hand side \(-(133, 57)^\top\). \(\det M = 11\) — nearly singular: \(\lambda_1 \approx 63.8\), \(\lambda_2 \approx 0.17\). Cramer: \(u = v = -19/11 \approx -1.73\). Reliability: no — \(\lambda_2 \ll \lambda_1\) signals an edge (aperture problem, Thm 7.2); the uncertainty ellipse is very elongated: the flow component along the edge is poorly observed. Remedies: pyramid (large → small motion) and Gaussian weighting of the window; failing that, reject the pixel (\(\lambda_2 < \tau\)).
  4. Stereo (solved). \(f = 721\) px, \(B = 0.54\) m. (a) Disparity of an object at 25 m, at 5 m? (b) Depth if \(d = 25\) px? (c) Depth uncertainty at 25 m if the disparity is known to ±0.25 px? (d) Why double the baseline? Solution. (a) \(d = fB/Z\): 15.6 px at 25 m; 77.9 px at 5 m. (b) \(Z = fB/d = 721\times0.54/25 \approx 15.6\) m. (c) \(\Delta Z/Z = \Delta d/d = 0.25/15.6 \approx 1.6\,\%\) → ±0.4 m (Thm 6.3). (d) \(Z = fB/d\): doubling \(B\) doubles \(d\) at equal depth, hence halves the relative error — at the price of a wider separation and increased occlusion areas between views.
  5. Harris in practice. Implement the structure tensor (Example 4.6), threshold \(R > 0.01\,R_{max}\), display the corners on a photo; compare with cv2.cornerMinEigenVal. Rotate the image by 30° and measure the stability of the detection (Prop. 4.3). Hint: smoothing with \(\sigma \approx 1{-}2\) before the Sobels; measure stability by the number of corners rematched within ±2 px.
  6. RANSAC. (a) Derive the formula for the number of draws \(N \geq \ln(1-p)/\ln(1-w^s)\) for an \(s\)-point estimator (independence of the draws). (b) Compute \(N\) for \(s = 4\), \(w = 0.3\), \(p = 0.999\). (c) Adaptive RANSAC: how to stop before \(N\) draws? Hint: (a) failure probability \(1-w^s\) per draw, independent ⇒ \((1-w^s)^N \leq 1-p\). (b) \(w^4 = 0.0081\), \(N = 6.908/0.00813 \approx 850\). (c) update \(w\) with the current best consensus and recompute \(N\) on the fly.
  7. ICP. Generate a noisy planar cloud, transform it (30° rotation), run the ICP of Example 10.5 from increasingly bad initializations; measure the basin of convergence. Test the point-to-plane variant on planes. Hint: plot the RMS error per iteration (log); the basin closes when matches cross the edges of the cloud — hence the value of rejecting distant pairs and of initial RANSAC.
  8. Segmentation, up close. On a photo, compare k-means on (a) RGB, (b) RGB + \((x, y)/10\), (c) Lab; choose \(k\) by the elbow method. Where do SLIC superpixels fit in a modern vision pipeline (ch. 8-9)? Hint: (b) forces spatial compactness — connected regions; (c) separates hue from luminance (Def. 1.5); connect to the Machine Learning module, ch. 6.
