8. Reproducible research: experimental protocol

Definition 8.1 — Minimal experimental protocol Any reported experiment: (1) a precise question; (2) a falsifiable hypothesis; (3) a protocol: fixed and versioned dataset, a single train/val/test split, a main metric declared before the experiment; (4) a baseline retrained under the same conditions; (5) \(n \geq 3\) seeds with mean ± standard deviation; (6) the ablations; (7) the compute budget reported; (8) code (and ideally data) published.

8.1. Seeds matter (really)

Picard (2021) — "torch.manual_seed(3407) is all you need" — trains identical classifiers over 10,000 seeds: the accuracy gap between seeds reaches several points; the choice of seed can flip a ranking of methods. Bouthillier et al. (2021) generalize: the variance due to seeds (init, data order, augmentations) must be estimated and reported; comparing two means without variability is comparing noise. Tools: Student's \(t\) confidence intervals over \(n\) runs; paired tests when the seeds are coupled; correction for multiple testing (see the Refresher in Statistics module for tests, the Machine Learning module for model selection).

Example 8.2 — A "gain" that isn't one Method A: 79.4% ± 0.6 (5 seeds); baseline B: 78.9% ± 0.5 (5 seeds). Difference of means 0.5; standard deviation of the difference \(\approx \sqrt{0{,}6^2/5 + 0{,}5^2/5} \approx 0{,}35\) → statistic \(t \approx 1{,}4\), to be compared with \(t_{0{,}975}(8) \approx 2{,}3\): not significant. The right conclusion: "comparable to the baseline", not "+0.5% state of the art". In the project (ch. 11), every results table carries mean ± standard deviation and the name of the test used.

8.2. Replication: the module's method

  1. pick a number: one row, one table of the pivotal paper;
  2. scale down: same task, model ×0.2 (µP, ch. 7, helps transpose the hyperparameters); a budget declarable in GPU-hours;
  3. instrument: experiment tracking (versioned configs + seeds + curves — W&B/tensorboard; determinism of the loaders; no leakage: the test set is looked at only at the end);
  4. report: paper vs replication table ± standard deviation; analyze the gap (data? recipe? hardware?) — that is where one learns.
Note — Leaks and common pitfalls Splitting per image on video data (nearly identical neighbors); normalization computed over the whole set; selecting the best checkpoint on the test set; an implicit "dev set = test set" after 50 config edits; comparison at unequal training budgets (see DeiT vs ViT, ch. 1; ConvNeXt, ch. 2). Each chapter of the module rereads its pivotal paper with this grid: what is the baseline? the seeds? the budget? the metric declared a priori?
