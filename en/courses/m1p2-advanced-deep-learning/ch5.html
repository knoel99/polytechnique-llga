5. Self-supervision II: masking (MAE, BEiT, DINOv2)

5.1. Masked Autoencoders (MAE)

He et al. (CVPR 2022): mask 75% of the patches and reconstruct the missing pixels. Two asymmetries make the whole design:

  • asymmetric encoder: only the 25% of visible patches enter the ViT — the training cost is reduced accordingly (and this is what makes high masking effective: the task remains difficult yet cheap);
  • lightweight decoder: the masked tokens (learned embeddings + positions) are re-inserted after the encoder; a small decoder (often narrow and shallow) reconstructs each missing patch; the decoder is discarded at transfer time.
Definition 5.1 — MAE loss With \(\Omega\) the set of masked patches and \(x_i\) the targets (normalized pixels: per-patch normalization by mean/variance; variant: targets from a VQ-VAE — BEiT): $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MAE}} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{i \in \Omega} \| \hat x_i - x_i \|^2,$$ averaged over the masked patches only. Per-patch normalization recenters the task on structure (relative to the patch) rather than on absolute luminance.
Example 5.2 — Why 75% and not 15% (BERT) Images have enormous spatial redundancy: masking 15% of an image can be solved by local interpolation (in the filter sense, not the network sense) — the encoder learns nothing semantic. At 75%, interpolation no longer suffices: reconstructing a half-masked face requires having understood "face". Text, on the other hand, is non-redundant: 15% is enough to force syntax and semantics. The same idea — "mask until the task becomes semantic" — governs the rates in audio (wav2vec, 40–75%) and in video — and we will find this principle again in the Multimodal GenAI module, chapter 3, on the diffusion side (how much noise is needed for denoising to be difficult?).

5.2. BEiT and DINOv2

  • BEiT (Bao et al., ICLR 2022): replace the pixels with discrete visual tokens — a pre-trained VQ-VAE quantizes the patches into codes; the objective becomes a classification of the masked patches toward the codes (like BERT's MLM — see the NLP module); in practice more stable than pixel regression;
  • DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023): the contrastive + masking duo, trained on a curated corpus LVD-142M (automated selection: similarity with reference corpora, deduplication); distillation between a teacher (momentum-updated, centered — no labels) and multi-resolution students produces features that are ready to use (linear) for classification, depth, segmentation — one of the best current open backbones, and the direct bridge to the lab session;
