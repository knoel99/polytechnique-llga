2. Modern convnets: the ConvNeXt lesson

After a decade of "transformers win everywhere", the ConvNeXt paper (Liu et al., CVPR 2022) asks the reverse question: what are modernized convnets worth under an equal training recipe? Method: start from a ResNet and align it step by step with the ViT choices — each step is ablated and measured:

#ChangeImageNet gain
1modern training (90→300 epochs, AdamW, augmentation, label smoothing…)76 → 78.8 (+2.8 — the largest gain from a single line!)
2ResNeXt block design: 3×3 conv → depthwise, controlled width+0.6
3macro-design: patch ratio 4 (stem conv 4×4, not 16), "stacked" proportion of blocks per stage (1:1:3:1)+0.8
4inverted bottleneck (thin→wide→thin) and large kernels (7×7 depthwise)+0.7
5micro: GELU, fewer normalizations/activations, LayerNorm instead of BatchNorm, "transformer-like" blocks+0.7

Paper's conclusion: the thus "modernized" convnet matches or exceeds Swin at comparable throughput. The methodological lesson is stronger than the result: training (line 1) and scale details matter more than the architecture family — an indispensable preamble to chapter 8 on reproducibility, and a spectacular application of the "one change at a time" protocol.

Note — Depthwise, the missing link Depthwise convolution (one spatial filter per channel, no cross-channel mixing — see MobileNet, ch. 6) is what enables large 7×7 kernels at contained cost: cost \(O(k^2 n d)\) instead of \(O(k^2 n d^2)\). ConvNeXt borrows it from mobile convnets; the wide receptive field is a reminder that vision does not need global attention to be effective — only a wide range of dependencies.
