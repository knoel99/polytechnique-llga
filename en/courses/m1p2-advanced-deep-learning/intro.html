Introduction and overview

ModuleAdvanced Deep Learning
CodeCSC_52087_EP
Team and volumeInstructors: Vicky Kalogeiton, Johannes Lutzeyer, Ye Zhu & Xi Wang (École Polytechnique) · 36 h · 4.5 ECTS · a reading seminar: each chapter takes apart a pivotal paper — ViT, ConvNeXt, mixture of experts, contrastive and masked self-supervision, efficient architectures, training at scale — down to the experimental protocol that underpins it, with replication projects.
Status in the programP2 OptionOutside recommended track

This module takes the form of a reading seminar: each chapter takes apart a pivotal paper of modern deep learning — Vision Transformers, ConvNeXt, mixture of experts, self-supervision, efficient architectures, training at scale. The point is not to lengthen the catalog of architectures, but to learn to read a paper like a researcher: identify the question, the experimental protocol, what is actually demonstrated and what is not. This is the M1 module that most directly prepares the research track of the master's program.

What you will be able to do

  • Place the pivotal papers (ViT, DeiT, Swin, ConvNeXt, MoE, SimCLR, MAE, DINOv2, MobileNet) in their lineage and their legacies.
  • Compare architecture families on measurable criteria rather than on an isolated benchmark.
  • Explain the two major families of self-supervision — contrastive and masked — and their trade-offs in data and compute.
  • Dissect an experimental protocol and distinguish the demonstrated contribution from the packaging.
  • Use training recipes at scale and interpret scaling laws.
  • Replicate an experiment from a paper and report on it reproducibly.
  • Present a research paper in writing and orally, in the seminar's format.

Where this course fits in the program

An option of period 2 of M1, outside the recommended track. The foundation — backpropagation, CNNs, attention, training in practice — comes from the P1 Deep Learning module, whose necessary elements (transformers, optimization, normalization) are reactivated in chapter 0. Downstream, this module directly prepares Advanced Topics in Large Language Models (M2), which extends the critical reading of papers on the LLM side, and above all the research track of the master's program: the seminar format and the replication project are its methodical training. The multimodal and generative cross-reference chapter connects to the generation modules of the same period.

Course outline

  • Refresher: transformers, optimization, normalization — the P1 foundation brought back to the level of rigor that reading papers requires.
  • Vision Transformers: ViT, DeiT, Swin — the transition from convolutions to attention in vision, generation after generation.
  • Modern convnets: the ConvNeXt lesson — what transformers taught convnets, and the modern rereading of CNNs.
  • Mixture of Experts (MoE) — growing capacity by conditioning the computation per expert, without exploding the cost.
  • Self-supervision I: contrastive (SimCLR and its lineage) — learning representations by bringing views of the same image closer together.
  • Self-supervision II: masking (MAE, BEiT, DINOv2) — learning by reconstructing the invisible, up to the DINOv2 lineage.
  • Efficient architectures: MobileNet and distillation — cutting costs for embedded targets and transferring knowledge from a large network to a small one.
  • Training at scale: recipes and scaling laws — the practical rules of heavy training and the laws linking size, data and performance.
  • Reproducible research: experimental protocol — seeds, datasets, honest measurements: the rules of experimental proof.
  • Multimodal and generative: cross-references — where to pursue these themes in the other modules of the master's program.
  • Research of the teaching team — the papers of the module's instructors, read with the same grid.
  • Projects: read, explain, replicate — the final project: choose a paper, explain it, replicate one of its experiments.
  • Exercises — short applications to anchor each paper.
  • References — the papers and sources of each chapter.

How to study this module

  • Read each paper before the corresponding chapter, even without understanding everything: the course is built for the second reading.
  • Redo the results tables yourself: which data, which seeds, which measurements — that is where credibility is at stake.
  • Choose your project paper early in the period, and keep a narrow question rather than a general summary.
  • Keep a reading notebook: one question, one method, one proof, one limitation per paper.
  • Return to chapter 0 whenever transformers or normalization become shaky — the whole seminar rests on them.
  • Confront your readings with the other P2 modules: the same papers come back seen from another angle.
The module in the LLGA master's program This seminar installs the experimental culture that runs through the entire master's program: the scaling laws and attention studied here reappear at the heart of the M2 LLMs, and the method — read, criticize, replicate — is exactly what a research internship, a PhD or a research engineer position requires. It is the natural bridge between the M1 deep learning and the research that the LLGA master's program aims to foster.
