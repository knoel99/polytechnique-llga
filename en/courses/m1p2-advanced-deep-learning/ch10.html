10. Research of the teaching team

The seminar closes with an encounter with local research: each instructor presents a slice of their work, in direct continuation of the chapters. Representative publications (all verified):

10.1. Vicky Kalogeiton — vision, video, action

  • V. Kalogeiton, P. Weinzaepfel, V. Ferrari, C. Schmid, Action Tubelet Detector for Spatio-Temporal Action Localization (ACT-detector), ICCV 2017 — detecting tubelets: series of boxes linked in time, forerunner of end-to-end spatio-temporal action detection;
  • V. Kalogeiton, P. Weinzaepfel, V. Ferrari, C. Schmid, Joint Learning of Object and Action Detectors, ICCV 2017 — unifying in a single network the detection of objects (ch. 1 of the Computer Vision module) and of actions;
  • R. Ghermi, X. Wang, V. Kalogeiton, I. Laptev, Long Story Short: Story-level Video Understanding from 20K Short Films (SF20K), 2024 — 20k films, long-term questioning; "story" understanding beyond the short clip;
  • Vicky Kalogeiton also teaches Multimodal Generative AI (CSC_52002_EP, period 2) — the module next door to this one.

10.2. Johannes Lutzeyer — graph representation learning

  • G. Michel, G. Nikolentzos, J. Lutzeyer, M. Vazirgiannis, Path Neural Networks: Expressive and Accurate Graph Neural Networks, ICML 2023 — learning salient paths for message passing (see the Graph ML module, ch. 5);
  • M. Chatzianastasis, J. Lutzeyer, G. Dasoulas, M. Vazirgiannis, Graph Ordering Attention Networks, AAAI 2023 — the order of neighbors as first-class information in attention;
  • Node Feature Kernels Increase Graph Convolutional Network Performance (HAL) — improving GNNs on attribute-poor graphs; themes: GNN expressivity, interpretability, applications (transportation networks: predicting the effects of road policies on Paris). Johannes Lutzeyer co-directs year M1 of the master's program and co-teaches the Graph ML module.

10.3. Ye Zhu — diffusion models, semantic control

  • Y. Zhu, Y. Wu, K. Olszewski, J. Ren, S. Tulyakov, Y. Yan, Discrete Contrastive Diffusion for Cross-Modal Music and Image Generation (CDCD), NeurIPS 2022 — discrete diffusion + contrastive loss for cross-modal music↔image generation;
  • Y. Zhu, Y. Wu, Z. Deng, O. Russakovsky, Y. Yan, Boundary Guided Learning-Free Semantic Control with Diffusion Models (BoundaryDiffusion), NeurIPS 2023 — controlling the semantics of frozen generators via boundaries in latent space, without retraining;
  • "Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models to Predict the ENSO" (2023, arXiv) — diffusion in the service of climate forecasting (El Niño): a bridge between data science × climate science. Research themes: discrete diffusion, generator control, scientific applications.

10.4. Xi Wang — video generation and generative cinema

  • R. Ghermi, X. Wang, V. Kalogeiton, I. Laptev, Long Story Short (SF20K), 2024 — co-author; benchmarks and instruction-tuning for long-term video understanding;
  • Z. Yi, X. Wang, I. Ounis et al., "Multi-modal Graph Contrastive Learning for Micro-video Recommendation", SIGIR 2022 — contrast (ch. 4) applied to multimodal recommendation graphs — a direct Graph ML × Multimodal bridge;
  • Xi Wang (VISTA/LIX post-doc, now assistant professor at l'X) works on cinematic video generation: camera, editing, narrative consistency — extending text-to-video models (see the Multimodal GenAI module, ch. 7) with a filmmaking grammar; profile and publications.
Note — How to read a team paper During these sessions, the chapter 8 instruction applies to the instructors' own work: identify the question, the baseline, the seeds, the budget — and ask the hard questions: this is exactly the scientific exercise the module wants to instill.
