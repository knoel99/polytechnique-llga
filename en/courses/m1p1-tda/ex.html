Exercises

  1. Boundary of the boundary over \(\mathbb{Z}\). Detail the proof of Proposition 1.3 in the case \(k = 3\) (tetrahedron): write \(\partial[a,b,c,d]\), then \(\partial(\partial[a,b,c,d])\), and verify the term-by-term cancellation with the signs \((-1)^i\).
  2. Homology of graphs. Prove the formula \(\beta_1 = m - n + c\) (Proposition 3.4) by induction on the number of edges (adding an edge between two components decreases \(c\) and leaves \(\beta_1\) unchanged; within one component, it increases \(\beta_1\)). Application: what is \(\beta_1\) of the Petersen graph? of a \(k \times k\) grid? (cf. Graph ML module, ch. 0.)
  3. Sphere and torus. Take up the complete computation 3.3 on the torus as a "square with identified edges" triangulated (2 triangles, 3 edges, 1 vertex — verify that it is a legal abstract complex): show \(\beta_0 = 1\), \(\beta_1 = 2\), \(\beta_2 = 1\), \(\chi = 0\). What is the minimal abstract complex for the "real" torus (Császár, 7 vertices)?
  4. Persistence by hand. Take up Example 4.5 while permuting indices 9 (triangle) and 10 (edge \([v_1v_4]\)) — the filtration remains legal if edge \([v_1v_4]\) appears before the triangle (the triangle does not contain it). Recompute the barcode; which bars change, which are invariant? Verify with GUDHI (Lab 3, note 4).
  5. Nerve theorem in dimension 1. Let \(\mathcal{U}\) be a family of open intervals of \(\mathbb{R}\). Show that the nerve of \(\mathcal{U}\) has the same homotopy type as the union: (i) each component of the union is an interval (connected, hence contractible); (ii) the vertices of the nerve within one component are connected by a path of edges; (iii) conclude with Proposition 3.4 (the nerve of intervals is a graph without independent cycles: why are triangles impossible?).
  6. Jung bracket. Verify Example 2.5 numerically (balls and intersections for an equilateral triangle of side 1), then prove the second inclusion of Proposition 2.4 in the plane: every set of diameter \(\delta\) of \(\mathbb{R}^2\) fits in a disk of radius \(\delta/\sqrt{3}\) (consider the smallest enclosing disk; if its center is not on the boundary…).
  7. Bottleneck and Wasserstein. For \(\mathcal{D}_1 = \{(1, 3), (2, 2.1)\}\) and \(\mathcal{D}_2 = \{(1.2, 2.8)\}\): compute \(d_B\), then \(d_{W,1}\) (sum of the displacements, optimal matching via the diagonal). Build two diagrams with the same \(d_B\) but arbitrarily large \(d_{W,1}\) — and interpret in terms of noise sensitivity (ch. 6).
  8. Landscapes. Compute \(\lambda(1, \cdot)\) and \(\lambda(2, \cdot)\) for the diagram \(\{(1, 3), (2, 4)\}\) (draw the tents; note that they cross at \(t = 3\)). Verify the bound of Proposition 5.6 against the diagram \(\{(1, 3), (2, 4.2)\}\).
  9. DTM stability. Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a sample and \(\mathcal{X}'\) obtained by adding an outlier at distance \(L\) from the rest. Bound the variation of \(d_B\) for the Rips complex (via 6.3: the bound degrades by \(L\)); explain why the DTM version of ch. 6 degrades only by \(O(\text{weight of the outlier} \times L^2)\).
  10. Mapper. Implement Mapper (30 lines: overlapping histogram + single-linkage from scipy + networkx) on MNIST reduced to 3 dimensions by UMAP; compare two filters (density vs eccentricity). Identify the "arm" of misclassified 1s (the fateful cluster of the Nicolau et al. 2011 paper — ch. 7).
  11. Paper. Choose a paper from ch. 9 (Oudot 2015; Chazal et al. 2009/2016; ToMATo 2013; sliced Wasserstein 2017; PersLay 2020), read it and present it in 10 minutes: problem, key theorem, sketched proof, experimental result, limitation. Grid: one figure re-explained, one figure reproduced, one open question.
  12. Guiding project (optional). Compute the H1 diagrams of the clique complexes of a collaboration graph (Graph ML module, ch. 7: ogbn-arxiv) and train a sliced Wasserstein kernel SVM to predict the topic; compare with a GNN (Advanced GNN module); what information does each family capture?
