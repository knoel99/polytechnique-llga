8. TDA × machine learning

A diagram is neither a vector nor a probability: it is a variable-size multiset of the half-plane. To plug it into a classifier — SVM, forest, network — one must either vectorize it (landscapes, images), put it into a kernel, or turn it into a differentiable layer. This chapter is the bridge toward the heart of the master's program; it ends with the flagship applications: shapes, neuroimaging, sensor networks.

8.1 — Kernels on diagrams: the sliced Wasserstein kernel (Carrière, Cuturi, Oudot, ICML 2017) The sliced Wasserstein distance between diagrams projects the points onto the lines through the origin and averages the 1D transport distances: $$\widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\mathcal{D}_1, \mathcal{D}_2) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} W_1\big( \theta_i^\top \mathcal{D}_1 \cup \Delta,\; \theta_i^\top \mathcal{D}_2 \cup \Delta \big),$$ where the \(\theta_i\) are sampled directions and \(\Delta\) the projected diagonal (with multiplicities). The exponential kernel \(k(\mathcal{D}_1, \mathcal{D}_2) = \exp\big( - \widehat{\mathrm{SW}}(\mathcal{D}_1, \mathcal{D}_2) / \varepsilon \big)\) is then positive definite, and computes in \(O((n + m) \log(n + m))\) per direction. One obtains diagram SVMs (Machine Learning module, ch. 3; Optimal Transport module for the Wasserstein family) that beat fixed vectorizations on graph and shape classification benchmarks. This is one of the founding papers of "topological machine learning" — work of the team, ch. 9.
8.2 — PersLay: a network layer for diagrams (Datashapes, AISTATS 2020) PersLay transforms each point \((b, d)\) of the diagram by a learned function \(\phi(b, d)\) (mixture of persistence weights, Gaussians, transform of the point toward \((b, d - b)\)), weights the points by an attention function \(w(b, d)\), then aggregates (sum, mean, max, permutation-invariant): $$\mathrm{PersLay}(\mathcal{D}) = \mathrm{AGG}\big( \{ w(b, d) \cdot \phi(b, d) : (b, d) \in \mathcal{D} \}\big) \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^q.$$ Plugged in front of an MLP, the layer generalizes landscapes and persistence images (particular choices of \(\phi\) and \(w\)) and trains end to end. The paper applies it to graph classification via the extended persistence of continuous signatures (heat kernel signatures) — SOTA on several benchmarks of the time. Master's link: the permutation-invariance of the aggregator is the same problem as that of graph functions (Graph ML module, ch. 5 — Deep Sets vs message passing).
Topological losses for deep learning Another use: persistence as a regularizer. Hofer et al. (NeurIPS 2017) penalize the loss by a soft sum of the diagram points above a threshold — forcing e.g. a generative auto-encoder to preserve the connected components of its activations; the gradient exists thanks to Theorem 6.1 (when the activations move by \(\delta\) in sup norm, the diagram points move by as much). Applications: regularization of segmentations (connectedness of vessels, nerves), latent topology of VAEs — bridge with the Deep Learning module (M1 P1).
8.3 — Flagship applications, with verified references
  • Neurosciences. Petri et al. (J. R. Soc. Interface, 2014): cerebral functional networks (connectivity of fMRI series) are compared by their diagrams; under psilocybin, the homological structure reorganizes — the "homological scaffolds" identify the functional cycles that mean connectivity masks. It is the most cited TDA study in neuroimaging (> 900 citations).
  • Sensor networks. de Silva and Ghrist (2007): the coverage of a zone by short-range communication sensors is equivalent to the vanishing of \(H_1\) of the Rips complex of communications (nerve theorem 2.3 in sensor form): homology detects coverage holes without GPS coordinates — only the topology of the communication network matters.
  • Shapes and Geometric DL. Topological descriptors complement the equivariant architectures of the M2 module "Analysis and Deep Learning on Geometric Data": a network can confuse two meshes close in geometry but different in topology (one extra handle); an H1/H2 persistence feature separates them trivially. PersLay + extended persistence signatures are the canonical representatives of this hybridization.
  • Materials, cosmology, sports… porosity of granular media, galaxy filaments, player trajectories — see the survey by Otter et al. (2017).
Critical positioning (for the exam) TDA is not a predictive model: it is an extractor of structural features that are robust and interpretable, with guarantees (stability) that few deep descriptors have. Its limits: combinatorial cost, loss of fine geometry (a barcode retains only the holes), choice of scale — attenuated but not resolved by persistence. In practice, one concatenates topological features and learned features.
