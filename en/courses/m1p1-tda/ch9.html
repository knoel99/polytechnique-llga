9. Research of the teaching team

The module is taught by Steve Oudot, Inria senior researcher (team Datashapes, Inria center of Saclay — the former Geometrica team), member of the computer science laboratory (LIX) of École Polytechnique. The Datashapes team is, with those of Gunnar Carlsson (Stanford) and Herbert Edelsbrunner (IST Austria), one of the three worldwide cradles of TDA: it produced the modern stability theorems, the GUDHI software, and the bridge toward machine learning. Four axes in direct connection with the program; the papers are to be read and presented in project (exercise 10).

9.1 — Persistence Theory: the theory of quivers (S. Oudot, AMS, 2015) The book Persistence Theory: From Quiver Representations to Data Analysis (Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 209) rebuilds all of persistence — from the decomposition theorem 4.2 to the algorithms — as the representation theory of the quiver \(A_m\): a persistence module is exactly a linear representation of \(0 \to 1 \to \dots \to m\), and Gabriel's theorem (classification of the indecomposables by intervals) becomes its key. Chapter 4 of this module follows this algebraic reading: pivots of the boundary matrix = canonical matching of the indecomposables. Posterity: it is the standard reference for moving on to zigzags, multi-dimensional modules and categorical stability questions.
9.2 — Stability: from modules to diagrams (Chazal, Cohen-Steiner, Glisse, Guibas, Oudot, 2009; Chazal, de Silva, Glisse, Oudot, 2016) The paper Proximity of persistence modules and their diagrams (SoCG 2009) defines interleaving between modules and proves the algebraic stability 6.2 — ridding Theorem 6.1 of its tame-function hypotheses; the book The Structure and Stability of Persistence Modules (Springer, 2016) gives the definitive version, with the constancy of barcodes under continuous perturbations. This is the theoretical foundation that allows the statistics of ch. 6: without stability, no bootstrap, no tests, no machine learning on diagrams.
9.3 — ToMATo: clustering by persistence (Chazal, Guibas, Oudot, Škraba, JACM 2013) Persistence-Based Clustering in Riemannian Manifolds combines mode-seeking (gradient ascent on a density estimate, mean-shift style — Machine Learning module, ch. 6) and a merging of modes guided by persistence: two neighboring modes merge if the "pass" separating them has low persistence. Guarantee: under sampling hypotheses (reach conditions, cf. ch. 2 of the book by Boissonnat–Chazal–Yvinec), ToMATo recovers exactly the connected components of the density excursions — the "ideal" clustering — where k-means fails on non-convex shapes. It is the perfect example of an algorithm with topological guarantee.
9.4 — TDA × ML: kernels and layers (Carrière, Cuturi, Oudot, ICML 2017; Datashapes, AISTATS 2020) The sliced Wasserstein kernel (8.1) puts diagrams into the world of SVMs; PersLay (8.2), developed in the same team (M. Carrière, F. Chazal, Y. Ike, T. Lacombe, M. Royer, Y. Umeda — several of them PhDs supervised at LIX), puts them into that of networks. Together, they founded "topological machine learning": stable topological features + learning capacity. The natural extension in the master's program: plugging PersLay on graphs — the graph classification of the Graph ML module, ch. 2, with a descriptor that 1-WL cannot see (Advanced GNN module, ch. 1).
GUDHI: the software of the course The team develops and maintains GUDHI (Geometry Understanding in Higher Dimensions, gudhi.inria.fr): simplex trees, witness Rips, persistence, landscapes, bottleneck — the reference library (C++/Python), written with the team of Mariette Yvinec (former co-author of CGAL). The labs of ch. 10 use it, as well as scikit-tda/ripser for speed comparisons. Let us also mention Geometric and Topological Inference (Boissonnat, Chazal, Yvinec, Cambridge University Press, 2018 — free PDF on HAL): it is the reference textbook of chapters 2, 3 and 6. Classic attribution trap at the exam: that book is by Boissonnat, Chazal and Yvinec; Oudot's book is Persistence Theory (9.1). The two share the same editorial house of spirit — Geometrica/Datashapes — but not the same authors.
