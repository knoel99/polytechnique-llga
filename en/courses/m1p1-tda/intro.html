Introduction and overview

ModuleTopological Data Analysis
CodeCSC_51056_EP
Team and workloadInstructor: Steve Oudot (École Polytechnique & Inria) · 36 h · 4.5 ECTS · making the shape of data speak: simplicial complexes, persistent homology, persistence diagrams and Mapper — the algebraic backbone that GNNs cannot see, and the favorite tool of the Inria Datashapes team.
Status in the programP1 optionOutside recommended track

Two datasets can share the same descriptive statistics yet have very different "shapes": clusters, loops, cavities, branches. Topological Data Analysis (TDA) extracts this shape by building simplicial complexes from a point cloud, then measuring the persistence of holes at all scales. Within the LLGA master's program, this module teaches you to read in the data a structure that linear methods and GNNs do not capture natively: it is the favorite tool of the Inria Datashapes team and one of the best gateways toward a research orientation.

What you will be able to do

  • Build simplicial complexes (Rips, Čech) from a point cloud and understand what they encode.
  • Compute simplicial homology groups by hand on small complexes.
  • Run the persistent homology algorithm and read diagrams, barcodes and landscapes.
  • State the stability theorems and leverage them to do statistics on diagrams.
  • Vectorize persistence in order to plug it into a machine learning pipeline.
  • Apply the Mapper algorithm to visually explore a complex dataset.
  • Carry out TDA in practice with the GUDHI, scikit-tda and ripser libraries.

Where this course fits in the program

Module of period 1 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS), offered as an option outside the recommended track: a "data analysis" specialization choice particularly well suited to research-oriented profiles or demanding data science.

  • Prerequisites — the probabilistic background of the Refresher in Statistics and linear algebra (vector spaces, eigenvalues); the refresher section covers topology, distances and algebra before tackling simplicial complexes.
  • Downstream modules — the fine reading of data and shapes prepared here directly feeds Analysis and Deep Learning on Geometric Data (M2), where persistence and topological descriptors join learning on non-Euclidean data; it is also a strong marker for a research orientation, Steve Oudot's Inria Datashapes team supervising work along this line.

Course outline

  • Refresher: topology, distances, algebra — the topological and algebraic veneer indispensable for the rest of the course.
  • Simplices and simplicial complexes — the bricks that approximate a continuous space from points.
  • From point cloud to complex: Rips and Čech — building a shape at several scales from raw data.
  • Simplicial homology — counting the components, loops and cavities of a complex.
  • Persistent homology: the algorithm — tracking the appearance and disappearance of holes as the scale grows.
  • Diagrams, barcodes and landscapes — the canonical representations of persistence, and their uses.
  • Stability and statistics — why persistence resists noise, and how to do statistics with it.
  • The Mapper algorithm — an exploratory map of a dataset from a function of interest.
  • TDA × machine learning — plugging persistence into standard ML pipelines.
  • Research of the teaching team — a tour of the research work of the Inria Datashapes team.
  • Hands-on: GUDHI, scikit-tda, ripser — going from theory to code on real data.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Do the refresher section without skipping it: the topological vocabulary (open sets, connectedness, distances) is used as-is everywhere afterwards.
  • Compute by hand the homology of small complexes (a few triangles) before running ripser: the algorithm becomes crystal clear once the trivial cases are mastered.
  • Draw the barcodes: a long feature is a true signal, a collection of short features is noise — the eye gets trained.
  • Simulate: add noise to a circle, a union of two circles, and observe the stability of the diagrams promised by theory.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next; the final software implementation reuses all of them.
  • For a project or an internship, confront TDA early with a dataset familiar to you: it is the best test of understanding.
The LLGA positioning TDA gives the master's program its "beyond averages" analysis tool: the word and document embeddings produced by the Introduction to Text Mining and NLP and Large Language Models modules are so many point clouds whose persistence reveals the structure, and the graph analysis of the Graph ML module benefits from being crossed with topological descriptors. Finally, it is an excellent springboard toward academic research, at École Polytechnique as at Inria.
