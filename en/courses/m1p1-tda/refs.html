References

  • H. Edelsbrunner, J. Harer, Computational Topology: An Introduction, AMS, 2010 — the reference textbook of the module: complexes, homology, persistence algorithm (ch. 1, 3, 4).
  • J.-D. Boissonnat, F. Chazal, M. Yvinec, Geometric and Topological Inference, Cambridge Texts in Applied Mathematics 57, Cambridge University Press, 2018 — publisher page (free PDF on HAL): reconstruction, sampling, DTM (ch. 2, 3, 6 of the module).
  • S. Y. Oudot, Persistence Theory: From Quiver Representations to Data Analysis, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 209, AMS, 2015 — AMS page: the algebraic reading of ch. 4 (work of the team, ch. 9.1).
  • F. Chazal, V. de Silva, M. Glisse, S. Y. Oudot, The Structure and Stability of Persistence Modules, Springer, 2016 — arXiv:1207.3674: interleavings, algebraic stability (ch. 6; team, ch. 9.2).
  • H. Edelsbrunner, D. Letscher, A. Zomorodian, Topological Persistence and Simplification, Discrete & Computational Geometry 28, 2002 — the founding paper of the algorithm of ch. 4.
  • A. Zomorodian, G. Carlsson, Computing Persistent Homology, Discrete & Computational Geometry 33, 2005 — the modules-over-a-field version (Theorem 4.2).
  • D. Cohen-Steiner, H. Edelsbrunner, J. Harer, Stability of Persistence Diagrams, Discrete & Computational Geometry 37, 2007 — Theorem 6.1.
  • F. Chazal, D. Cohen-Steiner, M. Glisse, L. J. Guibas, S. Y. Oudot, Proximity of Persistence Modules and Their Diagrams, SoCG 2009 — algebraic stability (Theorem 6.2; team, ch. 9.2).
  • P. Bubenik, Statistical Topological Data Analysis Using Persistence Landscapes, JMLR 16, 2015 — the landscapes of ch. 5.
  • F. Chazal, B. Fasy, F. Lecci, A. Rinaldo, A. Singh, L. Wasserman, Stochastic Convergence of Persistence Landscapes and Silhouettes, SoCG 2013 — the convergence of empirical landscapes (note of ch. 6).
  • G. Singh, F. Mémoli, G. Carlsson, Topological Methods for the Analysis of High Dimensional Data Sets and 3D Object Recognition, Eurographics Symposium on Point-Based Graphics, 2007 — the Mapper paper of ch. 7.
  • M. Nicolau, A. J. Levine, G. Carlsson, Topology Based Data Analysis Identifies a Subgroup of Breast Cancers with a Unique Mutational Profile and Excellent Survival, PNAS 108(17), 2011 — Example 7.2.
  • G. Petri, P. Expert, F. Turkheimer, R. Carhart-Harris, D. Nutt, P. J. Hellyer, F. Vaccarino, Homological Scaffolds of Brain Functional Networks, Journal of the Royal Society Interface 11, 2014 — the neurosciences application of ch. 8.
  • V. de Silva, R. Ghrist, Homological Sensor Networks, Notices of the AMS 54(1), 2007 — sensor coverage, Rips = Čech in \(\ell_\infty\) (ch. 2, 8).
  • G. Carlsson, Topology and Data, Bulletin of the AMS 46(2), 2009 — the great survey of the field.
  • N. Otter, M. A. Porter, U. Tillmann, P. Grindrod, H. A. Harrington, A Roadmap for the Computation of Persistent Homology, EPJ Data Science 6:17, 2017 — the practical guide (software, pitfalls) of ch. 10.
  • M. Carrière, M. Cuturi, S. Oudot, Sliced Wasserstein Kernel for Persistence Diagrams, ICML 2017 — the kernel of ch. 8.1 (team, ch. 9.4).
  • M. Carrière, F. Chazal, Y. Ike, T. Lacombe, M. Royer, Y. Umeda, PersLay: A Neural Network Layer for Persistence Diagrams and New Graph Topological Signatures, AISTATS 2020 — the layer of ch. 8.2.
  • C. Hofer, R. Kwitt, M. Niethammer, A. Uhl, Deep Learning with Topological Signatures, NeurIPS 2017 — topological losses (ch. 8).
  • F. Chazal, L. J. Guibas, S. Y. Oudot, P. Škraba, Persistence-Based Clustering in Riemannian Manifolds, Journal of the ACM 60(6), 2013 — ToMATo (ch. 9.3).
  • U. Bauer, Ripser: Efficient Computation of Vietoris–Rips Persistence Barcodes, Journal of Applied and Computational Topology 5, 2021 — the fast matrix-free algorithm (ch. 10).
  • Software: GUDHI (Inria Datashapes — the library of the labs); scikit-tda (ripser, persim); PersLay.
