Exercises / Labs

  1. FGSM/PGD on MNIST (ch. 2-3 — opening lab). Train a small CNN; implement FGSM and PGD (thm. 3.2, def. 3.3); plot accuracy vs \(\varepsilon\) for \(K \in \{1, 7, 20, 50\}\) steps and 1/5/10 restarts; visualize the examples (MNIST grid at \(\varepsilon \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.3\}\)); measure white→black transfer to a second model (ch. 2).
    Guidance (expected results): clean CNN ≈ 99%; at \(\varepsilon = 0.3\), FGSM drops accuracy below 10% (each pixel moves by a third of the dynamic range — thm. 3.2 in action); \(K = 1\) (one-step PGD, null start) ≈ FGSM; the gain saturates quickly in \(K\) (7 steps suffice), restarts add only a few tenths of a point at this budget — the real role of restarts appears on defended models (lab 2-3); transfer degrades but does not disappear (30-60% of the attack carries over to the target model): the "black box by secrecy of the weights" defense is futile. ∎
  2. Adversarial training (ch. 4 — central lab). PGD-AT (def. 4.1, 7 steps) on MNIST then CIFAR-10 at \(\varepsilon = 8/255\); plot standard and robust accuracy per epoch on the same figure — highlight the robust overfitting (def. 4.3) and the trade-off of thm. 4.2; compare to TRADES (error decomposition); report the RobustBench grid (standard vs robust).
    Guidance (expected results): MNIST — standard accuracy ~98% (the trade-off of thm. 4.2 is small), robust at \(\varepsilon = 0.3\) raised from ~0% to ≈ 90%; CIFAR-10 — standard ~95% → ~80% with AT, robust at 8/255 from ~0% to 45-50% (more with the augmentations of Rebuffi et al., 2021); robust overfitting is visible on CIFAR: test robustness peaks around the middle of training then decreases by several points while train robustness rises — early stopping and massive augmentation mitigate it (def. 4.3); TRADES gains a few robust points at equal standard by reading the boundary error. ∎
  3. Standardized evaluation (ch. 3, 10). Take the models from lab 2 and evaluate them with the AutoAttack library (APGD-CE, APGD-T, Square); compare to the "home-grown" PGD numbers; discuss the gap — then break a naive defense (e.g. re-training on FGSM adversaries only) with an adaptive attack (Athalye/Tramèr, ch. 4) and write the evaluation report in RobustBench format.
    Guidance (expected results): on an adversarially trained model, AutoAttack and the home-grown PGD are close (the model has no masked gradient — a gap on the order of one point); on the naive defense (trained against FGSM only), the "home-grown" PGD overestimates robustness by tens of points and AutoAttack reveals the collapse: FGSM training masks the gradient (piecewise degraded, thm. 3.2 trapped) — the adaptive attack (reparameterization, smoothing) restores it; the honest report: standard accuracy, robust, budget \(\varepsilon\), attack, restarts, cost — without these elements, no conclusion (def. 10.1). ∎
  4. Certification (ch. 5). Implement randomized smoothing: estimation of \(g(x)\) and of the bounds \(\underline{p_A}, \overline{p_B}\) (Clopper-Pearson, example 5.3) on MNIST at \(\sigma \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1.0\}\); plot certified accuracy vs radius (thm. 5.2) and overlay it with the empirical accuracy (\(L_2\) attacks); discuss the certification/empirical gap and the \(\sigma\) accuracy/radius trade-off.
    Guidance (expected results): at \(\sigma = 0.25\), the smoothed classifier keeps a high clean accuracy but the certified radius quickly vanishes (R_max ≈ 0.3-0.4 on MNIST); at \(\sigma = 1.0\), the certified radius reaches ~1 but the clean accuracy of the smoothed classifier drops — the certified accuracy vs radius curve is below the empirical curve everywhere, the gap measuring our ignorance (imperfect attacks from below, conservative certification from above); with \(n = 10^4\) draws and \(\alpha = 0.01\), Clopper-Pearson costs ~2 points of radius — the draw budget is a hyperparameter of the guarantee (example 5.3). ∎
  5. Backdoor and detection (ch. 6). Insert a BadNets trigger (3×3 patch) on 5% of MNIST; check clean accuracy vs trigger success rate; implement the Neural Cleanse reverse-engineering (def. 6.3: minimize \(\|\delta_c\|_1\) per class, detect the MAD anomaly, reconstruct the trigger); repair by pruning then unlearning and measure the clean/trigger/repair triangle.
    Guidance (expected results): with 5% of contaminated data, clean accuracy ≈ unchanged (98-99% — the attack is invisible to standard metrics, def. 6.1) and trigger success rate ≈ 99%; Neural Cleanse (def. 6.3) finds \(\|\delta_t\|_1\) abnormally small for the target class only (MAD > 3, the shortcut already exists); pruning the hyperactive neurons under the reconstructed trigger drops the trigger success but eats into clean accuracy from ~10% of removed neurons — hence the targeted unlearning (fine-tuning on reconstructed inputs) which restores the clean behavior without resurrecting the trigger; conclusion: repair is a trade-off, not a restoration. ∎
  6. Poisoning and influence (ch. 7). Label flipping on a tabular MLP: compare random flips vs "large margin" flips (prop. 7.2) at equal budget; compute the influence functions (Hessian-vector products) and show that the highest-influence flips explain the degradation; test the defenses: filtering by influence, truncated loss, regularization — conclude on the federated setting (referral to Emerging ML module ch. 4).
  7. Extraction (ch. 8). (i) Exact extraction of a logistic regression exposed by an API (prop. 8.2): verify the exact reconstruction, then with rounded probabilities (linear programming); (ii) distillation attack (Jagielski): measure accuracy and fidelity as a function of the query budget; (iii) discuss the defenses (rounding, top-1, quotas) and circumvent them.
  8. Membership inference (ch. 8). On a tabular MLP: shadow models + confidence attack (Shokri) then simplified LiRA (Carlini); plot ROC and TPR at FPR ∈ {0.1%, 1%, 10%}; retrain with DP-SGD (Opacus) at \(\varepsilon \in \{1, 8\}\) and plot utility vs attack advantage — the trade-off curve of the Emerging ML module ch. 5, revisited from the attacker's side.
  9. Final synthesis (ch. 9-10). Write the complete threat analysis (def. 1.1) of a proposed real system (bank scoring, content filter, internal RAG agent): surface, capabilities, goals, applicable attacks chapter by chapter, defenses in depth (ch. 10) with their costs — a 10-minute defense with demonstrations drawn from the previous labs.
