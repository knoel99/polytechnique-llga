1. Threat model and taxonomy of attacks

Definition 1.1 — Threat model Every security analysis starts with three explicit assumptions: the attacker's capabilities — white-box (architecture and weights known), gray-box (architecture known, weights not), black-box (access only to the predictions, possibly limited in queries or without scores); the timing of the attack — at training time (poisoning, backdoor) or at inference time (evasion, extraction, membership inference); the goal — untargeted error, targeted error (one specific class), information leakage, theft of intellectual property, denial of service. Changing one assumption changes the whole analysis: most misunderstandings in the field come from different implicit threat models.

Reference framework This taxonomy (evasion / poisoning / extraction / inference) is the one from the survey by Biggio & Roli (Wild Patterns: Ten Years After the Rise of Adversarial Machine Learning, 2018), which also structures the evaluation frameworks (ch. 10). The important conceptual point: unlike classical software security (hunting for bugs), here the attack surface is the prediction function itself — every non-trivial classifier has decision boundaries, and boundaries have two sides.
