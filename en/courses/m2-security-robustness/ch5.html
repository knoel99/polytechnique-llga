5. Certification: randomized smoothing and formal verification

Chapters 3-4 bound robustness from below (attacks) — no guarantee about what has not been found. Certification bounds it from above: a radius within which no perturbation of a given budget can change the prediction.

Definition 5.1 — Randomized smoothing (Cohen, Rosenfeld & Kolter, 2019) Let \(f\) be a base classifier and \(\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)\). The smoothed classifier is $$ g(x) = \arg\max_{c}\ \mathbb{P}\big(f(x + \varepsilon) = c\big), $$ the most likely class when the input is perturbed with Gaussian noise. \(g\) inherits a tight \(L_2\) guarantee (see thm. 5.2), computable for any black-box \(f\) — it is the only certification method that has scaled (ImageNet).
Theorem 5.2 — Certified radius (Cohen et al., 2019) Let \(c_A = g(x)\), \(p_A = \mathbb{P}(f(x + \varepsilon) = c_A)\) and \(p_B = \max_{c \neq c_A} \mathbb{P}(f(x + \varepsilon) = c)\). If one knows bounds \(\underline{p_A} \leq p_A\) and \(p_B \leq \overline{p_B}\) with \(\underline{p_A} > \overline{p_B}\), then \(g\) predicts \(c_A\) over the entire \(L_2\) ball of radius $$ R = \frac{\sigma}{2}\Big(\Phi^{-1}(\underline{p_A}) - \Phi^{-1}(\overline{p_B})\Big), $$ where \(\Phi^{-1}\) is the inverse of the standard Gaussian cumulative distribution function.
(Sketch — the full version is in the paper.) Separation lemma: for every Borel set \(S\) and every \(\delta\), if \(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2I)}(\varepsilon \in S) \geq p\) and \(\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon \in S - \delta) \leq q\), then \(\|\delta\|_2 \geq \tfrac{\sigma}{2}(\Phi^{-1}(p) - \Phi^{-1}(q))\). Proof of the lemma: the most powerful test for distinguishing \(\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2I)\) from \(\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(\delta, \sigma^2 I)\) (Neyman-Pearson lemma) is thresholding the likelihood ratio \(-\delta^\top \varepsilon + \tfrac{1}{2}\|\delta\|_2^2\) — Gaussian linear, hence \(\delta^\top \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2\|\delta\|_2^2)\). Express the two probabilities via \(\Phi\) and eliminate the threshold: this yields the crude bound \(\|\delta\|_2 \geq \sigma\,(\Phi^{-1}(p) - \Phi^{-1}(q))\); the tight analysis of Cohen et al. then refines the constant to \(\tfrac{\sigma}{2}\) (the factor \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) comes from the optimal allocation of the budget between the two hypotheses). Application: set \(S = f^{-1}(c_A)\); \(p_A = \mathbb{P}(x + \varepsilon \in S)\) and every point of the ball \(B_2(x, R)\) satisfies \(\mathbb{P}(\cdot \in S) > \mathbb{P}(\cdot \in S')\) for every competing class \(S'\) — class \(c_A\) remains the majority, so \(g\) equals \(c_A\) there. ∎
Example 5.3 — Certification in practice \(p_A, p_B\) are unknown: they are estimated by Monte Carlo — \(n_0\) draws to choose \(g(x)\), \(n\) draws to build a binomial confidence interval (exact: Clopper-Pearson) on \(p_A\); \(\overline{p_B}\) is taken as one minus that bound. The procedure is sound with probability \(1 - \alpha\) — the final guarantee combines sampling randomness and a deterministic theorem. One then plots certified accuracy vs radius: a curve necessarily below the empirical curve (attacks), the gap measuring our ignorance. Precious link: randomized smoothing is the classification analogue of the Gaussian mechanism of DP (cf. Emerging ML module ch. 5); the "PixelDP" variant (Lecuyer et al., 2019) makes it explicit.
Deterministic certification For ReLU networks, local robustness is decidable: encode the network and the ball as arithmetic constraints (mixed linear/ReLU) and ask a solver — Reluplex (Katz et al., 2017) extends the simplex to ReLU constraints and has verified aircraft collision-avoidance networks. Complete but exponential: in practice one uses incomplete methods (interval bounds, zonotopes) that quickly certify a smaller region. Decision trees: trivial exact certification by enumerating the leaves reachable within the ball — one of the rare cases where "classical" tabular is structurally more certifiable than deep.
