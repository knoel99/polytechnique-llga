7. Data poisoning

Definition 7.1 — Poisoning The attacker modifies a fraction \(\rho\) of the training set before learning. Variants: label flipping (\((x_i, y_i) \to (x_i, 1 - y_i)\); or chosen "optimal" flips), feature manipulation (\(x_i \to x_i + \delta_i\), labels intact — covers the clean-label attack of ch. 6), and insertion of forged examples. Goal: global degradation, targeted bias on a subgroup, or a backdoor. The optimal attack (Biggio et al., 2012, on SVMs) maximizes the attacker's validation risk on the contaminated data — solved by deriving the optimality condition (gradient of the final model with respect to the poison point, via the implicit function theorem).
Proposition 7.2 — Where flipping matters With a fixed budget of flips, the impact on the risk is maximized by moving the boundary as close as possible to well-classified points with a large margin: flipping a point very close to the boundary moves the classifier little (it was already almost an error); flipping points deep inside a class pulls the boundary toward the dense zone of the other class. Empirical consequence: a few percent of "smart" flips degrade more than many random flips — and strong regularization dampens both (the regularized classifier "resists" isolated points).
Definition 7.3 — Influence functions (Koh & Liang, 2017) The influence of a training point \(z\) on the prediction at \(x_{test}\) measures what would happen if \(z\) were upweighted by an \(\varepsilon \to 0\): $$ \mathcal{I}(z, x_{test}) = -\nabla_\theta \ell(x_{test};\hat\theta)^\top H_{\hat\theta}^{-1}\, \nabla_\theta \ell(z; \hat\theta), \qquad H_{\hat\theta} = \nabla_\theta^2 \hat R(\hat\theta), $$ estimable without retraining (Hessian-vector products, LiSSA). It is the "defensive" tool: poisons with strong negative influence are candidates for audit/removal — and the symmetric offensive tool: crafting poisons with maximal influence. In practice: filtering by influence, anomaly detection in the gradients, truncated losses (robust to outliers), and for the distributed setting, Byzantine aggregates (cf. Emerging ML module ch. 4: trimmed mean, coordinate-wise median...).
Example 7.4 — Data hygiene, the real defense Deduplication (poisons are often amplified duplicates), human validation of examples with high loss + high influence, provenance (data lineage, cf. ML Ops module), and trust budgets: every external source enters with a fraction cap. DP (Emerging ML module ch. 5) bounds by construction the effect of an individual point — an \(\varepsilon\)-DP robust to poisoning at the price of utility: the trade-off is the same as in ch. 8 for privacy.
