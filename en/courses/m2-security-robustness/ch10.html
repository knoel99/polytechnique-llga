10. Evaluating and deploying

Definition 10.1 — RobustBench (Croce et al., 2021) Standardized protocol: fixed threats per dataset (\(L_\infty\), \(\varepsilon = 8/255\) on CIFAR-10; \(L_2\); self-supervised attacks), evaluation by AutoAttack + adapted attacks, public leaderboard, reproducible models and details. Reporting rules: standard AND robust accuracy, attack budget, number of restarts, training cost. Without these elements, a robustness number is not information.
In-depth deployment (layered defense)
  1. Reduce the surface: do not expose full scores (ch. 8), query quotas, query monitoring (volumes, distributions);
  2. Detect: adversarial-example detectors, drift/OOD detection (cf. ML Ops module): rejection is an honest defense;
  3. Resist: adversarial training (ch. 4) or certification (ch. 5) where the risk justifies it — otherwise: a secondary "hardened" version;
  4. Limit the damage: fallback models (falling back to a certifiable GBDT or rules), confinement of agent actions (ch. 9), "human in the loop" for sensitive decisions;
  5. Trace and respond: logs of suspicious adversarial queries, post-mortems, adversarial tests in the CI — treat the attack as a reproducible bug;
  6. Framework: the AI Act (EU 2024/1689) classifies critical systems as high risk (ch. 10 of the Explainability-Security-Privacy module for the LLM variant); the NIST AI RMF provides the governance vocabulary.
Conclusion of the module The security of learning is not a property one adds at the end: it is an optimization constraint (min-max, ch. 4), a statistical guarantee (certification, ch. 5) and a process (protocols, monitoring, ch. 10) — on a common foundation: make the threat model explicit before anything else (ch. 1). The three "R"s to remember: Realistic (the adaptive attacker), Rigorous (proven attacks and guarantees), Reproducible (standardized evaluations).
