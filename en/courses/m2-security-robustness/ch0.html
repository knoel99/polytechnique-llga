0. Recap: the foundation and the vocabulary

Prerequisites (M1/M2 modules)
  • Networks and optimization (cf. Deep Learning module): stochastic gradient descent, backpropagation, softmax and the cross-entropy loss \(\ell(f(x), y) = -\log p_f(y \mid x)\), convolutions — the playing field for all the attacks of ch. 2-5;
  • Generalization (cf. Machine Learning module ch. 7): empirical risk vs true risk, overfitting — the vocabulary to understand why robustness generalizes poorly (ch. 4);
  • Statistics (Refresher Statistics): hypothesis testing, type I/II errors, the Neyman-Pearson lemma — the hidden tool behind randomized smoothing (ch. 5) and membership inference (ch. 8);
  • Differential privacy (cf. Emerging ML module ch. 5): the \((\varepsilon, \delta)\) definition, Gaussian mechanism, composition — reused without re-proving in ch. 7-8;
  • Byzantine robustness and federated learning (cf. Emerging ML module ch. 4): malicious clients, robust aggregation — ch. 7 is its "centralized data" counterpart;
  • LLM security (cf. Explainability, Security and Privacy of LLMs module): jailbreaks, injections, extraction — ch. 9 systematically refers to that module.
Definition 0.1 — Robustness, security, safety Three properties that are often confused: robustness is a mathematical property of the model (bounding the variation of the output under a constraint on the input); security is a property of the system faced with an intentional attacker (it requires a threat model); safety covers even unintentional harm (bias, in-distribution errors). This module covers the first two; the third crosses over with the Responsible AI and Explainability modules. A model can be robust and not safe, and vice versa.
Module method Each chapter follows the same grid: (i) formalize the attack as an optimization problem; (ii) prove what is provable (FGSM in ch. 3, the hidden regularization of adversarial training in ch. 4, the certified radius in ch. 5); (iii) give the standard defense and the way it breaks — a defense not evaluated by adaptive attack is not a defense. Labs on MNIST/CIFAR (FGSM, PGD, adversarial training) then full audits.
