9. Beyond images: LLMs and graphs

Definition 9.1 — LLM robustness (overview) Attacks move from the pixel to the token: jailbreaks by suffixing (concatenate a universal adversarial suffix optimized by gradient — GCG, Zou et al., 2023: the same mathematics as PGD, on discrete tokens), prompt injections (the payload sits in a document read by the agent), poisoning of the fine-tuning corpus or of RLHF preferences. The Explainability, Security and Privacy of LLMs module (M2) covers all of this in depth; we retain here only the mathematical continuity: maximize \(P(\text{harmful output} \mid x + \delta)\) under discrete constraints — hence coordinate-wise optimizations and tree searches rather than gradient signs.
Case study 9.2 — Graphs: Nettack (Zügner et al., 2018) GNNs inherit a relational attack surface: the attacker modifies edges, node features, or other nodes in the same component. Nettack perturbs the structure and features of a citation graph to change the classification of a target node, by greedy search on marginal gains — with a direct effect (neighborhood) and an indirect one (perturbations of the neighbors of neighbors modify the aggregations): the classification of a node flips with a few dozen modifications invisible at the scale of the graph. Non-robustness is aggravated by propagation; the dedicated defenses (certification by propagation, robust learning) are covered in ch. 7 of the Advanced GNN module (M2) — this chapter only lays down the prerequisites.
And tabular models? On GBDTs (piecewise constant), no gradient: threshold attacks (moving \(x_j\) just beyond the active splits), differentiable substitutes, or discrete search dominate — and exact certification by leaf enumeration (ch. 5) is trivial there. Robustness therefore depends strongly on the model family: the same system can be vulnerable via its scoring network and certifiable via its GBDT — one more argument for fallback architectures (ch. 10).
