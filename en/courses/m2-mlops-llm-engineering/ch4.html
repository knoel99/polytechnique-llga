4. Costs: euros per million tokens

Every LLM architecture decision sooner or later reduces to a cost per million tokens (Mt). APIs bill the input (prompt: amortizable by caching) and the output (generation: expensive) separately; the 2025-2026 orders of magnitude roughly span, from the small open-weight model to the frontier model, from a few cents to a few tens of euros per Mt — rates that change several times a year: any precise figure in course materials would be stale; the method of computation, on the other hand, remains.

Proposition 4.1 — Unit cost of self-hosted serving If a server delivers a sustained throughput of \(R\) tokens/s (outputs, continuous batching) on GPUs costing \(c\) €/h (rental all inclusive), the operational cost per million output tokens equals: $$\text{cost/Mt} \;=\; \frac{c}{R} \cdot \frac{10^6}{3600} \;\approx\; 278 \cdot \frac{c}{R} \;\; \text{€/Mt}.$$ Example: an 8B on a GPU at ~2 €/h delivering \(R = 2,000\) tokens/s in batch → ≈ 0.28 €/Mt; the same GPU on isolated requests (batch 1, \(R \approx 60\)) → ≈ 9 €/Mt. Batching is a factor of 30 on the bill.
The server produces \(R\) tokens per second; each billed hour at \(c\) € therefore produces \(3600\,R\) tokens; the cost per token is \(c/(3600\,R)\) €, and per million tokens \(10^6 c/(3600 R) = 277.8\, c/R\). The direct division by \(R\): doubling the throughput halves the cost per token at constant hardware — this is the economic justification of ch. 3. ∎

4.1. The saving levers

  • Prefix cache: shared system prompt + history → the prefill is billed/consumed once; on the API side, prompt caching reduces the input cost of repeated prefixes (often billed at a discount); on the vLLM side, the KV is reused for free.
  • Model routing (cascade): small model first, escalation to the big one if the small one "is not sure" (confidence score, agreement of two candidates, or a classifier). The sequential decision formalized as a bandit (cf. RL module ch. 6): one arbitrates cost vs quality request by request.
  • Output limit: output costs ~5-10× the input; constraining max_tokens and the format (answer in k points, short JSON) is the most underrated lever.
  • Quantization (cf. LLMs ch. 6; Advanced Topics ch. 6): 4 bits ≈ ×2-3 in throughput on the weights — and smaller GPUs.
  • Deferred batch processing: non-interactive tasks (archive classification) go in nightly batches; some APIs bill these modes ~50% cheaper.

Multilingual and real costs The per-token bill interacts with tokenization (cf. LLMs module ch. 1): a French corpus typically costs 15-25% more tokens than an equivalent English corpus with the dominant tokenizers. The "price per word" therefore depends on the language — an argument for measuring (exercise 5) rather than trusting round conversion tables.
