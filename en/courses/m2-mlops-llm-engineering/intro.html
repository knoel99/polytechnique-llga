Introduction and overview

ModuleML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles
CodeM2 · P2 (brochure)
Team and workloadInstructor: master's team · ~24 h · from notebook to system: versioning, testing, serving, monitoring, and making LLM-based applications profitable — RAG, fine-tuning, agents — with their latencies, their per-token costs, and their operational guardrails.
Status in the programM2Outside recommended track

A notebook reaching 92% accuracy is not a system: in production, one must version the data, test the models, serve under latency constraints, monitor drift, and pay — per token — every inference. This module covers the full path from prototype to service: ML lifecycle and CI/CD, LLM serving (continuous batching, P99), real costs in euros per million tokens, drift and monitoring, then the three current workstreams that are RAG, LoRA fine-tuning, and agents, up to operational security and green ops. It is the master's module that turns your modeling skills into an engineer's job.

What you will be able to do

  • Version data, features, and models with usable lineage (reproduce an experiment from six months ago);
  • Build a CI/CD pipeline that tests data and models, not just code;
  • Serve an LLM with continuous batching and reason in P99 latency rather than averages;
  • Quantify the cost of an application in euros per million tokens and arbitrate between APIs, fine-tuning, and distillation;
  • Detect and handle drift (data, predictions) with monitoring that alerts when appropriate;
  • Operate a RAG system: index freshness, continuous evaluation, unanswered queries;
  • Deploy versioned LoRA fine-tunings and traced agents with their guardrails;
  • Secure the whole: secrets, sandbox, supply chain, and master the carbon footprint of your services.

Where this course fits in the program

M2 module offered in period 2, outside the recommended track: it is the master's "systems" course, designed for profiles aiming at engineering as much as research. Prerequisites: Large Language Models provides the architecture and inference foundations instrumented here, and Deep Learning (P2 variant) raised the training-cost and serving questions now handled in production.

Downstream: this module directly arms the master's cross-cutting project and the end-of-studies internship — ML/LLM engineer job interviews probe almost word for word its chapters (latency, costs, drift, RAG, agents). It also gives research profiles the vocabulary and constraints for their prototypes to become deployable.

Course outline

  • Reminders: from notebook to system — why scaling up breaks the implicit assumptions of notebook work.
  • Lifecycle: versioning data, features, models — lineage, reproducibility, and long-term artifact management.
  • CI/CD for ML: testing data and models — extending continuous integration to datasets and model metrics.
  • LLM serving: continuous batching, P99 latency — what happens between the prompt and the first token, and how to control it.
  • Costs: euros per million tokens — modeling the total cost of a service and steering it.
  • Evaluation in production: drift and monitoring — detecting that the world has changed under the model's feet.
  • RAG Ops: freshness and continuous evaluation — operating a living index and measuring answer quality over time.
  • Fine-tuning Ops: versioned LoRAs — lightweight trainings, adapters managed like code, possible rollback.
  • Agents in production: tracing and guardrails — trajectory observability, action limits, error budgets.
  • Operational security: secrets, sandbox, supply chain — protecting the accesses, execution, and dependencies of an ML system.
  • Case study and green ops — a complete architecture examined end to end, financial and carbon costs included.
  • Exercises / Labs — instrumenting a mini-system: versioning, CI, serving, drift monitoring.
  • References — documented tools and reference texts of ML Ops.

How to study this module

  • Treat each chapter as a code review more than a course: what matters is the operational detail (alert thresholds, budgets, naming conventions), not the grand principles.
  • Instrument from the first lab onward: measuring the latency and cost of your API calls builds the intuition that reading alone does not give.
  • Keep a cost spreadsheet up to date throughout the module: it is the artifact that later serves in interviews and internships.
  • Read the public post-mortems and incident reports of ML systems cited by the module: the essentials of the trade are learned from others' failures.
  • Connect with Large Language Models in every chapter: every ops decision (batching, quantization, caching) corresponds to an architecture mechanism you have already studied.
The LLGA perspective The LLGA master trains profiles able to do research and to put it into production; this module is the pivot of that promise. LLMs are treated here as what they have become in companies: components of a system, with their latencies, their costs, and their failure modes — and the reflexes acquired here (measure, version, monitor) apply equally well to the graph models and tabular pipelines encountered in the other modules.
