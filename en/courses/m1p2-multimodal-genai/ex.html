Exercises

  1. Composition of noises. Show by induction that \(q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t} x_0, (1-\bar\alpha_t) I)\); why does this closed form make training efficient?
  2. DDPM ELBO. Decompose the log-likelihood \(\log p_\theta(x_0)\) into per-step terms and show that the noise MSE dominates (the KLs between Gaussians are closed-form).
  3. KL of Gaussians. Prove proposition 0.4 in the scalar case by computing \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log q - \log p]\); check that the KL is zero iff \(\mu_1 = \mu_2, \sigma_1 = \sigma_2\) and strictly positive otherwise.
  4. Mode collapse. Simulate a 2D Gaussian→ring GAN; observe the collapse; compare with a diffusion model trained on the same set (diversity via LPIPS).
  5. CFG. Show that \(w = 1\) recovers the conditional prediction and \(w = 0\) the unconditional one; generate a grid \(w \in \{0, 1, 5, 10\}\) and comment on the trade-off.
  6. CLIP zero-shot. Measure the zero-shot accuracy of a fine-tuned CLIP vs a ResNet trained on 10 custom classes as a function of training size; where do the curves cross?
  7. FID and Wasserstein. Recall the formula for \(W_2^2\) between Gaussians (OT module) and show that the FID is an estimate of it (features; sampling bias).
  8. Epsilon or score? Using \(x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon\), show that \(\epsilon = -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t \mid x_0)\).
  9. Noise vs mean. Parameterize the denoiser as predicting \(x_0\) (\(\hat x_0 = x_\theta(x_t, t)\)); express the corresponding loss and explain when this parameterization is preferable (small \(t\), vivid colors).
  10. Truncation. For a StyleGAN whose \(w\) approximately follows a centered Gaussian, show that the rule \(w' = \bar w + \psi(w - \bar w)\), \(\psi \in [0,1]\), reduces the variance of the generated style and comment on the quality/diversity trade-off as \(\psi \to 0\).
  11. InfoNCE temperature. In the contrastive loss of ch. 5, show the effect of \(\tau \to 0\) (peaked distribution, gradients concentrated on the hardest negatives) and of \(\tau \to \infty\) (uniform gradients, slowed learning).
  12. Project. Complete pipeline: light fine-tuning of a latent diffusion model on a small thematic set (LoRA on cross-attention), with parameterizable CFG; evaluate FID/CLIPScore before/after and analyze the effect of the guidance weight \(w\) — also testing variable schedules \(w(t)\) (cf. ch. 9).
Solution 1 — Composition of noises Induction: true at \(t=1\) by definition; if \(x_{t-1}\mid x_0\) is Gaussian with mean \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0\) and variance \((1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})I\), then \(x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}x_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\epsilon\) with \(\epsilon\) independent; prop. 0.1 (sum of independent Gaussians) gives mean \(\sqrt{\alpha_t\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0 = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0\) and variance \(\alpha_t(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}) + (1-\alpha_t) = 1-\bar\alpha_t\). The closed form makes training efficient because the first \(t-1\) steps are skipped: direct noising at any \(t\), a single forward pass per training example.
Solution 2 — DDPM ELBO (plan) (1) Write the ELBO of the chain \(p_\theta(x_{0:T})\) against \(q\) and telescope (Markov chain): \(L = L_T + \sum_t L_{t-1}\) with \(L_{t-1} = \mathrm{KL}(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t,x_0)\,\|\,p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t))\). (2) \(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t,x_0)\) is Gaussian (product of two Gaussians: Bayes + completing the square). (3) Every \(L_{t-1}\) is a KL of Gaussians: closed (prop. 0.4); with \(\sigma_t^2 = \tilde\beta_t\) there remains the gap between the means \(\propto \|\tilde\mu_t - \mu_\theta\|^2\). (4) The parameterization \(\mu_\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\theta)\) exchanges the gap between means for the noise MSE — full details in the proof of model 3.2.
Solution 3 — KL of Gaussians (scalar case) \(\mathrm{KL} = \mathbb{E}_q[\log q - \log p]\) with \(\log q(x) = -\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma_1^2) - \frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2}\) and likewise for \(p\). Difference: \(\log q - \log p = \frac12\log\frac{\sigma_2^2}{\sigma_1^2} - \frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2} + \frac{(x-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2}\). Now \(\mathbb{E}_q[(x-\mu_1)^2] = \sigma_1^2\) and \(\mathbb{E}_q[(x-\mu_2)^2] = \mathbb{E}[((x-\mu_1) + (\mu_1-\mu_2))^2] = \sigma_1^2 + (\mu_1-\mu_2)^2\) (zero cross term). Hence \(\mathrm{KL}(q\|p) = \frac12\big[\log\frac{\sigma_2^2}{\sigma_1^2} + \frac{\sigma_1^2 + (\mu_1-\mu_2)^2}{\sigma_2^2} - 1\big]\). This is \(\frac12[u - 1 - \log u] \ge 0\) with \(u = \sigma_2^2/\sigma_1^2\) (since \(\log u \le u - 1\)) plus a squared term: zero iff \((\mu_1,\sigma_1) = (\mu_2,\sigma_2)\).
Solution 4 — 2D mini-GAN (code) 
import torch
G = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(2, 64), torch.nn.ReLU(),
                        torch.nn.Linear(64, 64), torch.nn.ReLU(),
                        torch.nn.Linear(64, 2))          # noise -> 2D point
D = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(2, 64), torch.nn.ReLU(),
                        torch.nn.Linear(64, 1))          # point -> logit

def sample_ring(n, r=1.0, noise=.05):                    # true law: a ring
    a = torch.rand(n) * 2 * torch.pi
    return torch.stack([r*torch.cos(a), r*torch.sin(a)], 1) + noise*torch.randn(n, 2)

optG, optD = torch.optim.Adam(G.parameters(), 2e-4), torch.optim.Adam(D.parameters(), 2e-4)
bce = torch.nn.functional.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits
for step in range(5000):
    x = sample_ring(256); z = torch.randn(256, 2)
    lossD = bce(D(x), torch.ones(256, 1)) + bce(D(G(z)), torch.zeros(256, 1))
    optD.zero_grad(); lossD.backward(); optD.step()
    z = torch.randn(256, 2)
    lossG = bce(D(G(z)), torch.ones(256, 1))             # non-saturated form
    optG.zero_grad(); lossG.backward(); optG.step()
    if step % 1000 == 0: print(step, float(lossD), float(lossG))
# Observe: G concentrates on an arc of the circle (mode collapse);
# measure diversity: average distance between generated pairs.
The collapse is visible when the covered arc shrinks; compare with a 2D diffusion (same network for \(\epsilon_\theta\)): the denoising MSE covers the whole ring.
Solution 5 — CFG \(\tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(\varnothing) + w(\epsilon_\theta(c) - \epsilon_\theta(\varnothing))\): for \(w = 0\), \(\tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(\varnothing)\) (unconditional); for \(w = 1\), \(\tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(c)\) (conditional). For \(w > 1\), the proof of thm. 4.1 shows that \(\tilde\epsilon\) is the score of \(p(x\mid c)^w p(x)^{1-w}\): an "over-weighted" conditional distribution, more peaked — increased prompt fidelity, reduced diversity (fewer samples explored). Typical grid: at \(w=0\), off-topic images; \(w=1\), correct and varied; \(w=5\), highly compliant, repetitive; \(w=10\), saturation (burnt colors, artifacts).
Solution 7 — FID and W2 (Bures) For two Gaussians \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_1,\Sigma_1)\), \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_2,\Sigma_2)\): $$W_2^2 = \|\mu_1 - \mu_2\|^2 + \mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma_1 + \Sigma_2 - 2(\Sigma_1^{1/2}\Sigma_2\Sigma_1^{1/2})^{1/2}\big)$$ (proof: the diagonal case by rotation, then the general one — OT module). The FID is exactly this formula applied to the Gaussians fitted to the Inception features of the two image sets — an approximation of \(W_2\) both through the features (transport happens only in the backbone's space) and through the moment estimates (\(\hat\mu, \hat\Sigma\) on N samples: the FID is biased downward at small N — always compare at equal cohort size).
Solution 8 — Epsilon = -sqrt(1-a_bar) x score See the proof of theorem 3.3: for \(p(x_t\mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0, (1-\bar\alpha_t)I)\), \(\nabla_{x_t}\log p = -(x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0)/(1-\bar\alpha_t) = -\epsilon/\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\), hence \(\epsilon = -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t\mid x_0)\). Consequence: the denoising MSE is a regression onto the conditional score; integrating over \(x_0\), it targets the marginal score \(\nabla\log p_t(x_t)\) — the starting point of the SDE formulation (ch. 4).
