5. CLIP and vision-language alignment

CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) Encode images and texts jointly in a shared space: an image encoder (ViT) and a text encoder (transformer); 400M (image, caption) pairs collected from the web; contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on massive batches (up to 32k pairs).
Contrastive loss: derivation In a batch of \(N\) pairs \((v_i, t_i)\) (images and texts encoded and normalized), we compute the similarity matrix \(S_{ij} = \langle v_i, t_j\rangle / \tau\) (\(\tau\) the temperature). The symmetric InfoNCE loss: $$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\log\frac{e^{S_{ii}}}{\sum_j e^{S_{ij}}} + \log\frac{e^{S_{ii}}}{\sum_j e^{S_{ji}}}\Big].$$ Each term is a cross-entropy: in the image→text direction, classifying the right caption among \(N\) candidates; in the text→image direction, the right image among \(N\) — two crossed softmaxes on the same matrix.
Where does this form come from? Let \(p_i(j) = \mathrm{softmax}(S_{i\cdot})_j\) denote the "which caption for image \(i\)" distribution. The first term is \(-\log p_i(i)\): maximize the probability of the correct match. Expanding, $$-\log p_i(i) = -S_{ii} + \log\sum_j e^{S_{ij}} \;\ge\; -S_{ii} + \log e^{S_{ii}} = 0,$$ with equality iff the diagonal dominates its whole row: the loss pushes positive pairs up and the \(N-1\) negatives down (softmax = a softmax competition of similarities). Link with mutual information (Oord et al., 2018): for negatives drawn from the product law, one can show the bound $$I(v; t) \;\ge\; \log N - \mathcal{L}_N,$$ where \(I\) is the mutual information between modalities: InfoNCE maximizes a lower bound on \(I\), ever tighter as \(N\) grows — hence the value of giant batches (complete proof: NLP module, chapter on contrastive representations). ∎
Temperature \(\tau\) (learned, initialized at 0.07) rescales the logits before the softmax: small \(\tau\) = peaked distribution = gradient concentrated on the hardest negatives; large \(\tau\) = uniform gradients (exercise 11). The same parameter appears in LLMs (M2 module) — same role: dosing the "severity" of the competition.
Zero-shot classification: worked example No more fixed classification heads: the classes become texts. To classify an image among \(K\) labels \(\ell_1, \dots, \ell_K\): $$\hat k = \arg\max_k\ \big\langle v,\ t(\text{"a photo of a }\ell_k\text{"})\big\rangle.$$ Numerical example. Assume a 3-dimensional space, three classes {dog, cat, graph} and the normalized text embeddings \(t_{\mathrm{dog}} = (0.8;\ 0.6;\ 0)\), \(t_{\mathrm{cat}} = (0.6;\ 0.8;\ 0)\), \(t_{\mathrm{graph}} = (0;\ 0;\ 1)\). For an image \(v = (0.7;\ 0.5;\ 0.5)\): dot products \(0.56 + 0.30 = 0.86\); \(0.42 + 0.40 = 0.82\); \(0.5\). Dog wins (argmax) — without any classifier ever having been trained on these classes: the transfer happens through language. Enriched prompts ("a photo of a \(\ell\), a type of pet") act as a textual prior that refines the position of the prototypes.
CLIP, the multimodal Swiss Army knife CLIP is used everywhere: the text encoder of diffusion models; a similarity judge (R-Precision); image-text retrieval; moderation. CLIPScore for text-image alignment: \(w\cdot\max(\cos(v, t), 0)\) with \(w = 2.5\) (calibrated on human judgments; above ~0.3, the caption "sticks" to the image). ImageBind (sketch): aligning six modalities (image, text, audio, depth, thermal, IMU) with the image as the pivot: each modality is contrasted with the image only, and by the transitivity of the shared space, audio ↔ text emerge without ever having been paired. The bridge with the master's program: one can also align graphs and text in a common space (GNN ↔ LLM embeddings, Graph ML ch. 8) — the graph "modality" enters the same contrastive alignment machinery. 
from PIL import Image
from transformers import CLIPProcessor, CLIPModel
import torch

model = CLIPModel.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32")
proc  = CLIPProcessor.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32")

img = Image.open("graphe.png")
texts = ["a graph drawing", "a cat", "a molecule", "a city map"]
inputs = proc(text=texts, images=img, return_tensors="pt", padding=True)
with torch.no_grad():
    out = model(**inputs)
probs = out.logits_per_image.softmax(dim=1)   # zero-shot classification
print(dict(zip(texts, probs[0].tolist())))
