Introduction and overview

ModuleMultimodal Generative AI
CodeCSC_52002_EP
Team and workloadInstructor: Vicky Kalogeiton (École Polytechnique, CVLab/ECL) · 36 h · 4.5 ECTS · VAEs, GANs, diffusion, CLIP and text-to-image — how generative models bring together text, images and structured data.
Status in the programRecommended — LLGA track

Describing an image in words, generating an image from a sentence, aligning representation spaces: modern generative AI crosses text, images and structured data. This module lays out its complete toolbox — VAEs, GANs, diffusion, latent models, CLIP — up to assembling an entire text-to-image pipeline and its extensions (video, 3D, LLMs). A recommended option of the LLGA track in P2, it lays the generative foundations that the M2 modules will go on to control and extend.

What you will be able to do

  • Derive and train a VAE: ELBO, reparameterization, latent spaces and their properties.
  • Explain adversarial GAN training, its equilibria and instabilities, and the contribution of the Wasserstein variant.
  • Describe generation by diffusion: DDPM and score matching, and the link between the two viewpoints.
  • Use guidance (notably classifier-free), accelerated sampling (DDIM) and latent diffusion.
  • Understand CLIP and the alignment of vision-language spaces that makes text-to-image possible.
  • Assemble the complete text-to-image pipeline and identify the role of each component.
  • Situate the extensions of generation beyond images: video, 3D and multimodal LLMs.
  • Evaluate generative models (notably with FID), estimate their computational costs and discuss their ethical issues.

Where this course fits in the program

Module of period 2 of M1 (36 h, 4.5 ECTS): an option explicitly recommended for the LLGA track, taught by Vicky Kalogeiton. This is where the master's program shifts from learning representations to multimodal generation.

  • Prerequisites — Deep Learning for architectures and training (the review section covers probabilities, networks and the ELBO, hence VAEs and GANs); the Signal Processing module is useful for the signal and spectrogram processing mobilized by the audio and video extensions.
  • Downstream modules — it directly grounds Control in Generative AI (M2), which teaches how to steer these generative models; it prepares Advanced Topics in Large Language Models (M2) for multimodal LLMs, and Speech Technology for Conversational AI (M2) for speech generation.

Course outline

  • Review: probabilities, networks, ELBO — the probabilistic and neural foundation reactivated before the generative models.
  • VAEs and latent spaces — encoding and decoding with a distribution: the first generative building block.
  • GANs: adversary, equilibrium, Wasserstein — learning through a game between generator and discriminator.
  • Diffusion: DDPM and score matching — denoising progressively: the paradigm that dominates generation.
  • Guidance, DDIM and latent diffusion — controlling generation, speeding it up and making it computationally affordable.
  • CLIP and vision-language alignment — the common space where text and image meet.
  • Text-to-image: the complete pipeline — assembling tokenizer, latent space, diffusion and decoder.
  • Beyond images: video, 3D, LLMs — the current frontiers of multimodal generation.
  • Evaluation, costs, ethics — measuring quality (FID among others), weighing costs, questioning uses.
  • Research of the teaching team — a tour of the research work related to the course.
  • Exercises and References — systematic training and sources to go further.

How to study this module

  • Start with the review section and make sure you can derive the ELBO: VAEs, and later latent diffusion, collapse without it.
  • Train a small VAE on a simple dataset before tackling GANs: the contrast between the two ways of learning to generate is very instructive.
  • For each model, write down in black and white the loss function and the sampling algorithm: in generative modeling, these two lines summarize everything.
  • Follow the thread of latent spaces: VAEs, then latent diffusion, then CLIP form a progression, not three independent chapters.
  • Take this module alongside Computational Optimal Transport for ML and Fairness (same period): the Wasserstein distance, the Wasserstein-GAN and the FID illuminate one another there.
  • Redo the exercises of each section before moving on to the next, and keep the final chapter (evaluation, costs, ethics) as a critical reading grid for everything else.
The LLGA positioning This module is the multimodal generative component of the master's program: it crosses the LLM track (CLIP and multimodal LLMs) with the mathematical rigor of transport (Wasserstein, FID), and prepares the control of generation in M2 in Control in Generative AI. Creative industries, data synthesis, computer-aided design: industrial demand there is immense, and the questions of evaluation, costs and ethics it raises are at the heart of the field's current debates.
