0. Review: probabilities, networks, ELBO

This module builds the entire edifice of multimodal generative models on four bricks: Gaussians (the only probabilistic object diffusion will need), the evidence lower bound (ELBO), the reparameterization trick (making sampling differentiable) and encoder-decoder architectures (CNN, U-Net, attention). We prove them here once and for all: they will be reused in the five following chapters.

Reminders from previous modules
  • ELBO / VAE (Deep Learning, ch. 7): \(\mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x) \| p(z))\); reparameterization trick;
  • CNN: convolutions, U-Net (encoder-decoder skip connections);
  • Transformers & cross-attention: image queries, text keys/values — the mechanism that "injects" the prompt into a generator;
  • Gaussians: additive noise, \(\mathcal{N}(0,1)\), reparameterization — diffusion is just a chain of Gaussian noises (Refresher Statistics, chains);
  • EM / PCA (Machine Learning module): the VAE is a probabilistic cousin of PCA — alternating maximization of a lower bound, principal axes = latent directions.
Property 0.1 — Sum of independent Gaussians If \(X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2)\) and \(Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2)\) are independent, then \(X + Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1 + \mu_2,\ \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2)\). The means add, the variances add — it is this rule that makes diffusion "computable in a single step" (chapter 3).
Proof (characteristic function). For \(Z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\), one computes \(\varphi_Z(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{itZ}] = e^{i\mu t - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t^2}\) (a Gaussian integral after completing the square). By independence, \(\varphi_{X+Y}(t) = \varphi_X(t)\,\varphi_Y(t) = e^{i(\mu_1+\mu_2)t - \frac{1}{2}(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)t^2}\), which is the characteristic function of a \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_1+\mu_2,\ \sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)\); Lévy's uniqueness theorem concludes. ∎
Theorem 0.2 — ELBO (Jensen) For every \(x\), every proposal distribution \(q_\phi(z)\) whose support contains that of \(p_\theta(z \mid x)\): $$\log p_\theta(x) \;=\; \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x) + \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z) \,\|\, p_\theta(z\mid x)\big) \;\ge\; \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x),$$ $$\mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\big[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\|\,p(z)\big).$$ The gap between \(\log p_\theta(x)\) and the bound is exactly the KL: maximizing the ELBO brings \(q_\phi\) closer to the true posterior and maximizes the likelihood.
We write the integral as an expectation under \(q_\phi\), then apply the concavity of the logarithm (Jensen's inequality: \(\log \mathbb{E}[U] \ge \mathbb{E}[\log U]\)): $$\log p_\theta(x) = \log \int p_\theta(x\mid z)\, p(z)\, dz = \log \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\!\left[\frac{p_\theta(x\mid z)\,p(z)}{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\right] \ge \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\!\left[\log \frac{p_\theta(x\mid z)\,p(z)}{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\right] = \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x).$$ Moreover \(\log \frac{p_\theta(x\mid z)p(z)}{q_\phi(z)} = \log p_\theta(x) + \log\frac{p_\theta(z\mid x)}{q_\phi(z)}\); taking the expectation under \(q_\phi\), the second term becomes \(-\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi \| p_\theta(z\mid x))\), hence the exact decomposition. ∎
Definition 0.3 — Reparameterization trick Sampling \(z \sim q_\phi(z\mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x), \mathrm{diag}\,\sigma_\phi^2(x))\) blocks the gradient (\(\nabla_\phi\) of a random draw). Instead we write $$z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),$$ so that \(\nabla_\phi\, \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[f_\phi(z)] = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}\big[\nabla_\phi f_\phi(\mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x)\odot\epsilon)\big]\): the noise distribution no longer depends on \(\phi\), and the gradient passes under the expectation. The same trick will make diffusion differentiable end to end.
Proposition 0.4 — KL between diagonal Gaussians (closed form) For \(q = \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \mathrm{diag}\,\sigma_1^2)\) and \(p = \mathcal{N}(\mu_2, \mathrm{diag}\,\sigma_2^2)\): $$\mathrm{KL}(q \| p) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_i \left[\frac{\sigma_{1,i}^2 + (\mu_{1,i}-\mu_{2,i})^2}{\sigma_{2,i}^2} + \log\frac{\sigma_{2,i}^2}{\sigma_{1,i}^2} - 1\right].$$ All of VAE training (and of diffusion, ch. 3) rests on the fact that this quantity is evaluated in closed form. The proof (a direct expectation computation) is the subject of exercise 3, with solution.

Networks. A CNN applies the same filter locally; its receptive field grows with depth, trading resolution for context. The U-Net couples an encoder (downsampling, global semantics) and a decoder (upsampling, local precision) through skip connections that short-circuit the bottleneck: it is the reference denoising architecture for diffusion. Cross-attention injects the text: $$\mathrm{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right)V, \qquad Q = W_Q\,h_{\text{image}}, \quad K = W_K\, e_{\text{text}}, \quad V = W_V\, e_{\text{text}},$$ where \(h_{\text{image}}\) are the spatial activations of the denoiser and \(e_{\text{text}}\) the tokens of the prompt: every image position "queries" the text (cf. Deep Learning module, ch. 6).
