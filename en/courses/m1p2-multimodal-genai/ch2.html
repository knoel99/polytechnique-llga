2. GANs: adversary, equilibrium, Wasserstein

Definition 2.1 — The minimax game and its refinements $$\min_G \max_D\ V(D, G) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))]$$ \(D\) classifies "real/fake", \(G\) forges counterfeits. In practice one uses the non-saturated form \(\max_G \mathbb{E}_z[\log D(G(z))]\) (same equilibria, stronger gradients early in training), or alternative losses: least-squares GAN, hinge. Conditional version: \(G(z, c)\), \(D(x, c)\) — Pix2Pix (image-to-image with an L1 reconstruction constraint).
Theorem 2.2 — Equilibrium of the game
  1. For fixed \(G\), the optimal discriminator is $$D^*(x) = \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x) + p_g(x)}.$$
  2. At this \(D^*\), \(V(G^*) = -\log 4 + 2\,\mathrm{JS}(p_{\mathrm{data}} \| p_g)\); the global minimum is attained only for \(p_g = p_{\mathrm{data}}\): at equilibrium, the generator exactly reproduces the data distribution.
(i) The problem separates pointwise: for each \(x\), maximize \(u(D) = p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\log D + p_g(x)\log(1-D)\) over \(D \in (0,1)\). The derivative \(u'(D) = \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}}{D} - \frac{p_g}{1-D}\) vanishes at \(D^* = \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}}{p_{\mathrm{data}} + p_g}\) (a maximum, since \(u \to -\infty\) at the boundaries). (ii) Substituting: \(V(D^*) = p\log\frac{p}{p+q} + q\log\frac{q}{p+q}\). Subtracting and adding \((p+q)\log\frac{1}{2}\), we obtain $$V(D^*) = -\log 4 + \Big[p\log\frac{2p}{p+q} + q\log\frac{2q}{p+q}\Big] = -\log 4 + 2\,\mathrm{JS}(p\|q),$$ where \(\mathrm{JS}(p\|q) = \tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(p\|\tfrac{p+q}{2}) + \tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(q\|\tfrac{p+q}{2})\) (definition; it is a KL toward a probability law, hence always finite). Since \(\mathrm{JS} \ge 0\) with equality iff \(p = q\), the minimax value is \(-\log 4\), attained only when the generator matches the target law. ∎
Example 2.3 — The Gaussian case If \(p_{\mathrm{data}} = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\) and \(p_g = \mathcal{N}(\mu_g, \sigma_g^2)\), formula (i) gives an explicit \(D^*\) (via the densities): the optimal classifier is a sigmoid of a quadratic score — two Gaussians separate along a quadratic boundary. Backpropagating \(\log D^*(G(z))\) pushes \(\mu_g \to \mu\) and \(\sigma_g^2 \to \sigma^2\): this is the toy version (exercise 4) of the theorem.
Mode collapse: analysis and mitigations The theorem describes the global equilibrium, but the game is non-cooperative: \(G\) may find it profitable to produce in a loop one highly deceptive image (one mode of the distribution), then switch to another — oscillations and loss of diversity. The gradient of \(G\) only "sees" the generated points: never-sampled modes are never corrected. Mitigations:
  • Minibatch discrimination: for each \(x_i\) of the batch, add to \(D\) intra-batch similarity features \(o(x_i) = \sum_{j \ne i} \exp\!\big(-\|f(x_i) - f(x_j)\|\big)\,T_{ij}\) — the discriminator detects "all-identical" batches;
  • Unrolled GAN: \(G\) is trained against the \(D\) it will face (k lookahead steps of the discriminator), which removes pure pursuit cycles;
  • LLGA monitoring: intra-batch diversity (mean LPIPS), FID on large cohorts — the industrial diagnostics of ch. 8.
Construction 2.4 — WGAN: Wasserstein to the rescue of JS When the supports of \(p\) and \(q\) are disjoint or "nearly" so, JS is constant (value \(\log 2\)) and its gradient vanishes: no more learning signal. The Wasserstein distance \(W_1\), on the other hand, stays informative. By the Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality (OT module): $$W_1(p, q) = \sup_{\|f\|_L \le 1}\ \mathbb{E}_{p}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{q}[f(x)],$$ hence the adversarial learning \(\min_G \max_{\|D\|_L \le 1} \mathbb{E}_{p}[D] - \mathbb{E}_{q}[D]\): \(D\) becomes a 1-Lipschitz critic, and the loss is continuous in the parameters of \(G\) even at disjoint supports.
Sketch of the duality: for every 1-Lipschitz \(f\), \(\mathbb{E}_p[f] - \mathbb{E}_q[f] \le \mathbb{E}_{\gamma}[|x - y|] \le W_1\) (coupling \(\gamma\)), hence \(\sup \le W_1\); the hard part (the equality, attained by an optimal Kantorovich potential) belongs to the Lagrangian duality of transport — outside the syllabus, see the OT module. The Lipschitz constraint in practice: gradient penalty. Weight clipping (original WGAN) enforces Lipschitzness too brutally. Instead we penalize the deviation of the gradient norm from 1 on interpolations \(\tilde x = \epsilon x + (1-\epsilon)\hat x\) between data \(x\) and generation \(\hat x\): $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GP}} = \lambda\,\big(\|\nabla_{\tilde x} D(\tilde x)\|_2 - 1\big)^2.$$ Why \((\|\nabla D\| - 1)^2\) and not \(\max(0, \|\nabla D\| - 1)\): penalizing too-small gradients as well prevents \(D\) from flattening out (loss of signal); why interpolations: the supremum of the dual "lives" along the transport segments between the two distributions — that is where the constraint matters. ∎
Architecture 2.5 — StyleGAN: from noise to style
  1. Mapping network: \(w = m(z)\), an 8-layer MLP — \(w\) lives in a "disentangled" space where directions correspond to attributes;
  2. AdaIN (adaptive instance norm): at each generator layer, the style modulates the instance normalization: $$\mathrm{AdaIN}(x, w) = \sigma(w_s)\,\frac{x - \mu(x)}{\sigma(x)} + w_b,$$ where \((w_s, w_b)\) are affine transformations of \(w\) specific to the layer — each resolution receives its own style;
  3. Style mixing: during training, inject two different \(w\) depending on the layers (one for coarse resolutions, the other for fine ones) — this regularizes the mapping and uncovers the structure/texture hierarchy;
  4. Truncation trick: at generation, \(w' = \bar w + \psi\,(w - \bar w)\) with \(\psi < 1\), \(\bar w\) the mapping mean — one stays in the high densities: higher fidelity, reduced diversity (exercise 10).
CycleGAN: cycle consistency, proved useful Without training pairs, translate \(X \to Y\) with \(G\) and \(Y \to X\) with \(F\) using $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cyc}} = \mathbb{E}_x\big[\|F(G(x)) - x\|_1\big] + \mathbb{E}_y\big[\|G(F(y)) - y\|_1\big].$$ Why this is the right constraint. The adversarial loss alone allows \(G(x) = y_0\) for every \(x\) (a single highly "realistic" image fools \(D\): cross-domain collapse, and the content of \(x\) is lost). If \(F(G(x)) = x\) for every \(x\) in the support, then \(G\) is injective: it can neither collapse modes nor destroy content information — it can only change what \(F\) knows how to restore. Cycle consistency is an information-preserving constraint made differentiable; combined with the adversarial loss (which imposes the target-domain style), it achieves the translation. The L1 term (rather than L2) favors sharpness (less blurry averaging).
Evaluation: IS and FID Inception Score: \(\mathrm{IS} = \exp\big(\mathbb{E}_x\,\mathrm{KL}(\,p(y\mid x)\,\|\,p(y)\,)\big)\) — each image must be clearly classifiable (\(p(y|x)\) peaked) and the whole set diverse (\(p(y)\) spread). FID: fit a Gaussian to the Inception features of the real images \((\mu_r, \Sigma_r)\) and of the generated ones \((\mu_g, \Sigma_g)\), then $$\mathrm{FID} = \|\mu_r - \mu_g\|^2 + \mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma_r + \Sigma_g - 2(\Sigma_r\Sigma_g)^{1/2}\big) \;=\; W_2^2\ \text{(Bures) between the two Gaussians}$$ — a direct application of the Wasserstein-Bures distance of the OT module; analysis of sampling biases in ch. 8. Why diffusion eventually dominated: stable loss (a simple denoising MSE), no equilibrium to maintain, natural diversity — but GANs remain unbeatable in speed (1 step) and live on in super-resolution and editing.
