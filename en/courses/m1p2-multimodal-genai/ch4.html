4. Guidance, DDIM and latent diffusion

Classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) By Bayes, \(\log p(x_t \mid y) = \log p(y \mid x_t) + \log p(x_t) - \log p(y)\); taking the gradient in \(x_t\), the last term disappears: $$\nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t \mid y) = \nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t) + \nabla_{x_t}\log p(y\mid x_t).$$ Via thm. 3.3 (\(\epsilon \leftrightarrow -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla\log p\)), the guided denoiser is $$\tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p_\psi(y \mid x_t),$$ where \(p_\psi\) is a classifier trained on the noised \(x_t\). Powerful but costly: a classifier must be trained and evaluated at every step.
Theorem 4.1 — Classifier-free guidance (CFG) as a combined direction Train \(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c)\) with \(c\) sometimes replaced by \(\varnothing\) (condition dropout, ~10 %); at generation, extrapolate the conditional direction: $$\tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing) + w\big(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)\big), \qquad w > 1.$$ Exact interpretation: \(\tilde\epsilon\) is (up to the constant \(\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\)) the score of the unnormalized distribution $$p_w(x_t \mid c) \;\propto\; p(x_t \mid c)^{w}\, p(x_t)^{1-w}.$$
Again by Bayes, \(\nabla\log p(x_t\mid c) = \nabla\log p(c \mid x_t) + \nabla\log p(x_t)\) (the term \(\log p(c)\) is constant in \(x_t\)). The score of \(p_w\) is therefore $$\nabla\log p_w = w\,\nabla\log p(x_t\mid c) + (1-w)\,\nabla\log p(x_t) = \nabla\log p(x_t) + w\big(\nabla\log p(x_t\mid c) - \nabla\log p(x_t)\big).$$ Replacing each score by the corresponding denoiser (thm. 3.3), we obtain exactly the CFG rule: it combines unconditional score + \(w\) times the difference of the scores. For \(w = 1\) we recover the conditional score; for \(w = 0\) the unconditional one; for \(w > 1\), the effective distribution \(p(x_t\mid c)^w p(x_t)^{1-w}\) over-weights the conditioning: it is more "peaked" (higher fidelity, better prompt-following) and less diverse. This is the "creativity" slider of all text-to-image tools — and a research topic in its own right (cf. ch. 9, schedules \(w(t)\)). ∎
Construction 4.2 — DDIM: deriving a family of samplers DDPM fixes \(p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)\) as a Gaussian Markov kernel. The DDIM trick: any process whose marginals \(q(x_t \mid x_0)\) are those of model 3.1 and whose expectation is written as a function of \(\hat x_0\) leads to the same loss. We posit the non-Markovian family (parameterized by \(\sigma_t\)): $$q_\sigma(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_t^2}\;\tfrac{x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}},\ \sigma_t^2 I\big).$$
  • \(\sigma_t = \sqrt{\tilde\beta_t}\) recovers DDPM (the original Markov process);
  • \(\sigma_t = 0\) gives deterministic DDIM: same initial noise \(x_T\) ⇒ same image (editing via a shared trajectory, reproducible seed);
  • the marginals \(q(x_t\mid x_0)\) are unchanged ⇒ the training loss (\(\epsilon\)-MSE) is identical; only the sampler changes;
  • Step skipping: since the trajectory is deterministic, one can subsample \(\tau_1 < \tau_2 < \dots\) — 20 to 50 steps instead of 1000.
Let us check the consistency of the marginals. From \(x_{t-1}\mid x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})I)\) and the definition above, the proposed mean reads \(a\,x_0 + b\,(x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0)\) with \(b = \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_t^2}/\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\); conditionally on \(x_0\), \(x_t\) is Gaussian centered at \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0\), so the mean of \(x_{t-1}\) is indeed \(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,x_0\) and its variance \(b^2(1-\bar\alpha_t) + \sigma_t^2 = 1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}\) — the desired marginal. We thus obtain infinitely many samplers compatible with the same trained \(\epsilon_\theta\). ∎
The SDE viewpoint (Song et al., 2021) As \(T \to \infty\), the forward process converges to the SDE \(dx = f(x, t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw\). Then every SDE has a deterministic companion with the same marginal laws, the probability flow ODE: $$\frac{dx}{dt} = f(x, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x).$$ Generating = integrating this ODE backward: this is the theoretical bridge between DDIM (deterministic) and the fast solvers below.
  • Fast solvers: DPM-Solver handles the prob-flow ODE by separating the linear part (exactly integrable: it is a noised Brownian motion) from the non-linear part (the score): "integrating exponential" schemes at 10–15 steps; distillation (consistent models, LCM, adversarial distillation — cf. ch. 9, Di[M]o) targets 1 to 4 steps; quantization (fp16, fp8) compresses the compute — a panorama revisited in M2 ML Ops;
  • Latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022): an autoencoder (VQ or KL, ch. 1) compressing the image by a factor 4–8 per side, then diffusion in the latent; the denoiser (U-Net or DiT) receives the prompt via cross-attention (def. 0.3) at the levels of maximum resolution; CLIP text encoder: this is the Stable Diffusion architecture;
  • ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023): conditioning by edges/depth/pose. One copies the blocks of the frozen U-Net encoder ("locked" weights \(\theta_{\mathrm{lock}}\)), trains a clone \(\theta_c\) fed by the conditioning map \(c\), and reinjects $$y_c = \mathrm{ZeroConv}_c\big(\mathcal{F}(\cdot;\theta_c)\ +\ \mathcal{F}(x;\theta_{\mathrm{lock}})\big), \qquad y = y_{\mathrm{base}} + y_c,$$ where the zero-convolutions (1×1 convolutions initialized to zero) guarantee that at step 0, \(y_c = 0\): ControlNet starts as the unchanged model and learns only the correction — the same "identity start" trick as zero residuals;
  • Inpainting / img2img: noise the starting image up to a step \(t_0\) (img2img: \(t_0\) tunes fidelity to the start; strong = freedom), then denoise while imposing at every step \(x_t = m\odot x_t^{\mathrm{generated}} + (1-m)\odot x_t^{\mathrm{noised}}\) where \(m\) is the mask (the RePaint mixing rule; variants relax it at the end of the chain).
DiT: when the transformer replaces the U-Net The latent image is cut into \(2\times2\) patches that are "tokenized" (like a ViT); each block = self-attention over the patches + MLP, modulated by time and condition via adaLN-Zero: $$h \leftarrow h + \alpha_1\,\mathrm{Attn}\big(\gamma_1 h + \beta_1\big), \qquad h \leftarrow h + \alpha_2\,\mathrm{MLP}\big(\gamma_2 h + \beta_2\big),$$ where \((\gamma, \beta, \alpha)\) are affine functions of \(t_{\mathrm{emb}} + c_{\mathrm{emb}}\) and the \(\alpha\) are initialized to zero (every block starts as the identity). Quality grows steadily with depth, width and the number of tokens — a "scaling law" behavior typical of transformers, and the basis of modern video generators (ch. 7). 
# Minimal DiT: a block modulated by time (adaLN-Zero)
class DiTBlock(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d, n_heads):
        super().__init__()
        self.norm1 = torch.nn.LayerNorm(d, elementwise_affine=False)
        self.attn  = torch.nn.MultiheadAttention(d, n_heads, batch_first=True)
        self.norm2 = torch.nn.LayerNorm(d, elementwise_affine=False)
        self.mlp   = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(d, 4*d), torch.nn.GELU(),
                                         torch.nn.Linear(4*d, d))
        self.mod = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.SiLU(), torch.nn.Linear(d, 6*d))
        for m in [self.mod[-1]]:        # zero-init of the alphas
            torch.nn.init.zeros_(m.weight); torch.nn.init.zeros_(m.bias)
    def forward(self, h, cond):                     # h: (B, N, d) patches
        g1, b1, a1, g2, b2, a2 = self.mod(cond).unsqueeze(1).chunk(6, dim=-1)
        h = h + a1 * self.attn(self.norm1(h)*g1 + b1, self.norm1(h)*g1 + b1,
                               self.norm1(h)*g1 + b1)[0]
        return h + a2 * self.mlp(self.norm2(h)*g2 + b2)
