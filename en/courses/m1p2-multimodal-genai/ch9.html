9. Research of the teaching team

Vicky Kalogeiton (CVLab — École Polytechnique / ECL; formerly Inria Grenoble, LEAR team) has worked since her PhD on a coherent program: localizing and understanding objects and actions in videos, and more recently, generating them. The three publications below exactly mark out the syllabus of this course — with video as the common thread.

ACT-detector — Action Tubelet Detector (ICCV 2017) arXiv:1705.01861 — V. Kalogeiton, P. Weinzaepfel, V. Ferrari, C. Schmid. Problem. Spatio-temporal action detection: for each actor, find where (per-frame boxes) and when (temporal segment) each action is performed. Method. Extending anchor-based detection (SSD) to the spatio-temporal domain: the input is a tubelet — k consecutive frames — and the network regresses and scores 3D cuboid-anchors (position, spatial scale, duration), with oversampling of frame-level features for small actors. Results. State of the art at the time on UCF-Sports, J-HMDB and UCF101; the tubelet formalism fed the AVA benchmark. Position in the course. This is the "before" of transformers: 3D convolution + anchors, while chapters 3–4 of this module describe the "after" (DiT); the notion of temporal scale (pyramid of durations) foreshadows the temporal pyramid of modern video-to-text models.
Analysis of Classifier-Free Guidance Weight Schedulers (TMLR 2024) arXiv:2404.13040 — CVLab team. Problem. The guidance weight \(w\) of thm. 4.1 is constant in most tools — why not vary it during sampling? Method. Systematic analysis of schedules \(w(t)\) (increasing, decreasing, bell-shaped) on text-to-image diffusion, with fidelity (CLIPScore) and diversity measurements. Results. Variable \(w(t)\) profiles improve the fidelity/diversity trade-off compared to constant \(w\); the behavior depends on the step (start vs end of the chain). Position. Theorem 4.1 gives the theory of CFG at fixed \(w\); this work shows that the temporal degree of freedom is underexploited — a textbook example of "fine engineering of a generative hyperparameter", and a direct topic for the project of ch. 8.
Generating video: AKiRa and Di[M]o distillation (2024–2025) AKiRa — arXiv:2412.14158 (CVPR 2025) and Di[M]o — arXiv:2503.15457 (ICCV 2025). AKiRa (Augmentation Kit on Rays): an augmentation kit for optical video generation — camera on rays, multi-view consistency; the camera-control question (ch. 7) handled on the training side. Di[M]o: distilling a masked diffusion model into a one-step generator — the acceleration of ch. 4 pushed to the extreme (MDM → one-step generator). Position. The two ends of the value chain: quality/consistency (AKiRa) and latency (Di[M]o) — exactly the two axes of the industrial trade-off of ch. 8. Let us also mention Don't drop your samples! (CVPR 2024): a consistency loss that improves the training of conditional diffusion models — the field diagnosis of the pathologies of ch. 3.
The common thread Perceiving video (detection, 2017) → generating it (video diffusion, 2024–2025) → accelerating it (distillation): the CVLab research trajectory mirrors that of the field — and that of this course, from GANs (ch. 2) to guided latent diffusion (ch. 4) up to complete systems (ch. 6).
