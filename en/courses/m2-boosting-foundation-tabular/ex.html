Exercises / Labs

  1. Theorem 3.2 by hand (ch. 2-3). Implement in pure NumPy (without a library) a depth-1 tree boosting: residuals, leaf weights \(w_j^\star = -G_j/(H_j+\lambda)\), split gain — verify on a toy dataset that the objective of theorem 3.2 decreases by exactly the announced Gain at each split.
  2. GOSS and EFB (ch. 3). In a toy GBDT: implement GOSS (\(a = 0.2, b = 0.1\)) and numerically verify proposition 3.5 (compare estimated gains with exact gains over 100 splits); build a sparse one-hot and measure the achievable EFB fusion rate (greedy coloring of the conflict graph).
  3. Target encoding leakage (ch. 4 — central lab). On a dataset where the target is pure noise: (i) naive target encoding with an identifier column — measure train/test AUC; (ii) intermediate cardinality (100 categories) — show that partial leakage skews standard CV; (iii) repair with K-fold OOF and Micci-Barreca smoothing (sweep \(m\)); (iv) rerun CatBoost with native ordered TS and compare.
  4. Symmetric benchmark (ch. 5-6-9). On 8-10 OpenML datasets (Grinsztajn suite): XGBoost/LightGBM/CatBoost with an equal tuning budget (Optuna, 100 trials), FT-Transformer (tokenizer + periodic embeddings), TabPFN. Same folds, 5 seeds, mean ± standard deviation, paired test, total time reported — write the "TabZilla-style" conclusion (ch. 9) then the decision guide (ch. 10) on these data.
  5. Prior and in-context (ch. 6). Generate tables from random SCMs (def. 6.2 — DAG skeleton, piecewise mechanisms, marginal transformations); train a small PFN transformer to predict \(p(y|x, \mathcal{D})\); show in-context learning (performance curve vs context size) and test sensitivity to the prior (Gaussian vs causal prior — when does the PFN fail?).
  6. LLM on a table (ch. 7). Serialize a tabular dataset in 4 formats (CSV/JSON/Markdown/template): measure the effect of format and column order in few-shot (16/64 examples); compare with a GBDT at the same sample sizes — reproduce the crossover of study 7.2; finish with 5 text-to-SQL questions on a mini-database and audit the schema linking errors.
  7. Synthetic and privacy (ch. 8). Generate data with CTGAN, TabDDPM, and a serialized LM (GReaT-like); evaluate fidelity (KS, correlations, detectability) and utility (TSTR vs TRTR); launch a membership inference attack and a nearest-record-distance analysis; redo the generator with light DP and plot the fidelity-privacy curve.
  8. Rotation and useless features (ch. 1). Reproduce the two diagnostics of Grinsztajn et al. on 3 datasets: (i) random column rotation — GBDT vs MLP; (ii) adding noise features then importance-based pruning — who suffers most? Reread proposition 1.3 in light of the results.
