Most industrial data is neither images nor text: it is tables — customers, sensors, transactions, records — and on this ground gradient-boosted decision trees (GBDTs) still dominate deep networks. This module explains why, by descending into the mathematics of boosting and the engineering of XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost, then examines how foundation models (TabPFN, LLMs) move the needle: tables learned in-context, text-to-SQL, synthetic data. It is the master's module that connects "classical" machine learning — the kind you will meet in industry — to the generative foundations at the heart of LLGA. Official course description not published (the 2026-2027 SynapseS S3/S4 catalog is empty): these course materials expand on the brochure's announcement, and their own additions are flagged as going beyond the syllabus.

What you will be able to do

  • Justify with quantitative arguments the dominance of GBDTs on tabular data (small volumes, heterogeneous features, irregularities);
  • Derive boosting as gradient descent in a space of functions and connect approximation trees with shrinkage;
  • Compare the three engineering approaches — second-order regularization (XGBoost), histograms and leaf sampling (LightGBM), ordered encoding of categoricals (CatBoost) — and choose;
  • Encode categorical and numerical columns without information leakage;
  • Train deep tabular models (regularized MLPs, FT-Transformer) and know when they pay off;
  • Use TabPFN for in-context learning on a small table, and discuss its limits;
  • Serialize a table for an LLM, write and debug text-to-SQL queries;
  • Generate synthetic tabular data and build an honest benchmark, up to the final decision guide.

Where this course fits in the program

M2 module offered in period 1, outside the recommended track, designed as an applied deepening accessible right after M1. Prerequisites: chapter 5 (trees, forests, boosting) of Machine Learning is the direct foundation — ch. 0 consolidates it before pushing the theory; the scikit-learn practice acquired in M1 suffices for the labs.

Downstream modules: data versioning, rigorous benchmarking, and computation costs of the ML Ops and LLM Engineering Principles module naturally extend this course when tables become pipelines; and for your internships as for your first jobs, it is tabular data you will handle — this is the module you will use the soonest outside the School.

Course outline

  • Reminders: trees, forests, boosting — reactivates CART trees, bagging, and random forests before shifting to a higher gear.
  • Why GBDTs (still) dominate tabular data — comparative studies and the structural reasons for superiority on tables.
  • Boosting: functional gradient descent — the complete mathematical re-derivation of what a library fit call hides.
  • XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost: three engineering approaches — what each library exactly optimizes, and at what price.
  • Encoding columns: categorical and numerical — target encoding, binning, and the leakage pitfalls that skew comparisons.
  • Deep tabular: regularized MLPs and FT-Transformer — what networks truly bring, and when they still lag behind.
  • TabPFN: learning tables in-context — a pre-trained transformer that classifies a table without gradients: how, why, up to where.
  • LLMs and tables: serialization, text-to-SQL — turning a database into text, querying in natural language, measuring error rates.
  • Synthetic tabular data — generating realistic tables for augmentation, testing, and sharing.
  • Honest benchmarking — protocols, fair search budgets, validation-set overfitting pitfalls.
  • Choosing your method: guide and open questions — the practitioner's practical decision tree, and what research has not settled yet.
  • Exercises / Labs — from hand-computed gradient boosting to the full comparative study on real data.
  • References — founding papers of GBDTs and tabular foundation models.

How to study this module

  • Run the code as you read: each chapter comes with short experiments that must be reproduced to believe the curves.
  • Build your hyperparameter "reading grid" from ch. 2 onward: associate each parameter with its mathematical effect rather than its default value.
  • For GBDTs, read the feature_importance output with suspicion and train yourself to explain why it misleads; it is a job-interview reflex.
  • Redo the benchmark of ch. 9 on a dataset of your choice following the protocol: it is the most rewarding deliverable of the module.
  • Keep the final guide (ch. 10) at hand during the other modules and your internships: it is made to be used, not memorized.
The LLGA perspective A master dedicated to LLMs and graphs might give the impression that tabular data is outdated; it is the opposite: it is the ground where foundation models have the most to prove, and where the industrial value of AI remains the most massive. By crossing the "statistics/trees" culture of data scientists with the "generative foundations" culture of LLGA, this module makes you able to choose the dominant tool in industry and to judge, without a manual, the next wave of tabular models.
