2. Boosting: functional gradient descent

Boosting is most cleanly written as gradient descent in the space of functions — a view due to Friedman (2001), which generalizes AdaBoost (Freund & Schapire, 1997; cf. Machine Learning module ch. 5 for the historical account).

Definition 2.1 — Functional gradient descent We look for \(F^\star = \arg\min_F \sum_i L(y_i, F(x_i))\) in a space of functions. The "gradient at point \(x_i\)" is the variable \(r_i = -\left[\dfrac{\partial L(y_i, F(x_i))}{\partial F(x_i)}\right]_{F = F_{m-1}}\). The boosting iteration: (1) compute the \(r_i\); (2) fit a weak learner \(h_m\) (shallow tree) by least squares to the \(r_i\); (3) update \(F_m = F_{m-1} + \eta\, h_m\).
Proposition 2.2 — Residuals for the quadratic loss For \(L(y, a) = \tfrac{1}{2}(y - a)^2\), one has exactly \(r_i = y_i - F_{m-1}(x_i)\): "fitting on the residuals" is a special case of definition 2.1.
\(\partial L / \partial a = a - y\), so \(-\partial L(y_i, F(x_i))/\partial F = y_i - F(x_i)\). ∎
The role of each ingredient \(\eta\) (shrinkage): a small step ⇒ each tree corrects what the previous ones left behind — equivalent to a regularization of the descent step, to be combined with more trees. Subsampling (Friedman, 2002): fitting each tree on a fraction of the rows injects bagging into the descent and decorrelates the trees (direct link with the M1 forests). Limited depth: a tree of depth \(d\) models only order-\(d\) interactions — boosting learns a sum of "simple piecewise" functions, which matches the irregularity of tabular targets (study 1.2). Early stopping: \(M\) chosen on validation — the analogue of truncating an expansion.
Example 2.3 — One iteration by hand Regression, quadratic loss, current model \(F_0 \equiv \bar{y}\). Residuals \(r_i = y_i - \bar{y}\). A depth-1 tree fit to the residuals chooses the column \(j^\star\) and the threshold \(t^\star\) minimizing the within-leaf variance of the \(r_i\) — that is, best explaining what the constant model could not say. New prediction \(F_1(x) = \bar{y} + \eta\,(a\, \mathbf{1}[x_{j^\star} \leq t^\star] + b\,\mathbf{1}[x_{j^\star} > t^\star])\), where \(a, b\) are the per-leaf residual means. The following iterations attack the residuals of \(F_1\), and so on.
What ch. 3 adds Definition 2.1 says what to fit; XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost optimize how: closed-form leaf weights via a second-order Taylor expansion, histogram-based splits, large-gradient sampling, symmetric trees, ordered statistics for categoricals. All choices that take the GBDT from "nice idea" to industrial default on tables.
