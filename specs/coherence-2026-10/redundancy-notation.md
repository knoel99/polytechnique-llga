# LLGA redundancy map and site-wide notation convention

Lens: redundancy and notation. This is a read-only analysis of `/home/user/polytechnique-llga`. The per-course records in `the per-course reader records` (fields `topics_taught`, `chapters`, `notation`, `errors`) were checked against the course pages `courses/<slug>/index.qmd`: session plans, numbered results and the formulas quoted below. Analysis date: 2026-10-22. Objective ids O1 to O8 are those of `objectives.md`.

## 0. How "wasted in-class hours" was judged

Three facts from the home tree decide whether a duplicate actually costs a student time:

1. **Routes.** The Graph × LLM route (gllm) is DL P1, Emerging ML and TDA in P1; Multimodal GenAI in P2; Advanced GNN and Control GenAI in M2 P1; then MLOps, Security, Graph Generative Models, XSP-LLMs and Advanced Topics LLMs in M2 P2. The AI-systems route (sys) is Signal Processing, Computer Vision and Shapes in P1; DL P2, CV Applications and Real-time AI in P2; then MLOps, Security, XSP-LLMs and Speech in M2 P2. Both routes also include the mandatory core: ML, RL, Graph ML, NLP, LLM and GDL.
2. **DL P1 is a prerequisite of every other P2 "Choose 1" course.** Deep Learning is "required once, here or in P2", and P2 allows only one choice. A student who picks Multimodal GenAI, Advanced DL, OT, Optimization for AI or Image Synthesis in P2 therefore took DL P1 in P1. In those courses, any re-derivation of DL P1 material is duplicated for 100 % of the students.
3. **Overlaps inside the P2 Choose-1 group cost nothing.** A student takes only one course from the group. Examples: NeRF in Image Synthesis, Advanced DL and Multimodal; VAE/GAN/DDPM in DL P2 and Multimodal; adversarial training in Advanced DL and DL P2. These are left alone. Each page must stay self-contained.

A duplicate is flagged only when (a) the same result is taught at the same depth (statement plus proof or derivation) in 3 or more pages, or in 2 pages that a route student takes in the same period, and (b) a route student meets it more than once in class, or the copies contradict each other.

**Canonical-page rule.** The canonical page is the earliest mandatory page that teaches the result best. If no mandatory page teaches it, it is the earliest page on the route that teaches it best. Every other page keeps a 2–5 line recall (statement and notation) with a link to the canonical anchor. The time this frees goes to that page's distinctive content or exercises.
