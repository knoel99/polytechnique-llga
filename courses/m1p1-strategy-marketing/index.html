Stakes (Extrapolating)

Official unit for 2026–2027: IME_51456_EP — Introduction au marketing et à la stratégie (Philippe Ginier-Gillet, 36 h, Period 1, listed in LLGA 1 – S1). Its sheet starts from a fact: most innovative products and most start-ups fail, often because their founders misread what users and partners need before adopting something new, or misread the dynamics of their industry; the course teaches students to think like a strategist and a marketer rather than to recite the 4P or Porter’s five forces, through business case studies.

The rest of this page is our own framing. In period 1, the technical core teaches how to model. This management block asks why ship: positioning, innovation, and market reading. Empirically, a Graph×LLM pipeline only “counts” in industry if it sits inside an offer, a segment, and go-to-market constraints — otherwise it stays a demo.

In this course you learn to tie a technical capability (graph RAG, fraud GNN, …) to a value hypothesis and metrics beyond accuracy alone.

  • Separate competitive advantage, product differentiation, and a mere ML feature.
  • Read a strategy grid (competition, barriers, adoption) without empty jargon.
  • State a value proposition for a structured-AI case (graphs, multimodal, agents).

Place in the 2-year path

M1 · P1: alongside the scientific core. M1 · P2: entrepreneurship / startup–corporate extends the same thread. M1 · P3: the research internship tests a scientific question and its relevance. M2: the ethics seminar and end-of-studies internship close the loop — strategy → evidence → responsibility → deployment.

Industry and research links

Industry: AI product managers, tech strategy consulting, platform teams deciding build vs buy. Research / transfer: situating a paper (GNN, LLM, OT) in an application landscape and adoption bottlenecks — useful for a research internship or CIFRE thesis.

How to approach it

  • Keep one concrete track case (graph fraud, RAG, recommendation) and force every strategy/marketing concept to answer it.
  • Prefer falsifiable hypotheses (segment, cost of error, latency, data moat).
  • Later connect to Startup & entrepreneurship and Ethical Issues and Novel Applications of AI.
