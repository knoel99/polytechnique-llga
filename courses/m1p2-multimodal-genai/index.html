0. Review: probabilities, networks, ELBO

This module builds the entire edifice of multimodal generative models on four bricks: Gaussians (the only probabilistic object diffusion will need), the evidence lower bound (ELBO), the reparameterization trick (making sampling differentiable) and encoder-decoder architectures (CNN, U-Net, attention). We prove them here once and for all: they will be reused in the five following chapters.

Reminders from previous modules

  • ELBO / VAE (Deep Learning, ch. 7): \mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z\mid x) \\| p(z)); reparameterization trick;
  • CNN: convolutions, U-Net (encoder-decoder skip connections);
  • Transformers & cross-attention: image queries, text keys/values — the mechanism that “injects” the prompt into a generator;
  • Gaussians: additive noise, \mathcal{N}(0,1), reparameterization — diffusion is just a chain of Gaussian noises (Refresher Statistics, chains);
  • EM / PCA (Machine Learning module): the VAE is a probabilistic cousin of PCA — alternating maximization of a lower bound, principal axes = latent directions.

Property 0.1 — Sum of independent Gaussians If X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2) and Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2) are independent, then X + Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_1 + \mu_2,\ \sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2). The means add, the variances add — it is this rule that makes diffusion “computable in a single step” (chapter 3).

Proof. Proof (characteristic function). For Z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2), one computes \varphi_Z(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{itZ}] = e^{i\mu t - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t^2} (a Gaussian integral after completing the square). By independence, \varphi_{X+Y}(t) = \varphi_X(t)\,\varphi_Y(t) = e^{i(\mu_1+\mu_2)t - \frac{1}{2}(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)t^2}, which is the characteristic function of a \mathcal{N}(\mu_1+\mu_2,\ \sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2); Lévy’s uniqueness theorem concludes. ∎

Theorem 0.2 — ELBO (Jensen) For every x, every proposal distribution q_\phi(z) whose support contains that of p_\theta(z \mid x): \log p_\theta(x) \;=\; \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x) + \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z) \,\\|\, p_\theta(z\mid x)\big) \;\ge\; \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x), \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x) = \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\big[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)\big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\\|\,p(z)\big). The gap between \log p_\theta(x) and the bound is exactly the KL: maximizing the ELBO brings q_\phi closer to the true posterior and maximizes the likelihood.

Proof. We write the integral as an expectation under q_\phi, then apply the concavity of the logarithm (Jensen’s inequality: \log \mathbb{E}[U] \ge \mathbb{E}[\log U]): \log p_\theta(x) = \log \int p_\theta(x\mid z)\, p(z)\, dz = \log \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\!\left[\frac{p_\theta(x\mid z)\,p(z)}{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\right] \ge \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}\!\left[\log \frac{p_\theta(x\mid z)\,p(z)}{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\right] = \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x). Moreover \log \frac{p_\theta(x\mid z)p(z)}{q_\phi(z)} = \log p_\theta(x) + \log\frac{p_\theta(z\mid x)}{q_\phi(z)}; taking the expectation under q_\phi, the second term becomes -\mathrm{KL}(q_\phi \\| p_\theta(z\mid x)), hence the exact decomposition. ∎

Definition 0.3 — Reparameterization trick Sampling z \sim q_\phi(z\mid x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x), \mathrm{diag}\,\sigma_\phi^2(x)) blocks the gradient (\nabla_\phi of a random draw). Instead we write z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I), so that \nabla_\phi\, \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi}[f_\phi(z)] = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}\big[\nabla_\phi f_\phi(\mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x)\odot\epsilon)\big]: the noise distribution no longer depends on \phi, and the gradient passes under the expectation. The same trick will make diffusion differentiable end to end.

Proposition 0.4 — KL between diagonal Gaussians (closed form) For q = \mathcal{N}(\mu_1, \mathrm{diag}\,\sigma_1^2) and p = \mathcal{N}(\mu_2, \mathrm{diag}\,\sigma_2^2): \mathrm{KL}(q \\| p) = \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_i \left[\frac{\sigma_{1,i}^2 + (\mu_{1,i}-\mu_{2,i})^2}{\sigma_{2,i}^2} + \log\frac{\sigma_{2,i}^2}{\sigma_{1,i}^2} - 1\right]. All of VAE training (and of diffusion, ch. 3) rests on the fact that this quantity is evaluated in closed form. The proof (a direct expectation computation) is the subject of exercise 3, with solution.

Networks. A CNN applies the same filter locally; its receptive field grows with depth, trading resolution for context. The U-Net couples an encoder (downsampling, global semantics) and a decoder (upsampling, local precision) through skip connections that short-circuit the bottleneck: it is the reference denoising architecture for diffusion. Cross-attention injects the text: \mathrm{Attn}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right)V, \qquad Q = W_Q\,h_{\text{image}}, \quad K = W_K\, e_{\text{text}}, \quad V = W_V\, e_{\text{text}}, where h_{\text{image}} are the spatial activations of the denoiser and e_{\text{text}} the tokens of the prompt: every image position “queries” the text (cf. Deep Learning module, ch. 6).

1. VAEs and latent spaces

Concrete scene: text-to-image on a product brief. CFG too high → saturated OOD images; too low → the prompt is ignored. This chapter fixes generative objectives before architectures.

What breaks Posterior collapse (VAE); mode collapse (GAN); miscalibrated guidance (diffusion); evaluating only FID while missing semantic failures.

Definition 1.1 — Conditional & hierarchical VAE Conditioning the generation: p_\theta(x \mid c) with c a class, a text (encoded), another modality. Hierarchical VAEs (HiVAE) stack latents p(z_1 \mid z_2)\, p(z_2 \mid z_3)\dots; VQ-VAEs quantize the latent into a learned dictionary (discrete codes — the ancestor of image tokens); VQ-VAE-2 reached image fidelity before diffusion.

What the latent buys Compressing 512 \times 512 \times 3 into 64 \times 64 latents = \times 64 less computation; all large image generative models (Stable Diffusion, DALL·E 2/3) generate in latent space — the autoencoder (light) and the generator (heavy) are decoupled. The same principle applies to graphs: latent graph, discrete codes (Graph ML module, ch. 7).

Theorem 1.2 — ELBO decomposition: reconstruction + regularization \mathrm{ELBO}_\phi(x) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\big[\log p_\theta(x \mid z)\big]}_{\text{reconstruction}} \;-\; \underbrace{\mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\\|\,p(z)\big)}_{\text{latent regularization}}. Interpretation of the two forces: the first term asks for a z that explains x (auto-encoding), the second for a z that is typical under the prior. The tension between the two structures the latent space: without the KL, q can memorize (“hole” regions not covered by the prior); without reconstruction, z encodes nothing.

Proof. The decomposition is already the form obtained in thm. 0.2; what matters is seeing where each term comes from. Expand \mathbb{E}_{q}\big[\log \frac{p_\theta(x\mid z)\,p(z)}{q_\phi(z\mid x)}\big]: \mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] + \mathbb{E}_q[\log p(z)] - \mathbb{E}_q[\log q_\phi(z\mid x)] = \mathbb{E}_q[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi \\| p), where we recognize the definition \mathrm{KL}(q\\|p) = \mathbb{E}_q[\log q - \log p]. When p_\theta(x\mid z) is a diagonal Gaussian of mean D_\theta(z) and fixed variance \sigma^2, the reconstruction term equals -\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\\|x - D_\theta(z)\\|^2 + \mathrm{cst}: the “reconstruction MSE” of the labs is nothing but the negative of a Gaussian log-likelihood. When q_\phi and p are Gaussian, the second term closes via prop. 0.4. ∎

Posterior collapse: diagnosis and remedies The decoder (often an autoregressive transformer) becomes so powerful that it ignores z: q_\phi(z\mid x) \to p(z), the KL drops toward 0 and the VAE degenerates into a non-conditional model (the latent “collapses”). Causes: a strong autoregressive decoder, a dominant KL early in training, an overly simple prior.

  • KL annealing: weight the KL by a coefficient that slowly rises from 0 to 1 — let the reconstruction settle first;
  • \beta-VAE (Higgins et al., 2017): maximizing \mathbb{E}[\log p(x\mid z)] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL} with \beta \> 1 forces a disentangled latent (one direction = one factor of variation); in practice, against collapse one rather uses \beta \< 1;
  • Free bits: penalize the KL per dimension only beyond a budget \lambda: \max(\mathrm{KL}_j, \lambda) — every dimension receives a free “information allowance”, which prevents it from dying.

Construction 1.3 — VQ-VAE: quantizing the latent The encoder produces z_e(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}; a codebook \{e_1, \dots, e_K\} \subset \mathbb{R}^D is learned; each position is quantized to the nearest code: z_q(x) = e_k, \qquad k = \arg\min_j \\|z_e(x) - e_j\\|_2. The argmin is not differentiable. One uses the straight-through estimator (gradient copy) and the total loss: \mathcal{L} = \log p_\theta\big(x \mid z_q\big) + \big\\|\mathrm{sg}[z_e] - e\big\\|_2^2 + \beta\,\big\\|z_e - \mathrm{sg}[e]\big\\|_2^2, where \mathrm{sg} is the stop-gradient operator.

Proof. Why this three-term loss? (i) The reconstruction must be a function of the quantized codes z_q — but \nabla_{z_e} z_q = 0 (argmin): we define \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_e} \mathrel{:=} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial z_q} (straight-through); the estimator is biased yet empirically effective. (ii) The term \\|\mathrm{sg}[z_e] - e\\|^2 is quadratic in e (the codes migrate toward the produced encodings); the stop-gradient symmetrically prevents the encoder from moving. (iii) The term \beta\\|z_e - \mathrm{sg}[e]\\|^2 does the opposite: it pulls the encoder toward the codes. The two “gear” (commitment) terms synchronize encoder and dictionary, while the first term trains the decoder; together they implement a continuous K-means inside the training loop. ∎

Hierarchy and sampling. In a HiVAE, the ELBO becomes \mathbb{E}_{q(z_1, z_2\mid x)}\big[\log p_\theta(x \mid z_1) + \log p(z_1 \mid z_2) + \log p(z_2) - \log q_\phi(z_1 \mid x, z_2) - \log q_\phi(z_2 \mid x)\big], each level compressing the previous one (the “low” scales govern composition, the “high” ones the details — exactly the levels of a U-Net). To generate: sample z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) (or the levels of the learned prior, as in VQ-VAE-2), then decode in a single network pass; latent traversals (interpolating z between two images) are the visual test of the continuity of the learned space.

import torch

class MiniVAE(torch.nn.Module):
    """Minimal convolutional VAE for MNIST-like data (28x28)."""
    def __init__(self, d=8):
        super().__init__()
        self.enc = torch.nn.Sequential(            # x: (B,1,28,28)
            torch.nn.Conv2d(1, 32, 4, 2, 1), torch.nn.ReLU(),   # 14x14
            torch.nn.Conv2d(32, 64, 4, 2, 1), torch.nn.ReLU(),  # 7x7
            torch.nn.Flatten(), torch.nn.Linear(64*7*7, 2*d))
        self.dec = torch.nn.Sequential(
            torch.nn.Linear(d, 64*7*7), torch.nn.Unflatten(1, (64, 7, 7)),
            torch.nn.ConvTranspose2d(64, 32, 4, 2, 1), torch.nn.ReLU(),
            torch.nn.ConvTranspose2d(32, 1, 4, 2, 1), torch.nn.Sigmoid())
    def forward(self, x):
        h = self.enc(x); mu, logvar = h.chunk(2, dim=1)
        z = mu + torch.exp(0.5*logvar) * torch.randn_like(mu)   # reparametrization
        return self.dec(z), mu, logvar

def vae_loss(x, xh, mu, logvar, beta=1.0):
    rec = torch.nn.functional.binary_cross_entropy(
        xh, x, reduction="sum") / x.size(0)                      # reconstruction
    kl  = -0.5 * torch.sum(1 + logvar - mu.pow(2) - logvar.exp()) / x.size(0)
    return rec + beta * kl                                      # ELBO (prop. 0.4)

LLGA analogies The VQ-VAE manufactures image tokens: DALL·E 1 generates these tokens autoregressively (LLMs module), latent diffusion replaces them with continuous denoising (ch. 4). On graphs, the same discrete/continuous dichotomy opposes discrete VAEs and edge diffusion (Graph ML, ch. 7); in NLP, the CLIP “latent” (ch. 5) is the shared text-image space.

2. GANs: adversary, equilibrium, Wasserstein

Definition 2.1 — The minimax game and its refinements \min_G \max_D\ V(D, G) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))] D classifies “real/fake”, G forges counterfeits. In practice one uses the non-saturated form \max_G \mathbb{E}_z[\log D(G(z))] (same equilibria, stronger gradients early in training), or alternative losses: least-squares GAN, hinge. Conditional version: G(z, c), D(x, c) — Pix2Pix (image-to-image with an L1 reconstruction constraint).

Theorem 2.2 — Equilibrium of the game

  1. For fixed G, the optimal discriminator is D^\*(x) = \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)}{p_{\mathrm{data}}(x) + p_g(x)}.
  2. At this D^\*, V(G^\*) = -\log 4 + 2\,\mathrm{JS}(p_{\mathrm{data}} \\| p_g); the global minimum is attained only for p_g = p_{\mathrm{data}}: at equilibrium, the generator exactly reproduces the data distribution.

Proof. (i) The problem separates pointwise: for each x, maximize u(D) = p_{\mathrm{data}}(x)\log D + p_g(x)\log(1-D) over D \in (0,1). The derivative u'(D) = \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}}{D} - \frac{p_g}{1-D} vanishes at D^\* = \frac{p_{\mathrm{data}}}{p_{\mathrm{data}} + p_g} (a maximum, since u \to -\infty at the boundaries). (ii) Substituting: V(D^\*) = p\log\frac{p}{p+q} + q\log\frac{q}{p+q}. Subtracting and adding (p+q)\log\frac{1}{2}, we obtain V(D^\*) = -\log 4 + \Big[p\log\frac{2p}{p+q} + q\log\frac{2q}{p+q}\Big] = -\log 4 + 2\,\mathrm{JS}(p\\|q), where \mathrm{JS}(p\\|q) = \tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(p\\|\tfrac{p+q}{2}) + \tfrac12\mathrm{KL}(q\\|\tfrac{p+q}{2}) (definition; it is a KL toward a probability law, hence always finite). Since \mathrm{JS} \ge 0 with equality iff p = q, the minimax value is -\log 4, attained only when the generator matches the target law. ∎

Example 2.3 — The Gaussian case If p_{\mathrm{data}} = \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) and p_g = \mathcal{N}(\mu_g, \sigma_g^2), formula (i) gives an explicit D^\* (via the densities): the optimal classifier is a sigmoid of a quadratic score — two Gaussians separate along a quadratic boundary. Backpropagating \log D^\*(G(z)) pushes \mu_g \to \mu and \sigma_g^2 \to \sigma^2: this is the toy version (exercise 4) of the theorem.

Mode collapse: analysis and mitigations The theorem describes the global equilibrium, but the game is non-cooperative: G may find it profitable to produce in a loop one highly deceptive image (one mode of the distribution), then switch to another — oscillations and loss of diversity. The gradient of G only “sees” the generated points: never-sampled modes are never corrected. Mitigations:

  • Minibatch discrimination: for each x_i of the batch, add to D intra-batch similarity features o(x_i) = \sum_{j \ne i} \exp\!\big(-\\|f(x_i) - f(x_j)\\|\big)\,T_{ij} — the discriminator detects “all-identical” batches;
  • Unrolled GAN: G is trained against the D it will face (k lookahead steps of the discriminator), which removes pure pursuit cycles;
  • LLGA monitoring: intra-batch diversity (mean LPIPS), FID on large cohorts — the industrial diagnostics of ch. 8.

Construction 2.4 — WGAN: Wasserstein to the rescue of JS When the supports of p and q are disjoint or “nearly” so, JS is constant (value \log 2) and its gradient vanishes: no more learning signal. The Wasserstein distance W_1, on the other hand, stays informative. By the Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality (OT module): W_1(p, q) = \sup_{\\|f\\|_L \le 1}\ \mathbb{E}_{p}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{q}[f(x)], hence the adversarial learning \min_G \max_{\\|D\\|_L \le 1} \mathbb{E}_{p}[D] - \mathbb{E}_{q}[D]: D becomes a 1-Lipschitz critic, and the loss is continuous in the parameters of G even at disjoint supports.

Proof. Sketch of the duality: for every 1-Lipschitz f, \mathbb{E}_p[f] - \mathbb{E}_q[f] \le \mathbb{E}_{\gamma}[\|x - y\|] \le W_1 (coupling \gamma), hence \sup \le W_1; the hard part (the equality, attained by an optimal Kantorovich potential) belongs to the Lagrangian duality of transport — outside the syllabus, see the OT module. The Lipschitz constraint in practice: gradient penalty. Weight clipping (original WGAN) enforces Lipschitzness too brutally. Instead we penalize the deviation of the gradient norm from 1 on interpolations \tilde x = \epsilon x + (1-\epsilon)\hat x between data x and generation \hat x: \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{GP}} = \lambda\,\big(\\|\nabla_{\tilde x} D(\tilde x)\\|_2 - 1\big)^2. Why (\\|\nabla D\\| - 1)^2 and not \max(0, \\|\nabla D\\| - 1): penalizing too-small gradients as well prevents D from flattening out (loss of signal); why interpolations: the supremum of the dual “lives” along the transport segments between the two distributions — that is where the constraint matters. ∎

Architecture 2.5 — StyleGAN: from noise to style

  1. Mapping network: w = m(z), an 8-layer MLP — w lives in a “disentangled” space where directions correspond to attributes;
  2. AdaIN (adaptive instance norm): at each generator layer, the style modulates the instance normalization: \mathrm{AdaIN}(x, w) = \sigma(w_s)\,\frac{x - \mu(x)}{\sigma(x)} + w_b, where (w_s, w_b) are affine transformations of w specific to the layer — each resolution receives its own style;
  3. Style mixing: during training, inject two different w depending on the layers (one for coarse resolutions, the other for fine ones) — this regularizes the mapping and uncovers the structure/texture hierarchy;
  4. Truncation trick: at generation, w' = \bar w + \psi\,(w - \bar w) with \psi \< 1, \bar w the mapping mean — one stays in the high densities: higher fidelity, reduced diversity (exercise 10).

CycleGAN: cycle consistency, proved useful Without training pairs, translate X \to Y with G and Y \to X with F using \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cyc}} = \mathbb{E}_x\big[\\|F(G(x)) - x\\|_1\big] + \mathbb{E}_y\big[\\|G(F(y)) - y\\|_1\big]. Why this is the right constraint. The adversarial loss alone allows G(x) = y_0 for every x (a single highly “realistic” image fools D: cross-domain collapse, and the content of x is lost). If F(G(x)) = x for every x in the support, then G is injective: it can neither collapse modes nor destroy content information — it can only change what F knows how to restore. Cycle consistency is an information-preserving constraint made differentiable; combined with the adversarial loss (which imposes the target-domain style), it achieves the translation. The L1 term (rather than L2) favors sharpness (less blurry averaging).

Evaluation: IS and FID Inception Score: \mathrm{IS} = \exp\big(\mathbb{E}_x\,\mathrm{KL}(\,p(y\mid x)\,\\|\,p(y)\,)\big) — each image must be clearly classifiable (p(y\|x) peaked) and the whole set diverse (p(y) spread). FID: fit a Gaussian to the Inception features of the real images (\mu_r, \Sigma_r) and of the generated ones (\mu_g, \Sigma_g), then \mathrm{FID} = \\|\mu_r - \mu_g\\|^2 + \mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma_r + \Sigma_g - 2(\Sigma_r\Sigma_g)^{1/2}\big) \;=\; W_2^2\ \text{(Bures) between the two Gaussians} — a direct application of the Wasserstein-Bures distance of the OT module; analysis of sampling biases in ch. 8. Why diffusion eventually dominated: stable loss (a simple denoising MSE), no equilibrium to maintain, natural diversity — but GANs remain unbeatable in speed (1 step) and live on in super-resolution and editing.

3. Diffusion: DDPM and score matching

Model 3.1 — Forward process (noising) A Markov chain that adds Gaussian noise: q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{1 - \beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\ \beta_t I\big), \qquad t = 1, \dots, T, with increasing \beta_t. Key property (composition of Gaussians): q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\, x_0,\ (1 - \bar\alpha_t) I\big), \qquad \alpha_t = 1 - \beta_t,\quad \bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s = 1}^{t}\alpha_s. For T = 1000, \bar\alpha_T \approx 0: the image has become pure noise.

Proof. By induction on t. Base case: t = 1: x_1 = \sqrt{\alpha_1}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \alpha_1}\,\epsilon, so q(x_1 \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_1}x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_1)I) by definition. Inductive step: assume x_{t-1} \mid x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})I). Then x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}\,x_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \perp x_{t-1} \mid x_0. By property 0.1 (sum of independent Gaussians): mean \sqrt{\alpha_t}\cdot\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,x_0 = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 (since \alpha_t \bar\alpha_{t-1} = \bar\alpha_t); variance \alpha_t(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}) + (1-\alpha_t) = 1 - \bar\alpha_{t-1} + \alpha_t\bar\alpha_{t-1} = 1 - \bar\alpha_t. ∎

Variance schedule: linear or cosine The choice of the \beta_t sets the speed of destruction. Linear (\beta_t: 10^{-4} \to 0.02, original DDPM): high frequencies die too fast, and the end of the chain is needlessly noisy. Cosine (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021): \bar\alpha_t = \frac{f(t)}{f(0)}, \qquad f(t) = \cos^2\!\left(\frac{t/T + 0.008}{1.008}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\right), which gives a gently sloped decay of \bar\alpha_t, effective also at low resolution. Rule of thumb: the schedule must destroy information uniformly across the scales of the image.

Model 3.2 — Learned reverse process and per-step ELBO p_\theta(x_{t-1} \mid x_t) = \mathcal{N}\big(\mu_\theta(x_t, t),\ \sigma_t^2 I\big), \qquad p(x_T) = \mathcal{N}(0, I), and training reduces to a simple MSE: \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0, \epsilon, t}\big[\\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\\|^2\big], \qquad x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon.

Proof. Step 1 — telescoping. Applying thm. 0.2 to the chain p_\theta(x_{0:T}) with q(x_{1:T}\mid x_0), the ELBO rewrites (Markov chain, telescoped products): -\mathrm{ELBO} = \mathrm{KL}\big(q(x_T\mid x_0)\,\\|\,\mathcal{N}(0,I)\big) + \sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}_q\!\left[\mathrm{KL}\big(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0)\,\\|\,p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)\big)\right] = L_T + \sum_t L_{t-1}. Step 2 — closed-form forward posterior. By Bayes’ rule q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0) \propto q(x_t \mid x_{t-1})\,q(x_{t-1}\mid x_0): a product of two Gaussians in x_{t-1}, hence Gaussian; completing the square: q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(\tilde\mu_t(x_t, x_0),\ \tilde\beta_t I\big), \quad \tilde\beta_t = \frac{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\beta_t,\quad \tilde\mu_t = \frac{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,\beta_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \frac{\sqrt{\alpha_t}(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})}{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,x_t. Step 3 — every L_{t-1} is a KL of Gaussians: closed by prop. 0.4. If we set \sigma_t^2 = \tilde\beta_t, what remains is to align the means: L_{t-1} = C + \frac{1}{2\sigma_t^2}\\|\tilde\mu_t - \mu_\theta\\|^2. Step 4 — noise parameterization. We choose \mu_\theta(x_t, t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\Big(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\,\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\Big), which amounts (substituting x_0 = (x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon)/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t} into \tilde\mu_t) to requiring \epsilon_\theta \approx \epsilon; all the remaining factors are known constants absorbed into the weighting. Hence the single MSE of model 3.2 — a remarkable result: five lines of theory for a one-line loss. ∎

Theorem 3.3 — Predicting the noise = predicting the score \epsilon = -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\;\nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t \mid x_0). The denoising MSE is therefore a conditional score matching: the network learns \nabla_x \log p — exactly the object of the “score-based” family of Song et al. (2021), which unifies diffusion and SDEs (ch. 4).

Proof. For a Gaussian density p(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(x_t;\ m, s^2 I) with m = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0, s^2 = 1 - \bar\alpha_t, we compute \nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t\mid x_0) = \nabla_{x_t}\left[-\frac{\\|x_t - m\\|^2}{2s^2}\right] + 0 = -\frac{x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0}{1-\bar\alpha_t}. Now x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 = \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon, so \nabla_{x_t}\log p = -\epsilon/\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}, that is \epsilon = -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t \mid x_0). ∎

import torch

def forward_diffusion(x0, t, alphas_bar, eps=None):
    """x_t = sqrt(a_bar_t) x0 + sqrt(1 - a_bar_t) * eps   (in one step!)"""
    if eps is None: eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    return alphas_bar[t].sqrt()[:, None, None, None] * x0 \
         + (1 - alphas_bar[t]).sqrt()[:, None, None, None] * eps, eps

# Training (typical DDPM loop):
# x0 = batch of images; t ~ U{1..T}
xt, eps = forward_diffusion(x0, t, alphas_bar)
loss = torch.nn.functional.mse_loss(unet(xt, t), eps)   # predict the noise
loss.backward()

# Sampling (T steps, formula of model 3.2):
x = torch.randn(n, c, h, w)                    # x_T ~ N(0, I)
for t in reversed(range(T)):
    eps = unet(x, t)
    mu = (x - beta[t].sqrt()/(1 - alphas_bar[t]).sqrt() * eps) / alpha[t].sqrt()
    x = mu + beta[t].sqrt() * torch.randn_like(x) if t > 0 else mu

4. Guidance, DDIM and latent diffusion

Classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) By Bayes, \log p(x_t \mid y) = \log p(y \mid x_t) + \log p(x_t) - \log p(y); taking the gradient in x_t, the last term disappears: \nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t \mid y) = \nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t) + \nabla_{x_t}\log p(y\mid x_t). Via thm. 3.3 (\epsilon \leftrightarrow -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla\log p), the guided denoiser is \tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t) - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p_\psi(y \mid x_t), where p_\psi is a classifier trained on the noised x_t. Powerful but costly: a classifier must be trained and evaluated at every step.

Theorem 4.1 — Classifier-free guidance (CFG) as a combined direction Train \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) with c sometimes replaced by \varnothing (condition dropout, ~10 %); at generation, extrapolate the conditional direction: \tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing) + w\big(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t, \varnothing)\big), \qquad w \> 1. Exact interpretation: \tilde\epsilon is (up to the constant \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}) the score of the unnormalized distribution p_w(x_t \mid c) \;\propto\; p(x_t \mid c)^{w}\, p(x_t)^{1-w}.

Proof. Again by Bayes, \nabla\log p(x_t\mid c) = \nabla\log p(c \mid x_t) + \nabla\log p(x_t) (the term \log p(c) is constant in x_t). The score of p_w is therefore \nabla\log p_w = w\,\nabla\log p(x_t\mid c) + (1-w)\,\nabla\log p(x_t) = \nabla\log p(x_t) + w\big(\nabla\log p(x_t\mid c) - \nabla\log p(x_t)\big). Replacing each score by the corresponding denoiser (thm. 3.3), we obtain exactly the CFG rule: it combines unconditional score + w times the difference of the scores. For w = 1 we recover the conditional score; for w = 0 the unconditional one; for w \> 1, the effective distribution p(x_t\mid c)^w p(x_t)^{1-w} over-weights the conditioning: it is more “peaked” (higher fidelity, better prompt-following) and less diverse. This is the “creativity” slider of all text-to-image tools — and a research topic in its own right (cf. ch. 9, schedules w(t)). ∎

Construction 4.2 — DDIM: deriving a family of samplers DDPM fixes p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t) as a Gaussian Markov kernel. The DDIM trick: any process whose marginals q(x_t \mid x_0) are those of model 3.1 and whose expectation is written as a function of \hat x_0 leads to the same loss. We posit the non-Markovian family (parameterized by \sigma_t): q_\sigma(x_{t-1}\mid x_t, x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_t^2}\;\tfrac{x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}},\ \sigma_t^2 I\big).

  • \sigma_t = \sqrt{\tilde\beta_t} recovers DDPM (the original Markov process);
  • \sigma_t = 0 gives deterministic DDIM: same initial noise x_T ⇒ same image (editing via a shared trajectory, reproducible seed);
  • the marginals q(x_t\mid x_0) are unchanged ⇒ the training loss (\epsilon-MSE) is identical; only the sampler changes;
  • Step skipping: since the trajectory is deterministic, one can subsample \tau_1 \< \tau_2 \< \dots — 20 to 50 steps instead of 1000.

Proof. Let us check the consistency of the marginals. From x_{t-1}\mid x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})I) and the definition above, the proposed mean reads a\,x_0 + b\,(x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0) with b = \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}-\sigma_t^2}/\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}; conditionally on x_0, x_t is Gaussian centered at \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0, so the mean of x_{t-1} is indeed \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\,x_0 and its variance b^2(1-\bar\alpha_t) + \sigma_t^2 = 1-\bar\alpha_{t-1} — the desired marginal. We thus obtain infinitely many samplers compatible with the same trained \epsilon_\theta. ∎

The SDE viewpoint (Song et al., 2021) As T \to \infty, the forward process converges to the SDE dx = f(x, t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw. Then every SDE has a deterministic companion with the same marginal laws, the probability flow ODE: \frac{dx}{dt} = f(x, t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x). Generating = integrating this ODE backward: this is the theoretical bridge between DDIM (deterministic) and the fast solvers below.

  • Fast solvers: DPM-Solver handles the prob-flow ODE by separating the linear part (exactly integrable: it is a noised Brownian motion) from the non-linear part (the score): “integrating exponential” schemes at 10–15 steps; distillation (consistent models, LCM, adversarial distillation — cf. ch. 9, Di[M]o) targets 1 to 4 steps; quantization (fp16, fp8) compresses the compute — a panorama revisited in M2 ML Ops;
  • Latent diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022): an autoencoder (VQ or KL, ch. 1) compressing the image by a factor 4–8 per side, then diffusion in the latent; the denoiser (U-Net or DiT) receives the prompt via cross-attention (def. 0.3) at the levels of maximum resolution; CLIP text encoder: this is the Stable Diffusion architecture;
  • ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023): conditioning by edges/depth/pose. One copies the blocks of the frozen U-Net encoder (“locked” weights \theta_{\mathrm{lock}}), trains a clone \theta_c fed by the conditioning map c, and reinjects y_c = \mathrm{ZeroConv}_c\big(\mathcal{F}(\cdot;\theta_c)\ +\ \mathcal{F}(x;\theta_{\mathrm{lock}})\big), \qquad y = y_{\mathrm{base}} + y_c, where the zero-convolutions (1×1 convolutions initialized to zero) guarantee that at step 0, y_c = 0: ControlNet starts as the unchanged model and learns only the correction — the same “identity start” trick as zero residuals;
  • Inpainting / img2img: noise the starting image up to a step t_0 (img2img: t_0 tunes fidelity to the start; strong = freedom), then denoise while imposing at every step x_t = m\odot x_t^{\mathrm{generated}} + (1-m)\odot x_t^{\mathrm{noised}} where m is the mask (the RePaint mixing rule; variants relax it at the end of the chain).

DiT: when the transformer replaces the U-Net The latent image is cut into 2\times2 patches that are “tokenized” (like a ViT); each block = self-attention over the patches + MLP, modulated by time and condition via adaLN-Zero: h \leftarrow h + \alpha_1\,\mathrm{Attn}\big(\gamma_1 h + \beta_1\big), \qquad h \leftarrow h + \alpha_2\,\mathrm{MLP}\big(\gamma_2 h + \beta_2\big), where (\gamma, \beta, \alpha) are affine functions of t_{\mathrm{emb}} + c_{\mathrm{emb}} and the \alpha are initialized to zero (every block starts as the identity). Quality grows steadily with depth, width and the number of tokens — a “scaling law” behavior typical of transformers, and the basis of modern video generators (ch. 7).

# Minimal DiT: a block modulated by time (adaLN-Zero)
class DiTBlock(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d, n_heads):
        super().__init__()
        self.norm1 = torch.nn.LayerNorm(d, elementwise_affine=False)
        self.attn  = torch.nn.MultiheadAttention(d, n_heads, batch_first=True)
        self.norm2 = torch.nn.LayerNorm(d, elementwise_affine=False)
        self.mlp   = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(d, 4*d), torch.nn.GELU(),
                                         torch.nn.Linear(4*d, d))
        self.mod = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.SiLU(), torch.nn.Linear(d, 6*d))
        for m in [self.mod[-1]]:        # zero-init of the alphas
            torch.nn.init.zeros_(m.weight); torch.nn.init.zeros_(m.bias)
    def forward(self, h, cond):                     # h: (B, N, d) patches
        g1, b1, a1, g2, b2, a2 = self.mod(cond).unsqueeze(1).chunk(6, dim=-1)
        h = h + a1 * self.attn(self.norm1(h)*g1 + b1, self.norm1(h)*g1 + b1,
                               self.norm1(h)*g1 + b1)[0]
        return h + a2 * self.mlp(self.norm2(h)*g2 + b2)

5. CLIP and vision-language alignment

CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) Encode images and texts jointly in a shared space: an image encoder (ViT) and a text encoder (transformer); 400M (image, caption) pairs collected from the web; contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on massive batches (up to 32k pairs).

Contrastive loss: derivation In a batch of N pairs (v_i, t_i) (images and texts encoded and normalized), we compute the similarity matrix S_{ij} = \langle v_i, t_j\rangle / \tau (\tau the temperature). The symmetric InfoNCE loss: \mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\log\frac{e^{S_{ii}}}{\sum_j e^{S_{ij}}} + \log\frac{e^{S_{ii}}}{\sum_j e^{S_{ji}}}\Big]. Each term is a cross-entropy: in the image→text direction, classifying the right caption among N candidates; in the text→image direction, the right image among N — two crossed softmaxes on the same matrix.

Proof. Where does this form come from? Let p_i(j) = \mathrm{softmax}(S_{i\cdot})_j denote the “which caption for image i” distribution. The first term is -\log p_i(i): maximize the probability of the correct match. Expanding, -\log p_i(i) = -S_{ii} + \log\sum_j e^{S_{ij}} \;\ge\; -S_{ii} + \log e^{S_{ii}} = 0, with equality iff the diagonal dominates its whole row: the loss pushes positive pairs up and the N-1 negatives down (softmax = a softmax competition of similarities). Link with mutual information (Oord et al., 2018): for negatives drawn from the product law, one can show the bound I(v; t) \;\ge\; \log N - \mathcal{L}_N, where I is the mutual information between modalities: InfoNCE maximizes a lower bound on I, ever tighter as N grows — hence the value of giant batches (complete proof: NLP module, chapter on contrastive representations). ∎

Temperature \tau (learned, initialized at 0.07) rescales the logits before the softmax: small \tau = peaked distribution = gradient concentrated on the hardest negatives; large \tau = uniform gradients (exercise 11). The same parameter appears in LLMs (M2 module) — same role: dosing the “severity” of the competition.

Zero-shot classification: worked example No more fixed classification heads: the classes become texts. To classify an image among K labels \ell_1, \dots, \ell_K: \hat k = \arg\max_k\ \big\langle v,\ t(\text{"a photo of a }\ell_k\text{"})\big\rangle. Numerical example. Assume a 3-dimensional space, three classes {dog, cat, graph} and the normalized text embeddings t_{\mathrm{dog}} = (0.8;\ 0.6;\ 0), t_{\mathrm{cat}} = (0.6;\ 0.8;\ 0), t_{\mathrm{graph}} = (0;\ 0;\ 1). For an image v = (0.7;\ 0.5;\ 0.5): dot products 0.56 + 0.30 = 0.86; 0.42 + 0.40 = 0.82; 0.5. Dog wins (argmax) — without any classifier ever having been trained on these classes: the transfer happens through language. Enriched prompts (“a photo of a \ell, a type of pet”) act as a textual prior that refines the position of the prototypes.

CLIP, the multimodal Swiss Army knife CLIP is used everywhere: the text encoder of diffusion models; a similarity judge (R-Precision); image-text retrieval; moderation. CLIPScore for text-image alignment: w\cdot\max(\cos(v, t), 0) with w = 2.5 (calibrated on human judgments; above ~0.3, the caption “sticks” to the image). ImageBind (sketch): aligning six modalities (image, text, audio, depth, thermal, IMU) with the image as the pivot: each modality is contrasted with the image only, and by the transitivity of the shared space, audio ↔︎ text emerge without ever having been paired. The bridge with the master’s program: one can also align graphs and text in a common space (GNN ↔︎ LLM embeddings, Graph ML ch. 8) — the graph “modality” enters the same contrastive alignment machinery.

6. Text-to-image: the complete pipeline

Architecture 6.1 — From sentence to pixel The industrial pipeline of a latent text-to-image generator:

Step Component Role / details
1. Tokenization BPE / SentencePiece prompt → token sequence (NLP module); truncated to 77–225 depending on the model
2. Text encoding CLIP text encoder + T5/ByT5 tokens → (L \times d) tensor; CLIP’s pooled embedding also conditions adaLN (DiT)
3. Latent diffusion U-Net or DiT + cross-attention noise → denoised latent (20–50 steps, solver of ch. 4, CFG \tilde\epsilon)
4. Decoding VAE decoder latent (64\times64) → image (512\times512) in a single pass
5. Refinement refiner / super-resolution ×2–4 upsampling (by diffusion at higher resolution or a super-resolution GAN — ch. 2)

Training the large models often freezes the text encoder and the VAE, learning only the denoiser; LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) even freezes the denoiser and learns only low-rank corrections in the cross-attention weights — the “domestic” thematic fine-tuning (a few dozen images, a few GPU-minutes).

  • Prompt engineering, quantitatively: the prompt is a gradient on the conditioning: adding “photorealistic, 85 mm” shifts the embeddings and hence every CFG step. Measurable effects: position (the first tokens weigh more), saturation (beyond ~50 effective tokens, diminishing returns), syntactic weighting “(word:1.3)” = rescaling of the attention logit during inference training;
  • Negative prompt: the generalized version of CFG with two conditions — \varnothing is replaced by c_-: \tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,c_-) + w\big(\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,c_+) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t,c_-)\big), the negative direction is repelled (thm. 4.1): “blurry, watermark, extra fingers”;
  • Seeds: the seed fixes x_T (and the solver’s randomness); same seed + same prompt = same image under deterministic DDIM; one sweeps the seeds to pick the best realization before refining the prompt — the actual workflow of practitioners;
  • Inference cost: (number of steps) × (denoiser cost) + decoding; latency governs the industrial choice (fast solvers, 1-step distillation when diversity matters less).

7. Beyond images: video, 3D, multimodal LLMs

  • Video: spatio-temporal latents (the VAE compresses time as well: 2–4 frames per latent token), the denoiser interleaves spatial attention (intra-frame) and temporal attention (inter-frames); cost grows like (frames × resolution²), hence the cascades (low resolution → temporal upsampling); camera control and long-horizon coherence: open problems (cf. ch. 9, AKiRa);
  • Captioning / VQA: image encoder (ViT) + text decoder (transformer); trained on pairs; graphical VQA (“what is the shortest path?”) — visual reasoning;
  • Multimodal LLMs: how to make an LLM “see”?
    • Linear projector (LLaVA): h_v = W\,p_i + b on each ViT patch — the image “words” are projected into the LLM’s embedding space: simple, effective, lots of tokens;
    • Q-Former (BLIP-2): 32 learned query vectors that cross-attend to the image features and produce 32 embeddings — a compact “visual summary”; freezes the LLM while pretraining the bridge;
    • Two-stage training protocol: (1) alignment pretraining on immense (image, caption) corpora — learning the correspondence; (2) multimodal instruction tuning — conversations, reasoning, answering;
    • Audio: discrete tokens of spectrograms or signals (neural codec) injected in the same way — same pattern, another modality (M2 module for the rest);
  • 3D: NeRF (sketch): an MLP (x, d) \mapsto (\sigma, c) (density, color) is optimized by differentiable volume rendering: C(r) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\,\sigma\big(r(t)\big)\,c\big(r(t), d\big)\,dt, \qquad T(t) = \exp\!\Big(-\!\int_{t_n}^{t}\sigma\big(r(s)\big)ds\Big), that is, “the opacity accumulated along the ray weights the colors”; recent 3D generative models prefer diffusion over point clouds and meshes — discretization, operators, correspondence: this is exactly the syllabus of the M2 Geometric Deep Learning module (direct link);
  • World models: action-conditioned generation of interactive videos (learned simulators; link with the RL module, latent world; mention of Genie/Sora-like systems);
  • Generative structured data: text-to-graph (generating the schema of a network), graph-to-text (a report from a knowledge graph) — the exact intersection of the Graph ML and NLP modules;
  • Multimodal agents: perceiving (screenshot) and acting (click, code) — GUI agents combine vision + LLM + tools (RL module, ch. 7).

Vicky Kalogeiton (CVLab — École Polytechnique / ECL; formerly Inria Grenoble, LEAR team) has worked since her PhD on a coherent program: localizing and understanding objects and actions in videos, and more recently, generating them. ACT-detector — Action Tubelet Detector (ICCV 2017) arXiv:1705.01861 — V. Kalogeiton, P. Weinzaepfel, V. Ferrari, C. Schmid. Problem. Spatio-temporal action detection: for each actor, find where (per-frame boxes) and when (temporal segment) each action is performed. Method. Extending anchor-based detection (SSD) to the spatio-temporal domain: the input is a tubelet — k consecutive frames — and the network regresses and scores 3D cuboid-anchors (position, spatial scale, duration), with oversampling of frame-level features for small actors. Results. State of the art at the time on UCF-Sports, J-HMDB and UCF101; the tubelet formalism fed the AVA benchmark. Position in the course. Analysis of Classifier-Free Guidance Weight Schedulers (TMLR 2024) arXiv:2404.13040 — CVLab team. Problem. The guidance weight w of thm. 4.1 is constant in most tools — why not vary it during sampling? Method. Systematic analysis of schedules w(t) (increasing, decreasing, bell-shaped) on text-to-image diffusion, with fidelity (CLIPScore) and diversity measurements. Results. Variable w(t) profiles improve the fidelity/diversity trade-off compared to constant w; the behavior depends on the step (start vs end of the chain). Position. Solution 1 — Composition of noises Induction: true at t=1 by definition; if x_{t-1}\mid x_0 is Gaussian with mean \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0 and variance (1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})I, then x_t = \sqrt{\alpha_t}x_{t-1} + \sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\,\epsilon with \epsilon independent; prop. 0.1 (sum of independent Gaussians) gives mean \sqrt{\alpha_t\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0 = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 and variance \alpha_t(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}) + (1-\alpha_t) = 1-\bar\alpha_t. The closed form makes training efficient because the first t-1 steps are skipped: direct noising at any t, a single forward pass per training example.

Solution 2 — DDPM ELBO (plan) (1) Write the ELBO of the chain p_\theta(x_{0:T}) against q and telescope (Markov chain): L = L_T + \sum_t L_{t-1} with L_{t-1} = \mathrm{KL}(q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t,x_0)\,\\|\,p_\theta(x_{t-1}\mid x_t)). (2) q(x_{t-1}\mid x_t,x_0) is Gaussian (product of two Gaussians: Bayes + completing the square). (3) Every L_{t-1} is a KL of Gaussians: closed (prop. 0.4); with \sigma_t^2 = \tilde\beta_t there remains the gap between the means \propto \\|\tilde\mu_t - \mu_\theta\\|^2. (4) The parameterization \mu_\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\epsilon_\theta) exchanges the gap between means for the noise MSE — full details in the proof of model 3.2.

Solution 3 — KL of Gaussians (scalar case) \mathrm{KL} = \mathbb{E}_q[\log q - \log p] with \log q(x) = -\frac12\log(2\pi\sigma_1^2) - \frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2} and likewise for p. Difference: \log q - \log p = \frac12\log\frac{\sigma_2^2}{\sigma_1^2} - \frac{(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma_1^2} + \frac{(x-\mu_2)^2}{2\sigma_2^2}. Now \mathbb{E}_q[(x-\mu_1)^2] = \sigma_1^2 and \mathbb{E}_q[(x-\mu_2)^2] = \mathbb{E}[((x-\mu_1) + (\mu_1-\mu_2))^2] = \sigma_1^2 + (\mu_1-\mu_2)^2 (zero cross term). Hence \mathrm{KL}(q\\|p) = \frac12\big[\log\frac{\sigma_2^2}{\sigma_1^2} + \frac{\sigma_1^2 + (\mu_1-\mu_2)^2}{\sigma_2^2} - 1\big]. This is \frac12[u - 1 - \log u] \ge 0 with u = \sigma_2^2/\sigma_1^2 (since \log u \le u - 1) plus a squared term: zero iff (\mu_1,\sigma_1) = (\mu_2,\sigma_2).

Solution 4 — 2D mini-GAN (code)

import torch
G = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(2, 64), torch.nn.ReLU(),
                        torch.nn.Linear(64, 64), torch.nn.ReLU(),
                        torch.nn.Linear(64, 2))          # noise -> 2D point
D = torch.nn.Sequential(torch.nn.Linear(2, 64), torch.nn.ReLU(),
                        torch.nn.Linear(64, 1))          # point -> logit

def sample_ring(n, r=1.0, noise=.05):                    # true law: a ring
    a = torch.rand(n) * 2 * torch.pi
    return torch.stack([r*torch.cos(a), r*torch.sin(a)], 1) + noise*torch.randn(n, 2)

optG, optD = torch.optim.Adam(G.parameters(), 2e-4), torch.optim.Adam(D.parameters(), 2e-4)
bce = torch.nn.functional.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits
for step in range(5000):
    x = sample_ring(256); z = torch.randn(256, 2)
    lossD = bce(D(x), torch.ones(256, 1)) + bce(D(G(z)), torch.zeros(256, 1))
    optD.zero_grad(); lossD.backward(); optD.step()
    z = torch.randn(256, 2)
    lossG = bce(D(G(z)), torch.ones(256, 1))             # non-saturated form
    optG.zero_grad(); lossG.backward(); optG.step()
    if step % 1000 == 0: print(step, float(lossD), float(lossG))
# Observe: G concentrates on an arc of the circle (mode collapse);
# measure diversity: average distance between generated pairs.

The collapse is visible when the covered arc shrinks; compare with a 2D diffusion (same network for \epsilon_\theta): the denoising MSE covers the whole ring.

Solution 5 — CFG \tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(\varnothing) + w(\epsilon_\theta(c) - \epsilon_\theta(\varnothing)): for w = 0, \tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(\varnothing) (unconditional); for w = 1, \tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(c) (conditional). For w \> 1, the proof of thm. 4.1 shows that \tilde\epsilon is the score of p(x\mid c)^w p(x)^{1-w}: an “over-weighted” conditional distribution, more peaked — increased prompt fidelity, reduced diversity (fewer samples explored). Typical grid: at w=0, off-topic images; w=1, correct and varied; w=5, highly compliant, repetitive; w=10, saturation (burnt colors, artifacts).

Solution 7 — FID and W2 (Bures) For two Gaussians \mathcal{N}(\mu_1,\Sigma_1), \mathcal{N}(\mu_2,\Sigma_2): W_2^2 = \\|\mu_1 - \mu_2\\|^2 + \mathrm{tr}\big(\Sigma_1 + \Sigma_2 - 2(\Sigma_1^{1/2}\Sigma_2\Sigma_1^{1/2})^{1/2}\big) (proof: the diagonal case by rotation, then the general one — OT module). The FID is exactly this formula applied to the Gaussians fitted to the Inception features of the two image sets — an approximation of W_2 both through the features (transport happens only in the backbone’s space) and through the moment estimates (\hat\mu, \hat\Sigma on N samples: the FID is biased downward at small N — always compare at equal cohort size).

Solution 8 — Epsilon = -sqrt(1-a_bar) x score See the proof of theorem 3.3: for p(x_t\mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0, (1-\bar\alpha_t)I), \nabla_{x_t}\log p = -(x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0)/(1-\bar\alpha_t) = -\epsilon/\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}, hence \epsilon = -\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_{x_t}\log p(x_t\mid x_0). Consequence: the denoising MSE is a regression onto the conditional score; integrating over x_0, it targets the marginal score \nabla\log p_t(x_t) — the starting point of the SDE formulation (ch. 4).
