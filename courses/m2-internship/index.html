Stakes (Extrapolating)

Extrapolated notes. The end-of-studies internship (24 ECTS) is the master’s capstone: broader ownership than in M1, often a professionalizing setting (company, applied lab, startup), and a sustainable deliverable. Empirically, this is where the Graph×LLM track turns into a job or a research direction: After the master, on the home page, lists the roles and problem spaces it opens onto.

Place in the 2-year path

Upstream: all of M1 including the research internship, M2 specialization, ethics seminar, transverse project. Here: long P3 execution. Typical difference vs M1 research internship: more product / team / ops integration, even when the topic stays R&D.

The Year 2 end-of-year projects, run as the Transverse project, end just before this internship and prepare it. Their last deliverable is a one-page open question for the final internship, or a PhD / CIFRE proposal (deliverables). Their charter already holds a module map, and their trust work package drafts the report’s risks, ethics and data section. Start the internship from that open question and its baseline number; the module map below names the project behind each topic family.

Industry and research links

Industry: productionization, impact measurement, multi-team collaboration. Research: possible PhD path (academic or CIFRE) if the internship produced a solid open question. P1/P2 management and M2 ethics help frame “should we deploy?” not only “can we run it?”.

How to approach it

  • One-sentence goal + measurable success criteria by week 1–2.
  • Explicitly link the topic to 2–3 modules of these notes (path evidence), using the module map.
  • Plan a risks / ethics / data section in the report — not a cosmetic appendix.

Report skeleton and rubric

The skeleton and the rubric are proposals of these notes; the programme and the host set the real format and grading. Draft sections 1–3 in the first two weeks, then check them against the final text.

  1. Goal and success criteria: the one-sentence goal and the 3–5 measurable criteria fixed in week 1–2, each marked met, partly met or missed.
  2. Starting point: the open question of your Year 2 project, or the host’s problem when it differs, and the baseline number you start from.
  3. Path evidence: your row of the module map and the 2–3 chapters reused, with what each one brought.
  4. System and method: what the host had, what you built, and the data with its provenance.
  5. Results: every headline number against the baseline, with a confidence interval over seeds or bootstrap resamples; impact or online metrics if the system shipped.
  6. Risks, ethics and data: licence and consent of the data, personal data, measured failure modes, misuse, and your answer to “should we deploy?”.
  7. Open question: what stays open after the internship; a solid one can seed a PhD or CIFRE proposal.
  8. Appendix: the commands, configurations and data versions behind each number.
