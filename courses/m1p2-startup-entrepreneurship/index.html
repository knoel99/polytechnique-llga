A worked case: the graph+text demo

Illustrative An invented scenario, with no real company behind it. It applies the bridge questions of How to approach it to one Period 2 deliverable.

The demo. A student team has built the question-answering system of capstone Y1.1: text becomes a knowledge graph (NLP), a retriever walks the graph (Graph ML), and an LLM writes the answer with its sources. A mid-size industrial company wants it for its maintenance manuals. One team member would rather found a startup around it.

Step 1 — Who pays, who uses, who operates.

  • The payer is the head of maintenance, who owns the budget and must see fewer hours lost searching manuals.
  • The users are about 300 technicians, often on a tablet in a workshop.
  • The operator is the company’s IT team on the corporate route, or the startup on the spin-off route. Whoever operates also answers the call when an answer is wrong.

Step 2 — Maturity. On the technology readiness level (TRL) scale, the demo is at TRL 4: validated in the lab, on a public proxy corpus. Run on the company’s own manuals, it reaches TRL 5–6 (validated, then demonstrated, in a relevant environment). A pilot used by technicians in the workshop is TRL 7. The gap is work the demo skipped: per-document access rights, refreshing the graph when a manual changes, and a test set written with technicians.

Step 3 — Risk register.

  • Data: confidential manuals leave the company network if the LLM is a remote API. Mitigation: a contract with no data retention, or a self-hosted open-weights model.
  • Wrong answers: an invented torque value can damage a machine. Mitigation: answer only from a cited passage, and abstain otherwise.
  • Dependency: the whole product rests on one model provider and one licence. Mitigation: keep a second model that passes the same tests, and check that the model licence allows commercial use.

Step 4 — Two routes.

Spin-off startup Corporate pilot (intrapreneurship)
Speed Weeks to a first pilot Months: purchasing, security review, legal
Money Founders’ time, grants, then a seed round An internal budget and a sponsoring manager
Who owns the IP The startup, under a licence from the school or lab if needed The company, as the contract says
Main risk One customer and no second source of revenue The pilot never leaves the innovation lab
Success looks like A second paying customer The system enters the IT run budget

Step 5 — Cost check. Ten questions per technician per working day (20 days a month) give about 60,000 requests a month. Take requests the size of the worked example in MLOps ch. 4 (1,500 tokens in, 300 out). Its break-even volumes for running your own model are about 185,000 to 580,000 requests a month, and at its illustrative prices a managed API costs about 160 € a month here. Price therefore favours the API; a case for self-hosting has to come from the data constraint of Step 3.

What the case teaches. The model was the easy part. The bridge is decided by who signs the run budget after the pilot, who answers for a wrong answer, and whether the data may leave the building.

How to approach it

  • Take a P2 technical deliverable (e.g. the graph+text demo of capstone Y1.1 or Y1.3) and write the startup/corporate “bridge”: who pays, who operates, what risk. The worked case shows the five steps.
  • For the cost argument, compute the cost per request and the break-even volume between an API and your own model with MLOps ch. 4.
  • If the sustainability option is chosen, cross-link with Optimization for AI (APM_52067_EP, Period 2; ch. 10 on training networks) and Optimization and Responsible AI for Sustainability, whose ch. 6 measures the energy and carbon footprint and whose ch. 3 gives the frugality levers.
  • The responsible-AI module is no longer a Period 2 companion: in 2026–2027 it is taught in Period 1 as CSC_51076_EP and is not in the LLGA lists.
