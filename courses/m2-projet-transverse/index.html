Stakes (Extrapolating)

Extrapolated notes. The transverse project is where the building blocks of the master meet one real question: messy data, users with constraints, a deliverable shared by a team.

It fights silos. The goal is not to pass course X, but to make a system or a study hold from end to end, with evidence that each component earns its place.

Place in the 2-year path

The project runs through M2 periods 1 and 2, beside the ethics seminar and the humanities, languages and sports courses, and before the final internship (24 ECTS).

It draws on M1 P1–P2, the research internship and the M2 courses of the same year. It is a dress rehearsal for the internship: same mix of brief, deadlines, code and report, at a smaller scale.

Industry and research links

Industry: project mode (backlog, milestones, review) and the integration of an ML or LLM component into a product, with its latency, cost and monitoring.

Research: a pilot study, a reproducible prototype, an early sketch of a paper or of an open-source repository.

How to approach it

  • Define “done” in usage terms, not only in model metrics.
  • Document decisions and technical debt: they are reusable for the internship report.
  • If the topic is Graph × LLM, place it among the career profiles listed under “After the master” on the home page.

Year-1 projects first

Year 1 ends with integrative projects (Y1-1 to Y1-8) that combine the mandatory P2 courses. A Year-2 capstone can extend one of them instead of starting from scratch: the team reuses the Year-1 data card, baseline and frozen evaluation script, and the brief states what is new.

Natural pairs: C1 extends Y1-1 (biomedical knowledge graph), C2 extends Y1-2 (Elliptic), C3 extends Y1-6 (molecules), C4 extends Y1-4 (recommendation), C5 extends Y1-5 (delivery tours) and C8 extends Y1-8 (statutory retrieval).

Capstone catalogue

Status Independent proposals of these notes, not official subjects. The official unit asks for “a challenging question either raised by an industrial partner or by a researcher”: a real brief from a partner or a researcher replaces the entry below as soon as it is assigned.

Every brief names a generic role (“a payments-compliance team”), never a real company. The assessment modalities have not been communicated: the milestones, weights and checklist below are one possible design, to be adapted by the supervisor.

Common frame

  • Team. 3–4 students. Each student owns one component; the integration, the evaluation and the report are shared.
  • Time. Half a day a week over M2 P1–P2, about 24 weeks, so about 100 h per student in the weekly slot. With personal work, 8 ECTS corresponds to about 200–240 h (25–30 h per ECTS).
  • Compute. One T4- or L4-class GPU per team (free Colab or school cluster); inference up to 8B parameters in 4-bit; training up to 1.5B parameters, with LoRA beyond 0.5B; a single run of at most 3 h. No paid API in the deliverable; an optional comparison with a stated budget is allowed.
  • Data. Public data with a named licence, recorded in the data card. Never credentialed or controlled-access data, never scraping against terms of use.
  • Catch-up. A student who did not take a Year-1 elective named on a card reads the linked chapters in weeks 1–4, and the brief says who does what.
  • Announced slots. Several M2 modules are announced slots (TBC). Each card says what to do if a slot does not open: the linked chapter is then read in self-study, or a simpler component from a mandatory course replaces it.

Required in every capstone (the ops milestones of the frame): a CI non-regression gate on the frozen evaluation set; a serving datasheet (P50 / P99 latency, € per million tokens or per 1,000 requests, Wh per 1,000 requests); a drift monitor; a model card and a data card; an AI Act section; at least one red-team test.

C1 · Health · Biomedical knowledge assistant with traceable evidence and abstention

Field Content
Brief (generic role) A medical-information team wants an assistant that answers drug / target / disease questions for professionals, cites its evidence, abstains when unsure and ignores instructions injected in retrieved text.
Data PrimeKG — CC0 1.0 on Harvard Dataverse (doi:10.7910/DVN/IXA7BM); its 20 upstream sources keep their own terms — about 129k nodes of 10 types, 4,050,249 relations, 17,080 diseases · PubMedQA — MIT — 1,000 expert-labelled questions (pqa-labeled) · PubMed abstracts through E-utilities — NLM terms; abstracts may be under publisher copyright, so keep a local index and cite PMIDs · 300 multi-hop questions generated by the team: 10 templates over PrimeKG paths of 1, 2 and 3 hops (100 each), seed 0, gold answer = the exact set of end nodes.
Architecture Entity recognition and dictionary linking to PrimeKG names → hybrid BM25 + dense retrieval over abstracts, in parallel with a prize-collecting Steiner-tree subgraph of PrimeKG → 7B model in 4-bit with JSON-schema answers {claim, citations} → conformal abstention → vLLM service with traces.
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: NLP ch. 2 (BM25), NLP ch. 6 (NER; entity linking is team work, no chapter teaches it), Graph ML ch. 6 (knowledge-graph completion: TransE, R-GCN + DistMult), Graph ML ch. 10 (GraphRAG). Year 2: LLMs ch. 7 (hybrid retrieval, RAG evaluation, GraphRAG), LLMs ch. 9 (hallucinations, injections), Advanced Topics 3.4 (RAG or long context), Advanced Topics 8.2 (G-Retriever and the PCST subgraph), Control ch. 3 (schema-constrained decoding), Privacy & UQ ch. 9, ch. 11, ch. 12 (conformal prediction, UQ for LLMs, abstention), ML Ops ch. 3, ch. 5, ch. 6 (serving and P99, monitoring, RAGAS), ESP ch. 5, ch. 6, ch. 10 (prompt injection, defenses in depth, red teaming).
Deliverables and metrics Exact-set F1 on the 300 KG questions, by hop count; PubMedQA accuracy against the majority-class baseline; faithfulness (RAGAS) and citation precision on 100 audited answers; risk–coverage curve and coverage at 5 % risk; P50 / P99 latency and € per 1,000 questions; injection success rate before and after defenses; Docker service and model card.
Critical responsible-AI items R1 (upstream licences), R4 (injection red team), R6 (abstention coverage), R7 (AI Act memo: Annex I lists the Medical Device Regulation, so argue from the intended purpose why the tool is, or is not, medical-device software), R10 (who reviews flagged answers).
Research variant When does the knowledge-graph subgraph beat text retrieval, as a function of the hop count?
If a TBC slot does not open Advanced Topics, Control, Privacy & UQ, ML Ops and ESP are announced slots. Fallbacks: abstention by a score threshold chosen on a calibration split (ML 4.5); serving as in LLMs 6.5; the injection test of LLMs 9.4; a JSON validator with retry instead of constrained decoding.
Objectives served O3 (RAG, evaluation), O5 (knowledge graph + LLM), O6 (health), O7 (deployed service), O8 (abstention, injection).

C2 · Finance · Real-time financial-crime monitoring with explanations and robustness

Field Content
Brief (generic role) A payments-compliance team scores transactions at each time step, raises calibrated alerts within an analyst budget, explains each alert and shows that simple evasion attacks fail.
Data Elliptic — CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, free Kaggle account; also loaded by PyG EllipticBitcoinDataset — 203,769 Bitcoin transactions, 234,355 edges, 49 time steps, 166 features; 4,545 illicit, 42,019 licit, the rest unlabelled; train on steps 1–34, test on 35–49 (a dark-market shutdown at step 43) · optional Elliptic++ (paper; adds wallet-address nodes; check the licence of its repository before use) · optional ULB credit-card fraud — ODbL (Kaggle metadata) — 284,807 card transactions, 492 frauds, for a tabular baseline.
Architecture Feature table → gradient-boosted trees, in parallel with a temporal GNN (TGN) → stacking and calibration → conformal alert sets → integrated-gradients attribution per alert → schema-constrained analyst note → PSI drift monitor → CI gates.
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: Graph ML ch. 5 (GCN, GraphSAGE, heterophily), Graph ML ch. 8 (fraud detection), ML 4.4–4.5 (imbalanced classes, calibration), ML ch. 6 (boosting), RL ch. 4 (bandits to choose the alerts to check). Year 2: Boosting ch. 3, ch. 4, ch. 9 (XGBoost / LightGBM / CatBoost, encodings, honest benchmarking), Advanced GNN ch. 6 (TGN), ch. 7 (Nettack, evasion and poisoning), Privacy & UQ ch. 7, ch. 9 (ECE, conformal sets), ESP ch. 4 (integrated gradients, SHAP), Control ch. 3, Security ch. 2, ch. 9 (adversarial examples, structure attacks on graphs), ML Ops ch. 1, ch. 2, ch. 5 (feature store and registry, CI, PSI drift).
Deliverables and metrics Illicit-class F1 and PR-AUC per test step, before and after step 43; alerts confirmed per 100 checked; deletion-test faithfulness of the attributions; success rate of an edge-insertion attack at a fixed budget; drift alerts against measured degradation; a replay of steps 35–49 as a stream.
Critical responsible-AI items R3 (errors per time step), R4 (evasion), R5 (faithful explanations), R7 (Annex III point 5(b) lists creditworthiness scoring as high-risk “with the exception of AI systems used for the purpose of detecting financial fraud”: classify this system and justify), R10 (an analyst decides; no automatic freezing, see GDPR art. 22). No attempt to link transactions to identities.
Research variant Does the temporal GNN degrade more than gradient boosting after the shutdown at step 43?
If a TBC slot does not open Boosting, Advanced GNN, Privacy & UQ, ESP, Control, Security and ML Ops are announced slots. Fallbacks: gradient boosting from ML ch. 6; a static GNN per time window (Graph ML ch. 5, “Temporal graphs”) attacked by random edge insertion; isotonic calibration (ML 4.5) and a fixed top-k budget instead of conformal sets; PSI computed by hand.
Objectives served O4 (temporal graphs), O6 (finance), O7 (streaming service), O8 (explanations, attacks).

C3 · Pharma · Molecule design copilot with guaranteed validity and uncertainty

Field Content
Brief (generic role) A small-biotech team wants a copilot that turns a plain-language target (“soluble, crosses the blood–brain barrier, QED above 0.6”) into valid molecules on a Pareto front, each with property intervals.
Data ZINC250k through TDC MolGen — “free to use for everyone; redistribution of significant subsets requires written permission” — 249,455 molecules · QM9 through PyG QM9 — CC0 (figshare deposit) — 133,885 molecules with up to 9 heavy atoms · TDC ADME — licence per task, CC BY 4.0 for the two used here — BBB_Martins (1,975 molecules), Solubility_AqSolDB (9,982) · GuacaMol — MIT — goal-directed and distribution-learning benchmarks.
Architecture Request → schema-constrained objective spec (JSON) → small SELFIES GPT with grammar and valence masks and property guidance → GIN or GPS ensemble and EGNN predictors with conformal intervals → Pareto ranking → dual-use filter.
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: Graph ML ch. 5 (GIN), Graph ML ch. 9 (graph generation; validity, uniqueness, novelty), RL ch. 5 (REINFORCE fine-tuning with a KL penalty), the decoder of DL ch. 6 or DL P2, text generation. Year 2: GGM ch. 3 (valence mask), ch. 5 (DiGress baseline), ch. 6 (MMD, validity, FCD), ch. 7 (conditional generation, SMILES and SELFIES), ch. 8 (inverse design, Pareto front), Control ch. 3, ch. 8, ch. 9 (masks, controlling graph generators, fidelity against diversity), Geometric Data ch. 7 (EGNN), Advanced GNN ch. 4, ch. 5 (encodings, GPS), Privacy & UQ ch. 8, ch. 9, ch. 10 (ensembles, conformal, interval regression), LLMs 8.1 (function calling for the request parser).
Deliverables and metrics GuacaMol goal-directed scores; validity, uniqueness, novelty and FCD against a held-out ZINC250k split; empirical coverage of 90 % intervals on a scaffold split; parsing accuracy on 100 written requests; ablation of masks and guidance.
Critical responsible-AI items Dual-use filter (refuse objectives that maximise toxicity; block a fixed list of controlled-substance scaffolds), R1 (ZINC redistribution terms), R6 (interval coverage reported per property), R9.
Research variant How far is per-step masked decoding from exact conditioning on validity? Compare it with rejection sampling on a small grammar where both laws can be computed (Control ch. 2).
If a TBC slot does not open GGM, Control, Advanced GNN and Privacy & UQ are announced slots; Geometric Data and LLMs are mandatory. Fallbacks: the generation chapter of Graph ML ch. 9 with a SELFIES decoder (valid by construction); GIN predictors only; ensemble spread without a coverage guarantee, stated as a limit.
Objectives served O2 (molecular generation), O4 (GNN predictors), O5 (text to graph), O6 (pharma), O8 (dual use, honest uncertainty).

C4 · E-commerce · Voice-first shopping assistant in French and English

Field Content
Brief (generic role) An online retailer wants a French / English voice assistant that answers questions from reviews and recommends products, with a P95 latency under 1.5 s, a disclosed synthetic voice and watermarked audio.
Data Amazon Reviews 2023, one category — no licence stated on the page: research use only, no redistribution — 571.54 M reviews in total · FLEURS fr_fr and en_us — CC BY 4.0 — about 10 h of training speech per language · Common Voice fr and en — CC0 — download after a free registration · recordings of consenting team members (signed consent kept) and noisy synthetic queries.
Architecture Voice-activity detection → Whisper-family ASR → 3B LLM with tools {hybrid search, graph recommender, review RAG} → TTS with an AudioSeal watermark → spoken disclosure; streaming end to end, with traces.
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: NLP ch. 2 (BM25), NLP 8.5 (recommender systems), Graph ML ch. 6 (link prediction, PinSage), RL ch. 4 (LinUCB, Thompson sampling). Year 2: Speech ch. 0 (WER), ch. 4 (Whisper), ch. 5 (VAD, streaming), ch. 7 (neural TTS), ch. 9 (cloning ethics, AudioSeal), ch. 10 (turn-taking, endpointing), LLMs ch. 7 (RAG), LLMs ch. 8 (tools, ReAct), ML Ops ch. 3, ch. 4, ch. 8 (TTFT and P99, € per million tokens, tracing and guardrails), ESP 5.3 (prompt injection through reviews).
Deliverables and metrics Latency per stage (VAD, ASR, LLM time to first token, TTS) and end-to-end P50 / P95; Recall@20 and nDCG@20 of the recommender against popularity; WER per language and per self-reported accent group; faithfulness on 200 review questions; € per 1,000 conversations; watermark detection rate at 1 % false positives after Opus or MP3 compression.
Critical responsible-AI items R2 (only consenting speakers; no cloning of a real person’s voice), R3 (WER gaps across accents), R4 (injection through reviews), R7 (art. 50(1): tell users they talk to an AI; art. 50(2): mark synthetic audio), R8.
Research variant Endpointing errors (users cut off) against latency: where is the knee of the curve?
If a TBC slot does not open Speech, ML Ops and ESP are announced slots, and Speech is the core here. If it does not open, treat ASR and TTS as open black-box models, measure WER and latency only, and read Speech ch. 4, 5 and 9 in self-study; serve as in LLMs 6.5; test injections as in LLMs 9.4.
Objectives served O3 (tool-using LLM), O5 (speech, text and graph together), O6 (e-commerce), O7 (latency budget), O8 (disclosure, accent gaps).

C5 · Logistics · City logistics planner from natural-language constraints

Field Content
Brief (generic role) A last-mile delivery operator wants dispatchers to state constraints in plain language (“customer 17 only after 2 pm”, “van 3 stays out of zone B”) and to get feasible, robust tours in seconds.
Data City road graph from OSMnx — MIT code; OpenStreetMap data under the ODbL · NYC TLC yellow-taxi trips, January 2024 — NYC open data — about 3 M trips in a 50 MB Parquet file, to learn travel times · CVRPLIB benchmark instances (CVRP set X, Solomon VRPTW), read with vrplib (MIT); cite the instance papers · 200 constraint requests written by the team, 40 per type (time window, vehicle exclusion, precedence, capacity change, forbidden zone), each with a gold JSON.
Architecture Text → JSON constraint schema by constrained decoding → validator → PyVRP or OR-Tools solver, warm-started by a learned constructive policy → gradient-boosted travel times with conformalised quantiles → robust time-window check → served API.
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: Refresher CS ch. 7 (Dijkstra, A*), Graph ML ch. 5 (GAT encoder), RL ch. 5 (the attention policy for CVRP with a greedy-rollout baseline, the Y1-5 policy), ML ch. 6 (boosting for travel times). Year 2: Advanced GNN ch. 5 (graph-transformer encoder), ch. 8 (algorithmic reasoning), Boosting ch. 3, Privacy & UQ ch. 10 (conformalised quantile regression), Control ch. 3 (constrained decoding), LLMs ch. 8 (tools), Advanced Topics ch. 9 (planning and tools), ML Ops ch. 2, ch. 3 (tests, latency).
Deliverables and metrics Exact-match parsing accuracy on the 200 requests; optimality gap against PyVRP at equal time budget on CVRPLIB X instances with at most 200 customers; solver time saved by the warm start; time-window violations of the nominal and the robust plan on held-out days; end-to-end latency; a failure taxonomy of the parser on 30 cases.
Critical responsible-AI items R3 (workload fairness: spread of route durations across drivers), R10 (a dispatcher approves every plan; working-time rules are hard constraints), R1 (ODbL attribution), R9.
Research variant Does a learned warm start cut solver time on cities never seen in training?
If a TBC slot does not open Advanced GNN, Boosting, Privacy & UQ, Control, Advanced Topics and ML Ops are announced slots. Fallbacks: the GAT encoder of Graph ML ch. 5; boosting from ML ch. 6; empirical quantiles of residuals on a calibration split; a JSON validator with retry; the ReAct loop of LLMs 8.1.
Objectives served O3 (LLM parsing and tools), O4 (routing on graphs), O6 (logistics), O7 (served planner).

C6 · Chemistry · Uncertainty-aware machine-learned force field with active learning

Field Content
Brief (generic role) A computational-chemistry group wants a fast learned force field that says when its prediction is unreliable and calls the expensive quantum-chemistry solver only then.
Data rMD17 through PyG MD17 (the “revised” variants) — CC0 (figshare deposit) — 10 small molecules × 100,000 conformations with DFT energies and forces; the authors warn that the conformations come from MD trajectories and advise training on at most 1,000 of them · QM9 through PyG QM9 — CC0 — 133,885 molecules, for pre-training. The “expensive solver” is simulated: active learning reveals held-out DFT labels.
Architecture E(3)-equivariant GNN with forces = −∇E by autodiff → deep ensemble or MC dropout → conformal force intervals → active learning by query-by-committee (label the conformations where the ensemble disagrees most; budget 1,000 labels) → model registry and a rotation-invariance CI test → short molecular-dynamics run with ASE (LGPL).
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: Graph ML ch. 8 (geometric graphs; forces as −∇E; SchNet), ML ch. 6 (a boosting baseline on distance descriptors), DL ch. 3 or DL P2, equivariant models. Year 2: Geometric Data ch. 7 (EGNN and its equivariance proof), Advanced GNN 8.1 (3D molecules), Privacy & UQ ch. 8, ch. 9, ch. 10 (ensembles against MC dropout, conformal prediction, interval regression), ML Ops ch. 1, ch. 2, ch. 10 (registry, CI tests, green ops). Active learning is defined on this card: no chapter teaches it in full.
Deliverables and metrics Energy and force MAE against the number of training conformations (50, 100, 250, 500, 1,000); empirical coverage of 90 % force intervals per molecule; labels saved by active against random selection at equal MAE; energy drift over a 10 ps constant-energy run; Wh per training run (CodeCarbon).
Critical responsible-AI items R6 (honest uncertainty: coverage per molecule, not only on average), R8 (energy), R9 (model card with the validity domain), reproducibility of every number.
Research variant Does ensemble disagreement, or the conformal width, predict instability of the MD run?
If a TBC slot does not open Advanced GNN, Privacy & UQ and ML Ops are announced slots; Geometric Data is mandatory and carries the core. Fallbacks: ensemble spread without a coverage guarantee, stated as a limit; a versioned model folder and a pytest rotation test in place of a registry.
Objectives served O1 (equivariance, gradients), O4 (geometric graphs), O6 (chemistry), O7 (registry, CI).

C7 · Enterprise · Trustworthy internal assistant: red team, privacy and compliance dossier

Field Content
Brief (generic role) An HR / IT department wants an internal assistant over policies and tickets that never leaks personal data, resists prompt injection and stays within the AI Act; the team delivers the full trust dossier.
Data A synthetic company corpus generated by the team, seed 2026: an org chart of 200 employees (random tree, 2–6 reports per manager), 300 policy documents and 5,000 tickets written by a local model of at most 8B parameters from templates; fake names, e-mails and phone numbers (Faker, seeded) planted in 10 % of the tickets; 20 canary strings inserted 1, 10 or 100 times · BIPIA, an indirect prompt-injection benchmark (paper; check the licence of its repository) · JBB-Behaviors — MIT — 100 misuse behaviours and benign counterparts · AdvBench — MIT, click-through on Hugging Face — 520 harmful behaviours.
Architecture PII recognition and pseudonymisation at ingestion → hybrid RAG and GraphRAG over the org chart → least-privilege tools → DP-LoRA fine-tuning on tickets → layered guardrails → watermarked outputs → red-team suite in CI.
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: NLP ch. 6 (NER for PII), Graph ML ch. 10 (GraphRAG), Emerging ML ch. 5 (DP-SGD; P1 elective). Year 2: ESP ch. 5, ch. 6, ch. 7, ch. 8, ch. 9, ch. 10 (attacks, defenses, canaries and extraction, membership inference and DP, anonymisation and watermarking, red teaming and the AI Act), Security ch. 8 (LiRA), ch. 10, Privacy & UQ ch. 1–4 (DP, mechanisms, composition, DP-SGD), ch. 12, ML Ops ch. 8, ch. 9 (guardrails, secrets, sandbox), Control ch. 3, ch. 6 (constrained outputs, watermarking), LLMs ch. 7, LLMs 9.6 (privacy, memorisation, law and cost).
Deliverables and metrics Risk register; success rate and blast radius of adaptive attacks per defense layer; canary exposure; membership-inference TPR at 0.1 % FPR (simplified LiRA); utility against ε for ε in {1, 4, 8, ∞}; PII leak rate on 500 probing prompts; AI Act memo; model card.
Critical responsible-AI items All ten items: this is the O8 showcase. AI Act: Annex III point 4 lists employment and workers’ management (recruitment, decisions on promotion or termination, task allocation, monitoring and evaluation of workers) as high-risk, so the assistant is scoped to exclude these uses, and the scope is enforced by tools and tested.
Research variant Does DP fine-tuning remove the need for output filters?
If a TBC slot does not open ESP, Security, Privacy & UQ, ML Ops and Control are announced slots. If ESP and Security do not open, the attack side follows LLMs 9.4 and the privacy side LLMs 9.6 and Emerging ML ch. 5 (read in weeks 1–4 by students who did not take it); guardrails become input and output validators.
Objectives served O3 (RAG, fine-tuning), O5 (GraphRAG), O7 (CI red-team suite), O8 (privacy, security, law).

C8 · Public institutions · Public-service GraphRAG with voice access

Field Content
Brief (generic role) A public agency wants text or voice answers on administrative procedures that cite the official page, stay current when pages change and work for every accent. It extends Y1-8.
Data Service-Public.fr “Particuliers” guide, XML fiches on data.gouv.fr — Licence Ouverte (Etalab 2.0); the Service-Public partner page states the reuse terms · LEGI, consolidated codes, laws and regulations (DILA) — Licence Ouverte — a full archive of about 1 GB compressed plus daily increments · 300 questions written by the team, each with its gold fiche and article · Common Voice fr — CC0 · FLEURS fr_fr — CC BY 4.0.
Architecture XML ingestion → link graph (fiche ↔︎ fiche ↔︎ article) → hybrid retrieval and graph expansion → Self-Route between RAG and long context → 7B model in 4-bit with mandatory citations → abstention → optional ASR / TTS → incremental re-indexing with a freshness target.
Year 1 → Year 2 Year 1: NLP ch. 2 (BM25, inverted index), NLP ch. 6 (tagging article references), Graph ML ch. 0 (PageRank), Graph ML ch. 10 (graph expansion), the Y1-8 stack. Year 2: LLMs ch. 7, ch. 9 (RAG, hallucinations, injections), Advanced Topics 3.4 (Self-Route), ch. 4 (memory, GraphRAG), Speech ch. 4, ch. 5, ch. 7, ch. 11 (Whisper, streaming, TTS, multilingual speech), ML Ops ch. 3, ML Ops 6.1 (incremental indexing and freshness), ESP ch. 5, ch. 6, ch. 10, Privacy & UQ ch. 12 (abstention).
Deliverables and metrics Answer correctness and citation precision with bootstrap intervals; stale answers before and after re-indexing 20 changed fiches; WER per accent group; latency; Docker deployment; an accessibility statement.
Critical responsible-AI items R3 (equal access: WER gaps across accents), R4 (injection through edited pages), R6 (abstain rather than invent a procedure), R7 (art. 50(1) disclosure; the assistant informs and never decides eligibility, since Annex III point 5(a) makes eligibility assessment for public benefits high-risk), R10.
Research variant Does graph expansion help procedural questions more than factual ones?
If a TBC slot does not open Advanced Topics, Speech, ML Ops, ESP and Privacy & UQ are announced slots. Fallbacks: plain RAG with a fixed top-k; a text-only assistant; a nightly full re-index; the injection test of LLMs 9.4; a score threshold for abstention.
Objectives served O3 (RAG), O5 (graph + LLM), O6 (public institutions), O7 (deployment, freshness), O8 (equal access, disclosure).
