Extrapolons — LLGA study notes

Independent study notes organised around the brochure and reported curriculum information for LLGA: Large Language Models, Graphs and Applications. The focus is Graph × LLM: when to combine graph-structured data and language models, how to evaluate the combination, and when a simpler model is enough.

Independent, open-source notes — not an official programme publication. These materials do not replace official courses. The reading route is an Extrapolons proposal, not a course-selection plan; consult the official sources for current enrolment requirements.

Start here

Starting point: Python/NumPy and basic probability. A from-scratch affine MNIST classifier is a useful reference for the neural-network check; no prior language-model implementation is assumed. Try the checks first, then read only the support you need.

Quick positioning for selective reading
Area Show on a small example Support and next step
Statistics Compute an empirical mean and variance, explain sampling uncertainty, and distinguish training performance from held-out performance. Statistics refresher, then Machine Learning.
Computer science Implement batched affine scores in NumPy, check array shapes, and explain the time and memory costs of the main operations. Computer Science refresher, then Machine Learning.
Neural networks Connect affine logits, softmax/cross-entropy and a gradient update; check a gradient numerically and explain what a hidden layer changes. Neural Nets refresher — independent support — then Deep Learning.

An affine MNIST classifier has no hidden layer. The NN refresher prepares entry into Deep Learning; it is not automatically a substitute or an equivalent. If you can already demonstrate sound evaluation and neural representation learning, join the Graph ML and NLP branches directly. Skipping familiar reading does not waive a course requirement recorded in an official source.

Graph × LLM reading route

Extrapolons proposal: a sequence of readings and experiments, not an official track or an authorised combination of electives. Enter where the readiness evidence supports it; use complementary topics when the problem calls for them.

  1. Refreshers on demand. Use the positioning checks to select Statistics, Computer Science or Neural Nets support rather than restarting from the beginning.
  2. Machine Learning → Deep Learning. Establish evaluation and a simple baseline first, then study representation learning. Keep the split and metric fixed when comparing an affine baseline with a model that learns hidden representations.
  3. Coordinate two branches. Graph ML studies relational structure; NLP studies text representation and prediction. Use the same small technical corpus and its dependency graph to make the connection concrete, while retaining separate text-only and graph-only baselines.
  4. Large Language Models + Advanced GNN. Deepen both branches and make the first coupling decisions: graph-informed retrieval or context, learned relational representations, or no coupling when the evidence does not justify it.
  5. Complementary levers: Optimal Transport and Geometric Deep Learning. Read selectively for distribution comparison, alignment or geometric structure, according to the task and the knowledge already available.
  6. Generation, engineering and safety. Continue through Graph Generative Models, ML Ops and LLM Engineering, Security and Robustness and LLM Explainability, Security and Privacy. Evaluate validity, resource costs, failure modes and maintenance under constraints—not only predictive quality.
  7. Synthesise through independent work proposals. Use the research-internship page, transverse project, ethical-issues seminar framing, end-of-studies internship page and exit checkpoints to organise a reproducible comparison, failure analysis and statement of limits. These proposals are not official assessment requirements.

Proposed common experiment. On one held-out task over a small technical corpus and its dependency graph, compare a simple text baseline, a graph-only model and a coupled system. Make the split and allowed graph information explicit, prevent target leakage, and include a removed-edge or shuffled-edge ablation. Look for conditions where the graph adds no useful signal; report quality alongside latency and memory.

Problem-dependent bridges: OT, Topological Data Analysis and Reinforcement Learning can be useful without becoming universal prerequisites for LLMs, privacy or tool-using agents. Their place in this reading route is separate from any administrative course status.

Two-year coherence & synthesis checkpoints

Use the coherence map to inspect dependencies, Graph × LLM bridges, gaps and redundancies. Use the exit page to ask what you can build, compare and explain—not merely which pages you have read. Both are Extrapolons study aids, not statements of degree equivalence.

Two-year coherence Synthesis / exit checkpoints

  • Before coupling: retain a reproducible baseline, a documented split and metric, and an error analysis from each relevant branch.
  • At synthesis: show whether the graph improves the language-based system, include an ablation, and document resource, security and applicability limits. Return to the relevant module when evidence is missing.

Recorded divergences and limits

  1. M1 · P1 choices. The brochure records one ML choice between “Machine and Deep Learning” and “Foundations of Machine Learning”, then two choices from another four-course group. The earlier homepage instead reports Machine Learning in the curriculum core and three further choices. The detailed curriculum evidence needed to verify that second account is not available in the source summary for these notes. The combined catalogue is not a reconciled list of eligible choices, and the linked ML/DL pages are not declared equivalent to the two brochure titles.
  2. M1 · P2 obligations and choices. The brochure records two mandatory courses and two choices from five; the earlier homepage reports four curriculum core courses and one elective. The available summary does not map the brochure's mandatory pair to the expanded core list. These accounts remain separate. Reading both OT and multimodal material does not imply that both can be selected under a one-elective rule.
  3. Deep Learning: an internal inconsistency. The earlier homepage reported “at least one DL course during the year”, while the two-year coherence page described a curriculum path without DL. The complete source must be checked before stating an annual rule. Here the issue is a competence gap: advanced representation-learning work may assume skills that an affine classifier or NN refresher alone does not establish.
  4. Optimal Transport titles and focus. The brochure uses a generative-modelling-oriented OT title; the notes use ML-and-fairness-oriented wording. The catalogue preserves the linked notes' title and labels the brochure orientation separately, without inventing an exact source quotation or assuming an identical syllabus.
  5. M2 remains partly preliminary. The curriculum information is described as partially preliminary, and detailed choice rules are not documented here. Period placement and page availability are navigation aids, not guarantees of current offerings. No number of official tracks or maximum number of electives is established by this homepage.
  6. Refresher hours and credits. The Statistics/CS source discussion reports 12 h CTD, while their introductions also juxtapose 36 h and 4.5 ECTS. The cited course-sheet extract does not document those credits. These figures are not treated as one verified refresher workload. Reported contact time, other catalogue figures and any Extrapolons study-time budget remain distinct; a study budget is never converted into ECTS.
  7. Values, missing data and approximate totals. Explicit ECTS values above are retained as earlier-homepage reports, not newly verified source values. Standalone “4.5” and “2” retain their numbers without an invented unit. An earlier dash is recorded as “Value not documented”, not zero or “not applicable”. The repeated “~8 total” for Humanities, Languages and Sports has neither a documented unit nor a confirmed allocation scope and is not counted three times. Other unlisted workload or credit data are likewise not documented here.
  8. Independent support and framing. The Neural Nets refresher is outside the brochure: programme credit is not applicable to this independent support page, and no official course code is assigned. It prepares entry into DL rather than establishing equivalence with DL. Internship, transverse and other framing pages similarly provide Extrapolons work proposals, not official workload or assessment specifications.
