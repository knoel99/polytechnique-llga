Official description of the master

The LLGA Master’s specialization is a two-year, fully English-taught degree with a strong international outlook. Designed for ambitious students, the specialization equips graduates with both a solid theoretical foundation and hands-on industry expertise in Generative AI. With a special emphasis on Large Language Models (LLMs) and graph-structured data, students gain the skills to harness cutting-edge technologies and apply them across diverse fields—from healthcare and biology to finance, social networks, and beyond.

In recent years, Generative AI has grown explosively, with Large Language Models (LLMs) transforming how society and the economy interact with the digital world. From translation and code generation to creative content and advanced reasoning, these technologies achieve tasks once unimaginable. The LLGA Master’s specialization prepares students to master this field, combining theory with real-world applications. Graph-based models, such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), add another layer by capturing complex structures in networks, molecules, or logistics. Together, LLMs and graph methods enable multimodal generative AI, opening opportunities in healthcare, finance, recommendation systems, and beyond.

The LLGA specialization provides a strong foundation in machine and deep learning, plus hands-on experience with cutting-edge generative techniques. Through projects, case studies, and industry applications, graduates gain rare expertise, equipping them to lead the next generation of AI innovation.

Source: official LLGA programme page (École Polytechnique).

What is this website?

This website is an independent, open-source project. It is not created, maintained or endorsed by École Polytechnique or its professors. Its purpose is to share with the open community what may be taught during the master, based on the state of the art and on existing published work; the content is organised and completed with the help of AI and LLM tools. For enrolment rules, ECTS and official course lists, always refer to the official documentation.

Programme 2026–2027: course units and hours

This table lists the course units of the first-year LLGA track in the SynapseS 2026–2027 catalogue (semester lists LLGA 1 – S1 and LLGA 1 – S2), the second-year modules announced by the programme, and, for each one, how these notes fit its official volume. The track page and every course-unit sheet were read in full on 22 October 2026.

How to read “These notes”. Every course page has a Session plan: the official volume split into sessions (12 sessions of 3 h for a 36 h course, 4 for a 12 h refresher, 8 for a 24 h Year-2 course), each tagged with a level — 1 foundations, 2 core methods, 3 advanced — that never goes down from one session to the next. Chapters that do not fit in the official hours (instructors’ research notes, extensions) are listed as self-study with a time estimate, so the in-class part of every page holds about as much as its hours can cover. Across the two years, the difficulty rises with the periods: the refreshers stay at level 1, Period 1 courses assume them, Period 2 courses assume Machine Learning and one Deep Learning course, and Year 2 builds on Period 2.

What changed in the 2026–2027 catalogue

  • New refresher: the track lists a Refresher in Computer Graphics (CSC_51438_EP, Marie-Paule Cani, Pascal Guehl) next to the computer-science and statistics refreshers. These notes do not cover it yet.
  • Period 1 options no longer in the list: Probability Theory for ML (APM_51056_EP), Database Management System (CSC_51053_EP) and Statistical Learning Theory (APM_51059_EP) are still taught at the École in other tracks but are not part of the LLGA 1 – S1 list; their notes stay on this site for reference. The P1 options are now Deep Learning, Signal Processing, Collaborative Learning, Computer Animation, Image Analysis and Computer Vision, Topological Data Analysis and Shapes.
  • Moved course: Optimisation and Responsible AI is now CSC_51076_EP, taught in Period 1; its former code CSC_52073_EP (Period 2) is gone, and the course is not in the LLGA lists.
  • Period 2 list: LLGA 1 – S2 offers nine scientific units — Reinforcement Learning, Graph ML and Text Mining & NLP (mandatory), plus Multimodal Generative AI, Deep Learning (P2 variant), Optimal Transport, Optimization for AI, Advanced Deep Learning and Image Synthesis. Statistics in Action remains in the catalogue but not in this list; Social Media: Probabilistic Models has no 2026–27 sheet.
  • Management, culture and internship units: Period 1 has IME_51456_EP Introduction au marketing et à la stratégie (36 h); Period 2 has two new management units, IME_52441_EP Simulation d’entreprise (a one-day business serious game) and IME_52470_EP Digital Transformation and AI in Organisations and Society, which replace the wiki’s startup/entrepreneurship pair; about thirty humanities and French-culture options (for example HSS_51G16_EP Sociology of AI); and the internship is INT_52402_EP, a 16-week research-oriented internship.
  • Year 2: the catalogue shows empty LLGA 2 – S3 and S4 groups and a gap-year internship (INT_52400_EP, Stage de Césure); the Year-2 volumes below are the programme’s 24 h figure for Large Language Models (CSC_53432_EP), marked “assumed” where a course publishes none.

After the master

An overview of the roles and problem spaces the track opens onto — inferred from the curriculum and these notes, not an official employability claim.

  • Graph × LLM systems engineer — own the diagnosis: where relational structure and text together change the decision (fraud, recommendation, knowledge graphs), and where a simpler model is enough.
  • Retrieval and knowledge-platform engineer — graph-augmented RAG over document and entity graphs: multi-hop retrieval, hallucination control with neighbourhood constraints, structured citation of graph nodes.
  • ML infrastructure engineer for industrial graphs — neighborhood sampling, over-squashing, depth and GPU budgets on billion-edge graphs; versioning graphs, embeddings and evaluations.
  • Trustworthy-AI engineer — robustness, privacy and explainability inside generation pipelines: attacks on graph retrieval, leakage via sensitive subgraphs, honest explanations of GNN + LLM decisions.
  • Research engineer / PhD track — open problematics at the Graph × LLM frontier: reliable knowledge graphs feeding LLMs, neuro-symbolic reasoning, geometric deep learning beyond flat GNNs.
